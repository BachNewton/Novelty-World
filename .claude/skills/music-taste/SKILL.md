# The owner's taste

Everything here came from the owner's ear, or their own words. Claude can't hear, so this file is how a judgment survives from one session to the next. Each rule keeps its reason and what prompted it, since a reason applies to cases the rule never named.

## How this file grows

A rule goes in only when the owner says it, or agrees to it when Claude proposes it at the end of a session. One remark about one bar isn't a rule until it's confirmed as general. A rule that stops being true is rewritten, not added to. Cite the piece and bars that prompted it, and Sampler codes where they exist (`synth/vocabulary.ts`): "more like TGT-4" means more than an adjective.

## Sound

- **Native, not imitation.** A sound is judged on its own terms, never on how close it gets to a real instrument. In the owner's words, imitation is fake meat: never as good as the real thing, and it shouldn't try to be. Be natively vegetarian. They heard the fidelity of a SoundFont, and at its core it was recorded samples with a bad timbre; strings and brass were the worst.
- **No whine, ever.** The strongest dislike so far. Sampled strings and brass have it. `synth/whine.ts` measures it, but passing the check doesn't prove a sound is free of it; the ear decides.
- **No high chimes.** The tubular bells in The River Under the Ice were the sound they disliked most. Bells, glass and tinkling aren't welcome by default.
- **Rich, thick, full, whole.** They like a piece that feels whole. But richness can't come at the price of bleed: see the next rule.
- **Tight.** Too many overtones bleed into other instruments, and then there's a lot of sound coming through and lines are hard to pick out. Notes start and end cleanly.
- **Character, not the hardware's flaws.** What they love in Pokémon Emerald is its tone and treble: instruments that don't whine or sound fake. They don't want the bit crush.
- **A strong held bass is welcome.** It can act as a drone and carry great energy if done right. "Notes should decay" is a rule for upper instruments, never for bass.
- **If piano leads, it must be perfect,** and one piano won't suit every piece. The piano is chosen for each piece.

## Composition

- **Whether parts are easy to follow comes down to the composition and changes a lot with style.** It isn't an instrument problem. Don't reach for a new sound to fix a line that's hard to hear: look at the writing first.
- **They liked the writing of The River Under the Ice** even when they didn't like its sound.
- **They didn't want Porch Light.** It was removed. Nothing is known yet of why, so ask before reading a rule into it.

## Working together

- They know music theory: talk in bars, sections, themes, harmony and form. They don't have mixing vocabulary, so sound is discussed in plain words and Sampler codes.
- Never play audio aloud: listening is theirs to start.
- On a phone Composer is for listening; on a desktop it's a composition aid.
