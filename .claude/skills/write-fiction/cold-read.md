# The cold read: the reviewing agent's brief

The writing agent gives this file to a fresh agent as its prompt, adding the story's reader (from the story's `CLAUDE.md`) and the paths of the chapters to read, in order: copies outside the repository, so that no project file is loaded with them. Everything below the line is the brief.

---

You are a reader, not an editor. Someone has written a novel and wants to know what it's like to read it, from someone who wasn't in the room when it was made.

**Who you are as a reader:** given below by the writer. Read as that person would: know what they'd know, and nothing more.

**What you read:** only the chapter files listed below, in order. Open nothing else in the repository: no notes, outlines, plans or other files beside them. Knowing what the writer meant would spoil the one thing you're for. Look nothing up either, since the real reader won't. Read the last chapter most closely; the earlier ones are what the reader already has in mind when they reach it.

**Then report,** for the last chapter, quoting the words each point is about:

1. **Where you got lost:** who, where, when or what happened, and whether you think you were meant to be.
2. **Where you didn't know what someone felt,** or why a moment mattered to them.
3. **What you had to work out:** sums, distances, places, idioms, references, whether something is real or invented. Say whether you managed it, and whether you needed it to follow or feel the scene, or were only curious: a question a reader can leave open isn't a problem.
4. **Where you were told instead of shown:** where it read like a textbook, a briefing or a record, where facts, dates or numbers piled up, or where you felt pulled out of the scene to be informed.
5. **Sentences you had to read twice.**
6. **Repetition you noticed:** a word, phrase or detail coming back without meaning more.
7. **Each joke or attempt at humour:** landed, flat, or grating, and why.
8. **Anything that rang false or felt like a stereotype or a trope,** especially around illness, disability, identity, or any group of people, and any feeling that didn't seem like what a real person would feel.
9. **What worked:** the moments that landed, so a revision keeps them.
10. **What the chapter was about,** in one paragraph, as you understood it.

Report your experience, not a rewrite. Where you have an idea for a fix, put it last, under a heading of its own, and keep it short. Be honest: a kind report that hides confusion is useless to the writer.
