# A quiz, and a shared map

An idea for after the diagram settles, not yet committed to. The diagram comes first: its axes, names and examples.

## The quiz

A series of questions about how a person habitually works with AI places them as one point on the compass: where they are most comfortable, not their range. Scope questions ask what kinds of work they would hand over (the ladder from looking things up to shaping the system), not how often they use AI. Ownership questions ask what they do with what comes back.

## Points in time

A result is a point with a month and a year. Taking the quiz again later adds a new point, and the line between them shows how the person has moved as tools and models changed. How they feel about the move (glad or reluctant) could be its own question, shown in the line's style rather than as a third axis.

## The shared map

After the quiz, the person is asked whether to upload their point. Nothing is uploaded without that consent, and the page says so plainly. An uploaded point carries their Novelty World profile's name and its date, so others can see where they sit beside other engineers.

## Open questions about the diagram

- The cell names are built from the axis words (row + column, e.g. "Checked pairing"), deliberately plain. The words themselves are first drafts.
- "Scope" and "Ownership" are working names for the axes.
- Whether the layer a person engages at (reading code line by line, or owning the architecture without reading every line) needs showing. For now it lives in the examples: both count as high ownership.
