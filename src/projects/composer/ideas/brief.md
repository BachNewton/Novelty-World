# The first experiment: one brief, four formats

Which notation lets Claude write the best music? Each format gets the same brief from a Claude that sees only that format, so no piece is a transcription of another. What differs is the format, and what each writer reports about using it.

## The brief

Write an original piece of about a minute to ninety seconds that a listener would want to hear twice.

- **Character:** warm and wistful, turning hopeful, like the walk home after a good day.
- **Form:** A, a contrasting B, then A returning changed, with an ending that feels like an ending.
- **Parts:** a lead melody, an accompaniment in the `keys` role, and a `bass` line. A `pad` is optional. No drums.
- **Key and metre** are the writer's choice.
- **Original melodies only:** no quoting or paraphrasing any existing tune.
- **An analysis comes with it,** written as the project's `CLAUDE.md` says: sections, named themes with every occurrence, patterns and harmony, passing its checker.

## What each writer reports

What the format made easy, what it made hard, what errors it let through, and how much of the piece it could check without hearing it.
