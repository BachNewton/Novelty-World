## Game Overview (p. 3)

- Each player controls one **explorer** exploring a house. The house is built from room tiles as explorers enter new rooms, so it differs every game.
- Entering a new room may give the explorer something, or trigger something happening to them. Explorers' traits change during play, up or down.
- At some random point an explorer triggers a scenario called a **haunt**. When it is revealed, one explorer becomes the **traitor**, working to defeat the others; all other explorers become **heroes**. From then on the game is traitor versus heroes.
- The game contains 50 haunts.

## Object of the Game (p. 3)

- Before the haunt: explore the house and make your explorer stronger.
- After the haunt begins: be first to complete your own side's victory condition (traitor or heroes).

## Setup (p. 3)

- Set the two haunt books (Traitor's Tome and Secrets of Survival) aside; they are only used once the haunt is revealed.
- Each player chooses a character card. Each card has a different explorer on each side; the player picks one side.
- Character card anatomy (diagram): the card has four trait tracks around its edges. Labelled parts:
  - Physical Traits: Speed, Might
  - Mental Traits: Sanity, Knowledge
  - Vital Statistics: Age, Weight, Height
  - Portrait
  - Name (shown example: Zoe Ingstrom)
  - Birthday, Hobbies
- Attach 4 plastic clips to the explorer's card, one per trait (Might, Speed, Knowledge, Sanity), each pointing at that trait's starting value. The starting value on each track is printed in green.

## Setup (continued, p. 4)

- Shuffle each of the three card decks (omen, item, event) separately and place each face down as its own draw stack within reach of all players. (Card backs, pictured: Omen, Item, Event.)
- Take out the three starting room tiles: Basement Landing, Entrance Hall/Foyer/Grand Staircase, and Upper Landing. Lay them out left to right in that order, with a reasonable gap between them.
  - The Entrance Hall/Foyer/Grand Staircase is a single long tile (three rooms in a row); Basement Landing and Upper Landing are single tiles.
- Shuffle all remaining room tiles together into one face-down stack. The floor indicator on the tile backs does not matter for building the stack.
  - Diagram: a room tile's back shows a list of floors (UPPER, GROUND, BASEMENT) with the floors that room may be placed on lit up.
- Each player places their explorer's plastic figure in the Entrance Hall. Each figure's colour matches the colour behind that explorer's portrait on the character card.
- Put the dice in a shared pile.
- First player: the explorer whose birthday comes next (birthday is printed on the character card). Turns then proceed clockwise from that player.
- Table layout after setup (diagram): the three card stacks (Item, Omen, Event); the face-down room tile stack; the three starting tiles (Upper Landing, Entrance Hall tile, Basement Landing) set apart; each player's character card with clips; plastic figures on the Entrance Hall; the dice.

## How to Play (p. 5)

- Starting with the first player and proceeding to the left, each player in turn takes a turn exploring the house.

> Note: p. 4 says turns proceed "clockwise" and p. 5 says "to the left"; these describe the same direction around a table.
> Resolution (unresolved): not a rules question (an observation about the printed book), so there is nothing to settle; not researched.

- Terminology: the rules address "you" meaning whichever character is acting or being affected by a card or tile. That character may be an explorer (heroes and the traitor are both explorers) or a monster. A rule that applies only to a particular kind of character names that kind (e.g. "explorer").

### Traits (sidebar, p. 5)

- Every explorer has four traits, each a printed line (track) of numbers on the character card: Might, Speed, Knowledge, Sanity.
  - Physical traits: Speed, Might.
  - Mental traits: Sanity, Knowledge.
- Effects that raise or lower a trait move that trait's clip along its track by the stated number of spaces.
  - Example: Zoe Ingstrom starts at Might 3. An effect giving +2 Might moves the clip 2 spaces toward the maximum end, and her Might becomes 4.

> Note: the example moves 2 spaces yet the value goes from 3 to 4, so track values are not consecutive integers; a trait change counts spaces on the track, not numeric difference. Each explorer's track values must be taken from their character card.
> Resolution (community): confirmed. A trait's value is the number its clip points at, and tracks can repeat or skip numbers, so effects move the clip by spaces and comparisons (such as "highest Sanity") use the number shown, never the clip's position. Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2346196

- **Maximum value:** the highest number on a trait's track. A trait can never go above it, even if an effect would raise it further.
- **Skull:** every track has a skull symbol beyond its lowest number.
  - After the haunt has begun: if any one of your traits reaches the skull, your explorer dies.
  - Before the haunt has begun: no explorer can die. A trait cannot drop below the lowest number on its track; it stops at that lowest number instead.
  - Example: Zoe's starting Sanity is 5. An effect lowering Sanity by 2 moves the clip 2 spaces toward the skull, giving Sanity 3. If her Sanity were to drop by more than 2, she would die — unless the haunt has not started yet.
- **Damage:**
  - **Physical damage:** you split the amount however you choose between Might and Speed, moving those clips down a combined number of spaces equal to the damage taken.
  - **Mental damage** (dealt by attacks against Sanity or Knowledge): same as physical damage, but split between Sanity and Knowledge.

## How to Play (continued, p. 6)

### On Your Turn (p. 6)

- During your turn you may take any of these actions, as many times as you like and in any order:
  - Move.
  - Discover a new room.
  - Use item and omen cards.
  - Attempt a die roll.
  - Make an attack — limited to once per turn, and only after the haunt has started.
- Before the haunt has started: if you drew an omen card during your turn, you must make a **haunt roll** at the end of that turn (see "Making a Haunt Roll," p. 15).
- Further rules change once the haunt starts (see "The Haunt," p. 15).

### Move (p. 6)

- On your turn you may move a number of spaces up to your character's current Speed.
- Other actions (e.g. using an item, attacking) may be taken partway through your movement.
- Whenever any game effect makes you draw a card, for whatever reason, your movement ends for the rest of that turn.
- Example of Moving (diagram): the Entrance Hall/Foyer tile is placed with the Entrance Hall above the Foyer; a Kitchen tile adjoins the Foyer on its left, and a Dining Room tile is below the Kitchen. An explorer with Speed 3 can go Entrance Hall -> Foyer -> Kitchen -> Dining Room (3 spaces). Alternatively they could go through an unconnected doorway of the Entrance Hall or Foyer to discover a new room; if that room does not make them draw a card they may continue moving, but if it does, they must stop.

### Discover a New Room (p. 6)

- When your explorer moves through a doorway that has no room on the other side, check the top tile of the room stack: if its back lists the floor you are on (ground, basement, or upper), flip it over, connect it to the doorway you came through, and move into it. That room is now discovered.

> Note: the case where the top tile cannot go on your floor is covered under "Discover a New Room" continued on p. 8.
> Resolution (unresolved): not a rules question (an observation about the printed book), so there is nothing to settle; not researched.

- Diagram ("Possible floors"): a tile back shows UPPER, GROUND, BASEMENT; the floors the room may go on are lit.
- Place each new tile as logically as possible, lining up doors with neighbouring rooms' doors to make adjacent rooms wherever possible.

> Note: "as logically as possible" doesn't say whether lining up doors is required or only advice.
> Resolution (project): required. A new tile goes in a way that lines up the most doors with its neighbours without sealing a floor (p. 9); the player chooses among placements that tie, and placements that leave the same doorways count as one choice. This keeps the house as connected as the rule intends, and spares players choices that change nothing.
- If not all doors can be matched, the unmatched doorway becomes a **false feature** (e.g. a blocked door or a boarded-up window).

> Note: the book does not spell it out, but a false feature is presumably not passable.
> Resolution (official (1st-edition FAQ)): a mismatched door or window is a false feature that can't be moved through, and a false window doesn't count as a window. The 2nd edition has the same rule on p. 8 and in the glossary ("false feature", p. 21). Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf

### Special Rooms (sidebar, p. 7)

- Some room tiles have their effects summarised in text on the tile. Most of these have extra rules or explanations in this sidebar; such tiles show an asterisk (*) next to the room name.

#### Chasm, Catacombs, Vault, and Tower (p. 7)

- These are **barrier rooms**: each is split into two sides, and the barrier can block moving from one side to the other.
- Crossing the barrier requires the **trait roll** printed on the tile (Might, Speed, Sanity, or Knowledge, depending on the tile).
  - You may attempt the crossing roll once per turn.
  - Crossing does not cost a space of movement.
  - On a failed roll, your movement ends. On your next turn you may try the roll again, or go back the way you came.
- Explorers in the same barrier room but on opposite sides cannot fight or interact with each other in any way.
- Monsters always ignore barriers. If a monster ends its movement in a barrier room, the traitor chooses which side it is on.
- If a tile or card makes you land in a barrier room, you choose which side you end up on. If that arrival places a square token in the new room (as with the Collapsed Room or Secret Passage), the token stays permanently on the side you chose.

#### Entrance Hall, Foyer, and Grand Staircase (p. 7)

- These three share one tile but are three separate rooms. Moving from one to the adjacent one on the tile costs 1 space.
- The Grand Staircase and the Upper Landing are two separate rooms.

#### Coal Chute (p. 7)

- Entering the Coal Chute immediately moves you to the Basement Landing.
- Entering the Coal Chute and sliding to the Basement Landing together count as 1 space of movement.
- No character may end a turn on the Coal Chute tile.

#### Collapsed Room (p. 7)

- Only the explorer who discovers the Collapsed Room must make the Speed roll printed on the tile.
- After that, any explorer who enters it may either ignore the room's ability or deliberately use it to go to the basement.
- Falling to the basement does not count as a space of movement, but the explorer still takes the damage.
- Only the first explorer to fall from the Collapsed Room draws and places a basement tile:
  - Put the square "Below Collapsed Room" token on the room where they land.
  - The new basement tile is placed adjacent to any basement room already in play; if there are none, adjacent to the Basement Landing.
  - If every basement tile has already been placed, the explorer instead chooses an existing basement room to land in.
- If a monster is the first character to enter the Collapsed Room, no new tile is drawn: choose an existing basement room and place the monster's token there.

> Note: the Speed roll's target and the damage amount are printed on the tile, not in the rulebook. "Draws and places a basement tile" does not say whether to draw from the top of the stack or search it for a basement-legal tile.
> Resolution (project): Draw from the top of the room stack, as when exploring: a tile that can't go on the floor goes face down on the room discard pile. If the stack runs out, shuffle the discard pile into a new stack (rules.md, p. 9) and keep drawing; only when no basement tile is left anywhere does the explorer choose an existing basement room instead. One rule covers every "draw until a room fits" effect, so players learn it once. The only official wording for such a draw, the Widow's Walk FAQ's Drawing Room answer ("discard tiles from the room stack until you find a room with an omen symbol"), discards. Research: no ruling found on whether the new basement tile comes off the top of the room stack (discarding non-basement tiles, as when exploring) or is searched for. The 1st-edition FAQ only says where it goes: next to any basement room, or else the Basement Landing. Searched: the 1st-edition FAQ, the Widow's Walk FAQ and every thread in the BGG Betrayal rules forum. Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf

#### Junk Room (p. 7)

- If leaving the Junk Room lowers your Speed so that you would no longer have enough movement left to exit, you still get out: you stop on the tile adjacent to the Junk Room.

#### Mystic Elevator (p. 7)

- As soon as you enter this tile, it moves: roll 2 dice and place the tile adjacent to a connecting door on the floor the roll indicates.

> Note: the roll-to-floor mapping is printed on the tile, not in the rulebook.
> Resolution (unresolved): not a rules question (the mapping is tile data, to be recorded from the tile), so there is nothing to settle; not researched.

## How to Play (continued, p. 8)

### Special Rooms (sidebar, continued, p. 8)

#### Mystic Elevator (continued, p. 8)

- If there is no connecting door available on the rolled floor, the tile stays where it is.
- If the roll gives the floor the elevator is already on, you may move the tile to a different connecting door on that same floor.
- The elevator can be used only once per turn.
- Monsters and the traitor may each move the Mystic Elevator to any destination they want, without rolling. However, the tile moves only once per complete traitor/monster set of turns — the first time either the traitor or a monster enters it.
  - Example: if the traitor uses the elevator on the traitor's turn, it will not work when a monster enters it on the monsters' following turn.
- A hero must roll for the destination floor every turn they enter the Mystic Elevator, and also at the end of each full turn they spend on that tile without moving.
- If one or more explorers are already in the elevator when another explorer enters it and rolls a 0, every explorer in the elevator takes the damage.
- If another tile's or card's effect sends a character to the Mystic Elevator (e.g. the Collapsed Room or Secret Passage), the token placed by that effect stays on the Mystic Elevator tile even when the tile moves.

> Note: the floor table, the "0" result and the damage amount are printed on the Mystic Elevator tile, not in the rulebook.
> Resolution (unresolved): not a rules question (this is tile data, to be recorded from the tile), so there is nothing to settle; not researched.

#### Vault (p. 8)

- If a tile or card (e.g. the Collapsed Room) makes you land in the Vault, you arrive on the outside of the locked vault door.
- When the vault has been opened, place the square "Vault Empty" token on it.
- The traitor does not open the vault door automatically; the traitor must also attempt a roll to open it.

> Note: the opening roll itself is printed on the Vault tile, not here.
> Resolution (unresolved): not a rules question (this is tile data, to be recorded from the tile), so there is nothing to settle; not researched.

### Discover a New Room (continued, p. 8)

- Room tile anatomy (diagram, two example tiles):
  - Master Bedroom: doors (orange door markings on the tile edges), windows (marked along an edge), a symbol (omen symbol, a bird), and the room name.
  - Larder: a door on the top edge and one on the bottom edge, a symbol (item symbol, a horned skull), the room name, and rules text reading "If you end your turn here, gain 1 Might."
  - This diagram is an illustration of tile anatomy; the room tiles themselves are the source for each room's rules text.
- False doors cannot be moved through. False windows do not count as windows for any card or haunt effect, unless a haunt says otherwise.
- If the top room tile cannot be placed on your current floor, put it face down on a room discard pile. Keep discarding until you reach a tile that can go on your floor. (Some tiles are legal on more than one floor.)
- You may move through a door only when it connects to a door of an adjacent room. Doors are always open, with one exception: the **front door**, which is always locked. You cannot leave the house or use the front door unless a haunt says otherwise. "Outside" rooms such as the Patio still count as part of the house.
- Staircases link floors:
  - The Grand Staircase always connects to the Upper Landing.
  - The Stairs from Basement always connect to and from the Foyer via a secret door. The Stairs from Basement cannot be used until that room has been discovered in the basement.

### Can I Seal Off a Floor by Placing a Tile? (sidebar, p. 9)

- A tile may never be placed so that it seals off a floor — i.e. leaves no way for further rooms to connect to that floor.
- If a tile's only possible placement would seal off a floor, discard it and draw more tiles until you get one that leaves a free doorway.
- If every remaining tile for that floor would seal it off, the player whose turn it is rearranges the floor until there are more free doorways.
- The Mystic Elevator's movement can seal off a floor, even if the elevator later moves off that floor. If a Mystic Elevator roll would make it seal off a floor, the tile does not move.

> Note: read together, these say an elevator position counts as sealing a floor even though the elevator may later move away, so a roll whose result would seal a floor is void and the elevator stays put. The book does not say what happens if the elevator's departure from a floor, rather than its arrival, would seal that floor.
> Resolution (project): the case can't arise. The Mystic Elevator has one door, so leaving a floor frees the doorway it filled and never closes one; a departure can't seal a floor. The engine treats a seal-off on departure as a bug and fails loudly. Research: no ruling found on a floor sealed by the elevator's departure. This rule comes from the 1st-edition FAQ, which covers only where the elevator arrives, and BGG threads quote it without addressing departure. Searched: the 1st-edition FAQ, the Widow's Walk FAQ and every thread in the BGG Betrayal rules forum. Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1605234

### What Happens if We Get to the End of the Stack of Room Tiles? (sidebar, p. 9)

- When the room stack is used up, shuffle the room discard pile (the tiles set aside) to form a new stack and continue drawing from it.
- If there are no more room tiles for a particular floor (ground, basement, or upper), no more rooms can be discovered on that floor; it is complete.

## On Your Turn (continued, p. 10)

### Drawing Event, Item, and Omen Cards (p. 10)
- Some room tiles carry a symbol matching one of the three card types: [raven symbol] omen, [bull's head symbol] item, [spiral symbol] event.
- The first time a room with a symbol is discovered, the discovering explorer must end their movement in that room and draw the matching card. Only the first player to discover the room draws (and has their movement ended there); later visitors do not draw.
- Event symbol (spiral): draw an event card and read it aloud. Do what it says, which may include attempting a die roll. Afterwards discard it, unless the card says otherwise or has an ongoing effect.
- Item symbol (bull's head): draw an item card and read it aloud. Place it face up in front of you; you now possess the item (carrying or wearing it). Unless the card says otherwise, you may use the item once immediately and once on each of your turns.
- Omen symbol (raven): draw an omen card and read it aloud. Place it face up in front of you; you now possess the omen. The omen may require you to do something immediately. If the haunt has not yet begun, you must make a haunt roll at the end of your turn (see "Making a Haunt Roll," page 15).
- A room discovered because of a tile's or card's effect still triggers its card: if the new room has a symbol, draw the matching card.
- A room added to the board by some other means (for example, a haunt's instructions) does not trigger a draw: the first player to enter it draws no card.
- Drawing a card ends your movement, but you may still take other actions on that turn (for example, using an item).
- Illustrations (no rules weight): card backs for EVENT, ITEM and OMEN, and sample faces of Creepy Puppet (event), Revolver (item) and Dog (omen).

### Use Item and Omen Cards (p. 11)
- All explorers can use items. Monsters can use items only if the haunt's rules allow it.
- You may use an item once at any point during your turn.
- Most omen cards are treated as items: you keep the card in front of you and use it the same way as an item.
- There is no limit on how many items an explorer can carry.
- Once per turn, an explorer (or a monster that can carry items) may do each of these:
  - Trade an item with another explorer in the same room, if both agree.
  - Drop any number of items. Place a pentagonal Item Pile token in that room. Any explorer, including the one who dropped them, may later pick up some or all of the items in the pile.
  - Pick up one or more items from a pile. Remove the Item Pile token once every item in it has been picked up.
- Some items cannot be traded or stolen, but can still be dropped and picked up. The item card states whether a given action is allowed for that item.
- The Bite, Dog, Girl and Madman are not items, so they cannot be dropped, stolen or traded. Their cards say so.
- Per item, an explorer (or monster that can carry items) may take only one of these actions with that item in a turn:
  - Use it.
  - Trade it to another explorer.
  - Drop it.
  - Steal it (see "Special Attacks" on page 13).
  - Pick it up.
- "Using" an item covers making any attack or die roll with it, or any other action that involves the item in any way. Example: an explorer cannot attack with the Spear and then trade the Spear to another explorer on the same turn.
- Trait bonuses above the printed maximum: if an item would raise one of your traits past the maximum printed on your character card, note how far "over the top" the item puts that trait. If you later lose the item, the loss is taken from that "over the top" number, not from the printed maximum.
  - Example: an item gives +2 Might, but only 1 Might is gained before reaching the maximum. Losing that item costs only 1 from your maximum Might.
  > Note: the example says you "lose only 1 from your maximum Might" — read as: the trait drops by 1 from its maximum value, i.e. back to where it was before the item.
  > Resolution (official (1st-edition FAQ)): the reading is right. You note how many spaces the item pushed the trait past its printed maximum. Losing the item removes those noted spaces first and then any real gain, so the trait ends where it was before the item. Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/253903
- Illustrations: sample faces of Armor (item) and Dog (omen, companion) and an omen card back. The Armor card's printed text reads, in substance: any time (not only once per turn) you take physical damage, take 1 less point of damage; it cannot be stolen. ### Item Tokens (p. 12)
- Many haunts place one or more item tokens in the house; their special rules come from the haunt.
- Unless the haunt says otherwise, item tokens can be traded, dropped or stolen exactly like item and omen cards.

> Note: "Item Tokens" is a ruled box at the top of p. 12. The "Weapons" and "Companions" paragraphs below it sit outside the box with no heading of their own; they most likely continue "Use Item and Omen Cards" (p. 11), but the page does not say.

#### Weapons (p. 12)
- The weapons are: Axe, Blood Dagger, Revolver, Spear, Sacrificial Dagger.
- A weapon can be used only when making an attack, never when defending (see "Make an Attack," page 13).
- Only one weapon may be used per attack, but an explorer may carry more than one.
- Using a weapon in an attack is optional.

#### Companions (p. 12)
- The Dog, Girl and Madman omen cards are companions. A companion follows the explorer who has custody of it.
- Companion omens have no physical or mental traits.

### What Happens if the Rules in the Book and the Rules on a Card Conflict? (p. 12)
- When a card and the rulebook conflict, the card's rule wins.

### Attempt a Die Roll (p. 12)
- Each die has faces showing 0, 1 or 2 dots. (The picture shows two dice, one showing 1 dot and one showing 2.)
  > Note: the face distribution (how many faces of each value) is not stated on this page.
  > Resolution (community): each of the 8 dice has two blank faces, two 1s and two 2s (0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 2). Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/791600
- There is no limit on how many times you may roll dice in a turn. Example: drawing a card by moving onto a room tile, where the tile also requires a roll, means two rolls in one turn.
- You cannot attempt the same roll more than once per turn. Example: you cannot keep rolling on one turn to try to open the Vault.
- When a card, tile or other effect tells you to roll a specific number of dice, roll them and add up the dots on all of them; that total is the roll's "result". Then apply what the effect says for that result.

#### Damage Rolls (p. 12)
- "Take 1 die of physical damage" means: roll 1 die and take that many points of damage, split between Might and/or Speed.
- For more than 1 die of damage, add the dots on all the dice rolled.
- Mental damage works the same way, but is split between Knowledge and Sanity, as you choose.
  > Note: for physical damage the book does not say explicitly who chooses the Might/Speed split; it says "as you choose" only in the mental-damage sentence. The combat example on p. 14 has the damaged player choosing the physical split.
  > Resolution (official): the damaged explorer chooses. The rulebook says so for physical damage in the Traits sidebar (p. 5: you split it however you choose) and in the glossary ("physical damage", p. 20). Source: https://instructions.hasbro.com/api/download/F3148_en-us_avalon-hill-betrayal-at-house-on-the-hill-second-edition-cooperative-board-game.pdf

#### Trait Rolls (p. 13)
- A card, room tile or haunt may tell you to attempt a roll on one of your explorer's traits (Might, Speed, Knowledge or Sanity).
- Roll as many dice as the explorer's current value in that trait, and add the dots for the result. Example: an explorer with Sanity currently 4 rolls 4 dice for a Sanity roll.
- The card's or tile's text gives the outcome for success or failure.
- An attack roll is not a trait roll, even though it uses Might or another trait (see "Make an Attack," below).

#### Task Rolls (p. 13)
- Some haunts require a roll to succeed at a particular task (for example, an exorcism).
- Only one such roll may be attempted per turn, even if more than one kind of roll could accomplish the task (for example, either a Knowledge roll or a Sanity roll for the exorcism).

### Make an Attack (p. 13)
- No one can attack anyone until after the haunt starts.
- Once during your turn, you may attack an opponent in the same room. An opponent is an explorer or monster that wants to stop your movement or interfere with you.
- Attacking: you roll dice equal to your Might; your opponent does the same. The higher result wins and inflicts physical damage on the loser (explorer or monster) equal to the difference between the two results. Example: you roll 6, opponent rolls 5, so you inflict 1 point of physical damage.
- A tie means no one is hurt.
  > Note: the wording "Whoever rolls a higher result defeats his or her opponent" means the defender can also win and inflict the damage on the attacker.
  > Resolution (official (1st-edition FAQ)): yes, the defender can win and damage the attacker. The FAQ rules that a hero who loses an attack on a stunned monster "takes no damage", framed as an exception. The distance-attack rule (p. 13: an attacker beaten by the target takes no damage) is also framed as an exception. Both assume that an attacker who loses normally takes damage. Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf
- Attacks with another trait: some effects allow an attack using a trait other than Might. It works exactly like a Might attack, but both sides roll the other trait. Example: in a Speed attack both sides roll dice equal to Speed. Speed attacks deal physical damage.
- Attacks with Sanity or Knowledge deal mental damage.
- You cannot attack an opponent using a trait the opponent lacks. Example: a monster without Sanity cannot be the target of a Sanity attack.
- Sometimes defeating an opponent lets you do something other than inflict damage, for example steal an item (see "Special Attacks," below).
- Defeating a monster only stuns it, unless a haunt says it is killed (see "How Monsters Work," page 18).
- You may attack a stunned monster if there is another benefit to doing so (for example, stealing an item from it, or killing it with a special item).
- A stunned monster still rolls dice to defend, but if the attacking hero loses, the hero takes no damage.
- In the same turn you may both take a haunt-specific action (as described in the haunt's rules) and attack.

### Special Attacks (p. 13)

#### Distance Attacks (p. 13)
- The Revolver's ability is an example of a distance attack.
- A distance attack targets someone in another room within your "line of sight": a path that runs through an uninterrupted straight line of doors.
- If the target of your distance attack defeats you, you take no damage.
- Some monsters can also make distance attacks.

#### Stealing Items (p. 13)
- If your attack would inflict 2 or more points of physical damage, you may instead steal a tradable item or omen from the defeated opponent, inflicting no damage.
- An item's or omen's card says if it cannot be traded (and so cannot be stolen).
- Items and omens cannot be stolen with a distance attack.
- Some haunts have special stealing rules.

## The Haunt (p. 15)
- Once the haunt begins, the game changes (story framing omitted).

### Making a Haunt Roll (p. 15)
- Before the haunt starts, each time you draw an omen card you must roll 6 dice at the end of your turn. This is the "haunt roll".
- If the result is less than the total number of omen cards drawn by all players so far this game, the haunt starts. The player whose roll starts it is the "haunt revealer".
- Example: you draw an omen on your turn and it is the fifth omen drawn this game; a haunt roll of 4 or less starts the haunt.
- After the haunt has started, discovering a room with an omen symbol still makes you draw and keep an omen card, but no haunt roll is made.

> Note: the glossary entry "haunt roll" (p. 21) gives a different procedure (roll dice equal to the number of omen cards in play, haunt begins if the result is lower than that number). The two are not reconciled in the book; see the note under that glossary entry. The owner must choose which to implement.
> Resolution (community): p. 15 is the rule and the p. 21 glossary entry is a typo. At the end of a turn on which you drew an omen, roll 6 dice. The haunt starts if the result is less than the number of omen cards drawn so far this game, counting the one just drawn. The 1st edition, which has no glossary, has the same rule, and its official FAQ confirms the worked example on p. 15. No erratum was ever issued, and Hasbro's current printing of the 2nd edition still has the typo. The glossary's version (dice equal to the number of omens) resembles the haunt roll of Betrayal at Baldur's Gate and Betrayal Legacy. Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2338612, https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf, https://instructions.hasbro.com/api/download/F3148_en-us_avalon-hill-betrayal-at-house-on-the-hill-second-edition-cooperative-board-game.pdf

### Revealing the Haunt (p. 15)
- The haunt revealer looks up the haunt chart on the first two pages of the Traitor's Tome booklet. The chart gives the haunt revealed and who the traitor is.
- Reading the chart: omen cards run across the top and rooms down the left side. Take the omen card drawn just before the haunt roll and the room the haunt revealer's explorer was in when that omen was drawn; the cell where they meet is the haunt number to play.
- The haunt's entry below the chart says which player becomes the traitor. Give that player the Traitor's Tome. The haunt revealer is not necessarily the traitor.
- Special cases: if two or more players could qualify as traitor and one of them is the haunt revealer, the haunt revealer is the traitor. If none of them is the haunt revealer, the traitor is the qualifying player closest to the haunt revealer's left.
  > Note: the book says "the player closest to the haunt revealer's left" without repeating "of those who could be the traitor"; reading it as limited to the candidates is the natural sense, but it is not stated outright. It also says "neither one", worded for two candidates, though the rule covers "two or more".
  > Resolution (community): the tie-break is only among the tied candidates, and it works for any number of them. If the haunt revealer is one of the tied explorers, the revealer is the traitor. Otherwise the traitor is the first tied explorer going left (clockwise) from the revealer. Several independent BGG answers agree, and the 3rd-edition rulebook words the rule this way ("the player closest to the haunt revealer (in turn order)"). Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2936819, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1645119, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2326732

## The Haunt (continued, p. 16)

### Optional Rule: Selecting the Haunt (p. 16)
- Optional; lets the group avoid repeating haunts already played.
- If the chart gives a haunt you have already revealed and do not want to replay, find the placed room with an omen symbol [raven symbol] that is next closest to the haunt revealer. Cross-reference that room with the original omen on the chart to get a new haunt number.
- Keep moving on from room to room this way until you reach a haunt you have not revealed yet.
- If every placed room has been tried without success, take the next omen card you have and repeat the process with it.
> Note: "closest to the haunt revealer" is not defined (by spaces of movement or otherwise), nor is the room order after the first step. "The next omen card you have" is also unspecified (whose cards, in what order).
> Resolution (project): measure "next closest" by spaces along the route from the revealer's room, nearest first, the revealer choosing among rooms at the same distance. Once every placed omen room has failed, try the revealer's other omens in the order they were drawn, each against every omen room, nearest first. If nothing unplayed turns up, play the original haunt. This keeps the printed order (rooms, then "the next omen card you have") and always settles on a haunt. Research: no ruling found. In the one BGG thread on this optional rule, players read it differently: some try the revealer's other omens in the same room, others move to a nearby omen room. None cites an official answer. Searched: the 1st-edition FAQ, the Widow's Walk FAQ and every thread in the BGG Betrayal rules forum. Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2266084

### Haunt Setup (p. 16)
At the start of the haunt:
- The traitor takes the Traitor's Tome and leaves the room, and reads only the haunt being started. The traitor also needs to know "The Traitor's New Powers" (page 17) and "How Monsters Work" (page 18); a traitor who doesn't know them should take the rulebook along and read those sections, or have another player explain them.
- All other players become heroes. They look up the same-numbered haunt in Secrets of Survival and read it together, and should briefly discuss a survival plan.
- When everyone (traitor included) is ready, the traitor returns. Heroes and traitor carry out whatever the haunt's "Right Now" section tells them to do (for example, placing tokens in the house or drawing cards).

### Heroes and Secrets (p. 16)
- Heroes should not tell the traitor their goals, unless they are sure the traitor already knows them or they are doing something the haunt requires.
- Until the group knows how a given haunt works, heroes should not disclose that haunt's Secrets of Survival text. (Keeping it secret can give the heroes an advantage, since the traitor doesn't know what they are trying to do.)

### Playing the Haunt (p. 16)
- Turn order: the first turn always goes to the player on the traitor's left, then play proceeds clockwise (to the left, away from the traitor). Each hero takes a "hero turn".
- After every hero has taken a turn, the traitor takes a "traitor turn". After that, any monsters the traitor controls take a "monster turn". So the traitor effectively gets two turns: one for their own actions and one for the monsters. Then the first hero to the traitor's left goes again, and so on.
- Heroes and traitor are all still explorers and can do everything they could before the haunt, except that no one makes haunt rolls any more (even when drawing an omen card).
- Each turn, the traitor must tell the heroes what they are doing, but not why. The same applies to the heroes.
- Explorers can die once the haunt has begun. If any one of an explorer's four traits drops to the skull symbol [skull symbol], that explorer dies.
- In some haunts, a hero's "death" turns that explorer into a traitor instead.
- Some haunts require something to be done a number of times equal to the number of explorers; that count includes explorers who have died.

### What Happens if a Haunt's Rules and the Regular Rules Conflict? (p. 17)
- The haunt's rules win.
- All of the regular rules stay in effect unless a haunt says otherwise.
- Even if the traitor is transformed or otherwise removed at the start of the haunt, the traitor still gets a turn after all the heroes.
- Even if the traitor dies, the monsters still take their turn (controlled by the traitor's player) as long as the monsters can still complete the haunt's goals.
- During a haunt, if anyone in the house makes a successful Knowledge roll to learn something, all the other heroes learn that information too.

> Note: the three paragraphs after the ruled conflict box (traitor still gets a turn; monsters act after the traitor dies; shared Knowledge-roll information) have no heading of their own. They are listed here because they follow that box; they may belong to "Playing the Haunt" (p. 16).
> Resolution (unresolved): not a rules question (where the paragraphs sit on the page), so there is nothing to settle; not researched.

### Moving Past Opponents (p. 17)
- After the haunt starts, an explorer or monster must spend 1 extra space of movement to leave a room for each opponent in that room. Heroes slow the traitor and monsters, and vice versa.
- However many such penalties apply in a turn, you can always move at least 1 space. This also holds when a monster rolls 0 for movement (see "How Monsters Work," page 18).
- Stunned monsters do not slow an explorer's movement this way.

### The Traitor's New Powers (p. 17)
- On becoming the traitor, your explorer is freed from any ongoing effect of a previously drawn event card that was impeding them (for example, the Debris or Webs).
- In addition, unless a haunt says otherwise:
  - You may use any beneficial text on a room tile and ignore any harmful text. Examples: you pass through the Revolving Wall without rolling; you choose where the Mystic Elevator goes when you use it. But you still slide to the Basement Landing when you move onto the Coal Chute.
  - You may choose not to be affected by an event card or the Bite omen card. If you choose to be affected, you decide after reading the card but before making any rolls or taking any other action it states, and you must then accept the results of any rolls and any penalties.
  - After you finish your turn, you move and attack with all of the monsters, if there are any. You still control the monsters even if the traitor dies. (In some haunts the monsters can still complete the haunt's goals after the traitor is dead.)

### Haunts with a Hidden Traitor (p. 17)
- A few haunts have a "hidden traitor", whose identity is kept secret from all other players.
- When a haunt calls for a hidden traitor: take small monster tokens of one color numbered 1 up to the number of players, shuffle them, and deal one to each player face down (the "S" side showing). The player who gets token 1 is the traitor.
- Play proceeds clockwise starting from the player to the haunt revealer's left.
  > Note: the book does not say outright that this replaces the "first turn goes to the traitor's left" order (p. 16); that is the evident intent, since the traitor is unknown. It also doesn't say when the hidden traitor takes a traitor turn or a monster turn; the haunt presumably specifies.
  > Resolution (community): yes. In a hidden-traitor haunt, play continues in normal order, starting from the player to the revealer's left, since nobody knows who the traitor is. Any extra turn for the traitor or for monsters comes from the haunt's own text. Only one BGG answer addresses this directly, but nobody challenged it. Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/600958

## The Haunt (continued, p. 18)

### Haunts with a Hidden Traitor (continued, p. 18)
- Boxed note at the top of p. 18: a haunt with a hidden traitor has no entry in the *Traitor's Tome*. The hidden traitor's goal and abilities are printed under that haunt in *Secrets of Survival*, which every player reads.
- Unless the haunt says otherwise, the hidden traitor may reveal themself at any time by turning the token numbered 1 face up.
  - Example given: the traitor may reveal in response to a trap or hazard in the house, so as to avoid its effect or damage under the normal traitor rules (see The Traitor's New Powers, p. 17).
- When an explorer dies, that player turns their token face up, revealing their identity.
- No player other than the traitor may reveal a face-down token at any time. A player may claim not to be the traitor, but the others have only that player's word.
- Unless the haunt says otherwise, any explorer may attack any other explorer at any time, whether or not they really suspect that explorer of being the traitor. (The book notes that the traitor gains from suspicion and mistrust among the explorers.)
- Unless the haunt says otherwise, all conversation must happen where everyone at the table can hear it; no player may leave for a side conversation.

### How Monsters Work (p. 18)
- Monsters follow the rules below unless a haunt says otherwise.
- Each monster moves and takes all of its actions before the next monster goes.
- **Movement.** At the start of a monster's turn, roll a number of dice equal to its Speed. The result is the number of spaces that monster can move this turn.
  - For a group of monsters of the same type (examples given: Bats, Zombies), roll once for the whole group; every monster of that type can move that many spaces this turn.
  - Cross-reference (Moving Past Opponents, p. 17): a monster that rolls 0 for movement can still move at least 1 space.
- **Most monsters can't be killed.** If a monster would take any damage, it is instead **stunned** and misses its next turn.
  - When a monster is stunned, flip its token to the side showing "S". At the end of the monster's next turn, flip it back.
  - Stunned monsters can't slow an explorer's movement (i.e. they don't impose the Moving Past Opponents penalty).
  - Even when a haunt says to do something other than stun monsters when they take damage, those monsters can still be stunned by effects that specifically stun monsters.
  - Diagram: a numbered monster token (shown with the number 9) with an arrow to its flipped side, which shows a large "S".
- **One attack per turn.** Like explorers, a monster can attack only once during its turn.
  - Monsters often attack with a trait other than Might.
  - Monsters can't make any of the special attacks listed on page 13 (unless a haunt says otherwise).

## The Haunt (continued, p. 19)

### How Monsters Work (continued, p. 19)
- **Room tile text.** Like the traitor, monsters can ignore any harmful text on a room tile. The book adds these specifics:
  - A monster that moves onto the Coal Chute still slides to the Basement Landing.
  - Monsters pass through the Revolving Wall without rolling.
  - Monsters can move up or down freely from the Coal Chute and the Collapsed Room, and can climb up to the Gallery.
  - Monsters can't benefit from room tile text that increases a trait (examples given: the Larder, the Gymnasium).
- **Card movement.** Monsters can use the special movement options described on cards (examples given: Secret Stairs, Secret Passage).
- **No exploring.** Monsters can't explore new rooms.
- **No items.** Monsters can't carry items, unless the haunt says otherwise.
  - If a monster that is allowed to carry items is stunned, it drops all of its items: place an Item Pile token in its room. That monster can't pick the items back up until it has a turn in which it is no longer stunned.
- **Stuck in the basement.** If a monster is stuck in the basement with no way to reach the heroes, then on the traitor's turn the traitor's player may search the room stack for the Stairs From Basement tile and place it next to any open basement doorway, then shuffle the room stack.
  - This rule doesn't apply when the haunt lets monsters explore new rooms.

> Note: The book says "on the traitor's turn" for the stuck-monster search, not on the monster turn.
> Resolution (official (1st-edition FAQ)): confirmed as written. The FAQ ruling that introduced this rule also says the traitor searches "on his turn". Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf

### What Happens to My Stuff if I Die? (p. 19)
Boxed sidebar.
- **Companions** (the Dog, the Girl, the Madman): when your explorer dies, the companion's card and its token (if it has one) stay in the room where your explorer died. Set that omen card aside.
  - If another explorer enters that room, that explorer gains custody of the companion and takes its omen card.
- **Other items:** all your other items drop in that room. Place an Item Pile token there and set the item cards aside. Other explorers can go to that room to pick up the items, taking their cards.

> Note: The sidebar speaks of items only; it doesn't say what happens to a dead explorer's non-companion omen cards. Whether they count among the "items" that drop is not stated here.
> Resolution (community): an omen that works as an item drops into the item pile like an item card. That covers every omen except the Bite and the companions, and the companions stay in the room as the sidebar says. The 1st-edition FAQ already treats every omen except the Dog, Girl, Bite and Madman as droppable, tradable and stealable. No source rules on the Bite. Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2582950, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1498662, https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf

## Winning the Game (p. 19)
- The first side (the traitor or the heroes) to complete its goals for the haunt wins the game.
- A side's goal is given in its haunt book under "You Win When . . ."; it doesn't necessarily require killing the traitor or the heroes.
- At least one hero must survive for the heroes to win.
- Some haunts' goals let the traitor win even after the traitor dies; for example, the monsters the traitor's player controls may be able to win without the traitor.
- When a side completes its goals, a player from the winning side reads aloud the "If You Win . . ." section from that side's haunt book.

## Glossary (p. 20)
The glossary defines terms used in the rulebook, the haunt booklets, and on the cards and tiles. In the book, a game term is printed in bold the first time it appears. Entries are alphabetical; sub-entries (italic in the book) are nested under their parent term. Each entry's printed page is given in brackets.

- **adjacent** [p. 20]: Two rooms are adjacent if they share a side. Rooms that touch only diagonally are never adjacent.
- **attack** [p. 20]: Neither explorers nor monsters can attack until the haunt has started. After the haunt starts, you may make one attack roll against an opponent during your turn.
  - *attack roll* [p. 20]: You and your opponent each roll dice equal to Might. Whoever rolls higher deals physical damage to the loser equal to the difference. On a tie, nobody is hurt. See "Make an Attack," p. 13.
    > Note: The book literally says both roll dice "equal to your Might". Read with p. 13, this is taken to mean each side rolls its own Might; confirm against p. 13.
    > Resolution (official): each side rolls dice equal to its own Might, as p. 13 says (you roll dice equal to your Might, and your opponent does the same). The 1st-edition FAQ adds that all attacks use Might unless a card or ability says otherwise. Source: https://instructions.hasbro.com/api/download/F3148_en-us_avalon-hill-betrayal-at-house-on-the-hill-second-edition-cooperative-board-game.pdf, https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf
  - *distance attack* [p. 20]: Some weapons, or special items in a haunt, let you attack an opponent in a different room that is within your **line of sight** (see that entry, p. 22). If your attack roll is lower than your opponent's, you take no damage.
- **card** [p. 20]: There are three card types: event, item, and omen. Explorers draw cards as they discover new rooms. Whenever you draw a card, read its text aloud and follow its instructions.
  - *event card* [p. 20]: Marked with a spiral symbol. After following its instructions, discard it, unless the card says otherwise or has an ongoing effect.
  - *item card* [p. 20]: Marked with a bull's head symbol. Place it face up in front of you; the item is now in your possession. See "Use Item and Omen Cards," p. 11.
  - *omen card* [p. 20]: Marked with a raven symbol. Place it face up in front of you; the omen is now in your possession. It may require you to do something immediately. At the end of your turn, if the haunt hasn't started yet, you must make a haunt roll. Most omens work like items.
- **character** [p. 20]: Explorers, monsters, and haunt-specific opponents (example given: Dracula) are all characters.
- **character card** [p. 20]: The game has six character cards, each with a different explorer on each of its two sides. A character card shows the character's name, portrait, traits, and other information.
- **companion** [p. 20]: The Dog, Girl, and Madman omen cards are companions; a companion follows whichever explorer has custody of it. Companion omens have no physical or mental traits.
- **damage** [p. 20]: An explorer can take damage from losing an attack and from many card, tile, and haunt effects. Damage is either physical or mental. For each point of damage taken, lower the matching traits on your character card by that many spaces in total, in any one trait or split across the matching traits.
  - *physical damage* [p. 20]: Might and Speed are the physical traits. You choose how to split physical damage between those two traits.
  - *mental damage* [p. 20]: Knowledge and Sanity are the mental traits. You choose how to split mental damage between those two traits.
- **discover** [p. 20]: When an explorer moves through a door that has no room connected to it, draw a new room tile from the stack and place it beside that door. The explorer then moves into that room, discovering it. See "Discover a New Room," p. 6.
- **die roll** [p. 20]: Many cards, rooms, and haunt rules call for a die roll of X+ for an explorer, where X is a number (example given: a Knowledge roll of 4+).
  - There is no limit on how many times you can roll dice in a turn, but you can't attempt the same roll more than once per turn.
  - Each die face has 0, 1, or 2 dots. Roll the stated number of dice and total the dots; if the total is equal to or greater than the stated number, the roll succeeds. See "Attempt a Die Roll," p. 11.
    > Note: the glossary cites p. 11 as printed, but "Attempt a Die Roll" is on p. 12.
    > Resolution (unresolved): not a rules question (an observation about the printed book), so there is nothing to settle; not researched.
  - *trait roll* [p. 21]: A die roll based on one of the explorer's (or monster's) traits: Might, Speed, Sanity, or Knowledge. Roll as many dice as the character's current value in that trait (not its starting value, if the trait has changed).
  - *task roll* [p. 21]: Some haunts call for a roll to succeed at a particular task (example given: an exorcism). You can attempt only one such roll per turn.
> Note: the die roll entry's sub-entries trait roll and task roll are printed at the top of p. 21; they stay nested under their parent here.
> Resolution (unresolved): not a rules question (an observation about the printed book), so there is nothing to settle; not researched.

## Glossary (continued, p. 21)

- **door** [p. 21]: Doors connect rooms. You can move through a door if it connects to another door in an adjacent room. Doors inside the house are always open.
  - *front door* [p. 21]: The front door (in the Entrance Hall) is always locked, unlike other doors. You can't leave the house or use the front door unless a haunt says otherwise.
- **explorer** [p. 21]: The character each player controls. After the haunt begins, the explorers include both the traitor and the heroes.
- **false feature** [p. 21]: When it isn't possible to match two doors or windows in adjacent rooms, a false feature is created instead. You can't move through a false door. A false window doesn't count as a window.
- **figure** [p. 21]: Each character card has a matching plastic figure, matching the colour behind the portrait, which represents that explorer in the game.
- **haunt** [p. 21]: An explorer triggers the haunt scenario with a successful haunt roll. A haunt describes how to win the game and adds new rules, including monsters. Explorers can die during the haunt. See "The Haunt," p. 15.
  - *hero* [p. 21]: After the haunt begins, every explorer other than the traitor becomes a hero, trying to survive the house and the traitor's plans.
  - *traitor* [p. 21]: After the haunt begins, one explorer becomes a traitor and turns against their former companions. A few haunts have a **hidden traitor**, whose identity is secret from all other players (see "Haunts with a Hidden Traitor," p. 17).
- **haunt roll** [p. 21]: Before the haunt begins, you must make a haunt roll at the end of your turn whenever you discover a room with an omen symbol [raven symbol]. Roll dice equal to the number of omen cards in play. If the result is lower than that number, the haunt begins.

(3) p. 15 ties the roll to drawing an omen card; this entry ties it to discovering a room with an omen symbol (in normal play these coincide).   - *haunt revealer* [p. 21]: The player whose omen roll triggered the haunt. The haunt revealer looks up the haunt on the Haunt Chart to see who the traitor is.
- **haunt-specific action** [p. 21]: Many haunts require explorers to take special actions (examples given: an exorcism roll, destroying an object). You can take a haunt-specific action and also attack on the same turn.
- **item** [p. 21]: Explorers can carry and use item cards and many omen cards. Items can also be picked up, dropped, traded, or stolen. See "Use Item and Omen Cards," p. 11.
  - *item token* [p. 21]: Many haunts place one or more pentagonal item tokens in the house, with special rules for their use. Unless the haunt says otherwise, item tokens can be traded, dropped, picked up, or stolen exactly like item and omen cards.
  - *weapon* [p. 21]: The weapons are the Axe, Blood Dagger, Revolver, and Sacrificial Dagger item cards and the Spear omen card.
    - You can use a weapon only when making an attack, never when defending (see "Make an Attack," p. 13).
    - You can use only one weapon per attack, though you may carry more than one.
    - Using a weapon during an attack is optional.

## Glossary (continued, p. 22)
Entries continue alphabetically; sub-entries (italic in the book) are nested under their parent term. Each entry's printed page is given in brackets.

- **line of sight** [p. 22]: You have line of sight to an opponent if you can trace a path to them through an unbroken straight line of doors.
- **move** [p. 22]: Explorers and monsters can move through the house each turn.
  - A character can move up to as many spaces (rooms) as their current Speed.
  - Monsters instead roll dice equal to their Speed and can move as many spaces as the result.
  - You can take actions (examples given: using an item, attacking) partway through your movement.
- **opponent** [p. 22]: An explorer or monster that wants to stop your movement or interfere with you during a haunt. Monsters and the traitor are opponents of the heroes, and the heroes are opponents of the monsters and the traitor.
  - After the haunt starts, an explorer or monster must spend 1 extra space of movement to leave a room for each opponent in that room with it.
- **room** [p. 22]: The House on the Hill is made up of rooms that you discover and move through. Each room counts as 1 space of movement. Passages (example given: the Dusty Hallway) and outside areas (example given: the Patio) also count as rooms.
  - The back of each room tile names one or more floors: ground, basement, or upper. You can place the tile in any legal location on any of the floors it lists.
  - Some room tiles have rules text that triggers whenever an explorer enters, leaves, or takes a special action in that room.
  - Many room tiles carry symbols matching card symbols. Only the first explorer to discover such a room is affected by the symbol.
  - *barrier room* [p. 22]: A room in two parts, which can stop you from moving to its other side. The Chasm is an example.
- **stack** [p. 22]: Game cards and room tiles are shuffled and placed face down in stacks that players draw from.
- **steal** [p. 22]: If you attack an opponent and would deal 2 or more points of physical damage, you may instead steal a tradable item or omen from that opponent, in place of dealing the damage. See "Special Attacks," p. 13.
- **stunned** [p. 22]: Unless a haunt says otherwise, monsters usually aren't killed when defeated. A monster that would take any damage is stunned instead and misses its next turn. Stunned monsters can't slow an opponent's movement.
- **symbol** [p. 22]: Every card has a symbol printed on it:
  - raven silhouette = omen
  - bull's head = item
  - spiral = event
  - Some room tiles carry symbols matching the card symbols. The first explorer to enter such a room must stop moving there and draw the matching card.
- **token** [p. 22]: Cardboard pieces representing special items or features.
  - *monster tokens* [p. 22]: Come in seven colours and are numbered for tracking. Special monsters use large circular tokens with the monster's name printed on them.
  - *item tokens* [p. 22]: Pentagonal, and also numbered.
  - *trait roll tokens* [p. 22]: Triangular. They track special task rolls during haunts.
  - *event/room tokens* [p. 22]: Square. They mark special features created by cards or room tiles (examples given: the Hidden Stairs, the Mystic Slide).
- **trait** [p. 22]: Each explorer has four traits, shown as rows of numbers on the character card: Might, Speed, Knowledge, and Sanity. Each trait has a starting value (highlighted on the card) and a maximum value (the highest number for that character). See "Traits," p. 4.
  > Note: the glossary cites p. 4 as printed, but the "Traits" sidebar is on p. 5.
  > Resolution (unresolved): not a rules question (an observation about the printed book), so there is nothing to settle; not researched.
  - *physical traits* [p. 22]: Speed and Might.
  - *mental traits* [p. 22]: Sanity and Knowledge.
## Glossary (continued, p. 23)

- **turn** [p. 23]:
  - Before the haunt starts, players take turns in order: the player whose explorer has the next birthday goes first, then play passes to the left. On your turn you can move, discover rooms, use items, and attempt die rolls. After the haunt starts, you can also attack once per turn.
  - After the haunt starts, the first turn goes to the player on the traitor's left, and play passes to the left from there. Each hero takes a hero turn. Once every hero has taken a turn, the traitor takes the traitor turn. After the traitor turn, any monsters the traitor controls take a monster turn.
- **use** [p. 23]: All explorers, and some monsters, can use items (and many omens). Using an item means making any attack or die roll with it, or taking any other action that involves the item in any way. You can use an item once, at any point during your turn.
  > Note: The book's wording ("use an item once at any point during your turn") doesn't make clear whether each item may be used once per turn, or only one item use is allowed per turn. Check against "Use Item and Omen Cards," p. 11.
  > Resolution (official (1st-edition FAQ)): each item may be used once per turn, and you may use as many different items as you like in a turn. The FAQ says so directly. The 2nd edition has the same per-item wording: p. 10 says you may use an item once immediately and once on each of your turns, and p. 11 allows one action per item per turn. Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/66560, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/578295
- **window** [p. 23]: Some rooms have windows on one or more sides, as well as doors. You normally can't move through a window, but some haunts give windows special rules. The rooms with windows are the Bedroom, Grand Staircase, Master Bedroom, Chapel, and Dining Room.

### What If There Isn't a Rule for That? (p. 23)
Boxed sidebar.
- If the rules or haunt books don't clearly answer a question that comes up in play, the group agrees on whatever makes the most sense and plays on. If the group can't agree, flip a coin to decide.
