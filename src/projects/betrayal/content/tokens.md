# Tokens

Every cardboard token the game uses, base game and Widow's Walk. Counts come from the base rulebook's component list (rules.md, Game Components) and the Widow's Walk component list (widows-walk-rules.md, Game Components, also quoted in the Widow's Walk FAQ). Where a list gives only a total, the split is counted from the owner's own token set (numbered tokens print their numbers, so a kind's count is its number range) or worked out from the cards, tiles and haunts that use the tokens, the official FAQs and BoardGameGeek, and a note says how. Haunts and cards name tokens by shape, colour and size ("small purple monster token", "pentagonal item token", "triangular Sanity Roll token"); the headings below use those same words.

The counts record the physical box. In this digital version they are not a global limit: supply is unlimited unless a haunt's text makes running out a rule (haunt 24's "never more than 24 Bats", haunt 27's recycled Blob tokens, the star haunt's "if one is available"). Where a shortfall comes only from the cardboard, every token the rules call for is placed.

Haunts often give a token a role name ("the Girl token", "a Root token", "Bat tokens"). The role name never means a separate piece: the haunt's setup says which physical token stands for it.

## Large circular monster tokens

- Shape and size: large circle. Haunts call them "the large circular <Name> token".
- Two-sided. The front shows the monster's art and name; the back shows a large "S" and the name. A stunned monster is flipped to the "S" side and flipped back at the end of its next turn (rules.md, How Monsters Work).
- Not numbered.
- Purpose: special named monsters (and, in one haunt, a stand-in): haunts place them in setup.

## Small circular monster tokens

- Shape and size: small circle. Haunts call them "small <colour> monster tokens".
- Seven colours: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, magenta.
- Numbered. The front shows the colour's monster art and a number; the back shows a large "S" (the stunned side, and the face-down side when tokens are dealt secretly).
- Purpose: ordinary monsters and stand-ins for anything a haunt needs to track by number (pairs, hidden identities, secret draws). Hidden-traitor haunts deal tokens of one colour numbered 1 up to the number of players face down ("S" side up); token 1 is the traitor (rules.md, Haunts with a Hidden Traitor).

| Colour | Count | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| red | 24 | 1–24 |
| orange | 24 | 1–24 |
| green | 16 | 1–16 |
| blue | 9 | 1–9 |
| yellow | 6 | 1–6 |
| purple | 6 | 1–6 |
| magenta | 6 | 1–6 |
| **total** | **91** | |

- Set: base. The colours total 91, matching the rulebook.
- Supply limits the haunts run up against: haunt 24 sets aside all 24 red; haunt 47 sets aside all 16 green; haunt 27 sets aside at least 20 orange; haunts 07 and 23 pair orange and green tokens by number (up to 10 pairs in haunt 07); haunt 43 needs one colour numbered up to twice the number of explorers (12 at six players, so red, orange or green); haunt 50 uses red 1–20.

> Note: the rulebook gives the total (91) and the seven colours, but not how many of each. The per-colour counts are counted from the owner's token set; each colour's tokens are numbered 1 to N (red 1–24, orange 1–24, green 1–16, blue 1–9, yellow 1–6, purple 1–6, magenta 1–6), and the seven ranges add up to the rulebook's 91.
> Resolution (official): confirmed by the number ranges printed on the owner's tokens. The haunts corroborate parts of it: haunt 24 (haunts/24-fly-away-home.md) sets aside all 24 red; haunt 27 (haunts/27-amok-flesh.md) needs at least 20 orange; haunts 47 (haunts/47-worm-ouroboros.md) and 70 (haunts/70-to-reach-the-cosmos.md) set aside all the green, 16 in haunt 47; haunt 53 (haunts/53-till-morning-light.md) uses blue 1 to 9; haunt 97 (haunts/97-lambs-to-the-slaughter.md) deals yellow numbered 1 up to the number of explorers (6 at six players).

> Note: haunt 11 (Let Them In) puts a face-down small purple token in the Entrance Hall and in every room with an outside-facing window. Only 6 purple tokens exist. The base game has exactly six such rooms (the Entrance Hall plus the five window rooms haunt 24 lists: Grand Staircase, Master Bedroom, Bedroom, Chapel, Dining Room), so the base game fits exactly. Widow's Walk adds the Drawing Room and Sewing Room to the window rooms (widows-walk-rules.md, Glossary Update: Window), so with the expansion haunt 11 can need up to 8 purple tokens, 2 more than exist.
> Resolution (owner): every eligible room gets its token. In this digital version, supply is unlimited unless a haunt makes its limit a rule, and haunt 11 doesn't.

## Small circular explorer tokens (Widow's Walk)

- Shape and size: small circle, the same size as the small monster tokens.
- Six colours, matching the character cards: white, red, yellow, green, blue, purple. Two-sided: each side shows one of the two explorers on that colour's character card.
- Not numbered.
- Count: 36, 6 of each colour (printed on the Widow's Walk rule sheet).
- Purpose: a reminder that an explorer has already used a room's once-per-game power. When you use the power of the Chapel, Gymnasium, Larder, Library, Menagerie or Study, leave one of your explorer tokens in that room (see `widows-walk-rules.md`). The Widow's Walk FAQ amends the four base tiles to say so. Haunts may also use these tokens. The Letter omen lets you give one of your explorer tokens to another explorer.
- Set: Widow's Walk.

- Purpose: mark a feature that an event card, a room tile or a haunt has added to a room.

## Room token rules

A token prints only its name, so its rules come from the card or tile that places it (cards/events.md, rules.md); they are restated here. Rolls are trait rolls on the named trait, and a table's result applies to the explorer who rolled.

### Blessing (base)
- While a hero is in the Blessing's room, that hero rolls 1 extra die on every trait roll, to a maximum of 8 dice.
- Heroes only, not the traitor. Attacks are not trait rolls, so the Blessing never adds a die to an attack (see A Moment of Hope in cards/events.md).

### Burial Mound (Widow's Walk)
- Once the haunt has been revealed, each time an explorer leaves the Burial Mound's room, the corpse attacks that explorer: the player on that explorer's right makes a Might 4 attack against them.
- If the explorer wins that fight by 3 or more, discard the Burial Mound token.

### Closet (base)
- Once during each explorer's turn, that explorer may roll 2 dice to open the Closet.

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 4 | Draw an item card. |
| 2–3 | Draw an event card. |
| 0–1 | Draw an event card and remove the Closet token. |

> Note: the card doesn't say the explorer must be in the Closet's room to roll (see Closet Door in cards/events.md).
> Resolution (unresolved): see the resolution under Closet Door in cards/events.md.

### Drip (base)
- While an explorer is in the Drip's room, that explorer rolls 1 fewer die on every trait roll, to a minimum of 1 die. Attacks are not trait rolls.

### Safe (base)
- Once during each explorer's turn, that explorer may make a Knowledge roll to open the Safe.

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 5+ | Draw 2 item cards and remove the Safe token. |
| 2–4 | Take 1 die of physical damage. The Safe stays closed. |
| 0–1 | Take 2 dice of physical damage. The Safe stays closed. |

### Secret Passage (base, 2 tokens)
- The two Secret Passage tokens are linked. Moving from one to the other counts as moving 1 space; the passage itself is not a space.
- Any explorer may use the passage on later turns. No one may end a turn in the passage.
- When the card is drawn, the second token goes in an existing room chosen by a 3-die roll: 6 any room; 4–5 an upper floor room; 2–3 a ground floor room; 0–1 a basement room. The drawing explorer may use the passage at once, even with no movement left.

### Secret Stairs (base, 2 tokens)
- The two Secret Stairs tokens are linked, and sit on different floors. Moving from one to the other counts as moving 1 space; the stairs themselves are not a space.
- The explorer who drew the card may follow the stairs at once, even with no movement left, and draws an event card in the room they arrive in if they do. Using the stairs later draws no card.
- The stairs stay usable on later turns by any explorer, and by monsters (the 1st-edition FAQ's official ruling; see Secret Stairs in cards/events.md).

### Skeletons (base)
- Once during an explorer's turn, that explorer may make a Sanity roll to search the Skeletons.

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 5+ | Draw an item card and remove the Skeletons token. |
| 0–4 | Nothing found; take 1 die of mental damage. |

### Slide (base)
- Any explorer may make a Might roll to use the Slide.

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 5+ | You control the Slide: put yourself in any explored room on any floor below the Slide. |
| 0–4 | Draw room tiles from the stack until you draw a basement room, and place it (if the stack has no basement rooms, choose a basement room already in play). You fall to that room and take 1 die of physical damage. If it isn't your turn, don't draw a card for that room. |

- Unlike the Closet, Safe, Skeletons and Wall Switch, the Slide has no once-per-turn limit: the Mystic Slide card says only that on later turns any explorer may make the same roll.

### Smoke (base)
- The Smoke blocks line of sight from adjacent rooms.
- While in the Smoke's room, an explorer rolls 2 fewer dice on all trait rolls, to a minimum of 1 die.

### Wall Switch (base)
- The token sits on a wall (or corner) shared by two rooms. Once during an explorer's turn, an explorer in either room may make a Knowledge roll to use the Wall Switch.

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 3+ | The explorer finds the switch and goes through to the other room. This doesn't count as moving a space. |
| 0–2 | The explorer can't find the switch and can't go through. |

### Below Collapsed Room (base)
- Marks the basement room where the first explorer to fall from the Collapsed Room landed (rules.md, Collapsed Room). The Rope omen lets its holder move from the room with this token up into the Collapsed Room.

### Vault Empty (base)
- Marks the Vault once it has been opened (rules.md, Vault).

### Plant (Widow's Walk)
- Flytrap event card: the Plant goes in the drawing explorer's room. Once the haunt has been revealed, whenever any hero, traitor or monster enters the Plant's room, the explorer who holds the Flytrap card may make a Might 7 attack against them.
- Tree House tile: the Plant goes on any open door of the roof or upper floor, and the Tree House's larger door counts as adjacent to that door (rooms.md, Tree House).
- Haunt 86 (Rootstalks) and the star haunt (Food) give Plants their own meaning; see haunts/86-the-woods-in-the-cabin.md and haunts/star-seasons-of-the-witch.md.

### Lock (Widow's Walk)
- Locked Room tile: a Lock goes on each of the room's 3 doors. To go in or out through a locked door, an explorer tries a Knowledge roll of 3+ to take the Lock off (rooms.md, Locked Room, which also records the one-roll-per-turn ruling).
- The Key omen lets its holder pass through the Locked Room without removing a Lock, and, at the start of each of their turns, put a Lock token on an unlocked Locked Room door they are next to. It also adds 4 dice to rolls to open the Locked Room (cards/omens.md, Key).
- Haunt 53 puts a Lock on the Panic Room's door; its Surgers can get through only with three or more Surgers on either or both sides, which removes the Lock (haunts/53-till-morning-light.md).
- The traitor and monsters ignore Lock tokens unless a haunt says otherwise (see `widows-walk-rules.md`).

### Obstacle and Fountain (Widow's Walk)
- Placed only by Widow's Walk haunts, never by a card or tile. Each haunt that uses them names what they represent and gives their rules (see the Widow's Walk table above).
- The traitor and monsters ignore Obstacle tokens unless a haunt says otherwise (see `widows-walk-rules.md`).

## Pentagonal item tokens

- Shape: pentagon. Haunts call them "pentagonal item tokens"; the rulebook glossary says they are numbered.
- Base total: 14, made up of:

| Token | Count | Numbers | Faces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Item | 10 | 1–10 | front "ITEM" and a number; back "ITEM" with no number |
| Item Pile | 4 | none | "ITEM PILE" on both sides |

- Purpose of the numbered Item tokens: haunt-specific objects (the Sarcophagus, Roots, Paint, Parachutes, Keys, the Toy Airplane, and so on). Unless the haunt says otherwise they can be traded, dropped, picked up or stolen like item and omen cards (rules.md, Item Tokens). Haunt 34 hands them out face up by number, 1 up to the number of players.
- Purpose of the Item Pile tokens: mark items dropped in a room, including those dropped by a dead explorer or a stunned item-carrying monster. Remove the token when the last item in the pile is picked up (rules.md, Use Item and Omen Cards).
- Set: base. Widow's Walk adds none.

> Note: the rulebook counts 14 pentagonal tokens without splitting them. 10 are numbered Item tokens, which leaves 4 Item Piles. A player's count of their own box on BoardGameGeek confirms it: "10 numbered items" and "4 item piles" (https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/600958).
> Resolution (community): 10 numbered Item tokens and 4 Item Piles, per one player's count on BoardGameGeek, consistent with the rulebook total. Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/600958
> Note: haunts that need more than 10 item tokens at once can run short. The BoardGameGeek thread above was about haunt 34 (Guillotines), where players found more Key locations than numbered tokens.
> Resolution (designer): see haunt 34's own notes in haunts/34-guillotines.md (the designer says the collar numbers were meant to be a separate numbered set).

## Triangular Trait Roll tokens

- Shape: triangle. Haunts call them "triangular <Trait> Roll tokens".
- The trait is printed on both sides ("KNOWLEDGE ROLL" and so on). Not numbered.
- Purpose: count successful special rolls in a haunt (for example, tokens placed toward a banishing or a rescue, usually up to the number of players).

| Token | Set | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge Roll | base | 6 |
| Might Roll | base | 6 |
| Sanity Roll | base | 6 |
| Speed Roll | Widow's Walk replacement sheet (not in the box) | 3 |

- Base total 18, matching the rulebook.

> Note: the rulebook counts 18 triangular tokens without splitting them, so the per-trait count of 6 is inferred: 18 split evenly over the three base traits. It fits the haunts, which ask for up to "the number of players" (6) of one trait, and in haunt 49 for 6 Knowledge Roll and 6 Sanity Roll tokens at once. No base haunt uses a Speed Roll token.
> Resolution (unresolved): not researched; the inferred split is the only even one.
> Note: the Speed Roll tokens were meant for the Widow's Walk sheet but were left off it. The FAQ says to use an item token in their place, or print the replacement sheet.
> Resolution (official): Widow's Walk FAQ. Source: the Widow's Walk FAQ (One of the Master's Affairs; Seasons of the Witch).

## Turn/Damage track

- A counter. The base game has one Turn/Damage track (rulebook component list). Haunts use it to count turns or damage: each haunt says what it starts at and when it moves up or down.
- Treated as a plain number with no printed range, by the owner's decision. Its physical length matters only if a haunt counts past the end of the track.
