# 79 — The Twins

Summary: Two explorers have fused into the linked Twins, bound by a cord of energy and trying to open a Seam that will collapse the house; the heroes must kill one of them.

## Traitor (Traitor's Tome)

### Setup
- Two explorers stay in the game and are now the Twin traitors.
- For each trait, you may lower one Twin's trait to raise the other Twin's same trait by the same amount.
- Set aside Obstacle tokens, representing Nodes, equal to six minus the number of heroes. (With one hero, set aside five tokens.)

### Rules
#### How to Weave the Seam
- On your turn, you may drop a Node, the same way as dropping an item, on a doorway or other way of moving between rooms or floors. No one in the house can move through or see through that route afterward, including you and your Twin.
- Once all your Nodes are placed, you must remove a Node from the house before dropping a new one.
- You may not put a Node in a room that already has one.
- You may not drop a Node if it would seal off one room, or a group of rooms, from the rest of the house. Example from the book: if the Bathroom (one door) is next to the Arsenal (two doors), you may not put a Node on either of those doors, because it would seal off the Bathroom.
  > Note: The book gives only "it would seal off the Bathroom" as the reason for both doors. Blocking the Arsenal's other door actually cuts off the Bathroom and the Arsenal together, which is the "set of rooms" case; this is interpretation.
- If you do not move on a turn in which you start in a room with a Node, you may instead tap the Node's power to raise one of your traits to its starting value.
#### Special Attack Rules
- If you take damage, you may split it between you and your Twin, as long as each of you loses at least 1 point in a trait.

### Traitor wins when
- All your Nodes are placed and you can trace a path between you and your Twin that touches every Node and includes at least twenty rooms.
- The path may be any path of movement you could make as the traitor, without going through the same door or other exit twice.

> Note: the printed win condition doesn't say what counts as a path. The last bullet above is official errata.
> Resolution (official): errata. Any route you could move along as the traitor counts: you may ignore locked doors, go through the Secret Stairs, use dumbwaiters, or anything else you could do without playing cards, as long as no door or other exit is used twice. The FAQ adds the sentence to the end of You Win When. Source: https://www.dropbox.com/s/3y34f1s2tn6o4mx/Widow%27s%20Walk%20FAQ%20KB%2012.6.161.pdf?dl=0

### What they know about the other side
- The heroes are trying to stop you from making a Seam: an energy wave that will stretch between you and your Twin once you are far enough apart.

> Note: "Set aside a number of Obstacle tokens ... equal to six minus the number of heroes" — the book's only example is one hero (five tokens), implying very few heroes in this haunt.
> Note: A Node goes "on a doorway", yet "You may not put a Node in a room that already has one" treats Nodes as being in rooms.

## Heroes (Secrets of Survival)

### Rules
#### Nodes
- The Twins leave Nodes of energy around the house.
- You may attempt a Knowledge roll of 5+. On a success, choose one: destroy the Node (it goes back to the Twins), or move one of the Twins up to 5 spaces toward you.
  > Note: The book doesn't say whether you must be in the Node's room, how often you may try, or whether the roll targets a Node at all for the second option. The traitor's book doesn't specify either.
#### Special movement rules
- You cannot move through a Node or trace line of sight through it; passage from one side of a Node to the other is blocked.
- The Nodes can never cut off a room or group of rooms from the rest of the house.
### Heroes win when
- One of the Twins is dead.
### What they know about the other side
- The Twins have merged into a hybrid being, the two halves of a powerful energy connection. Together they are trying to create a Seam of energy that could bring the house crashing down.
