# 13 — Perchance to Dream

Summary: An explorer has fallen into an unwakeable sleep and its nightmares are escaping into the world; the heroes must wake the dreamer with the Holy Symbol before too many Nightmares get out.

## Traitor (Traitor's Tome)

### Setup
- Tip your explorer's figure over in the room it is in when the haunt is revealed. Your body is asleep: it cannot move or take any actions.

> Note: the setup says only that the sleeping body "cannot move or take any actions". It doesn't say whether the traitor still takes turns, or whether the body defends, makes rolls, can be moved, attacked or killed, or slows anyone down.
> Resolution (project): asleep means the body never chooses or does anything, but things can still happen to it. The traitor's seat keeps its traitor turn and its monster turn: a traitor taken out of play when the haunt starts still gets a turn (rules p. 17). The body has nothing to do on the traitor turn, but the player still makes every choice the rules give to the traitor rather than to the body: unleashing Nightmares, moving monsters when a hero uses the Bell or Spirit Board, choosing which side of a barrier room a monster is on, and searching for the Stairs from Basement for a stuck monster. The body can't move, explore, use, pick up, trade or drop anything, attack, or attempt a roll by choice. No effect can move it out of its room either (the Dark Dice's 5 can't pick it), though it goes with its room if the Mystic Elevator moves. It is still an explorer and the heroes' opponent, so heroes may attack it. It defends with Might as normal, because defending isn't an action. It makes any roll an effect forces on it (the Dynamite, an open Music Box), and it takes damage, with its player choosing the split. Like a stunned monster, it can't get in anyone's way, so it doesn't slow movement out of its room. This keeps the haunt's picture of a helpless sleeper, and it keeps the dreamer's room fixed as both the heroes' goal and the place Nightmares appear. Research: no ruling found. The BGG haunt 13 threads discuss only monster movement and balance, and the designer's revised 1st-edition Traitor's Tome words the rule the same way. Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1536030, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2005833, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1531522, http://web.archive.org/web/20060614164031/http://www.wizards.com:80/avalonhill/rules/BHH_TraitorRev_120105.pdf

- Drop all your items.

> Note: the setup doesn't say which cards count as "all your items". Nor does it say whether losing a card that costs traits when lost (the Holy Symbol, the Bell, the Book, the Amulet of the Ages) can kill the dreamer, when the clamp in the next step covers only the companions.
> Resolution (project): every item card and every omen that works as an item goes on one item pile in the body's room: every omen except the Bite and the companions, as when an explorer dies (rules p. 19). The Bite stays with the dreamer and does nothing more. Kept event cards aren't items and stay with the dreamer, but a sleeping body can't use them. Dropping a card loses it, so its loss text applies, but no setup step can kill the dreamer: any trait the drop would take to the skull stops at its lowest value above it, as the next step says for companions. A dead dreamer would end the haunt before it began, which the companion clamp shows the haunt means to prevent. Heroes may pick up the pile as usual. Nothing can be handed to the body afterwards, because picking up and trading are actions.

- If you have the Dog, the Girl or the Madman card, set it aside and adjust your traits to match. This adjustment cannot kill you: if it would, reduce the affected trait to its lowest value above the skull symbol instead.

> Note: "set it aside" doesn't say whether the companion can come back into play.
> Resolution (project): it is out of the game for the rest of this haunt. It doesn't stay in the room for a hero to take, as a dead explorer's companion would (rules p. 19): the haunt sets it aside instead of leaving it behind.

- Put small purple monster tokens, one per player (the number of tokens equals the number of players), into the room with your sleeping body. They are Nightmares.
- Secretly count the "escape rooms" in the house:
  - Rooms with outside-facing windows count: Grand Staircase, Master Bedroom, Bedroom, Chapel, Dining Room.
  - Rooms that open to the outside also count: Conservatory, Entrance Hall, Gardens, Graveyard, Patio, Tower, Balcony.
  - A room still counts as an escape room even if it is placed so its window is false (faces another wall).
  - If there are fewer escape rooms in the house than there are players, search the room stack and add rooms from the lists above to any appropriate floor until the number of escape rooms equals the number of players. Then shuffle the room stack.
  - Write down the number of escape rooms and keep it secret from the other players.

> Note: the two lists name base-game tiles only. They don't say whether the Widow's Walk window rooms (Drawing Room, Sewing Room) or its outside rooms (Roof Landing, Solarium, Tree House, Widow's Walk) count.
> Resolution (project): they count. The window list is the base glossary's list of rooms with windows, and the "open to the outside" list is exactly the base outside rooms plus the Entrance Hall. So the escape rooms are every room with windows, every outside room, and the Entrance Hall, from the rulebook's and the Widow's Walk rule sheet's own lists (rooms.md marks both). The Widow's Walk glossary update exists so that haunts referring to windows and outside rooms take in the new tiles. Research: no ruling found.

> Note: the top-up doesn't say who chooses the added rooms or where they go, whether the room discard pile is searched, what happens to a tile with no legal place, or what "the number of players" counts.
> Resolution (project): the traitor's player chooses each room to add from the escape rooms not yet in the house, searching the room stack and the room discard pile alike. Tiles set aside while exploring are still waiting to enter the house, as the Jump's search for the Patio is settled (cards/events.md). The traitor places each one at any legal spot on a floor its back lists, under the usual placement rule: as many doors lined up as possible, and never sealing a floor (rules pp. 6 and 9). A tile with no legal spot can't be added. If every remaining escape room has none, the count stays below the number of players. An added room draws no card, now or later (rules p. 10). "The number of players" means every player in the game, traitor included, fixed when the haunt is revealed. The number written down is the count after the top-up: it is the number the traitor's goal names. The setup is the traitor's part, so its choices are the traitor's. With twelve base escape rooms, two of them on the starting tile, the top-up can always reach six in practice.

- Set aside escape tokens equal to the number of escape rooms in the house. Any distinctive set of tokens (for example, item tokens) can serve.
### Monsters
- Nightmares — Speed 5 · Might 4 · Sanity 4

> Note: the haunt doesn't say how many Nightmares move each monster turn, or how their movement is rolled.
> Resolution (community): every Nightmare that isn't stunned moves on each monster turn, under the rulebook's monster rules (p. 18). One roll of 5 dice at the start of the monster turn sets the movement for all of them, since they are a group of the same type. The traitor moves them one at a time, in any order, and each finishes its move, its attack and any escape before the next one goes. BGG answers agree, against a group that had moved one Nightmare per turn. Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2005833, https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf

### Rules
#### How Nightmares Escape
- A Nightmare in an escape room escapes from the house by spending 1 space of movement.

> Note: the rule doesn't say whether a Nightmare can escape partway through a move, whether heroes in the room make escaping cost more, or whether a Nightmare pulled into an escape room by a hero's Bell or Spirit Board can escape.
> Resolution (project): escaping is the Nightmare's move out of the room, made on its own monster turn with its rolled movement. It may enter an escape room and escape with a space it has left, or escape from the room where it began. Leaving the room costs 1 more space for each hero there who isn't stunned (rules p. 17: moving past opponents). But a Nightmare can always move at least 1 space in a turn, so one that hasn't yet moved can always escape. A stunned Nightmare can't move, so it can't escape. A Bell or Spirit Board pull spends no movement and never lets a Nightmare escape. This makes heroes guarding an exit matter, which is the heroes' main defence, without letting them seal an exit by standing in it. Research: no ruling found.

- Once a Nightmare has escaped from a room, no other Nightmare can escape from that room. Put an escape token in the room to mark it as used.
- Escape rooms discovered after the haunt starts can also be used by Nightmares to escape, but they do not raise the total number of escapes you need.
- Whenever a Nightmare is killed or escapes the house, you can unleash another Nightmare: put a Nightmare token into the room with your figure. Tokens are reused as needed.

> Note: the rule doesn't say when the new Nightmare arrives, whether it can act on the monster turn in which it arrives, whether unleashing is optional, or how many Nightmares can be in play.
> Resolution (project): the traitor's player decides at once, whoever's turn it is, whether to unleash a replacement into the sleeping body's room. One that isn't unleashed then is lost, not saved for later. A Nightmare unleashed during a monster turn doesn't move or attack until the next monster turn (the general rule under How Monsters Work, rules p. 18). One BGG answer plays it this way, and it stops a lucky first roll from emptying the house in one turn. Each kill or escape lets in one replacement, so the Nightmares in play never outnumber the setup's one per player. "Tokens are reused" means no supply limit beyond that. Source: https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1536030

#### Special Attack Rules
- Nightmares can attack explorers using Might, but the damage they inflict is mental instead of physical.

> Note: the rule speaks of Nightmares attacking. It doesn't say what damage a Nightmare deals when it wins as the defender.
> Resolution (project): every point of damage a Nightmare deals is mental, whether it is attacking or defending. The heroes' half says plainly that Nightmares inflict mental damage instead of physical, with no condition. The damaged hero splits it between Sanity and Knowledge, and the Skull can still turn it into physical damage. The Armor doesn't reduce it, because it isn't physical.

- If a hero attacks a Nightmare and defeats it, the Nightmare is killed instead of stunned. If a Nightmare is defeated while it is the attacker, it is stunned as normal.

> Note: the rule doesn't say whether a hero may attack a stunned Nightmare to kill it, which other ways of beating a Nightmare kill it, or whether the trait used matters.
> Resolution (official): a hero may attack a stunned Nightmare and kills it on a win. If the hero loses, the hero takes no damage (rules p. 13: you may attack a stunned monster when doing so has another benefit, such as killing it, and a hero who loses that attack takes no damage). The 1st-edition FAQ allows the same attack on a monster that can be killed. Any attack a hero makes kills a defeated Nightmare: Might, the Ring's Sanity, the Revolver's Speed (a distance attack, so the hero takes no damage on a loss), or the attack the Bloody Vision forces. A Nightmare that fails the Speed roll against a hero's Dynamite is killed too: the FAQ treats that as being defeated in physical combat, stunned or killed as the haunt says, and the Dynamite replaces the thrower's attack. The designer's revised 1st-edition Secrets of Survival says simply that a defeated Nightmare is killed. Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf, http://web.archive.org/web/20060110122045/http://www.wizards.com:80/avalonhill/rules/BHH_SurvivalRev_120105.pdf

### Traitor wins when
- The number of Nightmares that have escaped equals the number of escape rooms that were in the house when the haunt was revealed (the number you wrote down). When this happens, show the heroes the number you wrote down.

> Note: the haunt doesn't say what happens if the dreamer dies, or if every hero dies.
> Resolution (project): either one ends the game at once with the traitor's win. Killing the traitor helps the heroes only if their goal says so (1st-edition FAQ), and a dead dreamer can't be woken, so the heroes can no longer win. The Nightmares keep coming back, and an escape can't be stopped from the room it starts in, so playing on could only end with the traitor's goal met. With every hero dead, the heroes can't win either (rules p. 19), and the general ruling under Winning the Game applies. The same holds if the Holy Symbol ever leaves the game. Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf

### What they know about the other side
- The heroes are trying to wake your physical body.

## Heroes (Secrets of Survival)

### Setup
- Set aside triangular Sanity Roll tokens equal in number to the number of players.
- Set aside the same number of triangular Might Roll tokens.
### Rules
#### How to Wake the Dreamer
- The Holy Symbol must be brought to the dreamer's room and used to wake that explorer:
  1. Carry the Holy Symbol to the room with the dreamer's body in it.
  2. As long as a hero in that room carries the Holy Symbol, any hero in the room can attempt a Sanity roll or a Might roll of 5+ to wake the dreamer. After each success, take a Sanity Roll or Might Roll token (matching the trait used). The dreamer awakens when the heroes have tokens equal in number to the number of players.
  3. The Smelling Salts card can't be used to awaken the dreamer.

> Note: the steps don't say how often a hero may roll, whether a hero may roll on another player's turn, whether the roll uses up the Holy Symbol for the turn, whether a mix of tokens counts, or what happens to tokens already won if the Holy Symbol later leaves the room.
> Resolution (project): the wake roll is a task roll. Each hero may attempt it once on each of their own turns, with Sanity or Might but not both (rules p. 13: one roll per turn for a task, even when several kinds of roll could do it). The 1st-edition FAQ rules the same way for exorcisms. It can be taken partway through a move, and in the same turn as an attack (p. 13). The roller needn't carry the Holy Symbol, but some hero in the room must carry it at the moment of the roll. One lying on an item pile doesn't count. The roll is the roller's own trait roll, so cards that change trait rolls apply to it (the Blessing adds its die). The Holy Symbol only has to be present, so the roll isn't a use of it, and its carrier may still trade or drop it that turn. Tying the roll to one explorer's item would make the same roll an item use for some rollers and not for others. Any mix of Sanity and Might tokens counts toward the target, as in haunt 2. The target is the number of players at the reveal, dead explorers included (rules p. 16). Tokens won are never lost. The heroes win the moment the last one is taken. Source: https://media.wizards.com/2015/downloads/ah/FAQ_BHotH_102605-2.pdf

#### Special Attack Rules
- Nightmares inflict mental damage instead of physical damage.
- If a hero attacks a Nightmare and defeats it, the Nightmare is killed instead of being stunned.
### Heroes win when
- The heroes wake the dreamer before an unknown number of Nightmares escape into the world. (The traitor knows that exact number.)
### What they know about the other side
- The dreamer's subconscious mind has released Nightmares into the house. If enough Nightmares escape the house, the heroes lose.
- The heroes don't know how many need to escape, so they must stop as many as they can. The traitor has written that number down, based on rules in the Traitor's Tome.
