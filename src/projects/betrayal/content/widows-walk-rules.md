# Betrayal at House on the Hill: Widow's Walk — Rules Sheet

The Widow's Walk rules are a single folded sheet: a front cover, two inside pages (pp. 1–2) and a back cover. The inside pages carry no printed page numbers; "p. 1" is the left inside page and "p. 2" the right.

## Cover

- Title: Betrayal at House on the Hill — Widow's Walk, An Expansion
- The rest of the cover is a designer's essay ("My Sudden But Inevitable Betrayal", signed Mike Selinker, Lead Developer), left out: it has no rules content.

## Triggering the Haunt (pp. 1–2)

- When the haunt is triggered, look it up on this sheet's haunt chart instead of the base game's chart. The chart covers haunts 1–100: the base game's 50 haunts and the expansion's 50 (51–100).
- Lookup works as in the base game (rules.md, Revealing the Haunt): omen cards across the top, rooms down the left; the cell where the omen drawn and the revealer's room meet gives the haunt number.

> Note: haunts 51, 64 and 79 make more than one traitor ("Everyone except the haunt revealer", "Everyone except the highest Knowledge", "Left and right of the haunt revealer"). The base rules assume a single traitor (one Traitor's Tome reader, one traitor turn). How several traitors share the tome and the traitor turn is not said here; presumably those haunts say.
> Note: haunt 64's "Everyone except the highest Knowledge*" applies the tie-break footnote to the one explorer left out, not to a traitor. Read that way, a tie for highest Knowledge leaves out the revealer if tied, otherwise the tied explorer nearest the revealer's left.
> Note: haunt 56 counts "items and omens", haunt 94 counts only "items that can be stolen or traded". Whether 94 counts omens that work as items (most omens) is not said; the word "items" in the base rules usually includes them (rules.md, Use Item and Omen Cards).
> Note: haunt 95 picks the oldest real-world player, not an explorer's printed age.
> Note: the ★ entry says "If all of your explorers have played haunts 57, 75, 86, and 93". "Your explorers" is not defined: it most likely means every player in the current game has already played those four haunts (a campaign-style unlock), but it could be read as the explorers in play. "Ignore the haunt roll" presumably means the haunt does not start and play continues as if the roll had failed; the omen stays drawn. The chart after haunt 100 in Secrets of Survival is the place to settle the details.
> Note: the ★ cells fall only where the Charred Room meets four of the new omens. The base rulebook's "Optional Rule: Selecting the Haunt" (rules.md, p. 16) and this sheet's "Optional Rule: Playing Only New Haunts" (below) do not say how they interact with a ★ result.

## New Cards and Tiles (p. 2)

- At the start of the game, shuffle the expansion's event cards, item cards, omen cards and room tiles into the matching base-game stacks.

## Glossary Update (p. 2)

These entries replace or extend the base glossary (rules.md, Glossary).

- **Companion:** the Cat, Dog, Girl and Madman are companions. (The base list was Dog, Girl and Madman; the Cat omen is new.)
- **Monster:** a being represented by a monster token that has one or more traits. Monster rules apply to a monster token only when it represents a monster. (Official errata.)
- **Outside:** when a haunt refers to outside rooms, these rooms count: Balcony, Conservatory, Gardens, Graveyard, Patio, Roof Landing, Solarium, Tower, Tree House, Widow's Walk.
- **Weapon:** the weapons are the Axe, Blood Dagger, Boomstick, Chainsaw, Revolver, Sacrificial Dagger and Spear. (Boomstick and Chainsaw are new.)
- **Window:** the rooms with windows are the Bedroom, Chapel, Dining Room, Drawing Room, Grand Staircase, Master Bedroom and Sewing Room. (Drawing Room and Sewing Room are new.)

> Note: the printed glossary update has no Monster entry, though some haunts use monster tokens for things that aren't monsters. The Monster entry above is official errata.
> Resolution (official): errata. A monster token is a monster when it is put into play under the traitor's control or otherwise used against the heroes; tokens with no physical or mental traits, such as the Flesh in haunt 59, the Electors in haunt 66 and the Fire in haunt 91, are not monsters, so rules that affect monsters don't affect them. The FAQ adds the Monster entry to the Glossary Update between Companion and Outside. Source: https://www.dropbox.com/s/3y34f1s2tn6o4mx/Widow%27s%20Walk%20FAQ%20KB%2012.6.161.pdf?dl=0

> Note: the base glossary's "outside" examples include the Patio only, and the base rules never list the outside rooms in full. This list is the first full one; it mixes base rooms (Balcony, Conservatory, Gardens, Graveyard, Patio, Tower) and expansion rooms.

## Possible Floors diagram (p. 2)

- A room tile's back now lists four floors, top to bottom: ROOF, UPPER, GROUND, BASEMENT. The floors the room may be placed on are lit (as in the base diagram, rules.md, Discover a New Room).

## The Roof (p. 2)

- The roof is a new floor, above the upper floor.
- At the start of play, put the Roof Landing tile in play.
- When discovering a room on the roof, use the first room tile in the stack whose back lists either the roof or the upper floor. (This is printed on the Roof Landing tile too.)
- The roof is its own floor, not a second upper floor. Example given: the Mystic Elevator goes to the roof only on a roll of 4, and only if the player chooses the roof.

> Note: the sheet doesn't say where the Roof Landing goes at setup. Presumably it is laid out with the other starting tiles, as the separate start of the roof floor; the Roof Landing tile itself may say more.
> Note: "only when you roll a 4 and choose the roof" implies the Mystic Elevator tile's 4 result lets you pick any floor; that table is printed on the tile (rules.md, Mystic Elevator), not here.
> Note: upper-floor tiles may be placed on the roof, but the sheet doesn't say the reverse (a roof-only tile is not legal on the upper floor). That follows from the normal rule that a tile goes only on a floor its back lists.

## Landings (p. 2)

- The expansion calls several tiles "landings": the Basement Landing, the Grand Staircase/Foyer/Entrance Hall tile (any of its three rooms counts), the Upper Landing and the Roof Landing.

## New Room Symbols (p. 2)

### Dumbwaiter (p. 2)

- A room tile with the dumbwaiter symbol [dumbwaiter symbol: a small box on a rope] has a dumbwaiter.
- From that room you may move to the landing of the floor directly above or directly below, if that floor exists. This costs 1 extra space of movement on top of the normal move, and on top of any other extra costs (such as leaving a room with an opponent in it).
- Worked examples given:
  - A dumbwaiter room in the basement: moving to the Foyer costs 2 spaces.
  - A dumbwaiter room on the ground floor: moving to the Basement Landing or the Upper Landing costs 2 spaces.
- If you don't have enough Speed left to pay the cost, you can't use the dumbwaiter.

> Note: the examples use the Menagerie as the dumbwaiter room. The ground floor's landing is any room of the Entrance Hall tile (see Landings); the example picks the Foyer.
> Note: the sheet doesn't say whether the dumbwaiter works in reverse (from a landing into a dumbwaiter room) or whether monsters may use it.

### The "?" symbol (p. 2)

- A room tile with the **?** symbol: when you discover the room, draw the top card of whichever stack you choose (event, item or omen).

> Note: an omen drawn this way presumably triggers a haunt roll like any omen (rules.md, Making a Haunt Roll); the sheet doesn't say.

## Explorer Tokens (p. 2)

- There are 36 small circular explorer tokens, 6 in each colour, each showing an explorer's face. They mark that the explorer has something, or has completed something.
- When you use a trait-gaining power of the Chapel, Gymnasium, Larder, Library, Menagerie or Study, leave one of your explorer tokens on that room tile. From then on, whenever you are in that room, you may not use its power to gain a trait.
- Haunts may also use these tokens.

> Note: the sheet says "6 of each color" and "bearing each explorer's face", but each colour's character card has two explorers. Whether each token shows both explorers (one per side) is not said here; tokens.md records them as two-sided, one explorer per side.
> Note: the sheet ties the ban to the explorer ("when you are in that room thereafter"), not to the token staying on the tile. It doesn't say what happens if a haunt or card later moves or removes the token.

## Obstacle and Lock Tokens (p. 2)

- Obstacle and Lock tokens are barriers: explorers must take certain actions to get past them.
- Traitors and monsters ignore these tokens, unless the haunt says otherwise.

> Note: the sheet doesn't say what actions get an explorer past an Obstacle or Lock token; that comes from the tile, card or haunt that places it.

## Optional Rule: Playing Only New Haunts (sidebar, p. 2)

- A haunt triggered by a base-game omen in a base-game room gives a base-game haunt (1–50).
- To play only expansion haunts, the haunt revealer may either:
  - discard the omen, then draw and discard omens until one from the expansion comes up; or
  - discard the room tile, then draw and discard room tiles until an expansion omen room for this floor comes up.
- If every expansion omen (or every expansion omen room) is already in play, use the last one that entered play.

> Note: the sheet doesn't say where the replacement room goes, whether the revealer moves into it, or whether the discarded omen goes back into the deck. It also doesn't say whether "omen room" means a room with the omen symbol that is legal on the revealer's floor (likely, given "for this floor").
> Note: "choose the last one that entered play" doesn't say whether the haunt is then looked up with that omen (or room) in place of the original.

## Game Components (back cover)

- 1 rule sheet
- 2 haunt books (Traitor's Tome and Secrets of Survival)
- 20 room tiles
- 8 omen cards
- 11 item cards
- 11 event cards
- 76 tokens, including:
  - 4 large circular monster tokens
  - 36 small circular explorer tokens
  - 36 square event and room tokens

> Note: the sub-counts (4 + 36 + 36) total 76, matching the stated total. The 8 omen cards match the 8 new omen columns on the haunt chart (Bloodstone, Box, Cat, Key, Letter, Photograph, Rope, Vial).

- Ages 12+.
- Widow's Walk is an expansion, not a complete game; the base game is required.
- The rest of the back cover is the credits list and publisher logos (©2016 Wizards of the Coast LLC), left out.

## Haunt book covers (no rules content)

- The Widow's Walk Traitor's Tome and Secrets of Survival covers (front and back) show only the titles, "Do not read until the haunt scenario begins", "Age 12+" and copyright lines. No rules, tables or charts.
