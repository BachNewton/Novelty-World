# Tetris Worlds: the look, and what Frogmino could borrow

Inspiration, not decided. This is a reference for the look of Frogmino; nothing here is a commitment. What is decided lives in the project's `CLAUDE.md`.

Tetris Worlds is THQ's 2001–2002 Tetris. Its trademark was a 2D well floating in front of a fully 3D, animated alien planet that visibly terraforms from barren to lush as the player clears levels. The music was trance and techno, and a whispering announcer called the player's moves.

## How sure this is

The research could not watch video or view the screenshot galleries: MobyGames and IGN blocked automated access. The details come from written reviews, wikis and video descriptions. The world list is confirmed by a chaptered GameCube longplay and by several wikis. **The palettes below are inferred** from the world themes and review descriptions, not sampled from the game. Check them against the videos before relying on them.

## The look

- **Blocks.** Each block is a small 3D cube seen head-on, "almost like 3D blocks being viewed in a 2D perspective". Colours are "colourful, yet not neon-like". On PC the colours are alpha-blended and lit, and a debug key cycles alpha modes, so the blocks have some translucency. GameSpot called the Xbox version "glossy", and one Xbox reviewer saw "lighting effects… shining seamlessly off of every block and background after line clears". The player's custom Mino adds a design to the blocks. (GameFAQs PS2 review, TetrisWiki PC hacks, GameSpot, HonestGamers.)
- **The well ("Matrix" or "Tetrion").** Deliberately quiet: "nothing but a border with a black background and a very soft 1x1 grid-pattern". In the story the well is a Tetrion, an ancient alien gateway opened by playing Tetris. Six next pieces are shown (three on GBA), and the queue "features the pieces dancing around". (GameFAQs PS2 review, Gaming Target, Xbox manual text via a fan wiki.)
- **Backgrounds.** These carry the game's identity. Each world is a "fully 3D-rendered" animated landscape that visibly grows and flourishes as the player's rank rises: "barren and uninhabitable" at first, then "beautiful landscapes". Reviewers mention jellyfish, erupting volcanoes, icy tundra, ponds, mountains and jungle, and a tree that "starts growing suddenly" in the forest. The most common criticism was that all this was distracting. (GameSpot, GameFAQs reviews, Gamecritics.)
- **Effects and feedback.** "Particle and lighting effects" on clears (GameSpot). A "lightning" animation on level-up (GameFAQs). On GBA, cleared lines "break into individual blocks, which fly off of the screen". There was an optional "block bounce" effect. The ghost piece projects the landing outline.

## The Minos

The Minos are a "robust mechanical species": cube creatures with one eye (fan sources call them cycloptic) that hop around next to the well and pull faces. The player can customise their own Mino's colour, face and hat. The FMV intros differ by version: PS2 and PC have one; GameCube and Xbox share a newly rendered one, and the GameCube's uses a swipe transition. (GameFAQs reviews, a Tumblr lore post (fan source), a YouTube rip description.)

## The six worlds

Hadar 4 is the doomed Mino homeworld; its star, Hadar, is about to go nova. Six "Tetrinauts" travel to six worlds named after real stars, and each world has its own rule variant. The longplay chapters confirm the world-to-mode pairings. **The colour swatches are inferred.**

## Music, sound and mood

- **Sound design.** Landing is a metallic "clang"; bonus blocks sound "like metal fusing together". Ambient beds fit each world, such as "animal noises for jungles or flowing water for rivers". The whispering announcer calls "single", "double" and so on. The Xbox version supported custom ripped soundtracks. One reviewer missed the classic Tetris-clear "zoop". (GameFAQs reviews, GameSpot.)
- **Mood.** Calm and spacey, "new-age and spaced-out" (Gamecritics): a chill, ambient Tetris, not a frantic one. Techno builds up as the speed increases, and a whispering voice sits close to the player's ear. Critics were split on whether this was relaxing or grating.
- **Versus its era.** The New Tetris (N64, 1999) and The Next Tetris (1999) had already moved toward 3D backdrops. Worlds went further: a story, progress-driven backdrops that change with play, and a mascot species. It dropped Korobeiniki as the main theme, and many reviewers missed it.
- **Radical versus Blue Planet.** The GameCube and Xbox versions have "improved graphics, a different title screen, and a newly rendered opening cutscene" (TV Tropes). IGN called those improvements "a waste of time". One PS2 reviewer reported framerate drops at high levels.

## Ideas for Frogmino

Each idea has a cost tag and a note on how it fits the current look: touching cubes with a border on each cell, toon shading, dark outlines, one ambient plus one directional light.

1. **Planet that heals as you pass.** *Cheap to moderate; fits toon.* Borrow the Worlds hook, "your play grows the world", not its look. Each course starts barren and greyish, and every wall cleared saturates the palette a step and pops in props along the roadside: grass tufts, flowers, a tree that springs up with a squash-and-stretch hop. Built by lerping a palette uniform and toggling a few instanced low-poly props. Keep it at the periphery so it never hurts readability; that was Worlds' big criticism.
2. **One theme per course, sky as a gradient.** *Cheap; fits toon.* Forest, mountain, underwater, volcano, cloud and ice map naturally onto frog-friendly courses (a pond instead of undersea). A gradient sky shader (two or three stops) plus two or three layers of flat, outlined parallax cutouts: hills, cloud puffs, volcano silhouettes. No HDR skybox needed. Name the worlds afresh; don't reuse Deneb, Aludra and the rest as a set.
3. **Candy-gloss cube material.** *Cheap; fits toon if the highlight is stepped.* Worlds' blocks read as lit, glossy and slightly translucent. In toon terms: a hard-edged, stepped specular "window" highlight on the top-left of each cube (a toon ramp with a small white band), plus a lighter inner bevel from the per-cell border. Avoid real transparency: sorting problems on mobile, and it muddies the outlines.
4. **Per-world accent, not per-shape colours.** *Cheap; fits toon.* Give each course a five-colour palette: sky, ground, prop, wall and accent. Tint the obstacles with the course accent. Keep the frog in its own colours (green or player-chosen) so it pops against every palette. This also steers clear of the Guideline mapping of seven colours to seven shapes; see the legal caution below.
5. **"Gateway" clear burst.** *Cheap; fits toon.* Worlds dressed the well as a portal to unlock. Frogmino's opening can be the portal: when the frog fits through, the hole's rim flashes white, then 8–16 instanced cube particles in the course accent pop outward and fall. Add a quick camera punch (field of view up 3° for 120 ms). Pure instancing with no postprocessing, so it's phone-safe.
6. **Near-miss and perfect-fit juice.** *Moderate; fits toon.* Worlds had a "lightning" level-up and pieces bouncing in the queue. For Frogmino: a squash on landing, a shiny sweep across the frog's cubes on a perfect fit (a UV band animated in the toon shader), and a shaky "close one" wobble when a cube grazes the rim.
7. **Mascot reactions.** *Moderate; fits toon.* The Minos hopped and pulled faces beside the well, and reviewers remembered that charm. Frogmino already has its mascot, the frog. Give it three or four expression swaps on its eye cubes (happy, scared, dizzy) triggered by events. Keep it clearly a frog, with two eyes and frog features; not a one-eyed cube creature.
8. **Ambient bed plus chill trance.** *Moderate; no visual impact.* The per-world ambient sounds (water, jungle animals) were quietly liked. Loop a short ambient bed per course under the music. For music, pick upbeat, bouncy electronic tracks; reviewers found Worlds' spaced-out trance hard to concentrate to. Use a friendly voice for "Perfect!" and "Close one!", not a whisper. Use CC0 or commissioned audio and credit it in the project's `credits.ts`.
9. **Clean, quiet play lane.** *Cheap; fits toon.* Copy Worlds' good instinct: its well was plain black with a faint grid while the spectacle sat behind it. Keep the road low-contrast with a faint tile grid, and put saturation and motion only in the far background and the obstacles. That keeps the frog and the opening readable on a 360px phone.
10. **Chunky HUD with bevelled type.** *Cheap; fits toon.* Draw the HUD from the same cubes as the game: score digits on small outlined tiles, and a next-obstacle preview drawn as a mini silhouette. A rounded, heavy display font from Google Fonts. Don't imitate any Tetris logo or font.
11. **Real translucency or bloom glow.** *Expensive; clashes with toon and with deferred postprocessing.* Glass-like blocks and bloomed particle glow are the most "2002" part of Worlds, and the worst fit: transparency sorting, overdraw on phones, and outlines that fight translucency. Fake glow with an additive, unlit sprite behind a burst if needed. Leave real bloom until postprocessing is back on the table.
12. **Planet map and story hook.** *Moderate; fits toon.* A course-select screen showing each world as a little toon globe that visibly greens as it is cleared, for a sense of progress like Worlds' terraforming. Write an original story frame (frogs finding new ponds?) rather than the "evacuate before the sun goes nova" plot.

## Inspired by versus copying

Worth knowing, and not legal advice: in [Tetris Holding v. Xio (2012)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Holding,_LLC_v._Xio_Interactive,_Inc.), a US court protected Tetris's look: brightly coloured pieces made of four visibly delineated blocks, the tall 10×20 well, the ghost piece and garbage lines. Frogmino's frog is a tetromino of bordered cubes, which sits close to the first of those. Distance it: a 3D road rather than a well; the frog clearly a frog (eyes, skin tone, feet); frog colours that are not the Guideline's seven-colour mapping; and no "Tetris" or "Tetrimino" naming, logos, fonts or Korobeiniki.

| Fine to take (ideas and mechanics of presentation) | Don't take (assets and trade dress) |
|---|---|
| A world that heals and grows with progress | Screenshots, textures, models, FMV or music from the game |
| A themed backdrop per level (forest, water, fire, sky, ice) | The world names as a set, the Mino cyclops design, the Hadar 4 story |
| A quiet play lane with a loud backdrop | The Guideline piece colours tied to tetromino shapes; a 10×20 well layout |
| A glossy, stepped toon highlight on cubes | The Tetris name and logo, "Tetrimino", Korobeiniki or Kalinka remixes framed as Tetris music |
| A mascot that reacts; ambient per-world sound | A whispering announcer that imitates eKim's delivery |
