# Åland Drive: boats under way

Boats moved by the world's clock. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down.

Boats under way (`sea/`, `render/boats-under-way.ts`) are where the world's clock puts them, and nothing is sent over the network: the clock is the seconds every machine's own clock counts (`sea/clock.ts`), run at real speed, and a boat's place, heading and speed are a pure function of its lane and that time (`sea/motion.ts`), so every player sees the same boats. Anything else on the water moved by time, such as the regatta, takes the same clock. It isn't the time of day: the day is worked out from it, faster than real time (see `time/CLAUDE.md`), while the boats keep their real speed, so the timetable is in cycles of real seconds and doesn't line up with the game's mornings and evenings. `?sea=<time>` (such as 2026-11-04T12:18:00Z) starts a page's clock at that moment on the drive's first frame, running on at real speed, so a link can open just before a boat does something; that page's boats are out of step with everyone else's. A lane is a line over water from `sea/lanes.json`, one small file for the whole island that `pipeline/sea_lanes.py` writes beside the code (see `pipeline/CLAUDE.md`), so no tile is rebuilt for it and it needs no publish. A voyage sails its lanes on a fixed cycle (`sea/timetable.ts`, an invented timetable on the real lines): along a lane it gathers way from a stop, slows for a tight corner and over the last stretch to a berth, and keeps to starboard of the line, so two meeting on one lane pass; at a berth a single-ended ship stops off the quay and comes round, then backs in, so it lies bow out and leaves ahead, or at Västerhamn, where the ships lie alongside the quay, turns to the quay's line and moves across on to it, and off it the same way; a double-ended ferry, the same at both ends, just goes back; a small boat lies at the far end of its trip and swings to face home; and a ship past the islands is out of sight until its next call. The ships call at Mariehamn's Västerhamn and Långnäs on through services between Stockholm, Turku and Tallinn, no two in one approach at once, and Eckerö's crosses to Grisslehamn every eight hours, all on the town kit's Baltic ferry (Eckerö's smaller); Ålandstrafiken's four archipelago lines run the town kit's double-ended car ferry (`art/town-kit/road-ferry.ts`), and the four road ferries, which all run on wires, `art/identity`'s cable ferry; the excursion boats to Kobba Klintar and the outer islands are `art/boats`' passenger boat; and one small boat to each harbour trip comes and goes on a cycle of its own rolled from its lane: a keeled one (`art/boats`' sailing yachts and double-ender) on a lane kept out of charted water too shallow for it, or a shallow one (the allmogebåt, launches, RIBs and fishing boats). `sea/motion.test.ts` holds that nothing jumps over a whole cycle, a passage keeps to its speeds and accelerations, a ship lies bow out or along its quay, no two small boats wait in one place, and no two ferries' hulls come within 10 m over a whole day; `tools/sea-times.ts` says when boats pass a place, as moments `?sea=` takes. Each model is one list of every vessel drawn with it, two draw calls however many sail and none for a version no vessel on it is in range of (`render/versions-in-reach.ts`), and each frame writes only the lists' places, a few floats a vessel; one out of sight folds to nothing. Boats and sea marks together add 8 to 18 draw calls and 2,000 to 15,000 triangles at the review's views. They keep their kits' levels of detail and a reach far past the shore: a ship to 20 km, a sail to 4 km, an engine boat to 2.5 km, the far versions a few dozen triangles. A boat under way rocks to a seed of its own rather than its place, which changes every frame. **The ferries don't stop the car.** The small boats keep to the season: the leisure and excursion boats sail only while boats are afloat, each on a threshold of its own, a fishing boat all year, and none over ice; the ferries run through the ice, with no channel drawn behind them. A berthed road ferry has no collision, so a car driven off the ramp's end goes into the water there as at any shore: the ground is static blocks and a moving deck isn't something the car can ride, and a wall that stood only while a ferry was in would make the same quay a wall at one moment and water the next, which reads as more broken than either. Riding a ferry is a feature of its own.
