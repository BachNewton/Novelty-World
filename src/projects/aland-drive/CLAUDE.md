# Åland Drive

A 3D open-road driving game inspired by the Test Drive Unlimited series, set on the main island of Åland at 1:1 scale, built from real public geodata.

Players see it as **Åland Drive Unlimited**, a nod to that series; the code keeps the name Åland Drive. It lives at Games → 3D Games → Åland Drive Unlimited. It is the whole main island, 54 tiles of road and the 40 around them, streamed in as the car moves: fourteen invented cars to drive on real roads, on land coloured by the real aerial photos, among forest grown from the laser data and buildings from NLS's 3D data and footprints, with a mini map, a world map and a GPS, and forty real places to find, and bucket list challenges to play solo or as co-op for two, against the clock or a speed camera, and 32 races, laps of a loop or a road from A to B, for medals. Everyone who opens it drives in one shared world, online free roam, with every other player's car on the road and on both maps.

The game menu (`components/game-menu.tsx`) opens on Resume, which B, Menu and Esc press too, and holds the car's tyres (summer, friction and studded side by side, named as the Nordics name them in English, the fitted set marked, each fitting its own when picked, so no button has to say whether it's the set on or the next), the sound on or off, and in a challenge Retry and Leave (see Challenges). The same dialog is a challenge's small choices on its own button: Solo or Co-op, and Join or Decline, each with Cancel. N toggles the sound too, with no prompt on the HUD. Esc opens it only when nothing else is open for it to close. It is driven by the shared pad navigation, and the game decides what it holds and whether it pauses (`render/game-menu.ts`): each time it opens, the game works out its items, which the page draws by id, and whether the drive stops. It pauses as the map does unless the game says otherwise, and a menu that doesn't pause lets go of the car's controls while the cars and the clock run on.

The world map pauses the drive and opens on the main island, north up, and zooms out to all of Åland. The wheel (about the cursor) or the triggers zoom, a drag, WASD or the right stick pans, a click or A sets a destination, with the left stick moving a controller's cursor, X clears the route, and Esc, M, B or View flies home. A pick on a place routes to it, and the cursor over one names it, with its note once it's found.

The dev menu (below) jumps the car to the end of the GPS route, facing the way the route arrives, or to any of the ten showcase spots (`render/showcase.ts`) by name, each with the first load's bar while the tiles there land and the spot's name on arrival. `tools/showcase.mjs` screenshots all ten through the probe, the repeatable view set for art and performance checks.

`~`, or the key under Esc on any layout, opens the dev menu (`components/dev-menu.tsx`), which pauses the drive as the map does. Its copy button puts where the car is on the clipboard, for the owner to paste into a chat when they see something wrong: the tile, the car's scene and ETRS-TM35FIN position, its heading, speed and camera, and the view on screen as `tools/look.mjs` arguments (east, north, height above the ground, yaw and pitch in radians). That look recreates what they saw, and the scene position finds the spot for any other tool. It copies a link too: `?at=east,north,height,yaw,pitch` (`render/start-place.ts`) opens the game there, loading that place first instead of the usual start, with the car on the nearest road and the free camera on the view; east and north alone look from a little above the ground. Its buttons jump to the showcase spots, closing the menu. Its Traffic switch turns the cars, cyclists and walkers on the lanes off and on again, offline only: in the shared world they are everyone's, and always on. And pick any of the fourteen cars, and the drive carries on in it from where it was, at the same speed: its looks, handling and sound all change. The same key or Esc closes it. It has no prompt and no controller button, and the choice lasts until a reload.

The free camera is for inspection: WASD, Q and E, Shift, drag to look, the wheel for its speed. There is no touch. Prompts come from the shared glyphs and follow the last input used.

## Platform

Web only, WebGPU only, three.js driven directly with no React Three Fiber, and Rapier for physics. It never moves to a native engine. A browser without WebGPU is told the game needs it.

The render (`render/game.ts`) is one renderer, one scene and one loop. The page (`components/aland-drive.tsx`) only starts it and stops it, and shows the speed and the prompts; nothing in the game renders through React. After `renderer.init()` the game checks the backend really is WebGPU, because three drops to WebGL2 by itself when a device can't be made, and the page shows the same message as with no adapter.

`window.__alandDrive` is the probe for tools, waiting on events rather than time: frames, draw calls, triangles, GPU time and the size drawn, the car's state and its drive log, what the stream has done, a hold that stops the simulation following the clock, `advance` to step it by hand, the scripted driver and the camera.

The tools run against local tiles, never the real host: `tools/serve-tiles.mjs` serves a folder of them and counts the bytes it sent, and a page takes it with `?tiles=local:<port>`. `tools/look.mjs` takes a screenshot from any place on the map. `tools/shader-count.mjs` counts what a cold page compiles, every WebGPU shader module and pipeline from the device's own calls, grouped by what each pipeline's vertex shader reads: the same on every run, so a change to the start-up compile is judged by it. A `select` in a shader that chooses between anything but plain values is written by three as two branches, each building its own copy of what both share, so a blend by a 0-or-1 weight is used instead where both sides are worked out anyway. `tools/drive.mjs` drives a route the game's own GPS sets (`--gps=<x,z;x,z;...>`) and prints what it did, cost, held and downloaded, held and stepped by hand, or with `--clock` at real speed, which is the one that says whether anything stalled. `tools/landings.mjs` walks the load focus round the world so tiles land over and over, and prints what each cost. `tools/film.mjs` makes a video from one, for judging what moves: it takes over the page's clock and renders each frame a frame's time after the last, so the video plays at real speed however slowly the machine renders, with the boats' clock set where the video starts and, for a ship's slow manoeuvre, sped up on its own (`--speed`); ffmpeg encodes it. `tools/drive.mjs` drives a route the game's own GPS sets (`--gps=<x,z;x,z;...>`) and prints what it did, cost, held and downloaded, held and stepped by hand, or with `--clock` at real speed, which is the one that says whether anything stalled. `tools/landings.mjs` walks the load focus round the world so tiles land over and over, and prints what each cost. `tools/road-check.ts` drives every motor road in the simulation alone, `tools/car-limits.ts` measures every car's grip, braking and pull on a flat test pad (`sim/test-pad.ts`) of each surface in each condition into the table the racers will read (`sim/car-limits.json`), `tools/bridge-check.ts` every span, both ways, and `tools/junction-check.ts` every way through every junction of a tile (see "How rough the roads are"). The browser tools use headless Playwright with `channel: "chromium"`, because the default headless shell falls back to WebGL2 here.

`?car=<id>` starts the drive in a car (its id in `sim/cars.ts`, such as `grus` or `berg`), and goes with `?at=`, `?condition=` and `?playground` in one link. `?weather=rain` holds a weather (`climate/held.ts`: clear, cloud, overcast, rain, snowfall, fog, or `ice` for black ice), as the dev menu's Weather does. `?weather-lab` is the climate's readout, every value of the weather and the season at a date and hour (`docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`).

`?playground` is the real game on a made-up map instead of Åland (`playground/layout.ts`), for trying how the cars drive without hunting the island for the right road: a long straight of asphalt and one of gravel side by side, one circuit laid twice, in asphalt and in gravel, a skidpad of each, a slab of bare rock, open grass all round and forest at the edges. The dev menu switches to it and back, and jumps to each part of it. It is one tile in the pipeline's own files and formats, built by `tools/playground.ts` into the site's public folder and committed, so the game reads it as it reads any tile, with nothing of its own changed, and a change to the layout is a rerun of the tool. It is a world of its own, its tile on the world's origin (positions are 32-bit floats, so a world far from its origin draws and drives less precisely), and what the game places by Åland's own positions rather than from tiles (the boats under way, sea marks, regattas and the places to find) is left out of it. A page in it drives alone. It's for feedback on feel; a change is still judged on Åland's real roads last.

`?lane-lab` is the real game with the lane map drawn over the road (`render/lane-overlay.ts`, `components/lane-lab.tsx`): every lane a strip with arrows the way it runs, every connector through a junction green where it has right of way, amber where it gives way and red where it leaves the drawn road, which is only where the road surface has a gap no turn can stay on, and a ring at each place the lane audit found. Cars, cyclists and walkers move along the lanes round the camera there, each kind at Åland's own density, four times it or none (`traffic/lane-driver.ts`; cars drawn as the parked cars' fleet by `render/lane-traffic.ts`, cyclists as `art/bikes`' crowd by `render/cyclists.ts`, walkers as `art/people`' walking crowd by `render/walkers.ts`): each a place on a lane, a speed and a body with steered front wheels and no physics, steered along its lanes by the steering every computer-driven car shares, the autopilot's pure pursuit (`pursuit`, `sim/autopilot.ts`), its wheels turning at a real car's pace, so traffic turns as a racer will. Its place on its lane is found from where its body is, as the autopilot finds itself on its line. It picks a way on at each junction and signals it with its indicators from 35 m or four seconds before, keeps its distance by the intelligent driver model, slows for bends and limits before it reaches them, and stops at its line while what its turn gives way to is coming, the player's car among them. A dead end's turning round is a U-turn tighter than a car can make on its wheels, so there a car stops and its trip ends. Where they come and go is the bubble (`traffic/bubble.ts`): every lane holds a slot every 40 m, each a seed that says whether it ever holds anyone, by its road's or path's density per kilometre, and who: a road's cars are its real vehicles a day from the lane map, at an average daytime hour's share of them over its limit, half as busy again as the real island so a drive away from town meets something about once a minute, and a path's cyclists a density per kind, so a road fills the same way each time and a vehicle that comes back is the same one. A slot fills while its vehicle is gone and it lies in the bubble, 250 m past where its kind is drawn, out of the player's sight; a vehicle goes once it's out of sight 200 m further out, or its trip has ended at a dead end. Out of sight is `traffic/sight.ts`: past its kind's reach (the fleet's 900 m, the bikes' 400 m, the people's 200 m), out of the camera's view, or behind the ground over a crest; trees and buildings hide things too, but only the ground is known on the CPU, so it errs towards seen. The first fill, as a page opens or a kind is turned on or up, goes anywhere: nothing was there to see appear, or the setting was changed on purpose. The drive has them too, at Åland's own density, offline and in the shared world, where every player sees the same ones (see Multiplayer), and a tile without lanes has none. A jump fills the lanes round the car afresh, as a page opening does. `tools/traffic-count.mjs` prints the draw calls and triangles with and without traffic at the showcase's views, and how many cars and cyclists ride round the car and are in sight. A cyclist rides cycleways, footways, paths and the roads' edges at a pace of its own, 15 to 23 km/h whatever a path's limit, always kinematic and never a simulated body, with no stand-in for the player's car to touch: from a path's end on to a road by the lane map's link there, waiting at the path's end, clear of the road, for a gap in what's coming, and off a road again at a link to a path now and then, and turning round on the spot at a dead end, where a car's trip ends and it stands until it's out of sight. A walker walks the footways, paths, pedestrian streets, steps and the cycleways it shares, never a road but across one, at a pace by who it is (an older person's slower): it keeps to its own side of a path and further right as someone comes, passes a slower walker on the left while nothing is coming, goes round someone the people files placed standing or sitting, steps to the path's edge and slows for a cyclist coming or catching it up, and walks straight on to a path that starts where its own ends. At a path's end by a road it crosses, from the kerb straight to the path starting on the road's other side, once nothing on that road is near or coming within a few seconds; cars and cyclists stop for one out on the road. It never takes a step that would touch anyone: the step is taken back and it stands, turning on the spot or sidestepping towards where it means to go, stepping aside when held up with nothing to wait for, and turning back after a while. A cyclist slows for walkers, passes them with room, and never rides into one. Each walker is drawn by its stride run on by the ground it covers (`art/people/gait.ts`), so its feet keep their place. Traffic slows behind one and overtakes, leaving 1.2 m, only once it has followed a few seconds and the oncoming side is clear for 150 m. It brakes for a simulated car lying across its path at any angle, the player's or one hit out of traffic, and goes round one standing in its lane once the oncoming side is clear for 150 m. On a road too narrow for two to pass at speed, an oncoming pair slows and eases to its edges, and where there's no room for both one stops on its verge while the other creeps by, a whole queue waiting for a whole queue, the two ways taking turns. Near a simulated car each traffic car has a stand-in in the simulation (`traffic/contact.ts`), as another player's car has, its own box and weight: a touch trades momentum by weight, and a car hit hard becomes a full simulated car (`Drive.addMovingCar`), the drivable car nearest it in size, moving across the road as the hit left it but not lifted, pitched or rolled by it, since a stand-in has no ground to take those as a car's wheels would and handed on they reared it up on two wheels to sink back slowly, that skids to a stop, stands with its hazards on, and after five seconds rejoins its lane where it stands and drives off, or, off its wheels or away from a lane running its way, stands until the player is 150 m off and goes. It is drawn as the world car it was, turned only about the vertical. They step at 30 Hz, every fourth step of the simulation, and are drawn between steps. `tools/traffic-check.mjs` runs them round a place for a few minutes, held and stepped by hand, from a camera a little above it turning round once a minute, and counts every car, cyclist or walker that came or went where that camera could see it, after the first fill has settled; it also lists every one that stood still too long, the places they strayed furthest from their lanes (but a walker out on a road, crossing it), and every pair that overlapped or passed within 15 cm, oncoming or the same way, and every walker that walked into someone placed standing or sitting, each with a link. The player's car stands where the view puts it, so it leaves that out unless asked (`--with-player`); `tools/film.mjs --traffic=<density> --bikes=<density> --walkers=<density>` films them the same way, and `--hide-lanes` leaves the lanes undrawn. Its list is the audit's for the loaded tiles, each a click away from above, and `pipeline/lanes.py --audit <page address>` prints the same with `?lane-lab&at=` links. `?junction-lab` is the real game with the hardest junctions of each kind a click away (`render/junction-spots.ts`, `components/junction-lab.tsx`), from above, at a driver's eye or at the wheel, with the car on the approach, to judge a change to the road surface. `?forest-lab` is the forest on its own (`render/forest-lab.ts`, `components/forest-lab.tsx`), on the game's own forest and terrain code and real tiles, to see how its detail works and to judge a change to it. What detail is measured from can be pinned away from the camera, so a tree goes through everything a drive does to it with the view held still. It colours trees by model, by whether they're mid-change and by how far their base slides, finds the worst trees nearby, changes the forest's settings live, and shows how many trees each list drew; `?date=` shows it in that date's season. `render/tile-reads.ts` is what the game and the lab share, so the lab's trees are the game's. `?deer-lab` is `art/deer`'s gallery running live (`art/deer/stage.ts`, `components/deer-lab.tsx`): every kind in every gait, each gait held through its cycle, a doe and her kids crossing a road, the seasons and the speed to judge the gaits by, flown with the free camera.

The scene is east +x, up +y and north -z. The renderer uses a reversed depth buffer.

## The car

`sim/` is the simulation, with no renderer in it, so it runs in tests as it does in the page. Each car's numbers are in `sim/cars.ts`, the shape they take and the world's own in `sim/tuning.ts`; the cameras' are at the top of `render/chase-camera.ts`.

- **A rigid body on four rays** (`sim/car.ts`): each wheel's ray finds the surface, a spring holds the body up, and the tyre pushes on the body at the contact, so weight transfer, squat, dive and lean all come from the same forces. Rapier's own ray-cast vehicle isn't used: its tyres are one clamped friction, with no slip curve to shape a handbrake slide or the grip at 200 km/h.
- **Fourteen cars, each its own** (`sim/cars.ts`). A car is its id (`CarId`), and everything about it follows from that: where its axles, track and wheels are, its weight and the height of its centre of mass, its springs, dampers and anti-roll bars, its tyres' grip front and rear and how they let go, its engine's torque curve, its gears, how its drive splits front to rear, its brakes, drag and downforce, and how far and fast its wheels steer. The Skerry GT is written out in full. The rest are each a character, the few numbers a driver knows a car by (its mass, ride, grip, drive, steering speed, engine and gears), and the springs, dampers, inertia, forces and collision box follow from those by one set of rules, so a new car is a character and not fifty numbers. The axles sit where the art has them, about the centre of mass, so the weight splits between them as the body is drawn, and each axle's springs are matched to its share of that weight, so every car sits level. `topSpeedMS` (`sim/gearbox.ts`) works a car's top speed out from its pull and drag, which is what each top gear is set against.
- **An engine and an automatic gearbox** (`sim/gearbox.ts`): from one gear (the electric car) to eight, and the pull at the road is the torque curve at the revs, through the gear, cut for the moment of a shift. A closed throttle brakes on the engine, or on regeneration in the electric car. Nothing caps the speed: a car stops gaining where drag meets the engine, and top gear's redline is past that, except the electric car's single gear, whose motor runs out at 230 km/h. Shifting is automatic only, and manual shifting is an idea for later. The sound hears these same gears and revs, and speed, gear and revs share one dial at the bottom right.
- **Steering narrows with speed** to the angle that asks for about all the grip the tyres have, plus some room for the slip angle they grip best at: a tyre only pushes sideways once it runs at an angle to where it rolls, and without that room the front tyres never reached their grip at speed. The yaw assist that steadies the car asks for no faster a turn than the tyres can give at that speed.
- **Grip by surface and by condition** (`sim/surfaces.ts`, the reasoning in `ideas/surface-grip.md`). Each wheel is on what its ray hit: the collision blocks know each triangle, so a road's carriageway is asphalt or gravel as it's drawn, its shoulder past the carriageway's edge is a gravel verge, and the ground is grass, or rock where the map has bedrock. A car over the road's edge grips on two wheels and slides on two. The condition (dry, wet, snow, ice) is every surface's at once, except on sea ice that bears a car, which is ice whatever the weather, so each wheel reads its own (`Underfoot.groundUnder`); it is set by the shared weather (`climate/`, see `docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`), or held by `?condition=` in a link or the dev menu's Ground until a reload; a tool's or a test's page stays dry unless it asks with `?weather`. A surface in a condition reshapes the car's own tyre curve rather than replacing it (its peak grip, the slip it peaks at, how much it keeps past the peak, and the rolling that soft ground adds, more with speed), so every car keeps its character on every ground. The order is real's and the gaps are squeezed, as in the open-world driving games, so off the tarmac is slower and looser but still drivable at speed. Each car also has a class, what it was built for (sports, road, rally or off-road), one field of its description as in Test Drive Unlimited 2, which sets how each surface and condition treats it: a sports car wins on asphalt and comes last on gravel, grass and snow, where the rally car and the four-by-fours win. A tuning shop could later move a car between classes, as Forza Horizon's tyres and The Crew's kits do. Each car also runs on the tyres the player fitted (summer, winter friction or studded), one more row reshaping the curve again: summer tyres on snow or ice are clearly worse and looser but still drive, studs are the best on ice and a little worse on a bare road, and `sim/tyres.test.ts` holds that order for every class on every surface. Tyres are the player's choice, not the law's: a field of the car, kept until changed, fitted per car in this browser by `garage/tyres.ts` (the game menu's tyre choices now, outside a challenge, and a home's garage or a car shop later), or `?tyres=` in a link for the car the drive starts in. They aren't in `CarState`, since another player's car is a stand-in with no grip of its own. Dry asphalt on summer tyres is every car exactly as tuned, whatever its class, a test holding it to the last digit. The steering's lock and the yaw assist ask for the grip under the wheels, so full lock on ice asks for an ice corner; brakes and throttle are capped by the grip there already. The sound and the tyre particles read the same wheels.
- **What comes off the tyres** (`render/tyre-particles.ts`, the rules in `render/tyre-kicks.ts`): each wheel's tread (where it touched, how fast it rolled and slid, and how far past its grip it was asked) is read off the simulation without changing it, and decides what that wheel throws up: dust and stones on gravel, clods and torn grass on grass, grit on rock, spray on anything wet, powder in snow and frost off ice, chips of ice under studs, some by rolling over loose or wet ground and more by sliding, spinning or locking. A dry road throws nothing until a tyre is past its grip, and then smoke. Only the player's car has them. A particle is written once, when it's thrown on a simulation step, and its flight (drag, gravity, a solid bit stopping at the ground) is worked out in the vertex shader from its age on the simulation's clock, so nothing moves on the CPU and a held page stepped by hand throws exactly what a drive would. Two fixed pools are two draw calls, soft puffs and small solid bits, neither drawn while nothing is in the air; a puff is never wider than about a tenth of the screen and fades as it nears the eye, since their cost is overdraw. Their colours are `tyre-*` tokens and the ground's own. The probe counts them (`tyreCounts`) and can leave them out (`showTyres`).
- **Nothing but the car is drawn inside it** (`art/car-volume.ts`): through the glass, a tuft it rolls over, a puff of its own dust or a tree it drives through would seem to fill the cabin. So the player's car is two boxes in its own frame, from its drawn art (the body up to where the glass starts, and the glass's bounds over the cabin), and every material that has a mask already (the particles, the plantings, the forest, and the kits' roadside, street, rural, people, boats and signs) drops its pixels inside them. Being in the mask adds no `discard` to a material that had none, so the GPU's early depth test is kept; the place in the car's frame is worked out per vertex. A material with no mask (terrain, water, buildings, landmarks) doesn't take it: the car can't drive into any of those, except water, which the car sinks into.
- **Swapping cars mid-drive** (`Drive.setCar`) builds the new car's body where the old one was, raised or lowered by the difference in their ride heights, moving as it moved, in the gear a gentle drive would be in at that speed.
- **More than one simulated car** (`Drive.addCar`): racers, and traffic once it's hit, are full cars beside the player's in the same Rapier world, each from its own input, touching each other and other players' stand-ins, with the ground loaded round every one (see `ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`). A car added elsewhere leaves the player's drive exactly as it was.
- **A fixed step of 120 Hz**, with no clock in the simulation: the same inputs play out the same. Each frame draws the car between its last two steps.
- **The car's state** (`CarState`, `sim/car-state.ts`) is one small record that survives JSON, and the car is drawn from it and nothing else, so it is also what other players are sent (see Multiplayer). It names its car by id, so a car heard over the network draws and rides as the one being driven. The drawing is `art/cars` (`createCarView`): a drivable car's art takes its name, axles, track, wheels and ride height from `sim/cars.ts`, so a change there moves the art too and every car sits on its wheels where the simulation has them, while its collision box is the simulation's own, held to the drawn body by `art/cars/cars.test.ts`. The car casts no shadow of its own. Its lamps are in `CarState` (headlamps, on whenever a car is driven, as Finnish and Åland law has them, brake and reverse), so another player's car lights as it brakes and backs up.
- **The end of the world** is a wall with a low coloured band on it, on every side of a tile with no tile beyond. **Water** has a seabed the pipeline lays under it, so a car that leaves the shore sinks into it, and it is dragged to a stop once it's past the drawn waterline, by the same test the picture uses. Nothing ever puts the car back by itself, in water or on its roof: a reset is always the player's, with R or Y. A road over water doesn't count as water. **Sea ice** that bears a car (`sim/sea-ice.ts`: water more sheltered than the season's `iceDrivable`) is ground: the collision blocks lie at the water's surface there instead of on the seabed (`World.drivenNode`), nothing drags, and its wheels are on ice whatever the weather. Thinner ice lets the car sink as open water does, and `sim/sea-ice.test.ts` holds both.
- **Buildings, stone walls, boulders, windmills, tractors, bales and cows stop the car; trees, outcrops and sheep don't.** A building's outline is a band of wall triangles in the same collision blocks, 6 m tall, from the pipeline's outlines, so the car never climbs a roof and a `sim/world.test.ts` case holds that it can't pass one at top speed. Every building has walls, open only where a road goes through it, as `pipeline/give_way.py` decides (see Pipeline), which each tile's `solids.roadsThrough` counts. A raised building (`pipeline/raised.py`) has none at the ground: only its posts stop the car. A cow's or a bale's walls leave with it out of season (`seasonalWalls`, `climate/placed-season.ts`), so the car never meets one that isn't drawn. A boulder is a ring of eight such walls round its foot, a quarter in from its widest (`modelWalls`, `render/tile-models.ts`), and a test holds that it stops the car at top speed too. So is a windmill, round its trestle out to its stone pads, with a wall along its steps, and a tractor, a bale or a cow is the box of its foot, as a parked car is, since a cow is the size and weight of a small car: a mill and a bale, the smallest, each have a case at top speed. A sheep isn't: nothing in the game can knock one over or shove it aside, a sheep that stopped the car dead would read worse than one passed through, as a mailbox would, and it grazes away from the roads in any case. Trees have no collision: they are a shader's, with no list to collide with. Nor do granite outcrops: the rock's own domes and steps are already the ground the car drives on, and an outcrop only lifts that by a few decimetres of skin. Nor do a boulder field's small stones, a shader's like the trees: a field's biggest stones are listed boulders, which do. A stone wall is both faces of each of its drawn sections, as tall as a building's: the car's box rides 0.4 m off the ground, so a wall only as tall as its stones overlaps it by a hand's width and at speed the solver lifts the car over it rather than stopping it, which another case holds. Power poles, street lamps, guard rails and bus shelters stop the car too, because a car at 200 km/h passing through a wooden pole or a steel mast reads as broken: the roadside list carries their outlines as walls, a pole's a square of 0.36 m, which the tile's walls take in, and a `sim/world.test.ts` case holds that the car can't pass a pole at top speed either. A parked car stops it too, as a ring of four walls round its body, the size of the world car the game picks for its place (`parkedWalls`, `render/parked-places.ts`), and a case holds that as well. Sign posts, edge posts and mailboxes don't: real ones are made to break away or bend, and with nothing in the game that breaks, a car stopped dead by a 3 cm post would be worse than one passing through. Street furniture splits the same way: a bench and a bike rack are iron anchored in the ground, so their outlines are walls in the roadside list, while a bin on its post, café tables and chairs and parasols are what a real car knocks flying, and stop nothing. The town dressing too: a linden's trunk, a flag or midsummer pole, a planter and a viewing tower stop the car, and a fence, a picnic table and a viewpoint platform's light timber don't.
- **What the car collides with is what's drawn** (`sim/ground.ts`): the same triangles of ground and road, in 120 m blocks on one lattice for the whole world, three by three round every simulated car. A triangle mesh rather than Rapier's heightfield, so the diagonals are the render's by construction; `sim/drive.test.ts` holds the two copies together. A block doesn't know which tile its heights and roads came from, so the car feels nothing at a tile's edge, and `sim/world.test.ts` pins that.
- **The world** (`sim/world.ts`) is all the land the game knows, with no GPU in it, for the simulation and the terrain alike. Every tile has far heights from the start, and those near the car have detail. Ground with no detail yet answers from the far heights, for what's drawn and what's driven on alike, so the car can't find a hole, only coarser ground with no roads.

## The day

The game keeps a calendar of its own (`time/calendar.ts`): Åland's year in 48 game days, four to a month, each game day `GAME_DAY_S` real seconds (`time/day.ts`: half an hour, the owner's choice), so a year passes in 24 hours of play. The world's time is one number, game days since the first of 2000's, running on from wherever it started on the world's clock (`sea/clock.ts`). Each game day stands for about a week of its month: the real date it shows runs on through it, a month's days spread evenly over its four, and the sun and moon stand where they really would over Åland at the game's hour on that date (`time/sky-places.ts`: NOAA's solar calculator and the Astronomical Almanac's moon, turned to the grid, whose north is 6.25° west of true north), held by tests to Mariehamn's published sunrises and sunsets and to 2026's eclipses. Read at the date the game day started on, blended to the next, so no turn of a day, month or year moves the sky by more than a hundredth of a degree, which a test holds. The boats keep real time.

The world's time is saved in this browser (`getProjectStorage`) each game hour, and a page picks up where it left off; with no save it starts at the real date and time. Online, the host's time is the world's: each guest asks for it once it's in and takes it, behind a short fade so the sky doesn't jump in sight, and a guest who claims the world when the host goes already has it (see Multiplayer). A tool's or a test's page neither reads nor writes the save. `?date=2026-06-21` holds that real date and `?hour=22.5` starts the day at that hour, which runs on at the game's pace; a page moved by a link isn't saved and keeps its own time online, though as a host it hands it on. The dev menu's Day picks a month and its day (1 to 4), the hour, holds the time, or goes back to the real date and time now. The HUD over the mini map shows the month's name, the time, and the air's temperature where there is weather: which of a month's four days it is is the dev menu's, not the player's. The weather and the seasons hang off the same `GameTime` (`docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`).

- **One live sun** (`art/sun.ts`): every material reads a vector towards the sun whose length is the sun's strength, falling to nothing as it sets, so a kit's own `dot(normal, toSun)` is the direct light at any hour; a gallery hands a kit a fixed place instead. The render's day (`render/day-and-night.ts`) moves it each frame.
- **The night follows the sun's height**: `art/night`'s one dial is set from it, so the golden hour, dusk and a midsummer night come when the real sun says, and below the deepest midsummer sun (−6.5°) a second dial darkens land and sky towards a winter night's dark, lightened by a risen moon but never so dark the road is hard to read, as an arcade game should be. Everything drawn goes through one function (`hazedAtHour` in `render/game.ts`): the hour's light on a colour, the headlights and the street lamps' light on it, then the haze. The headlights are the player's and those of the three nearest other players' cars drawn here, four cars in all, enough for four players together (`OTHERS_LIT`, `art/night/headlights.ts`): a fixed number of pools whatever the number online, so no shader is rebuilt as players come and go, and a pool whose car isn't there costs a pixel one comparison. Rooms go dark by the clock in the small hours, whatever the season.
- **The terrain under a moving sun**: the 2 m bake holds one fixed sun's light, cast shadow and open sky in one channel. The terrain keeps it and moves it by how much more or less the ground faces the sun now than it faced the baked one, by a smooth normal the clipmap works out from its own heights per vertex: identical to the bake where the sun stands where it was baked, slopes turning to or from the sun as it goes round, and the land dimming as it sets. Cast shadows stay where the bake put them, which on Åland's low relief shows little. `?terrain-light=` (`render/terrain-light.ts`) still offers the bake as it was and a flat version, to compare.
- **The night's lights** (`render/night-lights.ts`): every roadside and town lamp's glow, the sea's lights from OSM with their real colours, sectors and rhythms, each standing on the official register's mark at its place and taking its kind from it, so a light with no mark drawn to shine from is left out (`render/lights-on-marks.ts`); every one, a lighthouse too, flashes from its own lamp with no beam, as Åland's fixed lenses do. Then harbour lamps and other players' lamps: two draw calls of glows, none of them drawn by day. A lamp's light is light, not a patch added on top: each lamp within 700 m of the camera draws its pool, from straight above, into a light image that follows the camera (`createLampLight`, `art/night/lamps.ts`, 1.5 m a pixel), and every colour drawn is lit by it in `hazedAtHour`, so a lit road keeps its own grey and paint, and a car, a wall or a tree under a lamp is lit too. That is one small pass of its own a frame while lamps are on (634 lamps and 1,268 triangles at the Esplanade, 45 in the country) and one read of the image a pixel. Lit rooms are the wall shader's own, from each pane it draws. At Mariehamn at night the glows add about 3 draw calls and 40 k triangles, and nothing by day.
- **The winter's lights** (`docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`, Reference, Winter lights) follow the season's dials, never a date, each thing on its own threshold. In the wall shader (`art/night/festive.ts`): a home that has dressed its windows has an electric Advent candlestick on a sill (the stepped seven-light, or Advent's four candles lit as far as the Sundays have come) or a paper star hung high in a pane, each drawn as its share of the pixel so it settles into the pane's glow far off, lit all night and faint by day; and a string of lights along its eaves on a timer, a few of them coloured. Homes far more than flats, offices and shops, and barns and churches never. Out of season the shader skips them on a branch every pixel takes alike. Mariehamn's own (`render/winter-lights.ts`, `art/night/winter-town.ts`): Torget's tree (`art/town-kit`'s, which stands by day too), strings across Torggatan's Julgatan from wall to wall and swags between the Esplanade's town lamps, all as glows in one draw, string by string as `lightsTown` rises. On the Esplanade on a December night that is 3 draw calls and about 3,700 triangles, and none out of season. Garden trees strung with lights are still to do, in the forest's shader.
- **The aurora** (`art/night/aurora.ts`): a curtain low on the northern horizon, added over the sky for one draw call, as strong as the weather drawn says (`day.weather()`, which picks the nights and their bursts and fades it under cloud) and only on a night darker than midsummer's. `?aurora=0.8` holds one, to see it whatever the weather but the clouds.
- **The season** reaches every material as the sun does (`art/season.ts`): the season's dials (`climate/season.ts`) as uniforms the day's date moves each frame (`render/season-feed.ts`), which each layer reads against thresholds rolled from its own places, so nothing seasonal switches on a date and no list is rebuilt for it. `docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md` says what each layer does with them.

## The weather

The weather is shared and drawn from Åland's climate (`climate/`, `docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`): a pure function of the game's calendar (see The day), read each frame by `climate/feed.ts` with the date, how far through it, the hour and the game days the page's time shows, so everyone in the online world shares it, and `?date=`, `?hour=` and the dev menu's Day move it with the sky. The fronts move by game days, and the road's memory of rain and snow is in game hours, while the season's snow and ice follow the date, about a week a game day. The dev menu's Weather or `?weather=` holds one instead; a tool's or a test's page draws none and stays dry unless it asks with `?weather`. The render eases towards it (`render/weather.ts`), so a held one blends in as the shared one does, and it is arcade first: it changes how things look and drive, never so far the road is hard to read.

- **The sky**: over the hour's look, a grey lid by the cloud cover, a storm's darker grey under rain or snow, and fog paling the horizon, all at the look's own brightness so night stays night. The sun's disc fades into the lid rather than darkening: the sky draws it only ever brighter than the sky behind it. The gloom goes on the night's own dials (`dark` dims land and sky together, and `amount` brings the lamps on early once the evening has begun), and the live sun is shortened by the lid, so every material's direct light falls with it.
- **Near the camera** (`render/precipitation.ts`): rain as thin streaks slanted by the wind and snow as flakes drifting and swaying, each one instanced quad in one draw call, a fixed lattice of drops in a box that repeats through the world round the camera, as many drawn as the weather is hard. Nothing moves on the CPU but one offset; none is drawn inside the car or from the map.
- **The road** (`render/road-weather.ts`): water darkens it and mirrors the sky at a low angle, with the sun's glint a streak down the road; fresh snow lies over the carriageway but for two wheel tracks in each lane, and over the shoulder; black ice is a colder, glassier sheen. The terrain's painted far roads darken and whiten to match. Snow over the land is the seasons', in the same `snow` token: a fresh fall whitens the land with the road, and once the season's snow holds the land a road's shoulder lies under the ploughed bank's snow whatever falls (`render/season-feed.ts`, the road's `lying`).
- **Fog** closes the haze (`hazeM`) by the square of its thickness, so the climate's light fogs stay light and a challenge's thick one is still a road's length.

## Sound

`audio/` is the sound, all made in code: the continuous sounds on one small Web Audio graph (`audio/graph.ts`) whose parameters each frame steers, and the one-shots as ZzFX designs on the shared sound library (`audio/sounds.ts`). `audio/model.ts` holds the rules with no Web Audio in them (what the game tells the sound each frame, the jolts heard in the car's motion, and the levels and pitches those ask for), so they are unit-tested. `audio/synthesis.ts` renders every loop once as samples, seeded, so a loop is the same every visit.

- **Each car has its own engine** (`audio/vehicles.ts`): a voice per car (how many times it fires a turn and how evenly, the ring of a firing, its harmonics, exhaust, silencer, intake, and how hard load drives it into rasp) on that car's own gearbox from `sim/cars.ts`. In the game the sound hears the simulation's real gear and revs, so a car swapped mid-drive changes voice with its handling. The voices are a straight six, a turbo triple, a turbo five, a flat-plane V8, a cross-plane V8, a turbo diesel, a tired four, a twin-carb four, a high-revving four, a slow straight six, an electric motor, a V12, a V6 and a turbo flat four.
- **Tyres** are shared by every car and follow what each wheel is on, as the simulation has it (`SimulatedCar.surfaces()` and the drive's condition, `sim/surfaces.ts`): each surface's bed plays by its share of the wheels touching the ground, so two wheels on the verge are half grass and a wheel in the air is on nothing, and crossing the road's edge is heard. Tarmac roars, rock is a harder gritty roll with knocks at its joints, gravel and grass are their own loops. In a slide tarmac squeals, and loose ground scrabbles in its own bed and throws stones (gravel and rock) or turf (grass) into the arches as one-shots, at a rate the mix sets; fast gravel throws the odd stone just rolling. The condition lies on every surface at once: wet dulls the beds and adds a spray loop and a lower, splashier hiss in a slide, and keeps most stones down; snow covers the ground with a soft crunch of its own and squeaks in a slide, with no stones; ice is near silent rolling and hisses thin in a slide. Studded tyres tick and hum on a bare hard road, a loop of four wheels' studs, duller when wet and on ice and buried by snow. The tables of surface and condition are in `audio/model.ts`.
- **Wind and places**: wind by speed, and forest, shore and town beds by where the car is (`sim/surroundings.ts`). Forest is a stand-in, the land that's neither town nor shore, because canopy data is only on the GPU. The beds follow the season, the hour and the weather the render draws (`placeMix`, `audio/model.ts`), easing with them rather than switching: the forest's leaves in the wind as its broadleaves are in leaf, and winter's lower rush through bare branches as they aren't; birdsong from mid-March, full from May to mid-July and gone by September, by daylight from the sun's height, loudest in a spring dawn chorus and hushed by rain, with only a crow or a tit now and then on a winter's day; the shore's waves giving way to wind over ice as the sea freezes, and its gulls from April to September, by day; and the town quieter at night.
- **The weather**: rain on the roof and glass, two loops of drops, each a short broadband tap with the roof panel's dull thump under some, softened as a roof and glass would: light rain's drops heard one by one, and heavy rain's dense hiss, which takes over as it falls harder and as the car drives faster into it, partly covered by the car's own noise at speed; wet snow is a soft patter of it; and the weather's own wind, a gusting band from a breeze up to a gale, apart from the wind of the car's speed. Both play while the drive is stopped, as the place does. The audition has a slider for each.
- **`?sounds` is the audition** (`audio/sound-lab.tsx`), on the shared `SoundLab`: a season and an hour for the place beds (a day in each season, with the game's own sun and season for it), every car and the bicycle, each car on its own gearbox, where flooring it pulls as that car pulls in the game on the flat, through its gears to its own top speed. The left and right wheels each take a surface, so the road's edge can be straddled, and the condition and the tyres are chosen beside them. The revs can be held on one note, and the one-shots play below. `audio/tools/` renders scenes headless through the same graph and measures what comes out. The owner judges the sound by ear.

## Multiplayer

Online free roam, on the relay (`src/shared/lib/peer/`, see the root `CLAUDE.md`): every player **claims** one fixed room (`net/protocol.ts`'s `WORLD_CODE`), so the first to open the game hosts and everyone after joins, with no code to type, and when the host leaves the rest claim it again among themselves. Names are the shared profile's. `net/online-world.ts` is the whole of what's shared: each player's `CarState`, which names their car, 20 times a second (every sixth step), sent up to the host, which hands each on to every other guest naming whose it is, a co-op challenge's messages (see Challenges), and the world's time, which each guest asks the host for once on joining (see The day). Every state is the whole car, so a new host or a newcomer has the world back within one send, and a handover is no more than the relay's claim. A paused player (the map, the dev menu, a jump's load) sends their car once standing still, and again whenever someone arrives or their car is put somewhere. That's about 370 bytes a state, 7.4 kB a second for each car each player sees; the relay fans the host's frames out, so the host's own upload grows with the players and no one's grows faster. A challenge message goes to one player or to everyone, sent up to the host like a car state, which hands it on to the one it's for, or to every other guest, naming who it's from, and keeps it through a reconnect rather than lose it. The host only relays, and the relay needed no change for any of it.

**Each player is the authority over their own car.** Steering never goes through the host, so nobody drives on someone else's lag. Every other car is a stand-in body in this player's own Rapier world (`sim/remote-cars.ts`): its own car's box, mass and inertia (a player who swaps car gets a new stand-in), steered each step towards where that car is now, its last state carried on by its own velocity and spin since it arrived, for at most a quarter of a second, after which it waits. It's dynamic, not kinematic, so in a step where the two touch the solver trades momentum between the two cars by their weights: a van shoves a roadster aside and barely moves, and the next step the stand-in is steered back on its way. The other driver's client sees the same contact against this car's stand-in there, so both see the hit and each reacts with their own car. With latency, both sides see the other car slightly late and the hit comes out livelier than a real one, a parked car taking most of the speed of the one that hit it; `sim/remote-cars.test.ts` drives one car into another on two clients a tenth of a second apart and holds that the parked one is shoved, neither passes through the other, and nothing is thrown. The stand-in carries how fast it may close on its car (4 m/s, 3 rad/s), so a late state never fires it into anyone.

A stand-in touches only the car being driven (`sim/groups.ts`): no gravity and no ground, so a car whose ground isn't loaded here can't fall through it, and two other cars meeting is for their own clients. Wheel rays don't see stand-ins. A stand-in put somewhere rather than steered there, more than 6 m from where it should be (a first sight, a reset, a jump), touches nothing until it's clear of this car, so two cars on one spot are never thrown apart. The same goes the other way: this car put down near a stand-in (a reset, or a challenge's retry on to a start place where the partner's last state still stands) leaves it touching nothing until the two are clear. A remote car is drawn (`render/remote-cars.ts`, the same `art/cars` art, one set of geometry per kind of car) only where its ground has detail here, because its height is its own client's, from detailed ground; further off it is a marker on the maps. Both maps draw every other player wherever they are, in their own colour: off the map's edge a marker waits on the rim, the way they lie, and the world map names them.

A page on tiles of its own (`?tiles=`, every tool and test) or one driven by automation drives alone unless it names a relay (`?relay=local:<port>`), so a tool never turns up in the real world; `?junction-lab` always drives alone. `?drive-world=<name>` gives a test a world of its own.

Computer-driven cars go the same way (`net/computer-cars.ts`, piece 6 of `ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`): each is run by the player nearest it, who sends its state and its driver's record to everyone as their own car's, and hands it to another player once they've been clearly nearer for a moment and no other car is within a few metres of it; the host hands on the cars of a player who leaves. Everyone else sees it as a stand-in, as another player's car. Two players who take one car at once settle on the lower id, and the player running one can take it out of the world for everyone. In play the only ones are traffic cars hit out of the traffic (below); a test also has a scripted car (`net/scripted-car.ts`).

**Traffic is shared** (`net/shared-traffic.ts`, piece 14 of `ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`): every car, cyclist and walker on the lanes is run by the player nearest it, by the same rule, and every page near it drives it too, with the same driver, between the runner's messages, so a message only puts right what has drifted. A vehicle's whole record is its place on its lane, its speed, its body and a few flags (`LaneRecord`), and its choices at junctions are its seed's, so two pages driving it choose alike. A slot is filled only by the player nearest it, by 10 m, and only beyond every other player's reach as well as out of its own sight, since another player's camera isn't known here (`traffic/bubble.ts`); two players together fill each stretch once. Every distance in that is from a player's car, the place the others are told of, even with the free camera elsewhere, and a page fills nothing until it has heard where every player is, or one joining a world would fill what's already there. Online a vehicle's id says who made it, so a slot refilled where its old vehicle is far off and unknown makes a twin, never a clash. A vehicle is taken away only by its runner, once it's past every player's bubble edge; a page lets go of one it doesn't run as it leaves its own. Each runner sends what it runs only while another player is near enough to hold it, all of it in one message: a car twice a second, ten times within 60 m of another player's car, a cyclist or walker once a second, about 120 bytes a vehicle. A player who leaves (the host too) leaves each of their vehicles to whichever player still here is nearest it and holds it, and two that claim one at once settle on the lower id. A paused page (the map, a menu, a jump's load) keeps stepping its share of the traffic, since everyone else sees it go on. A traffic car hit by a player's car is hit on that player's page only, since stand-ins touch only the car being driven, so that page takes it out of everyone's traffic and runs it as a computer-driven car (above), sent 20 times a second, drawn by everyone as the world car it was with its hazards on; whoever runs it when it rejoins its lane puts it back in the traffic, and one that can't waits until every player is 150 m off. `tools/traffic-bandwidth.ts` measures what's sent, players standing together on the built tiles' lane maps with no browser: two players at the busiest spot of central Mariehamn (147 vehicles round them: 53 cars, 21 cyclists, 73 walkers) send 25 kB a second between them, each about 12 kB up and as much down; in Godby 10 kB; on a country road 3 kB. A third player there adds almost nothing to anyone's upload and downloads the other two's, 26 kB. Players apart send nothing. `e2e/aland-drive-online.spec.ts` plays it on the run's own relay: two pages meet with no code, see each other drive, hit, show on both maps, and the one left hosts when the host goes. `e2e/aland-drive-shared-traffic.spec.ts` has two pages on the fixture with its lane map, which only that spec serves (`serveTiles`' `withLanes`), see the same cars, cyclists and walkers in the same places both ways, after vehicles change hands and after the host goes; `net/shared-traffic.slow.test.ts` holds, with no browser, that two players fill a road once, and that nothing they could see jumps or vanishes through a handover or a player leaving.

## Floor

The owner's laptop is the hardware floor: a Framework 13 with a Ryzen 7 7840U and its integrated Radeon 780M, driving a 3440×1440 100 Hz screen.

**Mobile is not supported.** This project is an exception to the repo's phone-floor and 360px responsive principles. Input is keyboard and controller, with no touch. Nothing is optimised for a phone, and costs are judged against the floor laptop, never a phone.

The target is a steady 60 fps, drawing the 3D world at 100% of the screen's real pixels (`RENDER_SCALE` in `render/game.ts`, which may become a graphics setting). The frame meter is the shared stats.js one; a timestamp query gives the GPU time.

A chase view at 3440×1440 is 60–90 draw calls and 0.5–1.1 M triangles, of which about 0.4 M is the terrain, fixed wherever the car is. The forest follows what's in view: 0.05–0.15 M on open and coastal showcase views, 0.4 M in mixed forest and 0.63 M in Deep Forest.

## Streaming and distance

- **Tiles load round the car** (`render/stream.ts`): any tile within 2.2 km has its detail, and it goes at 2.8 km, so a car running along an edge doesn't load one back and forth. That reach is worked back from the top speed: the detail is first seen at 1.4 km, and 800 m is 11 seconds at 72 m/s, room for the two or three tiles that fall due together at a corner, one after another, over a slow connection. At this reach never more than four tiles are at full detail. Loading follows the car's position and each tile landing, with no timers. In the free camera it follows the camera.
- **Three steps of land, so nothing grows with the island.** The overview (`pipeline/overview.py`) is the whole world in two small files from the start: heights every 80 m with the forest on them, and the land's colour at 40 m with its light baked in. Far data, heights every 16 m, shading at 8 m and a surface mask at 4 m, is fetched per tile within 6 km of the car and dropped past 7.5 km, into a fixed pool of 16 layers. Detail is as above. Only the tiles round the start come before the drive does. The terrain fades from far images to the overview between 4.4 and 5.8 km, inside the reach every tile is sure to hold them, so tiles arriving at the edge never show as a line.
- **A landing costs a frame nothing** at a capped frame rate: about 1.4 s of slices, the worst frame near 20 ms. Uncapped, as a browser run with vsync off is, frames outrun the GPU by about 1.8 s of queued work and a texture copy blocks the main thread until that drains, for 200–700 ms a frame. No player meets that, because a display paces frames, so `tools/drive.mjs --clock` and `tools/landings.mjs` measure capped. A pipeline first used mid-drive would stall its frame while it compiled, so the game builds every mesh once before the drive starts.
- **Off the main thread** (`render/tile.worker.ts`): the fetch, inflating, image decoding and mipmaps, and turning heights and roads into the arrays the game uses. What's left on the main thread is copying to the GPU, a slice of a tile's images a frame, and a tile counts as loaded only once all of it's there. A fetch that fails stops the game with a message on screen, and a test holds that.
- **The terrain is one mesh** (`render/clipmap.ts`), a clipmap: seven square rings of 192 cells that follow the camera, each ring's cells twice the size of the one inside, out to 25 km. Each ring's heights are a layer of a small array texture, copied from the world when it moves, and every ring draws through one material that reads its own ring's layer, cell size and place per object, so the terrain's shader is one pipeline rather than one a ring. A vertex is only a place in that texture, so tiles don't exist as far as the mesh is concerned: a seam can't show. A ring's vertices fold on to the next ring's lattice with distance, so rings meet without a crack and nothing pops, and the innermost ring is exactly the 4 m mesh the car collides with. Its triangle count doesn't grow with the number of tiles. This beat per-tile chunks with skirts, which would have needed geometry built and uploaded for each tile, seams hidden rather than absent, and more of both at island scale.
- **One shader for every tile** (`render/tile-layers.ts`): each kind of image is an array texture with a layer per tile, and a pixel finds its tile from where it is. So tiles cost no draw calls, and two tiles meet within a pixel. The image coordinates jump at a tile's edge, so the sampling's rate comes from world position, or a line of the blurriest mip would mark every seam.
- **Roads with distance.** Ribbons are cut into 600 m cells, shown within 1.2 km. Terrain paints its own roads from the mask, fading in from 250 m: under the ribbons, it only shows where a coarser ring rises through one, in the same colour, and past them it is the road. Paint, lane lines and junction marks alike, is drawn as each mark's share of the pixel (`art/surfaces/coverage.ts`), as the buildings' walls are: crisp near, and thinning far off to its own share of the road, so no mark ever fades in or vanishes at a distance. A ribbon's look is drawn in metres by `art/surfaces/road.ts`: asphalt or gravel, as OSM's `surface` says where it has one (the pipeline writes each chain's `sealed`, by the most of its length that's tagged), else by class, with service and unclassified paved only in town (a path wears its own class instead, see Paths), wear, patches, cracks, kerbs and paving in town, and marks where a lesser road meets a main one, from what the worker works out per vertex (`road-attributes.ts`). An apron takes the road nearest it, with no marks. A car park or bus station is paving of its own instead, as wide as a street, since its middle can lie far from any road: the road surface file holds those aprons last, and the tile's manifest says how many points they have. The 2 m shading gives way to the far shading between 1 and 1.4 km.
- **The land's colour** is the photo at the same 2 m as the shading, near, and 8 m for every tile from the start, each a layer of an array texture. Its alpha fades the photo out over open water, for what reads the shallows from it; the terrain itself doesn't use it. Close up the ground is drawn over it (`art/surfaces/ground.ts`): each pixel's colour says whether it's grass, earth, rock or gravel, and that material's relief goes over the photo, gone by 150 m. A road's shoulder fades to the same image.
- **Snow on the land** (`art/surfaces/snow.ts`) is a change to the ground's colour before the roads are painted on it and before the hour's light, by the season's `snowCover` and `snowDepthCm`, and the overview past the tiles takes the same, so no line shows where the tiles end. It comes and goes patch by patch: each place's threshold is rolled from patches of about 50 m and 14 m, with a 4 m one ragging their edges close up (an integer-hashed noise on whole cells, `art/hash.ts`), and leaned by what the place is, so thin cover lies first on north-facing slopes, open fields and the ploughed bank beside a road, and last on south slopes, under the trees and on bare rock, and melts back the other way. Past 1.5–4 km the patches settle on the share of the ground they cover, so nothing crawls. Under full cover the ground still reads: thin snow lets the ground's own structure through, the forest floor is greyer and patchier, a town's hard ground is trodden grey, and a road's verge is a brighter ploughed bank, from the road mask, with a faint shadow at its foot. The carriageway is the road surface's own. Its colours are the `snow` and `snow-shade` tokens, which every snow in the game shares. Roofs, the rural kit's faces looking up (sheds, boathouses, mills, docks, stone walls, boulders; not the animals) and the granite's outcrops and field stones take the same snow: a roof or a place on a threshold of its own, so a village whitens roof by roof, steep faces shedding it, and a rock as the ground round it has it. It adds no draw calls and no triangles, only arithmetic per pixel: at `SUMMER` its share is nothing and the ground is exactly as before.
- **The town's ground** (`art/town-ground`), tabled and off (see `docs/HANDOFF.md`): what lies between a town's roads and buildings, lawn, flower beds, a yard's gravel, paving slabs, setts and a car park's tarmac with its bays, drawn by the terrain over the photo from the tile's town image (see Pipeline), a near layer like the photo's. A lawn is the one class it leaves as the terrain's own ground, with the verge growing on it, since a flat green of its own read as bare beside the rest of the island. Its three colour channels are signed distances, each to the edge of one bit of a class, so an edge is ruled and sharp however close the camera is and no filter can make a class between two; its alpha is the way the paving runs, read at the nearest texel because a direction can't be filtered. Slabs and setts lie on a street grid's own square lattice, so a street and the one across it share one. Every pattern fades by the size of a pixel on the ground, and the whole goes back to the photo between 320 and 760 m. A tile without the image draws the photo as before. It is 3000² RGBA at 2 m with its mips, 48 MB a layer and 290 MB for the six near layers, which doubles what the near photo holds: 4 m would be a quarter of that, with corners rounded by a few metres and strips under about 8 m lost, and the choice between them is the owner's, from pictures.
- **The forest** (`render/forest.ts`) has no list of trees: a tree is a place on a 6 m lattice of the world, its height is read off the tile's canopy image there, its species and whether it stands at all off the tile's stands, and the rest rolled from the place (`render/tree-place.ts`), so a tree comes back the same and moving costs nothing. Each frame a compute pass goes through every place within the forest's reach and keeps those where a tree stands, clear of roads and inside the view with a margin for its crown, cheapest test first, so a place behind the camera reads no image. Survivors go into one list per model, each with everything its vertices need, and only the lists are drawn, by indirect draws whose counts the pass writes. So a place with no tree and a tree behind the camera cost no vertices, and the triangle count follows what's in view. Nothing thins: every tree stays to the forest's reach, the full turned shape to 464 m and a far crown past that, handing over by a screen-door crossfade on complementary pixels of one pattern, so a tree stays whole, for those metres on both lists. A tree never changes height, and its ground is blended across the terrain's nodes and chosen by its distance, not its model, so a handover can't move it. Beyond the last trees the terrain's own vertices rise by the canopy, from 300 to 40 m inside the reach, so forest stands to the horizon for no extra triangles. A tree reads the road mask at its 8 m level, over a tile's edge too, so none stands on a road (held by a test and counted across the island by `tools/tree-check.ts`). Nor does one stand in a building: a tree reads the tile's clearing round buildings (see Pipeline) at its place, and is no taller than lets its crown, however far any tree's spreads and leans (`CROWN_REACH`, `render/tree-shapes.ts`, near and far crowns alike), clear the nearest building by a slack for what the image may read long; a pine too near one to fit a rock pine's lean grows straight instead. A tree near a building is shorter rather than gone, so gardens keep their trees, and one that would be under 3 m isn't there. The same count holds it, against the buildings' real shapes, the neighbouring tiles' included: across the 94 tiles, 2,887 trunks and 100,330 crowns stood in buildings before and none do now, for 0.24% of the island's trees. Full-shape reach, forest reach and far density are numbers a frame reads (`forestKnobs`), with no rebuild to change them: `FOREST_MOST` sizes the buffers for the top setting, `FOREST_DEFAULTS` in `render/game.ts` is the floor's. Far density below 1 lets a share of places dissolve past the full shape while those left fill out. It's for weaker hardware, and the default keeps every tree. three counts an indirect draw as one tree, so the probe adds the GPU's own count, read back without holding a frame up. `tools/forest-count.mjs` prints those counts at the showcase's views, and `tools/roadside-count.mjs` the roadside's, with and without it.
- **What a tree is** (`render/tree-shapes.ts`): one table of outlines, read by the forest's vertex shader, so a new shape is a row and never a mesh or a draw. A far crown reads a table worked out from the same rows, 6 triangles from its base, belly and tip, so the distant forest keeps its mix and its age and a handover keeps a tree's outline. Pine, spruce and birch come from the stands. MS-NFI lumps everything else as other broadleaf (12% by volume), which can't say where an oak stands, so that share is two general shapes in colours of their own and no grove gets one: round for the oak, ash and maple of Åland's wooded meadows, upright for the alder, aspen and rowan through its woods, with round more likely the more open a tree stands. A tree's height sets its stage of life, blended between young (branches to the ground), grown (a cleared trunk) and old (a long bare trunk under a small high crown; a pine's goes flat). Height is a tree's own, so a stage can't pop. A pine on rock is told by what the images say: short, with the canopy's mean over 32 m well under its own place's. That is what separates it from a young pine in a thicket, whose neighbours are as tall, and the aerial photo is about a quarter brighter under such trees than under short ones among others. Stand density doesn't help: it's near 1 on rock. It's about 13% of trees on the rocky L2433E and 2% on the mixed L2343C. A rock pine is thick and short, forks into flat plates, leans downwind of the south-westerly and its rings wander, each blended by how sure the signal is. A pine's crown is a known gap: a closed oval reads as a leafy broadleaf's, where a real pine's tufts are seen through, and four attempts to change it were rejected (`ideas/pine-crowns.md`, with their code and why).
- **How a tree is lit** (`render/forest.ts`, its leaves' light in `render/foliage-light.ts`): a tree is its species' colour under light worked out per pixel, so a crown reads as a solid mass with no texture and no extra triangles. Each face is lit by the way it points, a share of the face's own direction and a share of the crown's as a whole (out from its middle), so the facets show and the crown still turns from light to shade as one: the sun, wrapped a little round so the shaded side keeps its form, and the sky, brightest on a face looking up, so tops are bright and undersides dark. Then the crown shades itself, from the outline table: dark at its base and lit at its top, darker where a ring is tucked inside the crown's outer envelope (between a spruce's tiers, under a rock pine's plates), and the trunk stands in the crown's shade. That self-shade is deeper among other trees than standing alone, from the same canopy reading that tells a rock pine. Each tree rolls its brightness and leans its leaves a touch yellow or blue, and birch and broadleaf crowns glow at their edges looking towards the sun. A far crown has no tiers, so its table carries the near crown's mean tucked shade, and it is lit by the same rules: measured on far views with the whole forest on one model and then the other, it is within 1% of the near model's brightness, as before. The forest on the whole is as bright as the old flat shading, within a few per cent, so it still meets the terrain's canopy lift, the overview and the photo where they stand in for it. Through the year (`art/leaves.ts`, see `docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`) a broadleaf turns its species' colour, loses its leaves over a fortnight or so of its own and leafs out fresh, and snow lies on a conifer's upward faces, each tree on thresholds rolled from its place, near and far alike. A broadleaf short of any leaves is a different object (`render/bare-tree.ts`): a trunk forking into limbs and branches, low-poly tapered prisms fitted to the crown's outline, so one model fits every stage of life; many small, uneven clumps of leaves at its twigs' ends that grow out of the leafy crown as it shrinks away and then fall one by one, so the crown thins to a gappy mass and then to branches; and a crown-shaped haze of its fine twigs, blended see-through, never a screen pattern, none near, where the branches carry the tree alone, and denser with distance as the branches go below a pixel. These are four more lists, one draw each (birch, round and upright share each model, picked by kind), for trees not fully in leaf; far off a broadleaf's cone shrinks by the leaves it has lost over a far haze. In winter they add about 70–400k triangles at the showcase views, and the clumps up to about 330k more mid-fall.
- **The verge** (`render/verge.ts`) is grass and flowers beside the roads from `art/street`, a planting (`render/planting.ts`, the lattice passes, lists, counts and far versions' colours it shares with the reeds and shrubs) built as the forest is, with no list of tufts: a tuft is a place on a lattice of the world, and a compute pass goes through the places within reach each frame and keeps those beside a road, reading only images a tile already has (`landAt` and `roadsAt`, `render/tile-reads.ts`). So nothing is stored or streamed, no tile needs rebuilding, and the cost follows what's in view. None stands in a building: each planting reads the tile's clearing round buildings (see Pipeline), and a plant, tuft, clump, bush or birch, stands only where its whole spread clears the nearest one by the forest's slack. A tuft stands only on the land side of the drawn waterline, read from the shore line the terrain draws it by (the finest ring of the clipmap that holds the place, since the planting reaches past the first), so none stands in the water. How near a road is, how clear of its carriageway, how dry and how paved are each a mask or photo read at a level that suits it. None grows on a town's hard ground (`pavedAt`: gravel, paving, setts, tarmac and steps), but it does on its lawns and beds, where it hides the photo and gives the ground its detail as it does anywhere. It keeps the forest's rules for detail: a tuft never changes size, and it ends only where it's a few pixels. Two lattices hold the tufts, every 0.5 m to about 200 m and every 1.5 m to 500 m, and each ends by its tufts dissolving one at a time, each at a distance of its own. Each model has a list of its near version near the camera and one of its own far version past its swap: the kit's two versions, as the LOD check draws them, swapping where it measured the change unnoticed, scaled by the tuft's size (see `art/CLAUDE.md`), so the game draws no stand-in of its own. The two trade pixels over a band. A tuft takes a share of the colour and the baked shade of the ground it stands on, a growing share from 140 m, a flower's last. Shrinking far tufts, or growing them in at the reach, showed while driving even with the colours matched. Through the year its grass goes to straw, its flowers fold their heads away out of bloom, fallen leaves lie at its feet and deep snow buries it, tuft by tuft (`docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`). They have no collision.
- **Reeds** (`render/reeds.ts`) stand in the water along sheltered shores, clumps of the rural kit's placed as the verge's tufts are, from images every far tile has. Colour can't place them: in the photo a reed bed and bare granite are the same pale tan. Shelter can: within a kilometre, OSM's mapped reed beds have 36% open water at the median and its bare rock 71%. So a clump stands in the water within a few tens of metres of land, where the water round about is mostly land and the shallows are pale, and not round docks and boats, which the 8 m layer beside the boulder fields keeps clear (see Surface mask), nor on a town's shore, where roads are a fifth of the land within 250 m against a twentieth in the country: a rule that keeps a quarter of the mapped beds' water and 3% of the water off mapped rock. Shelter reads the mask's 256 m level on a grid of nine: a tile's coarser mips, halved rounding down, are stretched over more ground than they hold, and read water from up to 900 m away. It also stands only past the drawn waterline, by the verge's read of the shore, so none stands on the bank. A clump is stems and leaves each turned its own way, never a card: beds of upright panels looked flat up close. It keeps the verge's rules for detail: a clump never changes size, every metre to about 400 m and every 3 m to about 1 km, each lattice ending by its clumps dissolving one at a time, and from 200 m a clump takes on the photo's colour there, which over a bed is the reeds' own. Past its measured swap, scaled by its size, a clump is its own far version painted band by band in the near clump's own colours at that height. Its green goes to straw-gold for the winter, clump by clump. They have no collision.
- **Bushes, junipers and young birches** (`render/shrubs.ts`) are the rural kit's, placed as the verge's tufts are. Hazel, sallow and birch stand along forest edges, open ground with canopy close by, sallow likelier near water, and like the verge only on the land side of the drawn waterline. Juniper stands on open rock among trees: the photo's stone with forest within about 60 m, because in a spring photo a bare ploughed field is as pale as rock. Bushes and junipers are on a 2.5 m lattice to about 500 m, birches on a 6 m one to about 1.3 km, each ending by dissolving one at a time; nothing changes size, far off a plant takes on the photo's colour, and past its measured swap, scaled by its size, each model is the kit's far version painted band by band in its near version's colours. They are lit as the trees are, from `render/foliage-light.ts`: by the way each face points, darker at a plant's foot than its top, each rolling its brightness and a touch of its hue, and on the whole as bright as before, so a plant still meets the photo it turns to. Seven models, a near and a far list each, are 14 draw calls. Hazel, sallow and birch turn and lose their leaves through the year as the forest's broadleaves do: a bare one (`art/rural/bare-plants.ts`) is its stems and twigs near, a hazel's fanning straight from the ground, a sallow's arching, a young birch's a slim leader with hanging twigs, clumps of leaves on them while it has any, and a see-through haze of its twigs further off; all the bare forms share one list and the hazes another, two draw calls more. A juniper takes snow. They have no collision.
- **Granite** (`render/granite.ts`) is Åland's bare rock where it breaks through, a planting over the rock the Topographic Database maps (see Pipeline), which rides in the surface mask's alpha. Bare rock has nothing on it for the elevation model to see past, so its domes, whalebacks and steps are already the terrain: what the 4 m mesh can't carry is the rock's skin, its edge where the turf gives out and the low swell of each slab. So an outcrop is the rural kit's dome laid over the ground rather than stood on it, on a 7 m lattice to about 200 m: every corner lies on the innermost ring's own triangles (`groundOnMeshAt`, the diagonal `patchIndices` lays and the car collides with), raised by 0.12–0.35 m of the dome's profile, with its rim 0.4 m under. A corner is raised only as far as the mapped rock reaches and sinks at water and carriageways, so an outcrop's outline is the map's at 4 m, a shore slab slides under the sea, and none can cover a road. Overlapping, they make one sheet of rock. Places are fewer under trees, where the floor on rock is moss and needles. The spring photo bleaches bare granite nearly white, so wherever rock is mapped and no grass shows, the terrain itself takes the kit's rapakivi red at the photo's brightness, kept from glaring (`rockFloor`, `art/surfaces/ground.ts`), at every distance, and set beside photos of Åland's shores. An outcrop wears that same colour, with the photo's baked shade at 2 m, the kit's crystals, cracks and lichen over it at half strength, and the grass a spring photo shows on thin turf turned to rock; its light is the ground's times how much more or less its facets face the sun than the ground under them. So from afar an outcrop is the photo, and it ends by dissolving one place at a time between 180 and 260 m, inside the innermost ring, where the photo already carries the rock. Close up the terrain's own relief is rock's wherever the rock is mapped and the photo shows no grass, under trees too. Two seeds, near and far, are 4 draw calls; at the showcase's Getabergen view (8), the rockiest, they draw 258 outcrops and 8,200 triangles, and next to none at the others. A boulder field is a carpet of stones: the terrain there is the stones' colour, darker for the gaps between them, with a stony relief close up, and no verge grows on it; a second planting, the field stones, stands the kit's boulder on it at a third to three quarters of a metre, on a 0.9 m lattice, the rounded version near and the few-sided one past its measured swap, scaled by its size. Each stone lasts until it is about three pixels across on the floor's screen, so a small one goes at about 140 m and the biggest at 345 m, each dissolving over the last part of its own reach: ending them all at one distance, 26–34 m, was seen while driving. In the field with the most of them (L3122B) that is about 16,000 stones and 200,000 triangles in view, and nothing anywhere else. Boulders, mapped and the biggest in boulder fields, are the kit's boulder at places from the pipeline, drawn with the boathouses and sheds (`render/rural-buildings.ts`) at the kit's own reach, and they stop the car. Their four models are 8 draw calls, and since every boulder of a loaded tile runs its near version's vertices, they add 21–76k triangles to three's count at the showcase views, the most at Getabergen.
- **Buildings** (`render/buildings.ts`) stream with their tile, merged per 600 m cell, shown within the detail radius. There is no far version: past that a building is its roof in the photo. Their walls have no texture: boards, windows, doors, trim and shops are drawn in the shader from each facade's real width and eaves. A wall has one of ten looks, which its tint names: the pipeline's own timber, plaster and boards, or one of `art/facades`' villa, brick, block, glass, hall, barn and granite, in 32 paints, each with its own windows, doors, belts and joints, rolled per building so no two streets repeat. The looks are real branches of the shader, so a pixel pays for its own look only. Each shape is drawn as its share of the pixel (`art/surfaces/coverage.ts`), so far off it blurs into the wall's average rather than fading out: nothing shimmers and no distance shows a change. Only what can't be drawn that way fades, while it is still a few pixels across, and always to its own average: lines too fine to draw, shapes that aren't boxes, and colours that change from brick to brick or pane to pane. Roofs and shopfronts are drawn the same way, by `art/surfaces`, a roof's tiles and seams in its face's own metres across and up the slope, which the pipeline writes on its vertices as a wall's are. A shop is where OSM has a real shop, never a roll: a ground floor's glass front, a big shop's glass with its name high on the wall, or a whole wall that is a sign. The sign says the trade, or an invented name for a chain, never a real one, in road-sign letters from the sign sheet. Real boathouses and sheds are the rural kit's models instead (`render/rural-buildings.ts`), fitted to their footprints: one list of places per model across every loaded tile, so each model is two draw calls (near and far) however many tiles are near, and each place leaves at the buildings' own reach. A model's far version wears its near version's door, windows and trim painted on its walls, each as its share of the pixel, and its boards settle on their average, so the swap between the two shows nothing. A place past its swap still runs its near version's vertices, folded to nothing: three's triangle count includes them, though they draw no pixels.
- **Windmills and the farm** are the rural kit's. A post mill stands 14 m to its top sail on open farmland, in sight for kilometres, so every mill on the island is one list from the start, read from `world.json` as the church towers' far versions are (`render/windmills.ts`): it never leaves with its tile's detail, swaps to its far version at 320 m, and ends at 12 km, where it is a pixel and a half tall on the floor's screen, so its shrinking into the ground over the last of that can't be seen. Two draw calls, and since every mill runs both versions' vertices wherever it is, the island's 92 add about 41,000 triangles to three's count. Bales, tractors, sheep and cows stream with their tile in `models.json`, drawn with the boathouses (`render/rural-buildings.ts`) at the kit's own reach, which is where each is about a pixel and a half tall too: 750 m for a sheep, 1.15 km for a cow, 1.2 km for a bale, and 2.1 km for a tractor, a little short of it, inside the reach every tile's detail is sure of. A cow swaps to its far version at 260 m rather than the sheep's 60: its patches are drawn in its own metres, fading to their mean colour by 170 m, and the far version wears that mean, so the swap waits until there is nothing to lose. A sheep's coat and horns and a cow's breed and patches are each place's roll, read in both versions of the material, so a flock or a herd costs no more models than one animal. Eight models are 16 draw calls at most, and each place runs its near version's vertices: 19,000 (L2343C) to 33,000 (L2343F) triangles a farming tile with its detail loaded, and a few thousand more for its cows. Looking over those two tiles' fields and mills at 1600×900, in a world where only they have mills (12), they add 14–16 draw calls and 23,000–57,000 triangles.
- **Docks and boats** (`render/docks.ts`) are the pipeline's runs, drawn as the rural kit's jetty, pier and pontoon sections end to end, each stretched a little to fill its run whole and widened to its width, so a dock of any size is the same few models. A private jetty's head and rowing boat are the rural kit's too, and a marina's yachts and motor boats `art/boats`', which rock on the water as one body. Boats on buoys are `art/boats`' own, dressed with their pennant and buoy. Out of season the boats are laid up, each on a threshold of its own, and the docks stay (see "Placed things out only in their season"). Like the boathouses, each model is one list across every loaded tile (`render/placed-models.ts`, which both share), two draw calls, leaving at the buildings' reach, and a place past its swap still runs its near vertices folded to nothing. That is most of a marina's cost: three counts every berthed boat's near triangles whether drawn or not, about 0.6 M with Mariehamn's tile loaded.
- **People** (`render/people.ts`) are `art/people`'s figures where the pipeline placed them (see Pipeline): every file a tile lists under `people`, one for each module that places people, is read in the tile's worker and baked there into one crowd (`render/tile-people.ts`), handed over as plain arrays, so a tile with anyone on it is four draw calls (three meshes, and the transparent shadow drawn twice), landing and leaving with its detail. Each figure's detail is its own, by its own distance in the kit's shader: the full figure to 34 m, giving way to its outline by a screen pattern, which reaches to 200 m. Their motions run on one clock the game holds when paused. three counts every figure's near and far triangles wherever it is: 57,000 for Mariehamn's 120 waterfront people. `tools/people-count.mjs` prints the draws and triangles with and without them at the showcase's views. They have no collision. The lane lab's walkers are the same figures in a crowd that moves (`art/people/walking.ts`): each walker within the people's reach takes a slot, its shape written into the near, far and shadow meshes once as it takes it, and each frame writes only where it stands and how far through its stride it is, so it is three draws however many walk, its stride run on by the ground covered so its feet keep their place, shortening as it slows. Walkers go round the placed people, and cars and cyclists don't touch them.
- **Sea marks** (`render/sea-marks.ts`) stand at the official aids to navigation (see Pipeline), as `art/marina`'s spars, pillar buoys, fixed marks, striped leading marks, skerry lights and radar marks, and `art/identity`'s whitewashed cairns and lighthouses; a radar mark is scaled to the register's height. The file is the whole island, but each model's list holds only the marks within its reach of the camera's kilometre cell, rebuilt when the camera crosses into another, so a model is at most two draw calls while any is near, none for a version no mark is in range of, and none otherwise, and three counts only the vertices of the marks round the camera. A floating mark stands on the drawn sea and one ashore on the ground as the world has it when its list is built.
- **Boats under way** (`sea/`, `render/boats-under-way.ts`) are where the world's clock puts them, and nothing is sent over the network: the clock is the seconds every machine's own clock counts (`sea/clock.ts`), run at real speed, and a boat's place, heading and speed are a pure function of its lane and that time (`sea/motion.ts`), so every player sees the same boats. Anything else on the water moved by time, such as the regatta, takes the same clock. It isn't the time of day: the day is worked out from it, faster than real time (see The day), while the boats keep their real speed, so the timetable is in cycles of real seconds and doesn't line up with the game's mornings and evenings. `?sea=<time>` (such as 2026-11-04T12:18:00Z) starts a page's clock at that moment on the drive's first frame, running on at real speed, so a link can open just before a boat does something; that page's boats are out of step with everyone else's. A lane is a line over water from `sea/lanes.json`, one small file for the whole island that `pipeline/sea_lanes.py` writes beside the code (see Pipeline), so no tile is rebuilt for it and it needs no publish. A voyage sails its lanes on a fixed cycle (`sea/timetable.ts`, an invented timetable on the real lines): along a lane it gathers way from a stop, slows for a tight corner and over the last stretch to a berth, and keeps to starboard of the line, so two meeting on one lane pass; at a berth a single-ended ship stops off the quay and comes round, then backs in, so it lies bow out and leaves ahead, or at Västerhamn, where the ships lie alongside the quay, turns to the quay's line and moves across on to it, and off it the same way; a double-ended ferry, the same at both ends, just goes back; a small boat lies at the far end of its trip and swings to face home; and a ship past the islands is out of sight until its next call. The ships call at Mariehamn's Västerhamn and Långnäs on through services between Stockholm, Turku and Tallinn, no two in one approach at once, and Eckerö's crosses to Grisslehamn every eight hours, all on the town kit's Baltic ferry (Eckerö's smaller); Ålandstrafiken's four archipelago lines run the town kit's double-ended car ferry (`art/town-kit/road-ferry.ts`), and the four road ferries, which all run on wires, `art/identity`'s cable ferry; the excursion boats to Kobba Klintar and the outer islands are `art/boats`' passenger boat; and one small boat to each harbour trip comes and goes on a cycle of its own rolled from its lane: a keeled one (`art/boats`' sailing yachts and double-ender) on a lane kept out of charted water too shallow for it, or a shallow one (the allmogebåt, launches, RIBs and fishing boats). `sea/motion.test.ts` holds that nothing jumps over a whole cycle, a passage keeps to its speeds and accelerations, a ship lies bow out or along its quay, no two small boats wait in one place, and no two ferries' hulls come within 10 m over a whole day; `tools/sea-times.ts` says when boats pass a place, as moments `?sea=` takes. Each model is one list of every vessel drawn with it, two draw calls however many sail and none for a version no vessel on it is in range of (`render/versions-in-reach.ts`), and each frame writes only the lists' places, a few floats a vessel; one out of sight folds to nothing. Boats and sea marks together add 8 to 18 draw calls and 2,000 to 15,000 triangles at the review's views. They keep their kits' levels of detail and a reach far past the shore: a ship to 20 km, a sail to 4 km, an engine boat to 2.5 km, the far versions a few dozen triangles. A boat under way rocks to a seed of its own rather than its place, which changes every frame. **The ferries don't stop the car.** The small boats keep to the season: the leisure and excursion boats sail only while boats are afloat, each on a threshold of its own, a fishing boat all year, and none over ice; the ferries run through the ice, with no channel drawn behind them. A berthed road ferry has no collision, so a car driven off the ramp's end goes into the water there as at any shore: the ground is static blocks and a moving deck isn't something the car can ride, and a wall that stood only while a ferry was in would make the same quay a wall at one moment and water the next, which reads as more broken than either. Riding a ferry is a feature of its own.
- **Stone walls** (`render/stone-walls.ts`) are the rural kit's dry-stone wall laid end to end along each line of the tile's `walls.json`. The worker cuts a line into equal sections near the kit's 2.4 m and stands both ends of each on the drawn ground (`render/tile-walls.ts`), and each section is stretched to its length and sheared to its slope, so a wall follows its ground and never floats over a dip or cuts through a rise. Half the sections are laid the other way round, so the stones don't repeat. A tile's sections sit in cells, 150 m for the near version and 600 m for the far, and a cell is drawn only while some of it could be in its version's range and in view, so the near version's stones cost nothing past the first hundred metres; which version a section shows is still the kit's material's choice by its own distance, swapping at about 110 m, so a cell can't make anything appear. Past 700 m a wall is a pixel or two tall, and it grows out of the ground over its last stretch. Nothing planted stands up through one: the verge, the bushes and the birches each read a place's distance from the nearest wall (`render/wall-clearing.ts`), an image of its own that follows the camera, since a wall is too thin for any image a tile has, and a plant stands only where its own spread clears the wall. The same image keeps them off the street furniture, each piece a line along its length with a reach of its own: a café table's covers its chairs and the gap to the next table, so a terrace stands clear as a patio. Its square reaches past each planting's reach, and walls only stand on tiles with detail, which land beyond anything planted, so no plant vanishes as the square moves or a tile lands.
- **The regatta** (`render/regatta.ts`) is two fleets racing where Åland sails: off Mariehamn's Västerhamn, where the town's sailing club races, and across Lumparn in the middle of the island. Each is `art/boats`' `buildRegatta`, one instanced draw of three triangles a boat, beating up a line laid into the south-westerly and running back, drawn only past 1.5 km. The courses are a list in scene metres, each wholly over the tiles' water and 1.6 km or more from any land a road reaches, so no car comes near enough to see a fleet dissolve: islets with no road may lie nearer. A fleet sails by the world's clock (`sea/clock.ts`), wrapped at 24 hours, with every boat sailing whole laps in that time so the wrap shows no jump, and only in its season (each course on its own threshold of `boatsAfloat`) and on open water: every player sees the same boats in the same places, and pausing the game doesn't stop the race, though the probe's `hold` and `holdSea` do, as they do the boats under way.
- **The roadside** (`render/roadside.ts`) is the street lamps, power poles and their wires, guard rails, edge posts, mailboxes, bus shelters and road signs the pipeline placed (`pipeline/roadside.py`), the street furniture (`pipeline/furniture.py`): benches, bins, bike racks, café tables with their chairs, and parasols, and the town dressing (`pipeline/dressing.py`): lindens, flag and midsummer poles, fences, planters, picnic tables, viewing towers and viewpoint platforms, streaming with their tile as the boathouses do: one list of places per model across every loaded tile, so a model's draws don't grow with the tiles. Lamps, poles, mailboxes and shelters are `art/roadside`'s, rails, edge posts, the furniture, lindens, poles and planters `art/street`'s, picnic tables, towers and platforms `art/identity`'s, and the signs `art/surfaces`' own plates, posts and look, from Traficom's drawings, placed through `art/roadside/signs.ts`: plates cut to one outline share a mesh and a draw, each shifted on the sign sheet to its own sign, so the draws follow the outlines in use, not the signs. Nothing changes size: between a model's near and far versions the two trade pixels by a screen pattern, and at its reach the last version dissolves the same way, at a distance rolled per place so a row doesn't go on one line. A wire span goes whole at 700 m, dissolving too. Flags and ribbons move on the roadside's own clock, which the game holds while it's paused; a linden holds still, as the forest's trees do, and turns yellow through the year by the street material's season (`lindenThroughYear`, `art/street/linden.ts`), then loses its leaves as the forest's broadleaves do: its crown shrinks into its middle while clumps of leaves grow on its bare branches and fall one by one. A bare linden (`art/street/bare-linden.ts`) is a crown-lifted dome of arching limbs set with straight shoots over the leafy model's trunk, to 220 m, and past that a faint see-through haze of its twigs in the crown's outline; its wood, its clumps and its haze are three more lists at every linden, three draw calls, filled only while the season has a linden short of leaves (the clumps only while one is part way through them), so summer costs nothing. The street kit's objects dissolve the same way: they used to grow from the ground over the last share of their reach, which a bench, still several pixels tall at its 200 m, would show. The town kit's town detail rides in the same list (`render/town-roadside.ts`): a town's post-top lamps, traffic lights whose lamps cycle on the renderer's clock, each junction from a start of its own, street-name posts whose blades read a name sheet, and village signs whose boards read another. Each sheet holds only the names on the loaded tiles' plates and is redrawn, as a texture swap with no new pipeline, when that set changes. Outside the town detail, a model's near list holds only the places within its near version's range of the camera and 60 m more, written again whenever the camera has moved 60 m, as the parked cars' are: the material still picks each place's version by its own distance, so a list changing shows nothing. Its far list, a few triangles a place, holds every place on the loaded tiles, and runs their vertices folded to nothing past the reach. On the Esplanade and at Västerhamn at 3440×1440, with the town dressing loaded round Mariehamn (1,313 lindens and 6,100 fence sections among 21,000 places), the roadside adds 32 draw calls and 320,000–420,000 triangles as three counts them; before the near lists followed the camera, the roadside without the dressing cost 505,000.
- **Parked cars** (`render/parked-cars.ts`) are `art/cars`' eighteen world cars (`fleet.ts`) at the places `pipeline/parking.py` wrote: which model, paint, roof rack and height is rolled from each place (`render/parked-places.ts`), so a car comes back the same and the tile worker, which has no art, stands its walls to the same model. Each model is a near list (under 2,000 triangles) and a stand-in list (to 900 m) across every loaded tile, handing over by a screen-door dissolve at the swap the LOD check measured for that model (`modelSwapM`, see `art/CLAUDE.md`: 31–66 m, most 36–48 m). The stand-in is the near model simplified (`buildDistant`, `art/cars/body.ts`), 130–164 triangles with its indicators, most 130–140: the outline is the silhouette's own points, thinned between the places its surfaces change and its nose's and tail's corners pulled back as the near body's are, so the screen, side windows and rear window are glass where the near model's are; a dark face as big as each tyre with its tread below the body; the head and tail lamps laid on its ends from the near model's lenses; the indicators where the near model's stand; and the rack and roof signs as boxes. Its paint shades smooth across gentle bends, as the near body does, while its glass stays flat, since a pane bent towards the paint beside it catches the sky where the near one doesn't and dissolving the same way at the reach, so 36 draw calls at most. Each list holds only the cars within its version's reach of the camera (a near list its own model's swap) and a margin, rewritten when the camera has moved that margin, which no one sees: the material still picks each car's version by its own distance. With the tiles round Mariehamn loaded (5,295 cars) the showcase's Esplanade and Västerhamn views add 19–20 draw calls and 100–153k triangles, nearly all of it the 720–1,110 stand-ins in view: a stand-in of about thirty triangles, a flat wedge with no wheels or lamps, cost 40–61k there and was seen as a grey box at its swap. `tools/parked-count.mjs` prints those counts, with and without the cars. Every world car has indicator lamps at its four corners (`art/cars/indicators.ts`), which flash amber, out of step from car to car, when its place's `signal` says left, right or hazards, on a clock the game holds when paused (`indicatorClock`); a parked car shows none. A list of world cars is five vertex buffers, the car's own attributes packed into one, of the eight a WebGPU pipeline can read, so another per-car attribute has to fit in those. In winter snow lies on a world car's top on a threshold of its own, and on only about a third of the cars in traffic, which have been swept.
- **Placed things out only in their season** (`climate/placed-season.ts`, `render/in-season.ts`): moored and berthed boats by `boatsAfloat`, cows and sheep by `grazing`, bales by `bales`, a café terrace's tables, chairs and parasols by `terraces`, and the people seated at them, a sunbather and someone at a boat with them. Each thing leaves and comes back on its own threshold, rolled from its place on the GPU and the CPU alike, a terrace whole on one roll from the square of ground it stands on so its sitters roll with their table. A kit draws through one copy of its material whose vertex shader folds a place out of season to its foot, as a place past its reach folds, every kind of that kit through the same copy, each mesh marked with the share and salt its kind follows (a share of 1 for a kind out all year), so the season rebuilds no list, costs no draw and adds no pipeline a kind: a folded place still runs its vertices, as one past its reach does. A person carries their season on their vertices (`render/tile-people.ts`).
- **Water is a surface of its own** (`render/clipmap.ts`, `render/water.ts`): the clipmap's two nearest rings again, at the water's baked level instead of the ground's, on the same triangles. A pixel's depth is exact, but where the line falls is the shore's own smooth line, from the distance to it baked at every node: on a low shore the ground's corners sit within centimetres of the water, and a line where they cross it would follow the 4 m mesh in squares and teeth. Past that line the water is drawn and the ground is coloured as water; short of it the water is cut away and the ground is land. Depth sets the colour: pale and green-brown over the first two metres, the open water's dark past that, and land a hand's height above the water is darker, wet. Farther out than those rings, the ground under water is drawn as the water above it, on the same line, since a few metres of depth no longer shift anything on screen. Past the tiles, the sea plane lies under the deepest seabed and takes its shallows from the overview, as before. A triangle with a corner where no water is near isn't drawn, so the surface never shows through land.
- **Churches and landmarks** (`render/landmarks.ts`): the ten medieval stone churches of `art/facades` and the landmarks of `art/landmarks` stand at their real sites, with the places the data has no building for: the lookout towers on Getabergen, Storberget and Orrdalsklint, the Esplanade's two statues, Brännklint's tower ruin, Prästö's three garrison graveyards, Johannisberg's grave field and Kungsö battery, each built on the ground round it (`art/landmarks/places.ts`), drawn with the buildings' own material. Every site's far version is one mesh for the whole island from the start, so the skyline's towers are one draw call and stand at any distance, as the land under them does; past the buildings' reach a church tower is what's left of a village. A tile's near versions are one mesh, built when its detail lands, because a church's yard wall follows the ground. Each site shows one version, chosen by its distance on one flag per site that both meshes' vertices read, the other folding to a point, so a site is never both or neither. The swap is at 1.4 km, where the near version's finest parts are about a pixel, so the change isn't seen.
- **What stands at a building** (`render/building-add-ons.ts`): a villa's veranda or porch at its door, sometimes a bay and a fronton, and a big shop's entrance canopy with its name on a pylon, read from the tile's building cells in its worker (`render/tile-add-ons.ts`, from `art/facades/add-on-places.ts`): the facade with the door, which the pipeline put on the street side, and its seed. One that would stand on the road surface, road or path, is left out (a road is pushed only half a metre clear of a wall, a porch reaches two), and a pylon alone where only it would. The villas' add-ons are one instanced list per kind across every loaded tile, four draws, each folding flat on its wall between 260 and 380 m and gone where its building is; the entrances and the near-only sites' fuel stations are wall-format meshes that join the tile's building cells, beside the cell they stand in, so they show and hide with it. The terminal is the town kit's model at its place while its tile has detail, its near version to 700 m and its far one to 3 km. Porches, verandas and bays stop the car at their decks and steps, an entrance at its posts and pylon, a station at its pump islands and pylon, the terminal at its legs. With Mariehamn's two tiles loaded that is 106 add-ons, 11 entrances, 5 stations and the terminal: about 10,600 triangles as three counts them, and 6 draws plus one for each cell with shops in view.
- **The sky** (`art/sky`) is one coarse dome round the eye, drawn after everything opaque so the ground spares it every pixel it covers, and pinned to the depth buffer's far plane so that from any height it can't cover the ground: a gradient, the live sun (see The day) and five reads of one cloud texture, for one draw call and 224 triangles. Clouds are a matter of direction, so they stay overhead however high the camera goes. Haze fades to the sky's own foot along each pixel's direction, and open water melts into that foot near the horizon (`meltAlong`), so land, sea and sky meet with no line. Water reflects the clear sky, which reads no texture.
- **Past the last tile** is the sea plane and haze. The world's end is a deliberate wall, out at sea or on the far shore of the tiles around the island.
- **First load** shows a bar counting what has landed: the overview, each far tile round the start, the start's detail, and the shaders. The road network comes after the drive starts, so it can't hold the start up, and the maps show when it lands.

## Graphics approach

Start simple, and add a feature only when it fixes something visibly wrong and its cost is known.

- **Build a system that scales, not one sized to a machine.** A graphics system is efficient by design and scales to the hardware: better hardware scales up, worse scales down, through levels of detail, reach and render scale. A system is never built to a fixed budget, and the one thing to avoid is a system that can't scale. The floor laptop decides the default settings, not the design.
- **Bake ahead of time what can be baked**: terrain shading and soft shadows from the 2 m data.
- **Spend detail near the car.**
- **Keep distance cheap**: simplified terrain, simple crowns for far trees, haze.
- **A change of detail isn't noticed.** Good LOD works in the background and leaves the player to the game. While driving, a player shouldn't catch a tree fading in, a canopy shifting or anything growing out of the ground, and a crossfade doesn't excuse a change that would otherwise catch the eye. The bar is noticeable, not measurable: a change may be there if a driver doesn't see it, and it's judged by eye in motion. This holds for everything with levels of detail: trees, buildings, people, other cars and bikes. A handover goes further out, or the two versions are made to match, until it stops being noticed. Where a model swaps is measured, never chosen by hand (`art/CLAUDE.md`).
- **Rapier only needs the ground around the car.**
- **A shader never draws fine detail from the world's coordinates.** The island reaches 25 km from the world's origin, where a 32-bit float's steps are millimetres, about what a near pixel spans. A pattern laid from world position, or a normal from how world position changes across the pixel, turns to noise there: a roof's seams once did. So a pattern is laid in its surface's own metres, written on the vertices, and a flat face's normal comes from view space, which is measured from the camera (`art/flat-normal.ts`). World position is still fine for what varies over metres, such as weathering, and for looking things up in a tile's images.
- **What a face shares reaches its pixels a little different on some GPUs.** A value the same at every corner of a triangle, a model's place or kind, comes out of the blend across it a bit or two off at some pixels on Nvidia, and not on AMD, so anything that magnifies those bits sparkles there alone: a sheep's coat, a field stone's colour, a facade's panels did. So a model's own seed is a whole number made in the vertex stage, where its attributes are exact, and reaches its pixels as an integer, which isn't blended; every random roll is the integer hash in `art/hash.ts` on whole numbers, never `fract(sin(x) * big)` (`render/gpu-exact.test.ts` holds that); a float is rounded before it becomes an index; and a pattern on the ground reads its place from an anchor near the camera (`art/surfaces/tiled.ts`), not from the world's origin. Checking a change by eye on AMD alone can't show any of this: film it on both (the sparkle shows only in motion).
- **Measure by counts** (draw calls, triangles), per the repo's speed rule. The owner's laptop is always busy with other work, so a time taken on it, paired or not, doesn't decide whether a change made things better or worse. The GPU's timestamps and `tools/drive.mjs --clock` show that a stall exists and where it is, and nothing more.

## Bridges

The elevation model has no bridges: it drops to the water under them. So a stretch of road held up over a gap is a span (`pipeline/roads.py`): a tagged motor bridge, or any road over water in the model, at least 12 m long. Shorter is a culvert and stays an ordinary road. A span's stations shape no ground, so the water stays water under it; its profile runs from bank to bank, rising a little over open water, and the shoulder stays a flat deck. Each chain lists its spans as section ranges in `tile.json`. `bridgeKit` (`render/bridges.ts`) is the one function that says what a bridge looks like: kerb, parapet, rail, fascia, underside and piers, plus the walls the car can't pass. Replacing the kit is replacing that function. No span in the island crosses a tile's edge. Causeways are real ground in the model and need nothing.

**A pier is a dock, not a bridge.** OSM maps many marina piers and pontoons with a footway along them (`man_made=pier` and `highway=footway`, often `floating=yes`), and laid as a road, a footway over water is a span like any other. So Mariehamn's guest harbours at Österhamn and Västerhamn were concrete road bridges with parapets: 12 footway and path spans on OSM's piers across the island, about 1 km of them. Now a non-motor road is cut off wherever it runs on a pier (`pipeline/docks.py`), so only what runs on land is laid, and the pier is drawn by the shore kit as a dock instead (see "Docks and boats"). A pier that carries a motor road, a ferry or fish quay that OSM tags as a pier or a service road running out along one, stays the road's: its span is listed as a quay in `tile.json` (`quays`, indices into the chain's spans), and the kit gives it its kerb and fascia with no parapet or rail. The car can't drive on to a dock: a dock's outline is walls, as a building's is, so a footway ends at the pier's root. A wooden footbridge (`bridge=yes` with `surface=wood`, or `bridge=boardwalk`), such as the walkway along Pommern, is a timber deck too and is taken as a pier, with no berths. Any other footbridge OSM tags as a bridge is still a span and still drawn by the bridge kit, except the Västerhamn terminal's: its walkways, indoor corridors and car ramps are the terminal model's (see "Churches and landmarks" under Pipeline), so none is laid as a road.

## Maps and GPS

`map/` is both maps and the GPS, with no renderer in it. The world map isn't a screen of its own: it is the game's render, from straight above, north up, opening on the main island and zooming out to all of Åland. `mapCameraAt` (`render/map-camera.ts`) is the camera's timed flight there, a pure function of one number: it starts at the exact chase pose, rises with distance growing exponentially, looking at the car, tilts to straight down and turns to north, while the car slides across the frame to its place on the map. Zoom and pan change where the flight ends and work only once it has arrived, so zooming can't leave the map, and a pan can't take the view past the whole island's frame. Pitch and turn are separate numbers, so the camera can't roll: a direct blend of the two views' rotations rolls the horizon over unless the car faced north. Closing plays the flight back, and at 0 the drive picks up exactly where it stopped. The car waits meanwhile, and everything worked out by distance (detail, the rings of terrain, the forest, what streams) stays at the car, so opening the map fetches nothing. Looking closely is what moves them: under 10 km up, the place on the ground in the middle of the view takes the car's place, for detail, the terrain and what streams, with far data only until 3.5 km. Distance from that place ignores the camera's height, or nothing would ever be near. Whole-island views stay on the overview, and the car waits on landing until its own ground is back, as it does for a jump. Haze thins with height, the terrain's painted roads fade, and two more rings of terrain show, which a drive never needs. Over that render a clear canvas fades in with height: the road network at a width on screen, the route, the car, and names by zoom level, projected through the real camera on to sea level, so a hill's road sits a few pixels off from high up. Land away from the car is the overview's, 40 m a pixel, at every zoom. The road network is one file, `map.json.gz` (`pipeline/road_map.py`), built from OSM's whole ways, so tile edges mean nothing to it: junctions, roads between them with their line thinned to a metre, names, kinds, one-way flags, and whether the road can be reached from Eckerö. A road a ferry separates is drawn and never routed to. `road-graph.ts` is the network and an A* over it by rough speed per kind, starting the way the car faces where it can. `navigator.ts` follows the car each frame: the road it's on (a name has to last 25 m before it shows), progress along the route, a reroute once the car is 60 m off it, the distance still to go, which the HUD shows at the top middle with no turn arrow, since the mini map's route shows the turns, and the mini map, which turns with the car, redraws only when it or another player moved or its land changed, and draws each loaded tile's far photo over the overview. `world-map.ts` holds the map's view and input, and redraws only when something changes, another player included once they've moved a couple of pixels. The mini map's land is the overview's image.

Grid north is 6.25° off true north (see Data), and both maps are drawn to grid north.

## Challenges

Bucket list challenges, as `ideas/bucket-list.md` decides them: two kinds, a Route against the clock through gates in order and a Speed trap's camera, each one car, one place on the real roads and one target, passed or failed. Solo and co-op for two are built, the co-op with its synced quick retry and the average. **The system is the foundation; the list on it is still being found**: the first twelve were too short and beaten at the first try, and the second list, longer, with targets from a driver that brakes late (`sim/autopilot.ts`'s speed plan) and every car suited to its road, hasn't been played yet. Neither is a standard for new ones. A challenge is played in the weather of the moment, though its targets were measured dry. Co-op passes its e2e spec, but the owner hasn't played it.

- **The rules** are `challenge/`, with no renderer in it. `specs.ts` is the hand-written list, `places.json` and `targets.json` what `tools/challenge-places.ts` and `tools/challenge-par.ts` measured from it on the built tiles (start places, gates or camera, par and target), joined in `list.ts`. `run.ts` is one attempt as a pure state machine: the countdown, each line found by intersecting a car's path between two steps with it, results and the outcome, for one player or two. `line.ts` is a challenge's route as the GPS's own routes between its waypoints, the one line its gates, starts, par and the GPS during a run all come from.
- **A session** (`challenge/session.ts`) is one challenge played on this client, start to leave: the run, this client's seat on the start line, the car the player had before, and whether the car is still on its way to the line, which the run waits for. Every restart goes through its `retry` and a partner's measured result through its `report`, so the network drives a co-op run through the same doors as the menu does a solo one. It never moves the car itself: the game, its host, swaps the car (`Drive.setCar`) and puts it on the line.
- **The board** (`challenge/board.ts`) is what a drive knows of them: the list and any challenge of the page's own (a test's), those whose start is in this world, which are passed with their best, solo and co-op kept apart (`passes.ts`, kept in this browser with `getProjectStorage` as finds are, the maps showing a pass in either), and whose start ring the car is in, 12 m from the middle of its two start places.
- **On the maps** every start in the world is a rounded square, a chequered flag for a Route and a dial for a Speed trap, in the challenge colour, and the pass colour once passed (`drawChallenges`, `map/draw.ts`). The world map names them when zoomed in to streets, names one under the cursor, and a pick on one routes the GPS to its ring, as a place does. During a run the mini map also draws the lines still to cross as bars across the road (`drawGates`): the next one bold in the challenge colour, the later ones pale, the finish in the pass colour.
- **In the world** (`render/challenge-marks.ts`) each start is a ring of short posts with a tall pale beam over it, marking it from kilometres off, and during a run only the next line to cross stands: an arch over the road, the finish's banner chequered, or a dark gantry with its camera for a Speed trap. All boxes and prisms, flat shaded, in the challenge's own colours: the posts and beams are one instanced list each and the line one small mesh rebuilt when it changes, three draw calls and a few thousand triangles in all. Each stands on the ground as the world has it, read again as tiles sharpen it.
- **Starting.** In a ring the HUD prompts the challenge's own button (X, or E). With no other player in the ring it starts the challenge solo at once; with another there, free of any co-op challenge, it opens a choice of Solo or Co-op, and Co-op invites them. Starting swaps to the challenge's car and puts it on the first start place, the solo one, and the countdown's three seconds run with the car held on the handbrake and the player's input ignored until green. During a run the HUD shows the lights, the clock against the target and the gates passed (a Route) or the speed to reach (a Speed trap), the next line stands in the world, and the GPS holds the challenge's route, never rerouting from it, since its gates are on it. R or Y still puts the car back on the road, and a car put somewhere crosses no line on its way.
- **The HUD in a run** is one panel at the top middle, the distance still to go, to the finish line or the camera rather than the route's end, which can run on past them, with the challenge's title, lights, clock against the target and gates under it, with the road's name and a find's card below, clear of the frame meter at the top right.
- **The menu in a run** holds Resume, Retry and Leave challenge. Solo it pauses, and the run's clock only moves with the simulation's steps, so a paused run stands still. In co-op it doesn't pause, the result card neither: the clock and both cars run on, and the car's controls let go while it's open. When an attempt is over the menu is the result card: passed or failed, the time or speed against the target and the best kept, Retry first and Leave on B and Esc.
- **Retry** puts the car back on its start place at once where the start's tiles are still loaded, and otherwise goes back the way a dev menu jump does, loading round the start with the first load's bar and putting the car down once it has landed: the start is never kept loaded through a run, since a short load is better than a run that draws worse. **Leaving** gives back the car the player had before, where the car is now.
- **Co-op** (`challenge/coop.ts`) is exactly two players over the online world's messages (`net/protocol.ts`), each naming the challenge, the attempt and, through the host, the sender. Pairing is invite, then accept or decline, then start: the invited player's HUD prompts the same button, which opens Join or Decline, and the inviter's sends start, so an invite taken back while its accept was on the wire begins nothing. The inviter takes seat 0 and the one who joins seat 1, each on `starts[seat]`, both in the challenge's car. Start and leave go to everyone, so no client offers a paired player to a third. Either player's Retry, mid-run or on the result card, is applied at once and sent with the next attempt's number; the partner follows it unless that attempt is already under way there, so two retries at once are one restart. Each client puts its car on its place and sends ready once it's there, after any load, and the lights wait for both, the ready gate: an event, and each client's countdown runs from its own moment of hearing it, so each player is timed from their own green and no shared clock is read. Each client measures only its own car and sends its result, which the partner reports into its run, so both settle on the same average; while one is still driving after the other has finished, the HUD shows what it now needs ("Make it by 2:41"). A Route's loss is called only by a client still timing a player of its own, since its own clock is what bounds that player's time, and it sends the loss with the results it had, which the other takes as it stands: two clients never settle one attempt two ways. Either player leaving, or dropping out of the world, ends it for the other with a short notice, and both get their previous cars back.
- **The probe** has `addChallenge` (a challenge of the page's own), `startChallenge` (by id, resolving once the car is on the line), `challenge` (the run's phase, attempt, clock, gates and outcome), `driveChallenge` (the scripted driver along its route) and `challengeBest`; for co-op, `challenge` also gives the seat, the players, whether the partner is ready and each seat's result, and there are `coop` (where this client stands and the messages it has heard), `coopInvite` (by the player's name) and `coopAccept`, `retryChallenge`, and `holdChallengeMessages`, which holds what arrives so two pages can both press before either hears the other.

**Races** (`ideas/races.md`, whose section 8 is the order they're built in) are a third kind of challenge on the same machinery, `kind: "race"`: the board, the ring, the session, co-op for two, the GPS, the menu and the maps all take them as they take a Route. They differ where a race does:

- **The list** is `challenge/race-specs.ts`, the 32 tracks by hand (number, title, shape, ground, car, waypoints from the plan's measured lines, a circuit's last waypoint its first), joined in `list.ts` (`RACES`) with `race-places.json` and `race-laps.json`, which `tools/race-places.ts` and `tools/race-laps.ts` measure, and a test holds them to the list as it is. A race's ground is what its road is laid in, as the places tool reads it (road all asphalt, off-road all gravel, cross country both), and a test holds each to it, so a change to the road surface that moves a race shows; the plan's own labels came from OpenStreetMap's tags and four of them moved. The grid is six places two abreast where a two-lane road (a main road, a tertiary or a street) takes two of the race's car, and four in single file on a lane; a circuit's start line is a little ahead of the pole, and its lap's lines run round to it. The laps tool drives a circuit's lap with `tools/par-driver.ts`, the scripted driver the challenge tool shares, which counts a missed line as a failure at it and eases a spot on every lap, and sets the laps for a race of about seven minutes and never more than ten.
- **The run** (`run.ts`) crosses one lap's lines lap after lap, the last line of the last lap the finish, and can't be lost against the clock: it ends once everyone has finished. **A race shows no time**: only the place counts. Until the AI racers exist, a race has no one in it but its players, who race as a team, so a finish is first and gold (`standings.ts`); the racers bring places, and Forza Horizon's team points for co-op, with them. A race keeps its best place and medal, solo and co-op apart (`passes.ts`).
- **On the maps** a race is a diamond in its ground's colour (road, cross country, off-road), a loop for a circuit and a flag for a sprint, with its best medal on its shoulder; the world map draws its whole route and a card under the cursor. Its ring's posts and beam in the world are its ground's colour. **In the ring** its button opens the race's card (the track, laps, length, car, and the medal and best place won solo and in co-op, then Start, and Co-op with another player there). **In a race** the panel shows the lap and the lines this lap; the result card the place and its medal. The probe's `challenge` adds the lap.

**Places to find** (`map/places.ts`, `map/discovery.ts`) are forty real sights of the main island, each with a short note: the churches, Kastelholm and Bomarsund, museums, monuments, old sites, three viewpoints and the two golf clubs. Every one is on both maps from the start, as its kind's symbol on a faint disc, and turns the place's own colour once found; the world map names the found ones when zoomed in to streets and counts them in a corner. A place is found once the car comes within its nearest road's distance of it plus 50 m (60 m at least), so it takes a visit, not a drive past on the main road; a jump or a look round the map finds nothing. A find shows its name and note in a card while driving, one at a time. What's found is kept in this browser (`getProjectStorage`), and a place the list no longer has is dropped from it. The notes are the game's copies of `docs/HISTORY.md`'s, checked to its standard. `ideas/more-places.md` holds what the first set left out.

## Art direction

Clean, stylised realism: real land colour under a low-poly forest and flat-shaded buildings with real shapes, red houses with white trim. Detail comes from real data and simple shapes, not photographic textures. **All 3D models and textures are made from code:** no generative image models and no outside assets. Open geodata stays the world's base, aerial land colour included; other photos are reference only. Road signs are the one exception: they follow Finland's official drawings, shapes and lettering both, because a road sign has to be the real sign. Cars are invented, with no real brands, and match this style.

## Pipeline

`pipeline/` is Python in its own venv: rasterio and pyproj bring GDAL's readers and PROJ's transforms, and NumPy does the rest. Nothing in it touches the game's code.

    python src/projects/aland-drive/pipeline/run.py L2343C [more tiles]

That builds the venv on first use, downloads what is missing into `.data/raw/` (gitignored, one folder per source, read as untrusted data) and writes a folder of plain files per tile to `.data/tiles/`, described by its `tile.json`, and `world.json` listing them all with a version hashed from their contents, beside the overview and the road map. A tile takes under a minute on one core once its raw data is there. Its eight neighbours' elevation is downloaded too, because the seams and the shading need a rim of them. A tile the mirror doesn't have is open sea and stays out of the world. `run.py --island --jobs 7` builds them all, a core each. Each step prints a line as each of its items finishes, how many are done and about how long that step has left from its pace so far (`pipeline/progress.py`): the tiles, then the roadside, which needs every tile built and goes side by side too, then the world file's steps (the overview, the road map and the version, read from every file) as each starts. An agent runs a long build in the background with its output to a log, and opens `pipeline/watch.ps1 <log>` in a tab of the owner's Terminal panel, which shows only those lines as they come, so the owner can watch it without asking.

**Which tiles** is worked out, not listed by hand: `pipeline/island.py` starts on the road at Eckerö and walks OSM's motor roads out, fetching each tile they reach, until none leads anywhere new. Ferries aren't roads, so that's Fasta Åland and what bridges join to it: 54 tiles. Round those go the 40 neighbours the mirror has, so a shore has land and sea beyond it. `pipeline/island.json` holds the result. Seven of those outer tiles have no 2025 photo, and take 2022's; ten have no laser sheets, and so no trees. Past those, `pipeline/outlying.py` bakes Åland's outer islands for the overview alone: no tile, so nothing streams there, and the maps show them while nothing else can reach them. They take one colour gain to match the tiles, and any island the overview's edge would cut goes whole, so land only ever ends at a shore.

`pipeline/upload.py` publishes that folder (see Hosting), and won't unless `pipeline/check_seams.py` passes.

- **Space:** every position is ETRS-TM35FIN metres minus one world origin for the whole island (`pipeline/grid.py`). Here the grid runs 0.155% larger than the ground, and its north is 6.25° off true north. The game uses grid metres as they are; anything that needs a real compass bearing has to allow for that.
- **Heights:** 1501×1501 nodes, 4 m apart, in centimetres, 2.2 MB after delta coding and gzip. Nodes sit on the corners of the 2 m samples, each the mean of four, so two neighbours compute the same row. The car collides with these same nodes: off the road, 2 m heights would add only a difference of 0.5 cm at the median and 13 cm at the 99th percentile, and only if the drawn mesh changed to match, or the car would drive on ground that isn't there.
- **Shading:** a 3000×3000 image at 2 m of sun, cast shadow and open sky, under 1 MB, baked from the ground as the roads leave it. It carries the fine relief that the 4 m mesh can't.
- **Water:** the mask's red channel. A lake or the sea is one exact level in the elevation model, which real ground never is, so water is any flat area over a hectare, and any smaller flat patch near it and within a few centimetres of its level. The model writes each patch of the sea at its own level, a few centimetres from its neighbours', so a patch cut small by a map sheet's edge would otherwise read as land, and so would the line where two levels meet, which the water closes over when it lies as low as the water round it.
- **Water levels and the seabed** (`pipeline/terrain.py`): the model writes water as its flat surface, so the land would meet the water on a slope of nothing. Each height node within 40 m of water gets the level of the water nearby, as heights are coded, in `water-levels.bin.gz` and every fourth in `water-levels-far.bin.gz`; farther out it's `dryLevel`. Beside it, `shore.bin.gz` and `shore-far.bin.gz` hold each node's metres from the shore, out into the water and below zero on land, to 64 m, with a road's bank over the water counted as land: the line the game draws the waterline on and the car tests the water by. That level is an average over the flat water near it rather than the nearest sample, which two tiles could pick differently where two levels meet at their seam. The drawn surface sits 15 cm under the flat water, so the lowest shore stands clear of it. Under it the ground falls from the drawn surface at the shore, 22 cm a metre out, to 3.5 m down, along a blurred line round the water so the shore is a curve rather than the samples' steps. Ground a few centimetres above the water within three samples of it goes under too: the flatness test stops short of the shore, and the photo shows that strip as shallow water. Nodes the roads shaped keep their height, so a culvert stays a road.
- **Surface mask:** 1500×1500 at 4 m: water, then the coverage of main roads and of lesser ones, drawn from the road surface, and in alpha where reeds may grow, at half where docks keep them off. Half, not none: the game decodes the mask through a 2D canvas, which premultiplies, so a pixel at no alpha would lose its water and roads. The game takes that alpha off as the far data lands, into the green of the 8 m layer that holds the boulder fields, where a 4 m pixel kept clear keeps its whole 8 m pixel clear, and puts the rock in the mask's alpha instead (see Rock).
- **Far data:** every fourth height node, the same numbers as the 4 m grid where the two meet, and the shading reduced to 8 m.
- **Land colour** (`pipeline/ground.py`): the 2025 orthophoto reduced to 2 m and 8 m. It's flown high in spring, so one fixed grade takes off the haze, the same for every tile so seams don't step, with colour fading out in shadow, where it's the sky's blue. The deepest shadow is lifted, or it goes near to black once the render takes it to linear light. Under trees it's darkened, because the photo there is treetops, not ground. The photo leans, so a building's roof lies beside where it stands: round every building the nearest ground takes its place. A real spring shows: ploughed fields are bare.
- **Forest** (`pipeline/laser.py`, `pipeline/canopy.py`): the highest laser return in each 2 m cell less the elevation model, kept in `.data/derived/` (a tile's four sheets take about a minute), then what's tall, not bright in the photo (the laser is years older, so this drops what's been felled), off water and clear of roads and buildings, at 4 m in quarter metres. This was chosen over a simpler source because the laser's shape is exact: every field edge and clearing lands where the photo has it, at about 0.17 MB a tile.
- **Forest stands** (`pipeline/stands.py`): what the laser can't say, in `stands.bin.gz`, 125×125 at 48 m and about 33 kB a tile: gzipped bytes of the shares of pine, spruce and birch, other broadleaf being what's left, and the share of lattice places that should hold a tree. Raw bytes rather than an image, because a canvas premultiplies alpha and a low density would wreck the shares. A pixel of MS-NFI is a guess from nearby field plots, so every theme is read through a kernel the size of a stand (σ 48 m, about 3 ha), species by volume, with a rim so neighbours agree, and after that kernel 48 m loses under a point of pine share against 16 m. Land with no value takes the neighbourhood's (σ 240 m), then the island's. Density is the look, not stems: the share of places for our crowns to cover the ground as the real canopy does, less what the canopy image's own gaps already leave open, from a table made by throwing points at the real jittered lattice, because crowns on a lattice overlap less than the textbook formula says. It restates the lattice, height range and grown crown widths of `render/tree-place.ts` and `render/tree-shapes.ts` (a test holds the widths equal), so a change to those is a rebuild of this. `stands.py` alone adds the file to tiles already built, and `pipeline/stand_audit.py` plants the lattice and measures it.
- **Buildings** (`pipeline/buildings.py`): `solids.bin.gz` holds 16-bit columns by 600 m cell, then the outlines the car hits. Solids come from a tile's sheet and its neighbours', kept by their middle, repaired, with ground faces dropped and walls run down past the ground. A Topographic Database footprint less than half covered by solids gets a generated shape: a ridge along its long side, or a flat top on a big non-house, as tall as the laser saw, within limits by class because the laser sees trees too. Its walls, like a solid's, run down past the ground under each corner, so one on a slope never floats. Class gives the plain look and a generated shape's limits: 42211 houses, 42212 apartments, 42221 and 42222 commercial, 42231 and 42232 holiday cottages (one storey, timber in the houses' colours), 42241 and 42242 industrial, 42260, 42261 and 42262 outbuildings, and 42270, 42251 and 42252 churches and chapels (pale plaster; the medieval stone churches are models of their own). `pipeline/facades.py` holds the classes, read against every footprint on the island and OSM's tags on them; a seeded choice picks a colour from the palette in `globals.css`. Then the same file gives a building one of `art/facades`' looks where the data says it has one, in order of how much each source is worth: OSM's own word for its material, then its class, size, roof and street, and a seeded roll for the rest, so a rebuild gives the same town. Wood isn't assumed for being central: a villa needs a street on the short list where Mariehamn's wooden houses survive, a house's size, a pitched roof, and a share of those. A big rural outbuilding is a barn, a big low commercial box a hall, and apartments are rendered blocks or brick. Cottages, churches and sheds keep the plain look. A real shop in OSM within a few metres gives the building a front, its trade picking the word on the sign. Its tint bytes carry it all: look, paint, front, word, and whether it has the door. Roofs are the median of the photo inside the outline, with the sky's blue kept out, and get a strip of overhang past every outer edge. Each roof face's vertices carry its own metres, across the slope and up it from the building's middle, for the shader's tiles and seams. The street-facing facade gets the door, by the nearest road.
- **Boathouses and sheds** (`pipeline/outbuildings.py`): a footprint the data says is one gets a rural kit model in the tile's `models.json` (ODbL, since OSM's tags help choose) rather than a shape of its own, whether or not the 3D sheets have it, as long as a solid is that one building alone. An outbuilding (42260, 42261) whose outline comes within 4 m of the water is a boathouse: nine in ten of OSM's tagged boathouses do, and next to no other kind. One of 4–30 m² away from the water, and no more than 2.2 times as long as wide, is a shed. A shed stands at its outline's median ground, so one where the ground falls further than its walls reach below that keeps its generated shape. OSM's own tags count too. The list gives each its smallest rectangle: a boathouse's doors face the side nearest the water and a shed's the end nearer a road. A boathouse stands at the water line, the lowest ground of its outline's part on land, so ground under the water, wherever it lies, never moves it. The game fits the model's walls to it and scales its height with its width, within limits. The outline still goes to the car as a wall. About half the generated shapes of a country tile become sheds, and the 3D sheets' shore rows become boathouses: 302 models on L2341H, against 258 generated shapes before.
- **Rock** (`pipeline/rocks.py`): `rock.bin.gz`, 1500×1500 bytes at 4 m, each pixel's share of the Topographic Database's rock areas (class 34100: bedrock at or near the surface, bare or under thin pine), drawn at 2 m and kept off water, carriageways and buildings with 2 m round them, then 750×750 bytes at 8 m of each pixel's share of boulder field (34700), off water and carriageways. The game puts the fields in a texture layer of their own, at 8 m, its green the reeds' clearing from the mask (see Surface mask), 24 MB with its mips for its 16 far tiles. The game puts it in the mask's alpha as the far data lands, rather than the PNG's alpha, which a canvas would premultiply into the water and roads. 10–120 kB a tile, 4.1 MB for the island. Mapped boulders (34600, about a thousand on the sheets, 1.8–3.6 m across) and a scatter over boulder fields (34700, one place in eight of a 5 m lattice, 0.7–1.5 m) go into `models.json` as the kit's four boulder seeds, at their height on the mesh's own triangles, off water, roads and buildings: 12,700 across the island, 33 on L2343C and 993 on the rocky L2433E. `rocks.py` alone adds both to tiles already built, and adds the file to `world.json` once every tile has it. The evidence, from `pipeline/rock_audit.py`: OSM's 2,286 `natural=bare_rock` areas lie 87–94% inside the Topographic Database's rock, which maps 2 to 13 times as much, so OSM adds nothing to it. 58–87% of the mapped rock stands under trees over 2 m, the rock pine forest; on open ground the photo reads stone on 17% (L2343C) and 61% (L2433E) of it, against 2% and 21% of other open land, which is why the photo's colour alone can't place rock and the map can.
- **Docks and boats** (`pipeline/docks.py`): every OSM pier (`man_made=pier`, a line or an outline) becomes a dock in the tile's `docks.json` (ODbL), in the tile holding its middle, as runs: a start, a direction, a length and a width, at the water's level. An outline is cut into the largest straight rectangles it holds, each found along whichever of the outline's own edge directions holds the largest, so a finger at an angle to its walkway is a run of its own, not a staircase of squares on the walkway's grid. A line's runs are carried past each bend to close its outer corner. Another pier's run is cut back where its deck's edges would lie on one already laid, so a finger stops at its walkway's edge, and decks that still lie over each other, a bend's two runs or a finger's last sliver, stand 2 cm apart in height: two at one height would flicker. No boat lies in a landmark's plan (Pommern at her quay); a deck may run under one, which hides it. A floating pier is a pontoon, its deck a hand's width above the water; one by a marina (`leisure=marina`, within 50 m), wide, 40 m or longer, or with a footway on it is a harbour pier; the rest are private jetties. A width tag is used where it's believable, and otherwise the kit's own. Where a mapped quay is solid fill, the laser took its deck for ground, and that part of the outline is left to the ground, whose photo shows the real boards: a deck there would flicker through it. A run is trimmed back to where its deck stands clear of the land across its whole width, a pontoon's to where it floats, and a pier with no water within 60 m isn't a dock. A jetty that stops short of a boathouse is run on along its line to the boathouse's wall. Height is the water's as drawn: the flat water's level round the dock in the elevation model, less the drop the drawn surface sits under it. Boats are by rule: a jetty of 5 m or more has a head with a bench and ladder, and nearly half of those a rowing boat alongside or, now and then, a moored sloop off the end; a marina's piers and every pontoon have berths down both sides, seven in ten taken, from a guest harbour's mix, each boat bow to the pier, where its whole hull is on open water clear of every dock and boat and its keel clears the seabed as drawn, its draft and 30 cm more: that seabed shelves from the shore, so a pier's inner berths take launches and RIBs and only its outer ones the 45 ft yachts, and each berth's boat comes from the mix of those that fit there. Off the docks, boats lie on mooring buoys: in sheltered water, which has more land than water within 500 m (off a straight shore it has under half), 30 to 260 m from the shore, in a field off a harbour's berths (a marina's piers, every pontoon) of 3 boats a hectare at the berths, falling to none 300 m out, and off every other pier and jetty 2.5 a hectare, to none at 150 m: a field off a marina, and now and then a boat off a cottage's jetty. A buoy is placed only where the boat's whole swing round it, its pennant and its length, is water deep enough for its keel all the way round, clear of every dock, boat and landmark, the way in to every dock (30 m off a harbour's berths, 12 m off any other pier) and every other buoy's swing, and inside its own tile. A harbour's mix is mostly yachts with some double-enders, launches and allmogebåtar, a bay's mostly launches, allmogebåtar and Folkboats. Every boat on a buoy lies head to the summer's south-westerly, each up to 14° off it. The water round every dock and boat, and a harbour pier's slips out to its longest boat, is kept clear of reeds in the mask. Each run's outline goes to the car as walls. Across the 94 tiles: 1,290 jetty runs and 895 heads, 1,050 harbour pier runs, 40 pontoon runs, 1,279 boats, and 14 piers left to the roads as quays. A dock stays 4 m clear of a motor road, so none of its walls stands across one.
- **The clearing round buildings** (`pipeline/clearing.py`): `clearing.png`, each 4 m canopy pixel's signed distance to the nearest building, negative inside, in eighth metres to 24 m, the one thing every tree and planting reads to stay out of buildings. What counts as a building is what the game draws or stops the car at, read off the finished tiles: every roof triangle of `solids.bin.gz` from above (modelled, generated and raised buildings alike) and its walls (every outline, a raised building's posts, a landmark's plan), and the rural kit's sheds, boathouses and windmills, and every sea mark standing ashore (`sea/marks.json`), as a disc its foot's size, so no tree stands through a leading mark or a cairn; a neighbour's buildings count too, so one over a tile's edge clears both sides. It's measured on a 1 m raster, and a distance across a straight wall is a straight line, which a bilinear read gives back exactly, so 4 m pixels serve even a bush at a wall's foot; where they can't (round a small shed's corners) the readers' slack covers it, and the outer pixels hold the nearest building out to the tile's edge, since a read past their middles is clamped to them. In the game it rides in the canopy's layer as its green, so it costs no texture of its own. It reads only finished tiles, so `build.py` runs it last, after the farm, and `clearing.py` alone adds it to tiles already built, the island in about seven minutes on one core.
- **People** (`pipeline/people.py`): a tile's people are in files of their own, `people-<source>.json` (ODbL where OSM placed them), one for each module that places them, each listed under `people` in `tile.json`, so a module adding people never touches another's: a figure is its place in the scene, yaw, pose, seed and what's fixed about who they are, checked against the poses and looks the game knows (a test holds the two lists equal). The seed comes from where and why someone is placed, so a rebuild places the same people. The first module is the waterfront (`pipeline/waterfront.py`), sparse as a quiet summer's day, read off what a built tile already has: the docks and boats in `docks.json`, the landmarks, and the ground, shore, roads and buildings. About a quarter of jetty heads have someone fishing off the end (now and then with a child), one or two on the edge or the bench, or someone sunbathing; one rowing boat in seven has someone kneeling on the jetty beside it, and one berthed boat in twenty someone at its bow, kneeling from a pontoon or standing on a pier, often in a sailing jacket; along a marina's piers and pontoons, every 14 m, a few places in a hundred have a pair chatting, someone standing, a parent and child, or, where no boat lies, someone sitting on the edge or fishing; and each landmark has two or three groups 4–11 m out from its plan, on land, off the roads and clear of buildings. That is 588 people across the 94 tiles. `build.py` runs it after the roadside, `waterfront.py` alone adds it to tiles already built, and `--count` counts without writing. The second is the seated (`pipeline/seated.py`), on the street furniture's seats: every bench and café table rolls from where it stands whether anyone is there, and whether one or two. A café table is taken at about one in three in town (the roadside's test) and one in four elsewhere, since its terrace is out because the place is open, a bench at one in eight and one in twenty, a taken table is a pair at nearly half and a bench at a quarter, and a bench's sitters lean older. Each sits on the seat as written, its seed from the seat. `build.py` runs it after the roadside, over the same tiles, and `seated.py` alone adds it to tiles already built; a roadside rebuilt alone needs it run again, since the seats move.
- **Sea lanes** (`pipeline/sea_lanes.py`): the lanes of every boat under way, for the whole island in one file, `sea/lanes.json` (ODbL, CC BY 4.0, and Traficom's hydrographic data licence, which allows no navigational or commercial use; the file states each), written into the code rather than the tiles because it changes with no tile. It reads the built tiles, the kept OSM answers and the official sea chart (`sources.sea_chart_path`: Väylävirasto's fairway register and Traficom's depth areas, see `docs/ART.md`'s Data), and takes `--data` to read another checkout's tiles and OSM (a worktree has none). The water is each tile's mask, with the hairlines of land a sample or two wide that it draws across open water here and there closed, less roads over water, so no lane passes under a bridge, and less every dock and moored boat with 6 m round it; past the tiles, the outer islands' bakes; and only water joined to the open sea, so no lane reaches a lake. A lane is the cheapest path over that water at 8 m a cell (scikit-image's `MCP_Geometric`), where a cell nearer land than the vessel keeps clear of (60 m for a ship, 14 m for a small boat) can't be crossed except near a berth, a cell costs more the nearer land it is, so a lane keeps to the middle of a sound, and less on its guides: most on the official fairways' navigation lines, and less on OSM's lines (a ferry's `route=ferry` ways, every ship's for a ship, and a small boat's `waterway=fairway` lines), so a lane keeps to the chart where it has a line and falls back to OSM only where it has none. Cutting the path's corners keeps every straight stretch within 12 m of it, so a lane that found a fairway stays on it rather than cutting across open water. A keeled small boat may not cross water the chart's depth areas make shallower than its draft and half a metre; water the chart doesn't cover counts as deep, and the ferries, on fairways dredged for them that the chart's depth bands are too coarse to route by, aren't routed by depth. Its corners are cut to the fewest the water allows and rounded to the vessel's turn, tighter where the water says, and the result is checked every 2 m against the 4 m mask, across the hull's breadth, so a lane that brings a hull over land fails the build. A ferry's berth is where its whole hull lies on the water nearest the end of its OSM way, Mariehamn's the town kit's `FERRY_BERTH` (a test holds them equal); a single-ended vessel's lane also says how far out it comes round, where its whole length swings clear. Its berth is moved along the lane until the hull, lying as the game lays it, is all on the water, and the build fails unless it is at every place between the berth and where it comes round, backing in or moving across. Långnäs's two lines share their way in, from the berth to where a ship comes round, since a ship calls on one and leaves on the other. A line to Sweden or Finland ends where it leaves the grid, out of sight. A small boat's trips run from each harbour (`art/marina/places.json`, one per 500 m) to its two nearest other harbours or OSM anchorages 1.5 to 9 km off, every other one a keeled boat's where deep enough water joins its ends, and each trip's ends 30 m or more from any other boat's. Each lane carries how far its vessel keeps to starboard, halved until the hull's edge stays on the water either way along it. 20 ferry lines and 37 trips.
- **Sea marks** (`pipeline/sea_marks.py`): every buoy, beacon, leading mark, cairn and light round Åland in one file, `sea/marks.json`, from Väylävirasto's aids to navigation: each mark's kind (spar, pillar buoy, fixed mark, leading mark, cairn, sector light, lighthouse, radar mark), its IALA side or cardinal, its daymark's colours, whether it floats, and its height. A leading mark is the front or rear by its name (`alempi`, `ylempi`) and faces the nearest official navigation line. OSM's `seamark:*` places fill in only where the chart has no mark within 60 m (500 m for a lighthouse, which OSM puts on its building). 1,925 official marks and 60 from OSM. `render/sea-marks.ts` draws them (see "Sea marks").
- **Stone walls** (`pipeline/walls.py`): lines in the tile's `walls.json` (ODbL, since OSM's walls are in it), which the game lays its sections along. Mapped walls are few, and none of them say where a wall is in the fields: OSM has about 5 km of `barrier=wall` on the island, most of it Mariehamn's garden walls, churchyards and Bomarsund's ruins, and the Topographic Database has no class for a stone wall at all (its 44211 is any built fence, and 44213 a hedge). Nor does the elevation model show one: along OSM's mapped walls, stone or not, the ground on the line stands no higher than 4–6 m either side. So a wall is OSM's where it maps one in the country (one with a dozen buildings within 80 m is a town's, mortared or brick, and not this kit's), and otherwise one by rule along the edges of the Topographic Database's fields and meadows (32611, 32800), where the stones cleared from a field were laid. A field's outline is cut into sides at its corners and every 250 m, an edge two fields share is one side, and the database's own sheet cuts are no side at all. Each side is one kind by what most of its length has 9 m beyond it: a lane (within 12 m of a road's centre line), another field, forest (the canopy image's 3 m), or open land. Each kind walls a share of its sides by a roll from where the side's middle is, so a rebuild gives the same walls: a fifth of the sides along a lane or track (both sides of a stretch of lane share their roll, so a walled lane is walled either side) and a twentieth along a main road, 15% between fields, 12% against forest and 4% onto open land. A wall stops short of the laid road surface, shoulders and aprons, by 1.5 m, so there is a gap at every road, track or drive it meets, keeps 2.5 m off buildings and 12 m off water, so a field running down to a shore takes no wall, and a part under 12 m long is dropped. Churchyards, cemeteries, places of worship and castles in OSM, and 25 m round them, are left to the churches and landmarks, whose walls are their own. Across the 94 tiles that is 6,526 walls and 364 km, along a tenth of the 3,768 km of field and meadow edges: 223 km against forest, 91 km beside lanes and tracks, 25 km between fields, 24 km onto open land and 0.4 km of OSM's, about 0.5 MB of files in all. OSM's own can't measure the rule: of its walls in the country, 430 m after the gaps, only 64 m lie within 12 m of a field's edge. The shares are a judgement, kept low so the walls stay a feature of the farmland rather than a frame round every field. `walls.py` alone adds the file to tiles already built, and `--audit` prints each kind's edges and how the rule meets OSM's walls.
- **The countryside** (`pipeline/farm.py`): Åland's post mills where they stand, and by rule the bales, tractors, sheep and cows of a summer's farmland. The mills are OSM's `man_made=windmill`: 93 outlines and 3 points on the island's tiles, each checked by hand against the laser and the 2025 photo. Nearly every outline has a mill in both, the post mill's long shadow across the grass and 8–14 m in the laser (a few lower, built or rebuilt since the 2013 scan); the three points have nothing at them, and Holkkvarn's outline is a square house under a pyramid roof, so those four are left out, which leaves 92. An outline is the mill's body seen from above, 3–7 m, so the sails turn on its long side, facing whichever end lies to the south-west, into the prevailing wind, and the game scales the kit's mill to it within limits. A mill replaces any solid or generated shape under its outline and a metre round it, as a landmark does (`Landmarks.replace_also`), which on L2343C and L2343F was a generated shape at half of them and nothing at the rest; a tile lists its own mills in `tile.json`, standing on the lowest ground under the trestle's pads, and `world.json` carries them. The rest stands on the Topographic Database's fields (32611) and meadows (32800), as the stone walls read them, each rolled from its field's middle, so a rebuild gives the same countryside and two tiles agree on a field across their edge. Bales lie on a seventh of the grass fields of a hectare or more, a field being grass where three in five of its pixels are green in the spring photo, which a ploughed field isn't: half are wrapped silage in a row of 6–16 by the edge nearest a road, sometimes two deep, and half lie where the baler dropped them, hay or wrapped, in loose lines across the field. A third of those fields have a tractor at their edge, and one barn in fourteen (an outbuilding of 120 m² or more within 60 m of a field) has one in its yard. Sheep graze a fifth of the open meadows of a quarter hectare or more (three in five of the meadow under no trees: the database maps wooded meadows long gone over to forest too), and nearly half of those within 60 m of the shore, in a flock round one spot, about eight a hectare, five to two dozen, a fifth of them lambs. They are Åland sheep, the archipelago's own small landrace: the game rolls each one's coat from light grey to black, and keeps a ewe's small horns on one in five, from its place, in the kit's material, so the flock costs no more models. Dairy cows graze nearer the farms and fewer: a herd of four to a dozen, about three a hectare, on a third of the open inland meadows and one grass ley in seventeen within 200 m of a barn, a shore meadow being the sheep's; grazing, standing or lying, now and then a calf, red-and-white Ayrshire or black-and-white Holstein by a roll per cow. Everything keeps clear of roads (1.5 m past the laid surface and its own room), buildings, water, trees, stone walls, boulders, the roadside's walls and each other, so it runs after the roadside, in `build.py`'s last step. The props go into `models.json` beside the boathouses and boulders, their place's width the kit's scale. On L2343C that is 4 mills, 57 bales, 61 sheep, 22 cows and 5 tractors; on L2343F 8 mills, 97 bales, 111 sheep, 4 cows and 11 tractors. `farm.py` alone adds the props and mills to tiles already built, but a mill only replaces what stands under it when its tile is rebuilt.
- **The roadside** (`pipeline/roadside.py`): what stands beside the roads, read off the road surface as it was laid, so each thing stands by the drawn road and not by OSM's line, on the ground or the shoulder as drawn, in `roadside.json` (ODbL). OSM places what it has: street lamps, wooden poles and the minor power lines between them (a pole at every node, and more where a gap is over 70 m; lattice towers have no model), give-way and stop signs, bus stops with a shelter where it says so, marked crossings, and guard rails. That is little of what stands on Åland, so rules place the rest. Lamps every 32 m, on one side, along roads OSM tags as lit, and along a town's streets where it says nothing: OSM leaves most of Mariehamn's untagged. In a built-up place a street below the main roads (unclassified, residential and lesser) takes the town kit's post-top lamp at the kerb, mapped or by rule, and the main roads keep the galvanised one with its arm. Traffic lights at OSM's `highway=traffic_signals` (and crossings tagged so), nodes within 40 m one junction: a head on the right of each arm's incoming traffic, facing it, a crosswalk back from the crossing road, a road's two ends in one phase and the crossing road half a cycle off; a fire station's emergency signals get none, and no give-way sign stands within 40 m of a signalled junction. A street-name post at the left corner of each junction of two differently named streets in a built-up place, one per pair of names within 40 m, its blades each along its own street. A village sign where a primary, secondary, tertiary or unclassified road enters 200 m of an OSM `place=village` node (150 m for a hamlet), on the right of the traffic coming in. Lamps and traffic lights stop the car; name posts and village signs, wooden or breakaway, don't. On Mariehamn's L2334E that is 932 town lamps, 8 heads at two junctions on Ålandsvägen and 209 name posts. Edge posts every 50 m, both sides, along unlit main roads with a limit of 60 or over, clear of junctions. Guard rails 0.7 m off the edge for 40 m either side of a bridge and on embankments, where the ground 9 m out lies 2.5 m below the road for 40 m or more, broken where another road comes in; a run ends in its ramped ends. A give-way sign where a lesser road (secondary down to residential) meets a main one of higher class, or OSM's stop or give way where it maps one within 50 m. A speed sign each way where OSM's limit changes along a road, showing the limit the traffic enters; a limit read for under 50 m is a crossing road's, picked up where it crosses, and puts up none. One to three mailboxes beside a country road, 5–7 m from the mouth of a drive that leads within 60 m of a house, outside a town and off lit roads. In town is one test that the roadside, the furniture, the parked cars and the seated share (`pipeline/built_up.py`; the town's ground is `pipeline/town.py`'s, a different thing): the floor the Topographic Database's main buildings cover within 250 m, at least 6,000 m², leaving out outbuildings and holiday cottages. Counting buildings read a farm and its barns as a village and Godby's centre, a few big buildings, as country; by floor, every Mariehamn district and the parish villages' centres (Godby, Jomala, Hammarland, Geta, Eckerö, Saltvik) are in town at their own size and the farm villages round them are not. `built_up.py` alone prints the floor and the verdict at every named place OSM maps on the tiles given. Traffic keeps right, so a sign stands on the right of the traffic it is for, about 1 m past the carriageway's edge, and faces it. Nothing stands on a carriageway, an apron or within a metre of a building. The list also holds the walls the car can't pass. It needs every tile built first, because a pole by an edge stands on its neighbour's ground, so `build.py` runs it after the builds, over the tiles built and their neighbours, a core each as the builds were: a tile reads its neighbours' ground and writes only its own files, and `roadside.py` alone adds it to tiles already built, half a minute to a minute and a half a tile (Mariehamn's the longest), side by side with `--jobs`. Its OSM objects are kept apart from the roads, in `.data/raw/osm-roadside/`, under a version of their own.
- **Street furniture** (`pipeline/furniture.py`): benches, bins, bike racks and café terraces, placed in the roadside's own lists once its objects stand, so they keep clear of them. OSM places what it maps, kept apart from the roads in `.data/raw/osm-furniture/` under a version of its own: benches, waste baskets and bicycle parking (racks by its capacity, eight bikes to a rack, along the nearest path, their open ends towards it), and a terrace wherever a café, bar, pub, ice-cream shop or restaurant has outdoor seating. Untagged, a café, bar, pub or ice-cream shop has one and a restaurant has one by a roll of one in two; a mapped `leisure=outdoor_seating` outline is filled with tables in rows. A terrace is two to four tables in a row against the wall of the venue's building that faces its street, each with a chair either side along the wall, and with parasols at about three in five, spaced for them. It may stand on a sidewalk, as real ones do, but stays clear of carriageways and aprons. OSM maps few of the rest (about 140 benches, 45 bins and 5 bike parkings across the island), so rules place them: a bench every 30 m or so along a park's paths (OSM's `leisure=park` outlines) and a pedestrian street, facing the path, with a bin by every other; a bench every 40 m along a town's paths with water within 20 m, facing the water, with a bin by every third; a bin by every bus stop in a town; and bike racks against the street wall of a supermarket, mall, school, college, library, town hall, cinema or ferry terminal. Anything mapped that lands on paving is moved off it, up to 3 m, away from the nearest road or path; nothing stands on a carriageway, a path, an apron, in or by a building, or on anything placed already. Everything stands on what's drawn there: the road surface where it's on one, else the ground. Each bench and café chair also writes a seat in the roadside list's `seats`, by `art/people`'s pose for it (`sit-bench` or `sit-cafe`): the pose's origin on the ground and the way the sitter faces, so a person put there sits on the seat (see `TileRoadside` in `render/tile-models.ts`). L2334E, Mariehamn's centre, has 300 benches (61 OSM's), 60 bins, 32 racks, 71 café tables and 40 parasols, and 742 seats; Godby's L2343H has 25 benches and 11 tables. The five models are 10 draw calls, and on the Esplanade at 3440×1440 about 105,000 of the roadside's triangles as three counts them.
- **Town dressing** (`pipeline/dressing.py`): lindens, flag and midsummer poles, fences, planters, picnic tables and viewpoints, in the roadside's lists beside the furniture: what OSM maps stands before the furniture, which keeps clear of it, and what rules place after it. OSM's objects are kept apart from the roads in `.data/raw/osm-dressing/`, under a version of their own. A linden is `art/street`'s at every broadleaf tree OSM maps in town (a lime anywhere) and about every 8 m along every broadleaf tree row, each up to 1.2 m off even and one in 25 missing, each rolling one of three crowns, so an avenue isn't one tree on a ruler, the avenues of Mariehamn's esplanades and the manors, as tall as OSM says or a grown street tree's 10–16 m; its height rides in the list's `scales`, one per place of a model drawn at a size of its own, which a tile baked before lacks. A trunk may stand on a sidewalk or the strip beside it, never within 0.6 m of a carriageway, and stops the car. A linden near a building is no taller than lets its crown (`LINDEN_REACH`, held to `art/street`'s crowns by a test) clear every building the game draws, its neighbours' included, by exact distance; one that would be under 6 m isn't planted. A flag pole stands where OSM maps one and before a town hall, a ferry terminal or a hotel, flying Åland's flag (Finland's where OSM names it), every flag in one south-westerly; a midsummer pole where OSM maps one (`man_made=maypole`, or a flag pole noted as one), its bar to the road. OSM's wooden or untagged fences are a white picket fence in town and a roundpole fence in the country, in sections stretched and sheared to their run, broken where anything paved or a building is within 0.4 m; a car goes through them. Planters are wooden boxes down a pedestrian street, alternating sides every 24 m, and round concrete bowls across its ends where it meets a street. Picnic tables are `art/identity`'s, where OSM maps them and by rule at picnic sites, rest areas, bathing places and in a town's parks; viewing towers stand at OSM's observation towers and viewpoint platforms at its viewpoints, one to every 80 m, each looking where the land falls away most. Nothing stands within 8 m of a landmark's plan. Across the 19 rebuilt tiles round Mariehamn, Jomala and Godby: 1,332 lindens, 28 flag poles, 5 midsummer poles, 3,037 sections of town fence and 4,944 of roundpole, 13 planters, 137 picnic tables, 4 towers and 14 platforms.
- **Parked cars** (`pipeline/parking.py`): where each car stands, in `parked.json` (ODbL), run after the roadside, whose lamps and signs they keep clear of; `parking.py` alone adds them to tiles built. A car park on the ground (`amenity=parking`) has bays of 2.5 by 5 m: a row either side of each of its mapped aisles (`service=parking_aisle`, which aren't laid as roads, since the park's paving covers them), longest aisle first, else rows along its longest side, a pair back to back between 6 m aisles, and a strip one row deep faces its nearest road. A share of bays is taken, by who the park is for and a roll per park: a half, more for kerbside bays OSM maps as areas. An empty bay keeps its room, so no row across another's ends takes it. A kerb takes a car every 6 m, its side 0.2 m past the carriageway, on the kerb as Mariehamn's do, facing the way its side's traffic goes: where OSM's `parking:<side>`, `parking:both` or the older `parking:lane:*` allows it (on a tertiary road or lesser, 60% taken), and on a town's untagged residential and unclassified streets (built up as the lamps' rule has it), 15%. OSM names a side as its way runs, which may be against the chain. No car stands on a carriageway or a path, within 5 m of another motor road (a junction's or a drive's mouth), 6 m of a crossing, 15 m of a bus stop, 10 m of a bridge, against a building or the roadside, or on another car's room. Mariehamn's two tiles hold about 1,860 cars in car parks and 2,370 at kerbs.
- **Churches and landmarks** (`pipeline/landmarks.py`): each site's place and what it replaces come from the art, as `art/landmarks/sites.json`, which `tools/landmark-sites.ts` writes from the models and a test holds equal: NLS solids by name (the churches, S:t Görans, the town hall), every solid inside its plan (Kastelholm and Bomarsund's fort and tower), or nothing (the water tower and the two ships). A ground site (`art/landmarks/places.ts`) stands on the ground at its origin and is built on the ground round it, and it may name its own walls as a near-only site does, or none: a headstone or a fence stops nothing, as a sign post doesn't, while a graveyard's granite wall, a grave mound, a gun pit's ring wall, a tower and a plinth do. A site's plan is its far version seen from above, a wall's as a line a metre thick. A generated shape under any site goes too, or it would stand through the model. What goes keeps its outline as a wall the car hits, and the site's plan is one too, so a landmark the data had no building for still stops the car. A site's solids go from whichever tile holds them, so a site by an edge is whole, and the tile it stands in lists it in `tile.json`: the model, and its origin in the scene, at the height its art fixes or the ground's. `world.json` carries each tile's list, so the far versions stand from the start. Two kinds of site stand over roads and are near only, drawn with their tile's detail rather than from the start, and listed under `nearSites` instead: the Västerhamn terminal (`art/town-kit`), in place of its false solid, and the six fuel stations with a canopy (`art/facades`), each in place of what lies under its canopy. A near-only site names its own walls, the terminal's legs and a station's pump islands and price pylon, so the roads under a walkway or a canopy stay open, and the terminal's raised ways (bridges, indoor corridors and ways on a layer, mostly inside its plan) are taken off the road surface, since the model draws them.
- **Where a road meets a building** (`pipeline/give_way.py`), one gives way, before the roads are laid: every building is a wall, and OSM's lines are sketchier than NLS's laser-measured buildings, which match the photo. A road whose line enters a building stops at its wall, unless OSM tags it a passage (`tunnel=building_passage`, `covered=yes`) or it is a public road, unclassified or above, that goes in and out again; then the wall opens as wide as the road, and nowhere else. A road that only passes beside a building is pushed clear of it, its half width and half a metre more, the push fading over 8 m along the road; points where roads meet or leave the tile never move, so a few town roads between two buildings still touch one. A tile reads its neighbours' buildings too, so both sides of an edge cut and push a road alike. A raised building is no wall to a road.
- **Raised buildings** (`pipeline/raised.py`): NLS has only an outline for a canopy or a raised walkway, the same as for a barn, so OSM says which stand above the ground: `building=roof` and `carport` are a roof slab on posts, and a `bridge`, a building with a minimum height or level, or one OSM puts on a `layer` over a motor road at ground level (not a corridor, a footway, a tunnel or a basement ramp) is a box on pillars with a concrete floor under it. A layer alone never raises a building a laser model has, since the laser saw its walls reach the ground. Its floor and roof are heights above the sea, read from the elevation model for the whole OSM outline: clear of the highest ground a road under it crosses, else over the ground beneath, so a building two tiles share stands as one and one reaching out over the water isn't drawn down to the seabed. Posts stand evenly round the inside of its edge, leaving out any on a road, and are all the car hits there.
- **Roads:** OSM ways clipped exactly at the tile's edge. OSM's building footprints are still written but unused: NLS's took their place. The raw roads, the footprints and the road surface stay under ODbL, which each states. OSM comes from its own API, not Overpass, whose public servers are too often down: a tile's box at a time, in quarters if it's over the API's limit of 50,000 nodes, each answer kept in `.data/raw/osm-api/` so a fetch that stops resumes where it was. It's meant for this size of request. The whole island's 94 tiles came through it in a couple of minutes. What the build reads, the roads, buildings, car parks and bus stations, is kept beside them in `.data/raw/osm/`, and remade from the kept answers whenever `OSM_KEPT_VERSION` in `pipeline/sources.py` changes, with no fetch.
- **The town's ground** (`pipeline/town.py`, the bake itself `pipeline/town_ground.py`): read off a built tile, its road surface, buildings, photo and water, and OSM's land use, parks, squares, pedestrian areas, pitches, playgrounds, car parks, hedges and fountains, which `sources.py` keeps apart from the roads in `.data/raw/osm-town/`. Town is where OSM's land use says so, and round every building out of forest or field; there the photo says which of a yard is grass and which gravel, OSM's areas are laid over that, and a strip along every town street, and the ground from kerb to facade where the two are close, is paving. Under a road or a building the surface is whatever lies nearest, so what's seen runs right up to a wall or a shoulder. It writes `town.rgba.gz`, the image the terrain draws (about 1.2 MB in Mariehamn), and `town-ribbons.bin.gz`, a class byte per section of the road surface (see Paths). It runs in `build.py` after the rock only with `--town`, off by default while the work is tabled, so tiles built without it draw as they always have, and `town.py` alone adds it to tiles already built, about a minute and a quarter a tile, and `--pixel-m 4` writes the image at 4 m, to compare. Hedges and fountains are classified but not yet placed: `art/town-ground`'s models wait for them.
- **The road surface** (`pipeline/roads.py`): see below.
- **The lane map** (`pipeline/lanes.py`): the lines traffic, cyclists and walkers move along, in `lanes.json.gz` (ODbL), read off the road surface as it was laid, so `ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`'s lanes need no tile rebuild. A lane is one direction of one chain between junctions, at the middle of its half (on a one-way road, spread across it, by OSM's `oneway` and `lanes`), so it lies on the drawn crown and banking. A lesser road's end lands on the greatest road within its reach, and the junction covers the host from the lesser road's width and its corner, at most 30 m along it, so side roads close together stay junctions of their own. Connectors join every lane into a junction to every lane out of another of its arms, along the road's own lane straight on, and otherwise built to stay on the paving: the smooth arc from one lane to the next where that stays on it, else in along its lane's line and out along the next one's with the corner where they meet rounded as widely as the paving allows, else the arc swung out less, and never tighter than a car's turning circle (5 m), which comes first: where the paving is narrower than that, a car runs over its edge as a real one would. A lane leads into itself only going round, as a ring's does, never backwards on a stub whose two ends lie in one junction. A turn no shape keeps on the paving has no road surface between its two lanes at all: that is the road surface's fault, and the audit lists it; a hairpin at a plain junction is left out, and a dead end turns traffic round. Each connector lists the connectors it crosses or merges with that it gives way to: a road with the roadside's give-way or stop sign gives way (`Roadside.junction_sign`, the same test that places the sign), a ring has priority over its entries, a drive or track gives way to the street it comes out on, a signalled junction's arms run in the heads' two phases, and otherwise traffic gives way to the right and a left turn to oncoming traffic. A path meeting a road doesn't cut it: its end is a link on to the road's lane there. Speed is OSM's limit as the speed signs read it, and where it has none 70 km/h outside a built-up place and 50 in one, 20 on a living street, 30 on drives and tracks, 20 for cycleways and walking pace on foot. Each road lane carries its road's vehicles a day both ways (`pipeline/traffic_volume.py`), from Ålands landskapsregering's traffic counts on kartor.ax (CC BY 4.0, about 170 sites each counted for a week about twice a year, downloaded into `.data/raw/kartor-traffic/`): a site belongs to the laid motor road nearest it, a road with a site takes its count, one with none the nearest count on a road of the same name within 4 km, and the rest their class's typical count, in town or out of it, the counts' own medians where they reach and judged where they don't. A lane map without them doesn't load: run `lanes.py` on its tile again. Points are whole centimetres, each a step from the one before, so a seam's two halves match exactly; across the L2334E/L2334F edge 104 of 106 lane ends meet their partner. The audit lists, by place, every connector that leaves the carriageway and aprons by over half a metre, one tighter than a car can turn (a lane's ends that leave no room for its turning circle), a lane under another road's surface, a step of 15 cm in a metre, a grade that changes by 8% over 8 m, and a lane that can't be reached or leads nowhere, with the tile's seams counting as the rest of the island; it is where the road surface needs work. Central Mariehamn has 2,739 road lanes and 5,658 connectors, about 1 MB; a country tile 0.4 MB. `build.py` runs it beside the roadside, and `lanes.py` alone adds it to tiles already built, half a minute (country) to a minute and a half (Mariehamn) a tile. The game reads it with the tile's detail (`traffic/lane-map.ts`) and joins the loaded tiles' at their seams by place (`traffic/lane-net.ts`); `traffic/around.ts` is the index of every vehicle on its lane or connector, matched to the nearest lane running its way, and answers what's ahead through the junctions and merges coming, what's behind, and what's coming on the connectors a turn gives way to.

## The road surface

Roads are baked, so the render and the physics read one surface. A ribbon triangle steeper than 45° that rises over 1.2 m is dropped when the mesh is made: a track crossing high above a road would otherwise dive under it in one section, a cliff across the carriageway. OSM's lines are the only source so far: every road the tile needs is in them.

- **Chains.** Ways are cut at every shared point and rejoined into continuous drives, the greatest road first, each carrying on through a junction the straightest way until it meets a road already laid. A lesser road ends on the greater one, and a crossroads is two T-junctions. A roundabout's ring is laid first, whole and on its own, with a tight rounding of its own that keeps it its real size, so every road ends on it. Chain 0 is the tile's main road, or a ring if the tile has one.
- **Line.** Stations every 2 m, rounded along the line with a Gaussian scaled by class, both ends fixed.
- **Height.** A Whittaker smoother over the median of the ground across the road, with the shortest wavelength it keeps set by class (280 m on a primary road, 30 m on a path). A bridge's stations carry no weight, so it spans.
- **Cross-section.** Width by class, a crown, and banking on bends up to a class's limit. Each cross-section is five points: the carriageway's edges and centre, and a shoulder each side whose outer point lies on the ground. The shoulder fades to the ground's colour, which is what hides the join from a low camera. Where the land falls away from a road, on a bridge or over an underpass, the shoulder stops a metre down and stays a deck: reaching for the ground it would be a wall across whatever passes beneath.
- **Junctions.** The greater road carries on whole. A lesser road of service class or above stops at its first section wholly clear of the road it lands on, however far back a shallow merge puts that, and eases on to that road's surface as laid, level with its edge beyond the carriageway, over the 16 m before it. An apron (`lay_aprons`) paves what's between: a shape of its own rather than a section of a chain, with a corner each side that rounds by the sine of the angle squared, so a shallow merge keeps its sharp nose. Its edges take the heights of the ribbons they meet and the rest hangs between them. Aprons that touch are one, and an apron is bare road with no paint. Where it overlaps a ribbon to close the crack it sinks a centimetre, and a shoulder running on under it drops well clear, because two surfaces at one height flicker through each other. The land round an apron is shaped as it is round a ribbon, so an apron ends on no step, and a building on one is over a road. Aprons follow the sections in the road surface file, and reach the mesh the car collides with along with the ribbons. A chain's centre line stops at its cut, so road lines have gaps at junctions: nothing needs them joined. Respawning takes the nearest line, which is never more than about 15 m from any apron, no apron over water is out of reach of the rule that a road over water isn't water, and the scripted drives through junctions follow the map's network. Tracks and paths still flare and stop along the greater road's edge. A shoulder that would rise through another road is dropped under it. A lesser road or path that runs across a greater one with no junction passes just under it, and a track or path does the same under an apron, so the surface a car meets at any crossing is the greater road's.
- **Paved ground.** Every OSM line is its own ribbon with a shoulder fading to the land, which suits a country road and breaks in town, where lines run packed together. So two more kinds of apron pave what lies between. A car park on the ground or a bus station (`vectors.paved_areas`, from OSM's outlines), and a yard of service lanes side by side with no strip over 3 m between them for 15 m or more (`vectors.lane_yards`: a ferry's queue, a depot, a car park OSM gives no outline), is one surface (`lay_paved`), cut into 4 m cells on a lattice of the world so it follows the land, which it lies on, averaged over a few metres. Where it meets a road or apron it takes that one's height, easing back to the land over 12 m, as a road eases on to another. Near a tile's edge it lies just over the land instead, since the edge's heights can't be pressed down under it. A service way with 60% of its length on one, its whole OSM way measured so both tiles it crosses agree, grown by 2 m because a bay runs on out to meet the street, is an aisle or a bay of it, and so is a path with 60% inside the outline itself (a sidewalk along the edge stays). What lies on the area isn't laid: as ribbons they were a comb of strips, each fading to grass. What runs outside the outline, 2 m or more, stays a road, so every entrance still joins the area to the street. Then any strip of land under 3 m wide between laid roads, car parks and aprons is paved (`lay_gaps`): the gap between a street and its sidewalk, the halves of a split street, a car park's edge and the street. Such a strip is pinned to what lies either side and hangs between them, and is left as land where the two stand over 0.4 m apart in height, which is an embankment or one road passing over the other. An apron's shoulder that would lie on another road or apron is dropped, or it shows as a pale plate there. A gravel car park is still paved like its nearest road.
- **Paths.** A footway, cycleway, path, bridleway or flight of steps is a ribbon like a road's, so the car, the roadside, the parked cars and the street furniture read one surface, and walkers will follow its chains. It is laid as a small road, with a slight crown and the 30 m profile. Laying paths flat on the land instead, with no crown and a profile keeping the land's shape down to 10 m, so paths that meet or cross each lie at the land's height there, waits with the town's ground (see `docs/HANDOFF.md`), since it belongs with the work on junctions and roads close together. Where a tile has a town image, each section also has a class (`pipeline/town.py`), the nearest OSM path's: its `surface` where it says, a sidewalk's slabs, an untagged path's or bridleway's gravel, a pedestrian street's slabs, and otherwise a footway's or cycleway's pale walked tarmac, never a road's asphalt; steps are stone treads across the line, each with its riser's shadow. A motor road keeps its own look, save setts where OSM says. Beside soft ground, a lawn or the country, a path's shoulder wears into the grass as a road's does; beside the town's hard ground it's drawn as that ground. With no town image a path looks as it always has. Paths are ribbons and not painted into the town's image because a strip under about 3 m can't live in a 2 m distance field, and an image fine enough would double the near layers' memory again; as ribbons they also stay in the one file every reader of the road network already uses.
- **Ground.** Cut or filled to 12 cm under the road for the shoulder and one mesh cell beyond, then eased back to the real land over a blend set by class. Then any node of a cell a carriageway crosses is pressed under it, so no triangle comes up through the road.
- **Thinning.** Cross-sections are kept only where the road would otherwise move 2 cm, up to 14 m apart: 27,000 sections and 214,000 triangles from 127 km of road, 1.4 MB.

**Seams.** A tile's edge row of heights is never changed: the blend fades to nothing over 24 m and the build fails if the row moves. A road crossing the edge ends on a seam section read only from what both tiles see the same: the whole way's direction there, from a chord across the edge; its class's widths; no banking; and a height and slope from a plane fitted to the elevation model within 24 m of the crossing, lifted to clear the edge row. The last 40 m of road ease on to it. The section is rounded to the millimetre, so two tiles built apart write the same numbers, whichever way their chains run and whatever junction lies near. Dashes are counted from a seam, and centred on it. All 130-odd crossings of the nine tiles match exactly. The only ends without a partner are a track that dips a few centimetres over an edge for 6 m and back.

The shading beside a road at a seam still comes from each tile's own view of its neighbour, whose rim is the raw ground. Measured along every seam it steps a median of 3–4/255 near a road against 2 elsewhere, with the same top end, and it doesn't show.

**On Route 1,** measured over the baked ribbon: the worst vertical curve goes from a 781 m radius in the raw model to 4150 m, a 6 m-chord roughness from 6.3 mm to 0.7 mm, and the line stays within 1.03 m of OSM's and 0.25 m of the model's height.

## What the data does

**OSM's roads, on tile L2343C.** Centrelines were checked against the raised strip of the road in the 2 m heights, every 10 m. Primary, secondary and tertiary roads sit on the real road: a median offset of 1–2 m and a P90 of 3–5 m, with the strip found at 82–98% of stations. NLS's own lines measure the same (median 1 m), which is the method's floor; lines moved 40 m aside find a strip at only 9% of stations, with a median offset of 10 m. Tracks and service roads mostly have no strip to measure (found at 26–46%).

Line against line, OSM has all of NLS's classed roads and tracks, 90% of its drives, 66% of its tractor ways and 40% of its paths, with a median gap of 0.5–1 m where both have the line, and 97% of OSM's motor roads have an NLS line beside them. In the heights alone, with no map, 30.7 km reads as road. OSM has 21.8 km of it, which is 96% of what either map confirms as a way; 7.9 km is on neither map, and the longest of those, checked on the orthophoto, are nearly all ditch banks, pit rims and shores.

So OSM's lines are good enough to lay roads from, and the Topographic Database is there for the drives and tractor ways it lacks.

**The forest against MS-NFI, over all 94 tiles' MS-NFI land.** The mix planted from the stands image is 63/13/12/11 pine, spruce, birch and other by tree, against 60/15/13/12 by real volume. Tree height is what's off. MS-NFI's mean height is 1.20 times the canopy image's at the median (0.90–1.65 from the 10th to the 90th percentile, falling from 1.23 in low stands to 1.10 in the tallest), over 2.6 M well-wooded pixels, partly ten years' growth since the 2013 scan and partly that a lattice place reads the canopy's surface, not a tree's top. So `HEIGHT_RANGE` centres on 1.2. Crowns scale with height, so it decides cover too: a full lattice covers 40% of the ground against the real 41%, or 35% thinned by the stands image at 194 trees per hectare, which falls short because low stands can't reach their cover even full while tall ones are thinned to theirs. Whatever stands in for far trees (the terrain's lift, the overview) rises by the same mean. Real stems are about 510 per hectare, past the lattice's 278, which is why density targets cover.

**Edges.**

- **Sea has no nodata.** It is flat water, near 0.1 m in the open and a few centimetres apart between tiles (0.051 and 0.088 m meet at one seam), so the game must never take sea level from a tile's own numbers. Lakes are flat at their own level.
- **The land beside the sea** is close to water level, so water comes from the mask and not from a height test.
- **Water levels meet in a one-sample blend,** which the mask has to close or a line of land crosses the bay.
- **A mostly-sea tile is tiny:** K2444F builds to 0.04 MB. Its neighbour K2444D doesn't exist on the mirror: the pipeline treats a missing tile as open sea, and only a 404 means that.
- **Seams are clean.** Raw samples step across a seam no more than within a tile (median 7.4 cm against 8.1 on one, 1.7 against 2.0 on another), built neighbours share their edge row of heights exactly, and baked shading steps at a seam about as it does inside a tile.

**Mariehamn's roads.** L2334F, the town's northern tile, has eight times the road parts of a country tile and costs nearly twice the bytes. Its bypass runs on an embankment with cycle underpasses that OSM tags as tunnels. The elevation model has no tunnel: the lesser road cuts a slot through the embankment, under the greater road's ribbon, which stays up as a deck.

**How rough the roads are.** The scripted driver down all 843 km of the nine tiles' motor roads, each at a speed its class can carry (`tools/road-check.ts`), finishes every one, buildings and all. A wheel drop at the very start of a road is only the car being put down, four wheels at once, and doesn't happen on the way. Drops on the way are real, and on 63 of 1410 lines: narrow roads where a wheel runs off the edge or a crossing lifts it, nearly all at town speeds. 48 lines go over 0.6 g, at worst 2.7 g, in Mariehamn's service roads. The worst on a main road is 1.7 g, a tertiary crossing a greater road at 140 km/h, where the two approaches each ease up to the road between.

`tools/junction-check.ts` drives what those lines can't: every way through every junction of a tile, in the simulation alone. Its movements come from the map's network (`tools/junction-moves.ts`), not the surface, so any two builds can be compared: every road in to every road out, a roundabout's way round as well as the way back to the road a car came in on, one-way roads honoured, and hairpins at a plain junction left out as not a movement. Speeds suit the junction: a road's own through speed, less for a bend, walking pace for a sharp turn. It judges the junction and 40 m either side: whether the car got through, had its middle off the road surface for over 3 m, its worst jolt and wheel drops, and where. A failure there is a place to look at, not always a fault: nearly two thirds are turns of 100° or more, which this car can't make on a narrow road without running wide, on the old surface as on the new, some into a building beside the road. A big roundabout's sharp exits throw the scripted driver wide too. Across the eight tiles rebuilt with aprons, 23,478 movements, 23,425 finish, against 23,415 with the roads trimmed to fit; movements over 0.6 g fell from 962 to 613, over 1 g from 317 to 180, and wheel drops from 6,794 to 4,845.

A scripted drive can't follow a route that turns back on itself, as a route does for a U-turn at a junction: the driver holds the fold and the car runs on off the road, and `startLine` refuses one, saying where.

## Hosting

Tiles live in the public bucket `aland-drive-tiles` on the site's Supabase project, one folder per build under its version, and `render/tile-address.ts` is the one place that says so: the base address is the Supabase URL, the bucket and `TILES_VERSION`. Moving to another host is copying a folder and changing that one function. The files never change once published and carry a year's cache, so a browser keeps them; a rebuild is a new folder and a new `TILES_VERSION`, committed with whatever needs it, so a deployed page only ever sees the tiles it was built with. `pipeline/upload.py` creates the bucket if it's missing and uploads what isn't there, reading the service-role key from `.env.local`. Old versions stay until someone clears them.

Nothing in the tests or tools touches that host: they serve tiles to themselves on localhost.

**Data per drive**, counted by the bytes a local server really sent: starting the game is 18.8 MB (the overview, nine tiles' far data and the start tile's detail), the same whatever the island's size, of 406 MB published for 94 tiles. A tile is 5–8 MB in the country, about 1 MB in the outer sea tiles and up to 14 MB in Mariehamn. A tile once fetched comes from the browser's cache, and driving a route again fetches nothing. So a player's download is bounded by the map, not by the hours: 313 MB for one person seeing everything.

Against Supabase Free's 5 GB of egress a month that's about 15 new players who see everything. Pro's 250 GB is fifty times that. R2 has no egress charge and 10 GB free. So stay on Supabase while the game is the owner's own, and move before it's shared widely: the move is a copy and one address.
