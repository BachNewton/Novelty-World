# Åland Drive: traffic

Cars, cyclists and walkers on the lanes, and `?lane-lab`. How traffic is shared online is in `net/CLAUDE.md`; how the lane map is built is in `pipeline/CLAUDE.md` (The lane map). Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down.

`?lane-lab` is the real game with the lane map drawn over the road (`render/lane-overlay.ts`, `components/lane-lab.tsx`): every lane a strip with arrows the way it runs, every connector through a junction green where it has right of way, amber where it gives way and red where it leaves the drawn road, which is only where the road surface has a gap no turn can stay on, and a ring at each place the lane audit found. Cars, cyclists and walkers move along the lanes round the camera there, each kind at Åland's own density, four times it or none (`traffic/lane-driver.ts`; cars drawn as the parked cars' fleet by `render/lane-traffic.ts`, cyclists as `art/bikes`' crowd by `render/cyclists.ts`, walkers as `art/people`' walking crowd by `render/walkers.ts`): each a place on a lane, a speed and a body with steered front wheels and no physics, steered along its lanes by the steering every computer-driven car shares, the autopilot's pure pursuit (`pursuit`, `sim/autopilot.ts`), its wheels turning at a real car's pace, so traffic turns as a racer will. Its place on its lane is found from where its body is, as the autopilot finds itself on its line. It picks a way on at each junction and signals it with its indicators from 35 m or four seconds before, keeps its distance by the intelligent driver model, slows for bends and limits before it reaches them, and stops at its line while what its turn gives way to is coming, the player's car among them. A dead end's turning round is a U-turn tighter than a car can make on its wheels, so there a car stops and its trip ends. Where they come and go is the bubble (`traffic/bubble.ts`): every lane holds a slot every 40 m, each a seed that says whether it ever holds anyone, by its road's or path's density per kilometre, and who: a road's cars are its real vehicles a day from the lane map, at an average daytime hour's share of them over its limit, half as busy again as the real island so a drive away from town meets something about once a minute, and a path's cyclists a density per kind, so a road fills the same way each time and a vehicle that comes back is the same one. A slot fills while its vehicle is gone and it lies in the bubble, 250 m past where its kind is drawn, out of the player's sight; a vehicle goes once it's out of sight 200 m further out, or its trip has ended at a dead end. Out of sight is `traffic/sight.ts`: past its kind's reach (the fleet's 900 m, the bikes' 400 m, the people's 200 m), out of the camera's view, or behind the ground over a crest; trees and buildings hide things too, but only the ground is known on the CPU, so it errs towards seen. The first fill, as a page opens or a kind is turned on or up, goes anywhere: nothing was there to see appear, or the setting was changed on purpose. The drive has them too, at Åland's own density, offline and in the shared world, where every player sees the same ones (see `net/CLAUDE.md`), and a tile without lanes has none. A jump fills the lanes round the car afresh, as a page opening does. `tools/traffic-count.mjs` prints the draw calls and triangles with and without traffic at the showcase's views, and how many cars and cyclists ride round the car and are in sight. A cyclist rides cycleways, footways, paths and the roads' edges at a pace of its own, 15 to 23 km/h whatever a path's limit, always kinematic and never a simulated body, with no stand-in for the player's car to touch: from a path's end on to a road by the lane map's link there, waiting at the path's end, clear of the road, for a gap in what's coming, and off a road again at a link to a path now and then, and turning round on the spot at a dead end, where a car's trip ends and it stands until it's out of sight. A walker walks the footways, paths, pedestrian streets, steps and the cycleways it shares, never a road but across one, at a pace by who it is (an older person's slower): it keeps to its own side of a path and further right as someone comes, passes a slower walker on the left while nothing is coming, goes round someone the people files placed standing or sitting, steps to the path's edge and slows for a cyclist coming or catching it up, and walks straight on to a path that starts where its own ends. At a path's end by a road it crosses, from the kerb straight to the path starting on the road's other side, once nothing on that road is near or coming within a few seconds; cars and cyclists stop for one out on the road. It never takes a step that would touch anyone: the step is taken back and it stands, turning on the spot or sidestepping towards where it means to go, stepping aside when held up with nothing to wait for, and turning back after a while. A cyclist slows for walkers, passes them with room, and never rides into one. Each walker is drawn by its stride run on by the ground it covers (`art/people/gait.ts`), so its feet keep their place. Traffic slows behind one and overtakes, leaving 1.2 m, only once it has followed a few seconds and the oncoming side is clear for 150 m. It brakes for a simulated car lying across its path at any angle, the player's or one hit out of traffic, and goes round one standing in its lane once the oncoming side is clear for 150 m. On a road too narrow for two to pass at speed, an oncoming pair slows and eases to its edges, and where there's no room for both one stops on its verge while the other creeps by, a whole queue waiting for a whole queue, the two ways taking turns. Near a simulated car each traffic car has a stand-in in the simulation (`traffic/contact.ts`), as another player's car has, its own box and weight: a touch trades momentum by weight, and a car hit hard becomes a full simulated car (`Drive.addMovingCar`), the drivable car nearest it in size, moving across the road as the hit left it but not lifted, pitched or rolled by it, since a stand-in has no ground to take those as a car's wheels would and handed on they reared it up on two wheels to sink back slowly, that skids to a stop, stands with its hazards on, and after five seconds rejoins its lane where it stands and drives off, or, off its wheels or away from a lane running its way, stands until the player is 150 m off and goes. It is drawn as the world car it was, turned only about the vertical. They step at 30 Hz, every fourth step of the simulation, and are drawn between steps. `tools/traffic-check.mjs` runs them round a place for a few minutes, held and stepped by hand, from a camera a little above it turning round once a minute, and counts every car, cyclist or walker that came or went where that camera could see it, after the first fill has settled; it also lists every one that stood still too long, the places they strayed furthest from their lanes (but a walker out on a road, crossing it), and every pair that overlapped or passed within 15 cm, oncoming or the same way, and every walker that walked into someone placed standing or sitting, each with a link. The player's car stands where the view puts it, so it leaves that out unless asked (`--with-player`); `tools/film.mjs --traffic=<density> --bikes=<density> --walkers=<density>` films them the same way, and `--hide-lanes` leaves the lanes undrawn. Its list is the audit's for the loaded tiles, each a click away from above, and `pipeline/lanes.py --audit <page address>` prints the same with `?lane-lab&at=` links.
