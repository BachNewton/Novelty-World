# Åland Drive: the island's history and a light story

The researched basis for a light story: a timeline of Åland, real places with in-game notes, and one proposed story thread. It is a proposal, not a decision, and nothing here is in the game. The facts were checked against the sources listed at the end, Ålands museum, the parish churches' own site, Visit Åland and scholarly papers first, with Wikipedia used only as a pointer. Anything weaker is flagged in [What isn't confirmed](#what-isnt-confirmed), which should be read before any of this becomes in-game text.

The tone is a calm Nordic summer: exploring, no combat, and wars told as what they did to the people who lived here. The scope is text, places and light errands, with no cutscenes.

## Timeline

|---|---|
| c. 8000 BC | The highest parts of Åland rise out of the sea. The land is still rising. ([Visit Åland](https://visitaland.com/?p=15581)) |
| c. 5000–4000 BC | The first people come from the east, seasonal hunters and fishers of seal and sea bird, with camps at Orrdalsklint in Saltvik. ([Visit Åland](https://visitaland.com/?p=15500)) |
| c. 3400–2800 BC | Pitted Ware people live by seals at Jettböle in Jomala, one of their largest base camps. ([Götherström et al.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-archaeology/article/jettbole-middle-neolithic-site-on-the-aland-islands-human-remains-ancient-dna-and-pottery/664CDB64119A6B536D1E5D0895CDE48C)) |
| 1500–400 BC | Bronze Age: villages across the main island, and cairn graves. ([Visit Åland](https://visitaland.com/?p=15500)) |
| 400 BC–1000 AD | Iron Age: trade with the lands around, and Arabic coins among the finds. Settlement spreads quickly from about 500 AD. |
| 500s–1000s | Kvarnbo in Saltvik is Åland's central place: a great hall, and the Johannisberg cemetery beside it. ([Saltvik church](https://www.kyrkor.ax/churches/saltvik-church/), [Ilves](https://www.heritagedaily.com/2014/05/evidence-of-viking-settlement-on-the-aland-islands/103107)) |
| 1200s | Wooden churches give way to stone ones, Jomala's perhaps as early as 1200, and Åland comes under the Swedish crown. ([Jomala church](https://www.kyrkor.ax/churches/jomala-church/)) |
| 1388 | Kastelholm is first mentioned, among what Bo Jonsson Grip left when he died in 1386. ([Visit Åland](https://visitaland.com/?p=15581)) |
| 1521–23 | Swedes and Danes fight on Åland in Gustav Vasa's war of liberation. |
| 1544 | The Reformation reaches the chapels: Vårdö and Lumparland each lose a bell to the crown. ([Vårdö](https://www.kyrkor.ax/churches/vardo-stone-chapel/), [Lumparland](https://www.kyrkor.ax/churches/lumparland-chapelchurch/)) |
| 1556 | Gustav Vasa spends time at Kastelholm, by then a royal hunting castle. |
| 1571 | The deposed Erik XIV and Karin Månsdotter are held at Kastelholm for some months. |
| 1638 | Under Queen Christina the Stockholm–Turku post is organised across Åland, carried farm to farm. |
| 1665–68 | Witch trials at Kastelholm: seven people are executed. ([Visit Åland guide](https://aland.travel/kastelholm-en)) |
| 1714–21 | The Great Wrath. Russia attacks in August 1714, villages burn, and most people flee to Sweden. Several hundred who couldn't get away, mostly women and children, are taken and sold as slaves. ([Åland stamp notice](https://shipstamps.co.uk/forum/viewtopic.php?p=21567)) |
| 1718–19 | The Åland Congress: Sweden and Russia talk peace at Lövö on Vårdö, and fail. ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Congress-of-Aland)) |
| 1721–23 | Peace of Nystad. The islanders come home to burnt farms. The population recovers only towards 1800. |
| 1742–43 | The Lesser Wrath: Russian troops occupy Åland again. |
| 1745 | Fire ruins Kastelholm. |
| 1784 | The Vita Björn prison is built beside the castle. It runs until 1975. |
| 1809 | Russian forces cross the ice in March. By the Peace of Fredrikshamn, Sweden loses Finland and Åland to Russia. |
| 1828 | The huge post and customs house goes up at Storby, on the Russian Empire's western edge. |
| 1832–54 | Russia builds the Bomarsund fortress, with prisoners and military labour. ([Ålands museum](https://museum.ax/sites/default/files/attachments/page/Bomarsund%20fortress.pdf)) |
| 1854 | Crimean War. British and French forces land on 8 August, and Bomarsund surrenders on 16 August. It is blown up on 2 September. |
| 1856 | The Peace of Paris bans fortifying Åland: the root of today's demilitarisation. |
| 1861 | Mariehamn is founded on 21 February on land of Övernäs village, named after Empress Maria Alexandrovna. |
| 1886–1914 | The Önningeby artists' colony. |
| 1903 | The ship that will be Pommern is launched at Port Glasgow as Mneme. |
| 1910–11 | The last winter of the old post boats. |
| 1913 | Gustaf Erikson settles in Mariehamn as a shipowner. |
| 1916 | Russia, at war, builds coastal batteries on Åland. They are taken in 1918 and pulled down in 1919. |
| 1917 | Finland becomes independent. At Christmas nearly 8,000 Ålanders sign for reunion with Sweden. ([Julius Sundblom's biography](https://www.julius.ax/briefly-about-julius-sundblom)) |
| 1920 | Finland passes an Autonomy Act, which the Ålanders refuse. Julius Sundblom and Carl Björkman are arrested for treason and later pardoned. |
| 1921 | On 24 June the League of Nations awards Finland sovereignty, with guarantees for Åland's language, autonomy and demilitarisation, agreed by Finland and Sweden and approved on 27 June. A ten-state convention follows on 20 October. ([Åland Islands Peace Institute](https://peace.ax/en/the-aland-issue-what-happened-on-june-27-1921/)) |
| 1922 | The parliament meets for the first time on 9 June, now Autonomy Day. |
| 1923–39 | Pommern sails in Erikson's fleet, mostly carrying Australian wheat to Europe. |
| 1940, 1947 | Treaties with the Soviet Union confirm the demilitarisation. Finland keeps artillery positions on Åland during the Continuation War of 1941–44. |
| 1947 | Gustaf Erikson dies in Mariehamn. |
| 1949 | The last grain voyages from Åland. ([Sjöfartsmuseet](https://sjofartsmuseum.ax/en/?p=3217)) |
| 1953 | Erikson's children give Pommern to Mariehamn as a museum ship. |
| 1954 | Åland's flag is approved and first raised. |
| 1959–61 | The car ferries begin: Vikinglinjen in 1959, Eckerö Linjen in 1961. |
| 1984 | On 1 March Åland's first stamps appear. Åland Post runs its own from 1993. ([Åland stamps](https://alandstamps.com/en/blogs/nyheter-och-artiklar/alands-frimarken-fyller-40-ar)) |
| 1995 | Åland joins the EU together with Finland, keeping a special tax status. |

## Places

Positions come from the OpenStreetMap data in `.data/raw/osm-api/` (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL): a point's own position, or the middle of a building's outline. `x, z` is the game's scene, in metres from the world origin, east +x and north -z, as `--gps` takes it. The tile is the 6 km sheet. Every place here is within about 180 m of a junction on the network the GPS can route, except Jettböle, which has no position yet, and the group past the water at the end.

### Eckerö and the west

**Eckerö Post and Customs House** · 60.22020, 19.54367 · `-22537, -9282` · L2341H
> A grand building for a tiny village, and that was the point. Russia put it here in 1828 so that anyone arriving from Sweden would see an empire's front door. The old post road across Åland starts at its steps.

**Ålands Jakt- och Fiskemuseum, Käringsund** · 60.23218, 19.54019 · `-22578, -10633` · L2341H
> For most of Åland's history a family lived off the sea as much as the field. This little museum by Käringsund harbour keeps the seine boats and the seal-hunting gear that fed them.

**Eckerö Sea Rescue Station** · 60.21904, 19.53895 · `-22812, -9183` · L2341H
> Rocks, fog and a lot of shipping: these waters have always needed rescuers. Today volunteers keep boats ready at stations round the islands, day and night, all year.

**Eckerö church, S:t Lars** · 60.20778, 19.61246 · `-18897, -7476` · L2341H
> One of the smallest churches on Åland, in stone since the late 1200s. Its bell may be older than the walls, and its cup-shaped font was carved from a single pine trunk. It was damaged in the Northern War.

**Hammarland church, S:ta Katarina** · 60.21776, 19.74498 · `-11460, -7767` · L2343D
> Built bit by bit from the late 1200s, and you can read the stages in the walls. The tower stands oddly on the south side, over what was once the main door. Eckerö's church began as a chapel of this parish.

### Finström, Jomala and the middle

**Finström church, S:t Mikael** · 60.26354, 19.93086 · `-654, -11727` · L2343F
> Often called the best-preserved medieval building in Finland, with saints still painted on the walls. In the 1800s its long-serving vicar, Frans Peter von Knorring, built up Åland's schools, and his statue stands nearby.

**Jomala church, S:t Olof** · 60.15503, 19.94846 · `-977, 415` · L2334F
> Perhaps the oldest stone church in all of Finland, from around 1200. Look for the small stone lion with a human face in its mouth, older than almost anything else you'll find on the island.

**Jettböle, Jomala** · position not in OSM, see below
> Five thousand years ago this was the seaside. Families camped here for the seals and the eider, and left small clay figures that nobody has fully explained. The shore has moved on since: the land keeps rising.

**Kungsö battery** · 60.10613, 19.81428 · `-8990, 5036` · L2334D
> Sea views and old gun pits. Russia, at war in 1916, built ten coastal batteries on these supposedly unfortified islands. This one was pulled down in 1919.

**Önningeby museum** · 60.11829, 20.00657 · `1803, 4835` · L2334H
> In 1886 the painter Victor Westerholm began bringing artists out here for the summers, many of them women, to paint this very light. The colony lasted until the First World War, and the museum keeps its memory.

### Saltvik

**Orrdalsklint** · 60.32825, 20.12125 · `10593, -17793` · L3122A
> The highest point on Åland, at about 128 m, and where the story starts. When the first people came, this was one of the few pieces of land above the sea. Their old shorelines are still up here, high above today's water.

**Saltvik church, S:ta Maria, Kvarnbo** · 60.27525, 20.05173 · `6146, -12315` · L3122A
> The church stands on far older ground. Viking Age houses lie under its floor, and for centuries this was the heart of Åland: the richest Iron Age district on the islands.

**Johannisberg grave field** · 60.27498, 20.05505 · `6325, -12266` · L3122A
> Åland's largest Iron Age cemetery, a few steps from the church. Low mounds and stones among the trees mark the people of Kvarnbo, whose great hall stood close by.

**Borgboda hillfort** · 60.26721, 20.09545 · `8461, -11169` · L3121B
> The biggest hillfort on Åland, about three hectares of rocky height. It was probably never a home, more a place to run to when danger came.

### Sund and Kastelholm

**Kastelholm Castle** · 60.23285, 20.08069 · `7246, -7442` · L3121B
> Åland's only medieval castle, on record since 1388, and the seat of the Swedish crown here. Gustav Vasa came for the hunting, and his deposed son Erik XIV was held here with Karin Månsdotter in 1571. Fire left it a ruin in 1745.

**Vita Björn** · 60.23145, 20.08278 · `7345, -7275` · L3121B
> The White Bear: a prison next to the castle from 1784 until 1975. For nearly two centuries this small building held the people the islands locked away.

**Jan Karlsgården** · 60.23081, 20.08216 · `7303, -7207` · L3121B
> Farm buildings gathered from all over Åland, windmills included, so you can walk through an 1800s farm. A midsummer pole goes up here every year.

**Sund church, S:t Johannes** · 60.24939, 20.11362 · `9255, -9087` · L3121B
> The largest medieval church on Åland, built around 1250–75 on top of an Iron Age graveyard. Its west tower is the biggest on the islands, and it has come through two great fires.

### Bomarsund and Prästö

**Bomarsund Visitors Centre, at Nya Skarpans** · 60.21116, 20.23410 · `15464, -4153` · L3121C
> Nya Skarpans was meant to be Åland's new centre, a garrison town beside the fortress. It lasted about twenty years. Its bell survives, in Mariehamn.

**Bomarsund Fortress** · 60.21231, 20.23798 · `15691, -4259` · L3121C
> Russia's fortress on the edge of its empire, begun in 1832 and built by prisoners and soldiers: brick behind cut granite, with barracks for about 2,000. Twelve towers were planned. Less than half of it was finished when the war came.

**Brännklint tower** · 60.21582, 20.22273 · `14889, -4735` · L3121C
> In August 1854 about forty British and French warships arrived and 12,500 men came ashore. This tower was attacked on the 13th and stormed on the 14th.

**Notvik tower** · 60.22156, 20.23709 · `15748, -5290` · L3121C
> Besieged on 15 August 1854. The fortress surrendered the next day, and its garrison went off to prison camps in France and England. Britain and France offered the islands to Sweden, which said no. On 2 September they blew Bomarsund up.

**Prästö cemeteries** · 60.21840, 20.25710 · `16816, -4826` · L3121C
> The fortress buried its dead by faith: Orthodox, Muslim and Jewish graves lie apart in the woods here, and French graves further west. Soldiers came from all over a very large empire, and many never left this island.

**Prästö old pilot cottage** · 60.21188, 20.24372 · `16003, -4179` · L3121C
> A small house near the fortress, from the days when pilots took ships through these channels. Beyond the bridge is the sound, and over the water Vårdö, where the post road carried on.

### Lumparland and Lemland

**Lumparland church** · 60.11811, 20.25624 · `15627, 6299` · L3112C
> A low wooden church, white and shingled. Russian soldiers burned the one before it during the Great Wrath, and the people who came home had a new roof on by 1728.

**Långnäs ferry harbour** · 60.11648, 20.29757 · `17898, 6714` · L3112C
> Big ships still call at a very small place. Passenger ferries stop at Åland partly because Åland sits outside the EU's tax area, which keeps tax-free shopping on board, and with it the fares down.

**Lemland church** · 60.07479, 20.08381 · `5573, 10114` · L2334G
> Red granite, with medieval paintings that were only found again in 1957. The largest Iron Age grave field in the parish lies beside it, by a sheltered harbour. Lemland was also the home parish of the sailing-ship owner Gustaf Erikson, who went to sea at nine.

**Lemböte chapel** · 60.07529, 19.98126 · `-107, 9456` · L2334E
> A sailors' stop. The place is named in a Danish sailing guide of the 1200s, on the route to Tallinn, and over 500 coins have been found here. The roof was gone by 1674, and it was restored from the 1880s.

### Mariehamn

**Empress Maria Alexandrovna's memorial** · 60.09685, 19.94355 · `-1941, 6841` · L2334E
> Mariehamn was founded in 1861 on the fields of Övernäs, and named Marie's Harbour after the Russian empress. Its wide streets were laid out as fire breaks. Around 1900 Hilda Hongell, Finland's first woman master builder, drew many of its wooden houses.

**S:t Görans church** · 60.09730, 19.93481 · `-2420, 6739` · L2334E
> The one parish church here with no medieval past, built in 1927 to Lars Sonck's design with money from a shipowner couple who first gave anonymously. The town's organ is the biggest on Åland, and the old Skarpans bell, carried off as war booty in 1854, found its way here.

**Gustaf Erikson's home** · 60.09728, 19.93809 · `-2238, 6761` · L2334E
> The sailing-ship king lived here. When the world moved to steam, he bought big square-riggers cheap and kept them working. In the grain race of 1939, ten of the thirteen ships were his.

**Pommern** · 60.09743, 19.92498 · `-2963, 6666` · L2334E
> Launched in Scotland in 1903, and in Erikson's fleet from 1923. She carried Australian wheat around Cape Horn until 1939. She has been Mariehamn's own since 1953, the last of her kind still in the harbour she sailed from.

**Åland Maritime Museum** · 60.09725, 19.92653 · `-2879, 6695` · L2334E
> Family boats carrying firewood and fish, then timber, then the world. Upstairs is the real captain's saloon from Herzogin Cecilie, which ran aground on the Devon coast in 1936 and was lost.

**Julius Sundblom** · 60.09759, 19.94330 · `-1946, 6757` · L2334E
> In 1917 nearly 8,000 Ålanders signed to rejoin Sweden, while newly independent Finland held that the islands were its own. Sundblom led the Ålanders, was arrested and pardoned, and became the first speaker of their parliament. The League of Nations' peaceful answer of 1921 gave Finland the islands, and the islanders their language and self-rule.

**Mariehamn post office** · 60.10031, 19.94134 · `-2022, 6444` · L2334E
> Åland has had its own stamps since 1984 and its own post since 1993. For a place of thirty thousand people, a stamp says something: we're here, and we look after our own.

**Västerhamn ferry terminal** · 60.09205, 19.92908 · `-2800, 7287` · L2334E
> When the sailing ships stopped, the shipping families didn't stop. Car ferries began here in 1959, and shipping is still about a tenth of Åland's economy.

## The story: Märta's Round

Everything in this thread except the history is invented. **Märta Holm and everyone the player meets are fictional, and the game says so.** Real people from the past appear only in the place notes, as facts, and never speak. Parcel recipients are invented villagers, and no real family, farm or business gets a part. The stamps are the game's own designs: real Åland stamps are someone else's artwork.

**The idea.** The player's great-aunt Märta was a country postwoman for forty years, on a round that followed the old Post Road. She has left the player her old car, her round book and a half-empty stamp album. One summer, the player drives her last round: a parcel or letter for each stop, and at each stop a stamp for the album, with Märta's handwritten note on why she liked the place.

**Why it teaches.** The road is the real thing: the post crossed Åland for nearly three centuries, past the churches, the castle and the fortress, so a courier sees the history in the order the land holds it. The round runs geographically, clockwise from Eckerö. The album's pages are in date order, so the stamps land scattered through it, and the last chapter closes the gaps: the player turns the pages and reads the island's whole story, from the seal hunters to the ferries, built from places they have stood in.

**How an errand works.** Simple, and the same each time. The round book names a place, the world map shows it, and the GPS sets the route. Driving into the place's circle shows its note on screen, stops nothing, and gives the stamp. Delivering a parcel is parking near the door. There are no timers and no failure, and everything can be done in any order inside a chapter. A chapter's next one opens when its stamps are in. This needs only the GPS the game has (`map/`), plus two small new things: a list of places, and a note on screen. Distances below are measured on the game's road network, and the whole round is about 185 km.

**Cycling.** The game has no bicycle yet. Where it comes, chapters 4 and 7 suit it best, and the round book can mark them. The rest of the round should work in a car first.

### 1. The Horn: Eckerö

*From Storby to Eckerö church, 5 km, with a side trip to Käringsund.*

The ferry lands, and the car is waiting at the Post House with the round book. Märta's first note explains the horn: when it blew, the farmer had to go, with tax relief for doing it and prison for refusing, and many drowned on the winter crossing. **Errand:** take a parcel from the Post House to a fisherman at Käringsund, then a letter to the church. **Stamps:** Post House 1828, Fisherman's Boat, S:t Lars.

### 2. The Stone Parishes: Hammarland and Finström

*Eckerö church to Hammarland 10 km, on to Finström 18 km.*

The road inland, through farmland, from church to church. Märta's note: a medieval parish was the whole of life, and the same road linked them. **Errand:** carry a hymn book from Hammarland to Finström, and stop at the paintings. **Stamps:** S:ta Katarina, S:t Mikael, The Teacher (von Knorring's schools).

### 3. The Hall at Kvarnbo: Saltvik

*Finström to Saltvik church 13 km, Borgboda 4 km.*

Quiet villages over very old ground. **Errand:** a retired teacher in Kvarnbo sends the player to Borgboda to find the view she remembers, and to bring back a photograph. The game takes it from the car's position. **Stamps:** Silver Coin, Johannisberg, Hillfort.

### 4. The Old Shore: Orrdalsklint

*Borgboda to Orrdalsklint 10 km, the end on foot or by bike.*

The shortest errand and the highest place. The note says that the first people found only a few small islands here, and that the land under the car was sea floor. **Errand:** there's no delivery, just Märta's request to go up and look. **Stamps:** Seal Hunter, Clay Figure: the album's oldest page.

### 5. The Crown: Kastelholm

*Orrdalsklint to Kastelholm 15 km, Sund church 4 km.*

The Swedish centuries in one bend of the road. **Errand:** deliver a box of old farm tools to the open-air museum, then a letter to the church. The notes tell of the hunting king, the imprisoned one, and the witch trials, plainly. **Stamps:** Kastelholm, Vasa's Hunt, The White Bear.

### 6. The Fortress: Bomarsund

*Sund church to Bomarsund 12 km, with the towers 1 km apart.*

Russian Åland, the longest read of the round, told from the ground: the labourers, the town that didn't last, and the guns. **Errand:** visit the towers in the real order of the siege, Brännklint, Notvik, the fortress, with a note each giving its date. **Stamps:** Empire's Edge 1809, Bomarsund 1854.

### 7. The Dead and the Water: Prästö

*Bomarsund to Prästö 1 km by bridge.*

A quiet chapter after the loud one. **Errand:** put flowers at the cemeteries, which Märta did each summer, for soldiers no one here had known. Then the road ends at the water. The ferry to Vårdö is the post road's next stage, and Märta's note says what lay beyond it, up to the Åland Congress and on to Turku. **Stamps:** Three Graveyards, Pilot's Cottage.

### 8. Coming Home: Lumparland and Lemland

*Prästö to Lumparland 55 km, round the great bay of Lumparn, then Lemland 15 km.*

The long drive of the round, and a chance simply to cruise. At the end, the story of the Great Wrath, kept for here because this chapel was burnt and rebuilt by people who came back to nothing. **Errand:** take a repaired church-model ship from Lumparland to Lemland. **Stamps:** The Return 1721, Red Granite.

### 9. Sailors: Lemböte and the harbour

*Lemland to Lemböte 9 km, on to Mariehamn's western harbour 13 km.*

From the medieval sailors' chapel to the last sailing fleet: one idea across seven hundred years. **Errand:** a sailor's old letter goes from an invented family near Lemböte to the Maritime Museum. Then walk Pommern's deck in a note. **Stamps:** Lemböte Coin, Mariehamn 1861, Pommern, Herzogin Cecilie.

### 10. The Answer: Mariehamn

*In town: S:t Görans, the Sundblom statue, the post office, the ferry terminal, all within 2 km.*

The last stops tell how the island's question was settled: not by a war but at the League of Nations, with both sides' views given fairly. **Errand:** the last envelope, addressed to the player in Märta's hand, waits at the post office. It holds the final stamps, her thanks, and the empty line in the round book for the next rider. Then the album opens and the player reads the timeline. **Stamps:** The Skarpans Bell, The Answer 1921, Flag 1954, First Stamp 1984, The Ferries.

**Fairness on the sensitive points.** The Åland question is told with both cases: the Ålanders wanting to keep their language and way of life by rejoining Sweden, and Finland, just independent and just through a civil war, holding that the islands were its own. Sundblom and Björkman's arrests are stated with the pardons. The Russian rule at Bomarsund isn't a villain's story: soldiers and prisoners from across the empire built it, and many are buried on Prästö. The Great Wrath's slave-taking is told plainly, and briefly. The story never says who was right, and no one in it is mocked.

## What isn't confirmed

Check these before they become in-game text.

**Sources disagree:**

- **Date of the first people.** Visit Åland's timeline says 5000 BC, and its early-history page says about 6000 years ago. The timeline above gives the range.
- **Orrdalsklint's height.** Visit Åland says 128 m, and OSM's tag 129 m.
- **Lemböte chapel's date.** Mortar points to the late 1200s or about 1370. The place name is older, and the first chapel may have been wooden.
- **Jan Karlsgården's founding** in the 1930s rests on secondary sources.
- **The Post Road's end.** 1895 is given for when farmers stopped carrying the mail, and 1910–11 for the last traditional boats. The two may describe different things. The notes avoid both, except the 1910–11 timeline line.
- **3 June 1638** as the first run comes from a single French philatelic blog, so it isn't used.
- **The flag.** 1954 and President Paasikivi's approval are in most sources, and the first raising is usually 3 April 1954 in Mariehamn. Lex.dk says 24 April, and Visit Åland's timeline says 1953. That the red cross stands for Finland rests on weak sources. Blue-and-yellow unofficial flags from the 1920s, banned in 1935, are supported by several.
- **Hilda Hongell's exam** is 1893 in one source and 1894 in Visit Åland, and her Mariehamn buildings are 98 or 105. Finland's "first woman master builder" is the safe wording, not "first architect".
- **Sundblom's detention** in 1920: over a month by his own memorial site, a few days elsewhere. The sentence of 18 months and the pardon come from that site.
- **Herzogin Cecilie and Pommern's grain races.** The museum says Herzogin Cecilie won four times. Pommern's wins, 1930 and 1937 or 1930 alone, aren't settled, so the notes leave them out.
- **Pommern's gift** is 1953 on the ship's own site, and 1952 in one other.
- **The Erik XIV stay.** Some sources say "until his death", which is wrong: he died at Örbyhus in 1577, and Kastelholm was a few months.
- **Kastelholm in 1599.** The Visit Åland guide names Catherine Vasa as owner, which looks doubtful, so the name isn't used.
- **Gustav Vasa's 1556 stay and Johan's appointment** are from secondary sources.
- **Kungsö battery:** 1916 in Visit Åland, 1916–18 in another. "Taken in 1918" by Finnish, Swedish and German troops has one weak source.
- **The second petition** of 9,735 for and 461 against is undated on the Sundblom site, and is probably 1919.
- **The Åland Congress's exact island.** Lövö on Vårdö in most sources, and Sundsher in one Russian source.

**Not confirmed at all, and kept out of the notes or worded around:**

- **The Viking Age at Lemböte.** The brief asked for it, but no source found ties Viking graves to Lemböte itself. What's confirmed is the medieval sailing route and chapel. The Viking site is Kvarnbo and Johannisberg in Saltvik. Lemland's grave field is by the parish church, and is called Iron Age.
- **"More than 380 Viking Age burial sites" and "six hillforts"** come from a Wikipedia-derived summary, and aren't used.
- **Who commanded at Bomarsund.** Napier's naval role is confirmed by the London Gazette. Others weren't, so no commander is named. Most accounts say about 2,000 surrendered.
- **The Sea Rescue Society's founding.** Only "50 years in 2015" (so about 1965) was found, and six stations with about 130 volunteers then.
- **Rödhamn's pilot date.** One source gives 1928 for "Långö", which may or may not be Rödhamn's island. It isn't used.
- **Prästö's pilot cottage, Tombes Françaises and the museums at Degerby and Prästö** rest on their OSM names. Check what's there before the notes are final, especially the French graves' position (60.21532, 20.20495).
- **Jettböle's position.** It isn't named in OSM. It needs a position from the museum or kartor.ax before it can be a stop. Until then its stamp can be earned at the Jomala church.
- **Ten of thirteen ships in the 1939 grain race** is from Wikipedia only.
- **Åland in the Second World War** has one line in the timeline, from a government archaeology report on Möskatan and the foreign ministry's extract. It's thin: read a proper account before writing a note on it. Finland's wartime position on Åland is a sensitive point, and Kungsö's note deliberately stops at 1919.
- **The 1999 end of EU tax-free sales** and the Åland protocol's wording weren't reached. The Finnish foreign ministry's page refused the fetch. Finland's law text and Åland parliament's own pages weren't read either, so the Autonomy Act's language section and the first speaker's date rest on secondary sources.
- **The Post Road's line across the island.** Stops named in sources are Storby, past Kastelholm, and Vårdö. OSM has a few Postvägen pieces. The exact road between, and the claim that the post passed Sund church, need a proper check (Jan Andersson's *Postvägen över Åland*, 1999).
- **Gunnar Eklund's role** in founding Vikinglinjen has one account, and isn't named in the notes.
- **The founding date of the Åland Maritime Museum** wasn't found.
