# Road surfaces: what makes them look broken

Why Åland Drive's roads, tracks, paths and their junctions look janky, as the owner put it after walking through many examples, and what has been learned about each cause. The town's own ground (`art/town-ground`) waits on this: paving drawn up to these edges only shows them more.

## The problem, in the owner's words

> Three issues at its core:
>
> 1. "ways" that are close together, junction, merge, end abruptly, or overlap.
> 2. "ways" that are layered on top of each other, like an under or overpass.
> 3. "ways" that are placed on terrain / elevation data that is not cleanly flat or changes quickly near a road.
>
> If we can figure out how to make all these cases look good we can have a good looking road network.

## The same three, as the pipeline sees them

Every OSM way is laid on its own (`pipeline/roads.py`): its own centre line, its own height profile smoothed over tens of metres, its own crown, painted edges and a pale shoulder strip out to the ground. The ground is a separate 4 m mesh under all of them, cut and filled to fit. Everything that looks broken is a place where these separately made pieces have to meet.

1. **Ways close to each other.** Nothing lays two ways together. Where a sidewalk runs beside a street, both are full roads at almost the same height, overlapping: their paint and shoulders poke through each other as white flecks and pale smears, and the renderer can't tell which is on top. Where they're at different heights, the strip between them is squeezed into a wall. Junctions are joined after the fact by aprons, paving stretched between the pieces; the gap between an apron and a ribbon is tucked a centimetre under it and still flickers along the seam. Every small piece of paving gets its own pale shoulder, so an island between paths becomes a patchwork of slabs. A track or path ending on a road used to lie over that road's sloping shoulder as a raised slab.
2. **Ways stacked on each other.** The pipeline has no idea of an underpass. A way OSM marks as a tunnel, such as a cycle path under a road, is laid at its own ground level through whatever is above it, and the ground is dug down to it under the road. Bridges are handled (spans held up over what they cross); the way going under is not.
3. **Ways on uneven ground.** The survey's ground is nearly raw, banks, ditches and kerbs included, averaged only over 2 m, while every way's profile is smoothed over 30 m (a path) to 280 m (a main road). On a bank the smooth way and the raw land beside it can be a metre apart within a few metres, and the 4 m mesh can't bend that sharply. The pieces between (the land levelled round each way, the shoulder's drop capped at 1 m, the ground pressed under each carriageway) fight there: floating paths, carved pits, stretched walls of ground.

The three meet: a sidewalk beside a street on a bank is all of them at once.

## What has been measured

`pipeline/surface_audit.py` finds where drawn surfaces meet badly, read from the triangles the game draws: gaps between carriageways, steps, a lesser way on top of a greater, two surfaces too close to tell apart (flicker), a shoulder lying over another carriageway (plate), ground over a road, a road's outline standing above the ground (ledge), and folds in a profile. Each place gets an `?at=` link.

`pipeline/paving_audit.py` does the same for a paved tile, by cause: strips of ground under 1.5 m wide between pieces of paving (by the ways either side and whether the ground there is a ridge, a bank or flat), steep paving, steps where cut ground and paving disagree, a path's edge drawn as a street's or a driveway's, and streets whose OSM way maps a sidewalk on itself with none drawn beside it. Each place gets an `?at=` link and `tools/look.mjs` arguments.

What each attempt did, judged by the owner on screenshots from the same cameras:

- **Tracks and paths take aprons at their ends**, as roads do, instead of sliding over the greater road's shoulder. The counts fell 20–40% for gaps, steps and plates; the pictures barely changed, and the seam flicker now runs along track ends too.
- **Ground pressed under a way only as far as that way needs**, instead of every 4 m cell it touches being flattened to its level, which dragged the ground out from under a higher way beside it. It fixed a floating path and cut ledges by about 70%; the pale slabs between paths got slightly bigger.
- **The ground at the survey's full 2 m** instead of 4 m. It removed one stretched wall of ground and changed little else, broke the trees' detail distances (they read the ground rings), and would triple the heights' size. Dropped.
- **The ground smoothed near the ways** (fully within 12 m, back to the survey by 40 m) before any way is laid, so a way and the land beside it start out agreeing. On top of the gentler pressing: a bank's stretched wall became one smooth slope, the pale slabs between paths shrank and an underpass's banks smoothed; ledges fell a further 8–16% and folds about 10%. The ways themselves (slabs, shoulders, overlaps) are unchanged.
- **Holding a way's height near a greater way beside it** changed nothing visible: the heights weren't the problem there, the ground under them was. Dropped.

## Why the attempts stay small

Every attempt above is a layer over the one before: aprons hide the gaps between ribbons, a centimetre's tuck hides the apron's fight with the ribbon, shoulders hide the drop to the ground, a cap hides the shoulder's worst drops, skipped shoulders hide the slabs, levelling, pressing and smoothing each hide some of the ground's disagreement with the ways, and the audit carries exemptions for the system's own pieces. Each layer answers a seam the last one made, so each helps less and costs more. The seams come from one choice: the ways, the joins between them and the ground are made separately and reconciled afterwards. The design below makes them together, so the seams aren't there to hide.

## The design: paving first, then its heights, then the ground round it

How the published island is built (`pipeline/paving.py`, `build.py --paving`), with the game drawing either kind of tile. It began as a trial on the two Mariehamn tiles, which the owner judged the biggest improvement yet; "What the trial taught" below holds the rules it added.

### 1. Where paving is, in plan

Each way's footprint (its centre line, widened by its class's width, and for a motor road by its shoulder, where the kerb and paving or the stone edge is drawn) is merged with every other's into **one paved shape per level**. A junction, a merge, a way ending on another, a sidewalk along its street and a paved area are all just parts of the shape, with nothing left to join: issue 1 has no seams to make.

**One shape is not one look.** The shape is cut along every way's own outline and carriageway, and each piece belongs to the greatest way whose carriageway holds it, or failing that the greatest whose shoulder does, so a sidewalk beside its street stays the sidewalk's to the street's edge, with the sidewalk's surface and the street's paint ending on one clean line, and a side road keeps its own surface through the main road's shoulder. A piece no way's outline holds (a junction's rounded corner, a narrow gap closed between two ways) is drawn as the shoulders round it.

**Ways join only where their surfaces agree.** A gap is closed and a corner rounded only where the ways either side stand within 0.4 m of each other all across it, and two ways whose outlines touch but stand a metre or more apart along most of where they touch stay apart: the lesser stands back from the greater, and the land between them is a bank. Joined anyway, the lower way is dragged up the bank, or the paving between them is a cliff.

**Levels** answer issue 2. Every way is on the ground's level except across a **crossing span**: where a way tagged bridge passes over another, the span is on the level above; where a way tagged tunnel passes under another, the span is on the level below. Off its span the same way is on the ground level, so a bridge's approaches are embankments and an underpass's are cuttings, laid like any other way. Two ways crossing untagged, both on the ground, are a junction, as OSM means them. Åland has no stacked interchanges, so three levels (below, ground, above) are enough; a fourth is an error the build stops on.

The shape is clipped to the tile, and its edge on the tile's edge is cut at points both tiles derive the same way, so a tile's edge stays nothing to the paving.

### 2. The paving's heights, in one pass

Each way keeps the profile it has now: smoothed along its length by its class's wavelength, pinned to a greater way where it joins one, held over its bridge spans. That part is sound and stays. The new part is how the paving between centre lines gets its height.

Each way's own cross-section (its centre's height, its crown, its camber) gives a height anywhere near it. The paving's heights come from **one least-squares solve**: each point leans towards its own way's cross-section, as stiffly as that way's rank (a main road hardly bends, a path bends over several metres), and each edge between two points keeps the difference the greater of their two ways has between them. Inside one way's own piece that is only its own cross-section, crown and all; where two ways' surfaces disagree, at a junction, a merge or a sidewalk beside its street, the lesser bends smoothly to meet the greater, and a piece no way owns hangs between its neighbours. Paving's height is then one continuous surface by construction, with no step for anything to cover. A kerb, if wanted later, is a deliberate offset in that solve, not an accident of two overlapping roads.

A span on another level takes only its own way's cross-section and is never blended with the level it crosses. An underpass's own profile is dipped, smoothly as its class bends, to keep a clearance under the deck above it, and walls rise from its edges to under the deck where it runs beneath, with the deck's edge drawn down over it.

### 3. The ground, built round the paving

The ground keeps its 4 m grid everywhere a way doesn't come near. Each grid cell that paving touches is a **cut cell**: there the drawn ground isn't the grid's two triangles but a triangulation between the cell's own four corners and the paving's edge. Its outer points are the grid's own nodes, so it meets the grid exactly; its inner points are the paving's edge points, so it meets the paving exactly. The paving's own triangles fill the rest. Ground and road share their points; there's nothing between them.

Next to the paving the land follows one rule, the one real roads are built to: **an embankment or a cutting at a slope limit**. From each point of the paving's edge the land may rise or fall away no faster than the limit, so the survey's height is clamped between the edge's height plus and minus distance times the slope; past where the clamp stops biting, it is the survey. A road on a bank gets a cutting on the uphill side and an embankment downhill, a road in a dip gets its banks, and the land between two ways at different heights is a slope from one edge to the other. This replaces levelling, pressing and the smoothing near ways (issue 3).

The cut cells' grid nodes take their height from the same rule, so everything that reads the grid (below) sees the land as it's drawn, to within a cell's bending.

### What the game reads

The rest of the game reads the ground as the regular grid, and keeps doing so:

- **Drawing.** The clipmap's innermost ring skips its cut cells, and each tile's cut-cell triangles are drawn with its paving. Farther out the ring folds onto coarser grids and the cut no longer lines up, so beyond the innermost ring's exact zone the cut cells are drawn as plain grid again, under paving drawn over it, as all ground is today. Whether that change shows as the camera moves is the first thing the trial checks.
- **The car.** Collision blocks drop their cut cells' grid triangles and take the cut cells' and the paving's triangles instead: the car still touches what's drawn.
- **Trees, granite, verge, reeds, shrubs, the water's line, the cameras.** These read the grid's heights. In cut cells the grid follows the embankment rule, so it is near the drawn ground; the trial measures how near, at the paving's edge, where the verge and the reeds grow.
- **Road paint.** Paving vertices carry the across, along and class of the way whose footprint they lie in, as ribbons do now, so the road's shader draws the same paint. Junction paving, inside more than one way's footprint, carries no paint, as aprons don't now; junction paint (`JUNCTIONS.md`) is drawn from the paving's outline later. The fade from road to the ground's colour, which the shoulder carries now, moves to the cut cells' ground next to the paving's edge, which is ground and so takes the ground's colour anyway.
- **Chains.** The ways' centre lines stay in the tile as they are, for whatever drives on them.

### What goes away

Ribbons as separate meshes, aprons and their tuck, shoulders and their cap and skips, the gap fillers, the paved-area pieces (a paved area is part of the shape), levelling, pressing, the smoothing near ways, and the audit's exemptions. The audit's kinds mostly vanish with them: gaps, lips, through, flicker and plates can't happen on one shape with one height per point. What's left to measure is the surface's own folds (a kink in a blend, an embankment too steep for its limit) and where the drawn ground and the grid other things read come apart.

### What the trial taught

- **A road's edge carries its look.** Without shoulders the kerbs and sidewalk paving went, so a motor road's paving includes its shoulder; a path has none, since its shoulder made the old pale slabs.
- **The game keeps every triangle of paving.** Its rule dropping "cliffs" (a steep triangle rising over 1.2 m) was for ribbons diving through each other, which one surface can't; on paving it cut thin triangles out of steep paths and left holes.
- **Cut ground meets two heights at an underpass's portal.** Each corner of cut ground takes the height of the paving its own triangle borders, and an upright face closes any gap left along an edge, between two triangles of cut ground or between cut ground and paving. An underpass's walls are concrete, as the bridges are.
- **An underpass in the open is a way like any other** to the banks and cuttings beside it; left out, the ground was stretched from its floor up to the raw land.
- **A deck has no verge.** Where a road passes over an underpass, its paving stops at its carriageway's edge; with its shoulder the opening was a long dark box.
- **A path's look comes from its own way's tags, with no town image.** Each point of paving carries its OSM way's class, by the town bake's rules (`pipeline/path_surfaces.py`), and that way's direction, so a sidewalk is slabs, a footway or cycleway pale walked tarmac, a park path gravel and steps treads, laid along the way. The direction is one snapped value per OSM way: slabs are laid in world metres, so a direction that changed by a hair across a triangle turned the lattice into arcs.
- **A gap is judged a few metres at a time.** Judged whole, a strip running a hundred metres between a street and its path was left as ground wherever the two ways disagreed at any one place on it, or wherever a third way touching its far end did. Cut on a 4 m lattice, each piece judged by the ways either side that reach it, the strips of ground under 1.5 m wide on the two Mariehamn tiles fell from 421 places and 8,400 m² to 232 and 1,400 m². The look barely changed: a piece between ways is drawn as a shoulder, which fades to the photo's colour, so the pale band stays a band of paving, not of ground.
- **Between a street and its sidewalk is the sidewalk.** A street's shoulder beside a sidewalk is a kerb's strip, and the rest of it out to the sidewalk, with the strip paved between them, wears the sidewalk's own surface: drawn as shoulders, both faded to the photo's colour, the pale band. Each such piece wears one path's surface whole, the nearest running alongside the street, or its triangles blend two classes and two slab lattices. A sidewalk (a path beside a street, or one tagged so) also carries on across a driveway that crosses it, as real ones do; a street crossing one keeps its crossing, and a path out in the country is crossed by the drive.
- **A sidewalk tagged on its street is laid.** Many of Mariehamn's streets map their sidewalks on their own way (`sidewalk=both|left|right`, with `sidewalk:*:width` and `:surface`) rather than as ways of their own. On a side so tagged, with no path beside it, the street's paving runs on past a kerb's strip by the sidewalk's width (2 m where OSM gives none), and that band wears the sidewalk's surface. OSM names a side as its way runs, which may be against the chain; `separate` is left to the mapped way. Into a tile's edge the band eases away as the rest of a street's sidewalk rules do, so both tiles cut the road alike.
- **The roadside keeps off what a ribbon tile kept it off.** A paved tile's paving is the whole surface, shoulders and sidewalks included, and read as blocked ground it left no room beside any road: edge posts, rails, give-way and stop signs all went to none, and mailboxes to a tenth. What keeps things off it (`read_surface` in `pipeline/roadside.py`, which the furniture, dressing and waterfront share) is every way's carriageway, owned by that way, with all of a path's paving its carriageway since a path has no shoulder, and junction and gap pieces and car parks owned by none; a motor road's shoulder is clear ground, where a sign stands a metre past the carriageway, but for a crossing's own stretch. On the two Mariehamn tiles every kind of roadside object came back within about a tenth of the ribbon build's, but for crossing signs (+11%), give-way signs (+25%) and guard rails (+50%), which the ground's real embankments beside the roads now call for (1,357 m of embankment on the ribbon build, 2,889 m paved), and café tables and lindens, which may stand on a sidewalk that a gap apron used to cover.
- **The cut ground is cut from the heights as written.** The ground is lowered under the water (`terrain.seabed`) after the roads are laid, so cut ground taken from the nodes as the paving left them stood at the water's level where the grid beside it was lakebed or seabed, 0.1–3.6 m lower, at about 3,100 nodes on the two Mariehamn tiles: a shoreline of 4 m stair-steps. The cut is made once the tile's heights are final (`Paved.ground_cut`), from them rounded as the heights file holds them. The water surface also draws over cut cells now, as over any other ground: leaving it out there drew those cells as a flat band of water-coloured ground.
- **On a tile's edge the paving is only the seam sections.** Each tile sees only its own side of an edge, and every rule added since the first trial shaped paving there from that side: a sidewalk beside a street narrowed it and carried a kerb's strip on to the edge (its easing to the class's widths ran before the smoothing, which spread the sidewalk back on to the seam); a chain that carries on through a junction into a path gave the path a motor road's shoulder where the chain was a street's (whether a way has a shoulder went by the whole chain, not by the OSM way at each station); a way ending on a junction just past the edge was eased on to it at the seam, on one tile only; which way's surface a point on the edge took went by the pieces on this tile's side, a junction's greater road on one tile and its lesser on the other; a way running beside the edge reached over it on its own tile alone; and the lines the paving is cut along (carriageways, kerbs, centre lines) crossed the edge where each tile's own rules put them. The ways' rings, which place the roadside, read the paving at seam points past the edge, which is the neighbour's. So the two Mariehamn tiles met with 44 road ends 3–224 mm apart, paving on the edge up to 210 mm apart in 11 stretches and on one tile only in 32, and the surface with the cut ground up to 0.52 m apart.

  Now, within 1 m of the edge, every way's paving, carriageway, kerb and centre line is its seam sections' alone (`held_to_seams`, `on_seams`): each the class's own section of the OSM way it crosses on, run 4 m along it. Each point on the edge takes its height from the seam section that holds it (`seam_targets`): the greatest way whose carriageway holds it, then whose shoulder does, nearest its line, read off the seam's height and crown with nothing a junction added. A point of paving on an edge that no seam section holds stops the build. A sidewalk's widths ease in only from 4 m past a seam, a way's shoulder goes by its own OSM way, kept apart stands back from the edge as gap filling does, and a ring's seam end is the seam's own section, as on a ribbon tile. On the pair, `check_seams` finds every road end meeting exactly, the paving's points on the edge are the same on both tiles to the millimetre with the same heights, and the cut ground meets to within a millimetre along it, its points differing only by grid nodes one tile's cut cells hold and the other's plain grid shares.
- **A path has no shoulder to draw.** A point's place across its way is counted out to its carriageway's edge and then over its shoulder, and a path's shoulder is nothing wide: every point of a path's outline a hair past its edge, where the outline is simplified within 2 cm, counted as a shoulder's far side, a fifth of the paths' points on the two Mariehamn tiles. Each triangle from a path's middle to one of them faded to the photo's colour over half its width, as pale lenses along paths and wedges at their junctions, which a path's own darker surface made plain. Where a way has no shoulder, on a path or a deck, a point past its edge is on its edge.
- **A point is as far along its way as it lies.** Each point took its nearest station's distance along, which moves in 2 m steps, so between a way's middle and its edges, simplified to fewer points, the distance ran askew: a flight of steps, whose treads are laid across it, was drawn as stripes and chevrons. A point's distance is now its nearest station's and how far past that station it lies along the way.
- **A path lends its surface only to what lies between it and its street.** Paving no way's outline holds (a corner, a closed gap) wore the nearest path's surface, so a drive's rounded corners where it met a sidewalk, or a corner of two streets with a cycleway a few metres off across the grass, wore a path's dark asphalt and stood out as sharp flaps beyond the drive's or the street's pale paving. Beside a street such a piece now wears only a path running alongside that street, and only one reached across paving; anything else is drawn as the shoulders round it. Away from streets, where paths meet paths, a corner still wears the nearest path's.
- **A junction's corner with a street is drawn as the shoulders round it.** A sidewalk's corner round a side street, rounded where the sidewalk meets it, lies beside the side street, and a crossing over the main street runs alongside that, so the corner wore the crossing's asphalt: a dark flap beyond the side street's pale paving. A corner where either way is a street wears no path's surface; one between two paths still wears the nearest path's.
- **A junction's corners are only between its two ways.** A corner is rounded by closing the two ways' outlines together, which also rounded the inside of the other way's own bends nearby: on a loop street a side road joins, a flap of paving reached into the block on the far side. A part of a corner that doesn't touch both ways is dropped.
- **A paved tile stores only what the game can't work out, as whole numbers.** The trial's tiles were 47–65% larger than the ribbon build's (a village and a country tile), most of it the cut ground and the paving's points, held as float32 numbers that gzip packs badly; now they are 10–20% larger, and the two Mariehamn tiles smaller than their ribbon build. Cells the paving doesn't touch were cut too, as a margin, and each was only the grid's own two triangles, 37% of the cut cells, so only touched cells are cut now. Places and heights are millimetres (`pipeline/paving_file.py`), points listed in strips 32 m tall so each place is a small step from the last, a height as its offset from the grid's drawn surface, so a point on a node is the node's height exactly, and a place the paving and the cut ground share reads back as one height; across is in 1024ths, along in centimetres, a way's run as a byte of its snapped angle, and triangles sorted and coded as differences. Each comes back within half a unit, held by `pipeline/test_paving_file.py` and, on the game's side, `sim/paved.test.ts`, which also casts the car's rays at the decoded paving and cut ground.
- **A crossing's surface is the road's it crosses.** OSM tags a crossing's surface for the stretch over the road, and the paving read it all along the crossing's way: one tagged asphalt, mapped on a sidewalk's line across a side road's mouth, painted 16 m of Hamngatan's sidewalk dark, and the street's walk band beside it too; a short crossing from a street to its sidewalk owned its own carriageway through the street's kerb strip, its walk band and the gap to the sidewalk, which a carriageway takes over a shoulder, leaving square dark blocks. A crossing's carriageway now counts only as a shoulder (`crossing_stations`), so on the road the road owns it and off it the street's kerb and walk band and the sidewalk outrank it, and wherever a crossing is worn it wears the sidewalk's look (`edge_looks`): its own way's nearest OSM way that isn't a crossing, or the path it leads to.
- **The street's walk band carries on across a driveway** as a mapped sidewalk does. Only the sidewalk's own carriageway took a drive's mouth, so where the street's paving past its kerb wears the sidewalk's surface, the drive's carriageway, which beats the street's shoulder, cut across that band as a square mouth up to the kerb (Godbyvägen's band at a drive). The band now carries on across it, and the drive meets the street at the kerb.
- **A named service way is a street, not a driveway** (`is_driveway`). Every service way was a drive to the sidewalk, so Stava Mostersvägen, a named street OSM files as a service way, ended square on Hamngatan's sidewalk. A service way is a drive when it has no name, or when OSM says it is a drive, a car park's aisle or a drive-through (`service=driveway|parking_aisle|drive-through`); otherwise it keeps its mouth as a street does. Length and where it leads were weighed and left out: an unnamed lane joining two streets through a yard or a car park still meets them as a drive does in a Nordic town, its mouth kerbed across the sidewalk. On the two Mariehamn tiles that makes a few dozen named service ways streets; some 800 drives, 190 aisles and 800 unnamed service ways stay drives.
- **A junction's corner stops at a third way between its two.** A drive's corners with the street it joins were rounded as if nothing lay between them, over the cycleway running along the street, and what poked out past the cycleway's far edge was a pale sliver 0.28 m wide along it (Godbyvägen). A part of a corner with more than half its area under a third way is that way's and is dropped; the rest is cut off at that way's outline, and a part thinner than 0.1 m left over is dropped too.
- **A loop closing at a corner keeps each end's own direction.** A way that closes on itself took the bisector of its two ends as both ends' direction, which a roundabout needs so no notch shows where it closes; a loop street closing where a stem joins it, at a corner, took it too, so Handelslänken's first station ran 50 degrees off the street, across was measured on that skewed line, and the carriageway's colour bit into the kerb in a jagged wedge. Only a roundabout, or a loop whose ends turn less than 45 degrees across the closing station (`CLOSING_MOST_TURN` in `pipeline/roads.py`), runs on through where it closes; the island's smooth loops turn up to about 40, its cornered ones 60 or more. The rule is the ribbon build's too, so it reaches every tile when the island is rebuilt.
- **Posts keep off a crossing's line, whoever owns the paving there.** The roadside kept off a crossing because its paving was the path's carriageway; once the street's kerb and walk band owned that stretch, it read as a motor road's clear shoulder, and crossing signs stood on the crosswalk itself, a metre past the kerb (on L2334E and L2334F, 5 → 76 signs within 0.6 m of a crossing's line, 227 → 294 in all), with a town lamp in Stava Mostersvägen's mouth onto Hamngatan, where a crossing runs along the sidewalk's line. `read_surface` now keeps each crossing's own stretch off, its way's width along its line in the tile's `roads.json`, as a piece no chain owns: 226 signs, 1 within 0.6 m, and the four town lamps gone.
- **The carriageway's edge is one number, with room for how it's stored.** A point on a carriageway's edge is written a hair past it: the outline is kept within 2 cm, which over a shoulder of a metre or so is up to a few hundredths of across, and across is rounded to 1/1024. Read as "every corner at 1 or less", the motor roads' carriageways on L2334E and L2334F counted 63,716 and 104,339 triangles where they have 120,249 and 203,837, the rest reading as shoulder, so where no ring covers the carriageway (a side street's mouth) the roadside could stand things on it. The edge is now `render/carriageway.json`'s 1.03, just past where the stored edge points thin out into the shoulder's own, read by `pipeline/roadside.py` and the game alike: a triangle is carriageway when none of its corners is past it. On the two Mariehamn tiles the roadside lost what stood on those stretches: lindens 1,251 → 1,186, town lamps 2,973 → 2,958, edge posts 773 → 771, a street lamp and a crossing sign, and 324 of the walls the car hits; two landmark visitors moved, and the parked cars and the seated are unchanged.
- **A tyre is on what the paving is drawn as** (`sim/road-grip.ts`). A triangle is read at its middle: a class by each of its bits, as its look blends. A motor road's shoulder grips as the gravel verge, except on a sealed street in town, where the shoulder is a kerb and paving and grips as asphalt, whatever class its points carry: the road draws a shoulder over a class, and a junction's corner beside a sidewalk takes the sidewalk's slabs from its nearest way on some points, which read as asphalt under a tyre on what was drawn as the verge. Elsewhere a way wearing its own surface grips as that surface (gravel as gravel, slabs, setts, steps and tarmac as asphalt), a motor road's carriageway as its asphalt or gravel, and the cut ground as the ground. A chain runs on through a junction into a path, so a path continuing a track or a street grips as its own way's surface, not its chain's.
- **A piece no way owns carries on the shoulders round it.** A junction's corner, or a gap closed between ways, was drawn as one flat shoulder at 1.5 across, with no fade and the kerb's paving joints standing still, between shoulders that run from 1 at the carriageway's edge to 2 at the paving's and fade to the land past about 1.65: its straight sides are those shoulders' outer edges, so every corner stood out as a flat, lighter curved triangle (3,299 corner pieces on L2334E). Its points now take across from distance (`shoulder_across`): 1 and their distance from the nearest motor road's carriageway in that road's own shoulders, as its shoulder's points are measured, and at least 2 less their distance from the paving's outline in the same, as a shoulder fades at its edge. A corner past the shoulders is at their outer edge's 2, faded to the land as they are, and meets each at its own across. A piece wearing a path's surface keeps the path's own across, and a car park, a carriageway of its own, keeps a carriageway's. The way's own points are untouched. The roadside told such pieces by their 1.5; the paving file now lists their points after the ways' own and before the car parks', and the manifest says how many (`junctionPoints`).
- **Paving off the ground is cut as the ground's is.** An underpass's floor and a bridge's deck were cut only along their outlines, so a triangle ran from a road's centre to its shoulder's edge, and across, which the edge line is drawn at, was blended over the whole width rather than turning at the carriageway's edge: the edge line came in by a jog wherever a road went under a deck (Sundsvägen at Godby, under its footway), and stood off the carriageway on every bridge. Both levels are now cut along every way's carriageway and centre line too, and merged as an underpass's floor was, so two ways meeting on a bridge are one deck.
- **Each way's rings stand on its own level.** What places things beside a way (the roadside, the lanes) reads its rings, whose heights were read from whichever paving a point found first: under a deck or over an underpass, the floor's or the deck's. Each now reads the paving nearest its own surface there.
- **Godby's "footbridge" is a tunnel, and stays an underpass for now.** OSM tags 54 m of Sundsvägen (road 2) `tunnel=yes` where a track and a path cross 6–12 m over it, so it is laid as an underpass: the road runs between concrete walls as tall as that under the paths' thin decks, with upright faces of cut ground up to 12.8 m at its portals. The survey there is no cutting: across the road's whole width and 40 m either side, the ground stands at 37–40 m for some 30 m of road at 29 m, a hill over a short tunnel, and the track runs along the tunnel's roof for 15 m before the path meets it. Laid as a bridge over a cutting (the tagged road on the ground, each way over it held up as a span as far as the cutting's bank would pass under it), the track became a 40 m bridge running along the road, with the bridge kit's piers standing in the cutting: worse than the walls. Drawing it as it is needs a tunnel the game doesn't have: the hill left as the survey has it, a bore with walls and a roof, and portals cut into the ground.
- **No way is drawn as grass** (`WAY_SURFACES` in `pipeline/path_surfaces.py`). A path or a street's sidewalk tagged `surface=grass` (or `grass_paver`) wore the town ground's lawn, which read as more grass where the owner wants a path; before the path looks it was a gravel track. A way is trodden or driven, so such a way is gravel, and grips as gravel: 113, 752, 1,557 and 778 points of lawn on L2343H, L3121B, L2334E and L2334F before, none now. The town's own lawns and flower beds keep their classes; no way's tags ever gave a bed.
- **A gravel path out of town is a gravel road's gravel.** The path looks drew every gravel path as a park's raked gravel, with no tracks and a ruled edge, where a country path had worn like a gravel road, in two tracks with loose stone beside them and fading into the land at its sides. A gravel path now wears the road's gravel out of town and the park's in town, blended by how built up the place is (the same reading that gives a sealed street its kerb), so Mariehamn's paths keep theirs.
- **A gravel path wears into the land at its edge.** A ribbon path's shoulder faded it into the ground beside it; on paving a path has no shoulder, so its gravel stopped on a ruled line against the cut ground, flat beside the ribbon build's worn path (the dirt path on the bank above Sjöpromenaden, `bank-path` in the review set). Its gravel now thins into the ground's own colour over its outer third, by a broken line, and is the ground at its edge, so it meets the cut ground with no line. Where it meets other paving, a junction's corner faded to the land, it blends into that too. Slabs, tarmac and setts keep their edge.
- **The photo confirmed the geometry rather than improving it.** Closing gaps narrower than 3 m and a fixed shoulder per class looked like guesses, so a trial read the aerial photo at its full 0.5 m for where the ground is really hard: gaps paved only where the photo shows them hard, and each road's edge as wide as its hard ground. The owner saw almost no difference, and the narrower edges lost kerb paving the owner liked, so it was dropped. No open source measures Åland's street widths either: Digiroad's are the NLS road classes turned into constants, and the NLS class files most town streets as 4–5 m by rule.

### Trying it

On the two Mariehamn tiles, L2334E and L2334F, which hold most of the owner's examples and one underpass, built beside the current build rather than over it, and judged on the same cameras:

1. **The pipeline alone.** The paved shape, its levels, the blended heights and the embankment rule, written as tile data with the cut cells, with tests for what's easy to break: a point of paving has one height per level, the paving and the cut cells share their edge points, and two tiles agree along their edge.
2. **The game.** The clipmap skips cut cells and draws their triangles; the collision blocks follow; the road shader draws the paving.
3. **The owner's views,** before and after, and the far-ring change checked while driving.

**The review set.** Every change to the paving is shot from the same cameras the owner has judged before: `tools/road-views.json` names each view and the tile it's on, and `tools/road-views.mjs` shoots them all at one pinned date and hour, from a tile server of its own, or with `--before=<tiles folder>` from two builds side by side. A camera the owner judges a change from joins the file.

Only if the trial reads clean on all three does the old system get deleted and the island rebuilt. If step 1 shows a flaw in the design, the design is fixed here before step 2.

### Risks

- **The far rings.** Cut cells that turn back into plain grid at a distance might show as a change while driving. The fallback is a wider exact zone for the innermost ring.
- **Tile edges.** Paving cut at a tile's edge must be cut identically from both sides, from data both tiles see; a cut that differs shows as a crack.
- **Paving over water.** A causeway's embankments run into the water, which is drawn on the grid's triangles, not the cut cells'. The shore there may need the cut cells' own water line.
- **Odd OSM.** A footprint merged from bad data (a way drawn twice, a sliver between two lines) becomes odd paving. Today's layers hide some of it by accident; the new system shows it, and each case is fixed in how the shape is made.

## Getting it to players

Traffic, cyclists and walkers drive the lane map, which follows the drawn road surface, and road lanes must carry their traffic counts (`pipeline/traffic_volume.py`; `traffic/lane-map.ts` refuses a lane without one). The published island has both: `build.py --island --paving` runs the lanes over every tile, so one rebuild and one publish brought paving and lanes together. A later publish goes the same way: the island built, `pipeline/check_seams.py`, `pipeline/upload.py`, `TILES_VERSION` in `render/tile-address.ts` set to what it prints, main pushed, the game checked live on the published tiles, and `pipeline/upload.py --clear-old`.
