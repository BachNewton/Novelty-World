# Åland Drive: the car

The simulation: the car, its grip, its collisions and the world it drives on. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down.

`sim/` is the simulation, with no renderer in it, so it runs in tests as it does in the page. Each car's numbers are in a file of its own, `sim/cars/<id>.ts`, registered in `sim/cars.ts`, the shape they take and the world's own in `sim/tuning.ts`; the cameras' are at the top of `render/chase-camera.ts`.

- **A rigid body on four rays** (`sim/car.ts`): each wheel's ray finds the surface, a spring holds the body up, and the tyre pushes on the body at the contact, so weight transfer, squat, dive and lean all come from the same forces. Rapier's own ray-cast vehicle isn't used: its tyres are one clamped friction, with no slip curve to shape a handbrake slide or the grip at 200 km/h.
- **The cars, each its own** (`sim/cars.ts`, the registry, with each car in `sim/cars/<id>.ts`). A car is its id (`CarId`), and everything about it follows from that: where its axles, track and wheels are, its weight and the height of its centre of mass, its springs, dampers and anti-roll bars, its tyres' grip front and rear and how they let go, its engine's torque curve, its gears, how its drive splits front to rear, its brakes, drag and downforce, and how far and fast its wheels steer. The Skerry GT is written out in full. The rest are each a character, the few numbers a driver knows a car by (its mass, ride, grip, drive, steering speed, engine and gears), and the springs, dampers, inertia, forces and collision box follow from those by one set of rules (`sim/cars/character.ts`), so a new car is a character and not fifty numbers. **A car is its own files**: its tuning (`sim/cars/<id>.ts`), its measured limits (`sim/limits/<id>.json`), its art (`art/cars/models/<id>.ts`) and its voice (`audio/voices/<id>.ts`), each with one line in that kind's registry, so cars are built and reworked side by side without touching each other. **Each car has a tier** beside its tuning, outside the tuning print, with its maker, its engine in a few words and where it comes from, which the Cars page shows (`components/CLAUDE.md`), from Test Drive Unlimited 2's scheme (`ideas/cars.md`): A asphalt, B gravel, C classic, or none. A tier is a band of time to 100 km/h (on dry gravel in B, dry asphalt in A and C) and top speed on dry asphalt (`sim/tiers.ts`), and `sim/tiers.test.ts` holds every tiered car inside its band as measured, but for the few named there as still to be retuned. The registries list the cars by tier. The axles sit where the art has them, about the centre of mass, so the weight splits between them as the body is drawn, and each axle's springs are matched to its share of that weight, so every car sits level. `topSpeedMS` (`sim/gearbox.ts`) works a car's top speed out from its pull and drag, which is what each top gear is set against.
- **An engine and a gearbox, automatic or manual** (`sim/gearbox.ts`): from one gear (the electric car) to eight, and the pull at the road is the torque curve at the revs, through the gear, cut for the moment of a shift. A closed throttle brakes on the engine, or on regeneration in the electric car. The handbrake holds the clutch in, so it always beats the engine. Nothing caps the speed: a car stops gaining where drag meets the engine, and top gear's redline is past that, except the electric car's single gear, whose motor runs out at 230 km/h. Shifting is the player's choice on Settings' Gameplay tab, automatic to start and kept in this browser (`garage/shifting.ts`), the same in every car and kept through a swap: in manual the box never shifts by itself, the revs run to the limiter, and each shift the player asks for is made once the last is done, a downshift that would put the revs past the redline being refused; reverse is first's gear, from the brake as in the automatic. The racers and every measured table are automatic. The revs follow the driven wheels, not the car's speed, so a spinning tyre takes them to the limiter; the box shifts by the wheels too once the car is under way, and a car spinning its wheels on the spot stays in first. The sound hears these same gears and revs, and speed, gear and revs share one dial at the bottom right.
- **Each wheel turns at its own speed** (`sim/car.ts`). While a tyre grips, its tread turns with the road and gives what's asked of it up to its grip, along and across sharing it. Asked for more along than it grips, a driven wheel spins up, against the engine's flywheel through the gear as well as its own weight, and a braked one slows to a lock; then the tyre's slip along and across share one curve, so a spinning or locked tyre grips less either way, and a locked front steers nothing. `tools/handling.ts` measures it: flat out against a wall, launching, stopping, braking into a bend, holding a corner and a swerve, with each aid on and off, on any surface.
- **The driver's aids** are the player's, all on until turned off in the game menu, and kept in this browser (`garage/assists.ts`), as in Forza Horizon: ABS keeps a braked tyre at its grip rather than lock it, traction control keeps a driven one at its grip and holds the pull to what the car puts down off the line (`launchN`, or a cautious `looseLaunchN` on gravel and grass for a car that has one, as Myr does), and stability is the yaw assist below. With all three on the car is as it was tuned, and every measured table (the cars' limits in `sim/limits/`, the challenges' targets, the races' laps) is measured so. Brakes are tuned near the tyres' grip, so on a dry road ABS matters most braking into a bend; on loose or slippery ground the brakes lock a wheel easily without it.
- **Steering narrows with speed** to the angle that asks for about all the grip the tyres have, plus some room for the slip angle they grip best at: a tyre only pushes sideways once it runs at an angle to where it rolls, and without that room the front tyres never reached their grip at speed. And above town speeds, whatever the speed allows, the fronts turn no further past the way they're going than that best angle (slower, a hairpin needs its lock before the car has turned to it): braking into a bend, the lock the speed allows opens out as the car slows, and past it the tyres ploughed on, so that full lock turned the car less than half. The yaw assist that steadies the car asks for no faster a turn than the tyres can give at that speed. The fronts grip a little less than the rears, so each car understeers gently at its limit.
- **Grip by surface and by condition** (`sim/surfaces.ts`, the reasoning in `ideas/surface-grip.md`). Each wheel is on what its ray hit: the collision blocks know each triangle, and a road triangle is what it's drawn as (`sim/road-grip.ts`), read at its middle: a road's carriageway is asphalt or gravel, its shoulder past the carriageway's edge a gravel verge, or asphalt on a sealed town street, whose shoulder is its kerb and paving, and a way that wears its own surface (a path, a sidewalk, setts) is that surface's, gravel as gravel (a path OSM tags grass is gravel too: no way is drawn as a lawn), and slabs, setts, steps and tarmac as asphalt. The carriageway's edge is one number the game and `pipeline/roadside.py` share (`render/carriageway.json`): the paving stores its edge's points a hair past it. The ground, the paving's cut ground included, is grass, or rock where the map has bedrock. A car over the road's edge grips on two wheels and slides on two. The condition (dry, wet, snow, ice) is every surface's at once, except on sea ice that bears a car, which is ice whatever the weather, so each wheel reads its own (`Underfoot.groundUnder`); it is set by the shared weather (`climate/`, see `docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`), or held by `?condition=` in a link or the dev menu's Ground until a reload; a tool's or a test's page stays dry unless it asks with `?weather`. A surface in a condition reshapes the car's own tyre curve rather than replacing it (its peak grip, the slip it peaks at, how much it keeps past the peak, and the rolling that soft ground adds, more with speed), so every car keeps its character on every ground. The order is real's and the gaps are squeezed, as in the open-world driving games, so off the tarmac is slower and looser but still drivable at speed. Each car also has a class, what it was built for (sports, road, rally or off-road), one field of its description as in Test Drive Unlimited 2, which sets how each surface and condition treats it: a sports car wins on asphalt and comes last on gravel, grass and snow, where the rally car and the four-by-fours win. A tuning shop could later move a car between classes, as Forza Horizon's tyres and The Crew's kits do. Each car also runs on the tyres the player fitted (summer, winter friction or studded), one more row reshaping the curve again: summer tyres on snow or ice are clearly worse and looser but still drive, studs are the best on ice and a little worse on a bare road, and `sim/tyres.test.ts` holds that order for every class on every surface. Tyres are the player's choice, not the law's: a field of the car, set from one choice kept in this browser for every car (`garage/tyres.ts`, the game menu's tyre choices, outside a challenge), or `?tyres=` in a link for the drive. While tyres are a menu setting and cars are swapped freely, tyres kept per car only read as the choice being forgotten; once a home's garage or a car shop fits tyres, they become each car's. They aren't in `CarState`, since another player's car is a stand-in with no grip of its own. Dry asphalt on summer tyres is every car exactly as tuned, whatever its class, a test holding it to the last digit. The steering's lock and the yaw assist ask for the grip under the wheels, so full lock on ice asks for an ice corner; brakes and throttle are capped by the grip there already. The sound and the tyre particles read the same wheels.
- **What comes off the tyres** (`render/tyre-particles.ts`, the rules in `render/tyre-kicks.ts`): each wheel's tread (where it touched, how fast it rolled and slid, and how far past its grip it was asked) is read off the simulation without changing it, and decides what that wheel throws up: dust and stones on gravel, clods and torn grass on grass, grit on rock, spray on anything wet, powder in snow and frost off ice, chips of ice under studs, some by rolling over loose or wet ground and more by sliding, spinning or locking. A dry road throws nothing until a tyre is past its grip, and then smoke. Only the player's car has them. A particle is written once, when it's thrown on a simulation step, and its flight (drag, gravity, a solid bit stopping at the ground) is worked out in the vertex shader from its age on the simulation's clock, so nothing moves on the CPU and a held page stepped by hand throws exactly what a drive would. Two fixed pools are two draw calls, soft puffs and small solid bits, neither drawn while nothing is in the air; a puff is never wider than about a tenth of the screen and fades as it nears the eye, since their cost is overdraw. Their colours are `tyre-*` tokens and the ground's own. The probe counts them (`tyreCounts`) and can leave them out (`showTyres`).
- **Nothing but the car is drawn inside it** (`art/car-volume.ts`): through the glass, a tuft it rolls over, a puff of its own dust or a tree it drives through would seem to fill the cabin. So the player's car is two boxes in its own frame, from its drawn art (the body up to where the glass starts, and the glass's bounds over the cabin), and every material that has a mask already (the particles, the plantings, the forest, and the kits' roadside, street, rural, people, boats and signs) drops its pixels inside them. Being in the mask adds no `discard` to a material that had none, so the GPU's early depth test is kept; the place in the car's frame is worked out per vertex. A material with no mask (terrain, water, buildings, landmarks) doesn't take it: the car can't drive into any of those, except water, which the car sinks into.
- **Swapping cars mid-drive** (`Drive.setCar`) builds the new car's body where the old one was, raised or lowered by the difference in their ride heights, moving as it moved, in the gear a gentle drive would be in at that speed.
- **More than one simulated car** (`Drive.addCar`): racers, and traffic once it's hit, are full cars beside the player's in the same Rapier world, each from its own input, touching each other and other players' stand-ins, with the ground loaded round every one (see `ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`). A car added elsewhere leaves the player's drive exactly as it was.
- **A fixed step of 120 Hz**, with no clock in the simulation: the same inputs play out the same. Each frame draws the car between its last two steps.
- **Rapier runs round the physics centre** (`sim/physics-centre.ts`, the reasoning in `ideas/floating-origin.md`), not the world's origin. Rapier keeps positions as 32-bit floats, whose steps grow with the distance from its origin, and it moves a body in whole jumps of a few of them a step, so out at 22 km a car crawling or reversing moved in jerks of nearly 1 m/s. So Rapier's origin is a place in whole metres near the player's car: every place handed to Rapier is the world's less the centre and every one read back is plus it, in the four files that talk to Rapier and nowhere else, so the car's state and everything outside the simulation stay in world coordinates. Once the player's car is more than a kilometre from it the centre moves to the car, every body and collider moving with it, after the step's wheel rays and before the world steps (a ray finds a moved collider only once the world has stepped). A move is exact and on the same steps every run, so nothing jumps and the simulation stays deterministic. The collision blocks are built from their own corners, so the ground is as precise anywhere as at home, and a move shifts them rather than building them again. Other simulated cars share the one centre. `Drive.castDown` is how a test or tool asks what the ground is at a place, since Rapier's own coordinates aren't the world's.
- **The car's state** (`CarState`, `sim/car-state.ts`) is one small record that survives JSON, and the car is drawn from it and nothing else, so it is also what other players are sent (see `net/CLAUDE.md`). It names its car by id, so a car heard over the network draws and rides as the one being driven. The drawing is `art/cars` (`createCarView`): a drivable car's art takes its name, axles, track, wheels and ride height from `sim/cars.ts`, so a change there moves the art too and every car sits on its wheels where the simulation has them, while its collision box is the simulation's own, held to the drawn body by `art/cars/cars.test.ts`. The car casts no shadow of its own. Its lamps are in `CarState` (headlamps, on whenever a car is driven, as Finnish and Åland law has them, brake and reverse), so another player's car lights as it brakes and backs up, and so is its paint, its driver's choice by its index in the car's list (`art/cars/paints.ts`): the simulation only carries it, as a field of the car kept until changed, so a paint changed while paused is in the state at once.
- **The end of the world** is a wall with a low coloured band on it, on every side of a tile with no tile beyond. **Water** has a seabed the pipeline lays under it, so a car that leaves the shore sinks into it, and it is dragged to a stop once it's past the drawn waterline, by the same test the picture uses. Nothing ever puts the car back by itself, in water or on its roof: a reset is always the player's, with R or Y. A road over water doesn't count as water. **Sea ice** that bears a car (`sim/sea-ice.ts`: water more sheltered than the season's `iceDrivable`) is ground: the collision blocks lie at the water's surface there instead of on the seabed (`World.drivenNode`), nothing drags, and its wheels are on ice whatever the weather. Thinner ice lets the car sink as open water does, and `sim/sea-ice.test.ts` holds both.
- **Buildings, stone walls, boulders, windmills, tractors, bales and cows stop the car; trees, outcrops and sheep don't.** A building's outline is a band of wall triangles in the same collision blocks, 6 m tall, from the pipeline's outlines, so the car never climbs a roof and a `sim/world.test.ts` case holds that it can't pass one at top speed. Every building has walls, open only where a road OSM tags as a passage goes through it, as `pipeline/give_way.py` decides (see `pipeline/CLAUDE.md`), which each tile's `solids.roadsThrough` counts: a building drawn on a road the car could drive through is the one thing this rules out. A raised building (`pipeline/raised.py`) has none at the ground: only its posts stop the car. A cow's or a bale's walls leave with it out of season (`seasonalWalls`, `climate/placed-season.ts`), so the car never meets one that isn't drawn. A boulder is a ring of eight such walls round its foot, a quarter in from its widest (`modelWalls`, `render/tile-models.ts`), and a test holds that it stops the car at top speed too. So is a windmill, round its trestle out to its stone pads, with a wall along its steps, and a tractor, a bale or a cow is the box of its foot, as a parked car is, since a cow is the size and weight of a small car: a mill and a bale, the smallest, each have a case at top speed. A sheep isn't: nothing in the game can knock one over or shove it aside, a sheep that stopped the car dead would read worse than one passed through, as a mailbox would, and it grazes away from the roads in any case. Trees have no collision: they are a shader's, with no list to collide with. Nor do granite outcrops: the rock's own domes and steps are already the ground the car drives on, and an outcrop only lifts that by a few decimetres of skin. Nor do a boulder field's small stones, a shader's like the trees: a field's biggest stones are listed boulders, which do. A stone wall is both faces of each of its drawn sections, as tall as a building's: the car's box rides 0.4 m off the ground, so a wall only as tall as its stones overlaps it by a hand's width and at speed the solver lifts the car over it rather than stopping it, which another case holds. Power poles, street lamps, guard rails and bus shelters stop the car too, because a car at 200 km/h passing through a wooden pole or a steel mast reads as broken: the roadside list carries their outlines as walls, a pole's a square of 0.36 m, which the tile's walls take in, and a `sim/world.test.ts` case holds that the car can't pass a pole at top speed either. A parked car stops it too, as a ring of four walls round its body, the size of the world car the game picks for its place (`parkedWalls`, `render/parked-places.ts`), and a case holds that as well. Sign posts, edge posts and mailboxes don't: real ones are made to break away or bend, and with nothing in the game that breaks, a car stopped dead by a 3 cm post would be worse than one passing through. Street furniture splits the same way: a bench and a bike rack are iron anchored in the ground, so their outlines are walls in the roadside list, while a bin on its post, café tables and chairs and parasols are what a real car knocks flying, and stop nothing. The town dressing too: a linden's trunk, a flag or midsummer pole, a planter and a viewing tower stop the car, and a fence, a picnic table and a viewpoint platform's light timber don't.
- **What the car collides with is what's drawn** (`sim/ground.ts`): the same triangles of ground and road, in 120 m blocks on one lattice for the whole world, three by three round every simulated car, each laid from its own corner (see the physics centre above). A triangle mesh rather than Rapier's heightfield, so the diagonals are the render's by construction; `sim/drive.test.ts` holds the two copies together. A block doesn't know which tile its heights and roads came from, so the car feels nothing at a tile's edge, and `sim/world.test.ts` pins that.
- **The world** (`sim/world.ts`) is all the land the game knows, with no GPU in it, for the simulation and the terrain alike. Every tile has far heights from the start, and those near the car have detail. Ground with no detail yet answers from the far heights, for what's drawn and what's driven on alike, so the car can't find a hole, only coarser ground with no roads.
