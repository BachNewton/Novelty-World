# Åland Drive art

**Art is built to fit the game, not its gallery.** A gallery shows a model on its own; the game places it hundreds of times across the island, at every distance, beside everything else on that ground. So before a model is built, say how the game will place it (from what data, as one list or per tile, how it fits a length or a size) and what it stands among, and build it to that. Art made only to look right in a gallery scene ends up replaced when it reaches the game: the harbour's first piers, pontoons and moored boats were.

**Integrating art corrects the kits.** It's where art first meets the real data, so:

- **Art that can't be used is deleted** in the same change that finds it: a piece superseded by a better choice, the wrong shape for what the data holds, or a duplicate of another. Its gallery entry, tests, exports and inventory row go with it, so nothing stays behind as a gallery-only leftover. Only art in the change's own scope goes, never art that planned work still needs (riders for cyclists, cars for traffic and racers, people for walkers), and the owner decides when that's in doubt.
- **Art that's missing is built**, not worked around: a kind of thing the data has and no kit draws, or a piece that doesn't fit where it has to stand. Each missing piece is a subagent's task, with the kit it belongs in, the real thing it must match and that kit's conventions, and it's reviewed on the kit's gallery before it's placed. New art gets its inventory row, and a credit for any third-party design it reproduces.

**No two faces share a plane.** Two faces in one plane fight for its pixels, a flicker that shows only as the camera moves, so no screenshot or gallery review catches it. `faces.slow.test.ts` checks every model of every kit, near and far, from its kit's `catalogue.ts` (`artModels`, which a kit with a gallery must have): a pair counts where a camera can see it, from above the ground and through open air, so faces under the ground or inside a model don't. New art has none. Where two faces meet, lift one 1–2 cm or cut the hidden one away. Art from before the check keeps what it had, listed in `known-overlaps.json`, which a fix brings down and nothing raises. A model that stands on another or on the ground fights there too, which no one model shows: that is the placement's to keep clear.

**A far version keeps its near version's shape and colour.** At its swap a model's far version stands in for its near one, and anything that changes there, an outline, a colour, a part that goes, is noticed as a pop however good each looks alone: a flat-headed daisy that turns to pointed tips, a daymark that changes its paint. So the far version is the near one simplified, read at the size the swap leaves it, and never a different object. A change has two cures: a later swap, which shrinks it on screen for the near version's cost over more ground, or a far version that matches, which costs nothing to draw. A far version that is a different object has to be remade, since no sensible swap hides it.

**Where a model swaps is measured, never chosen.** `lod-check/check.mjs` draws every model of a kit that says how it's drawn (`drawn` in its catalogue) near and far, on the kit's own materials, at the size a swap gives it on the floor's screen and from all round, and judges the change by two established measures (`NOTICED`, `lod-check/page.ts`): how far the outline moves, in pixels, as Simplygon judges a level of detail, and how different the two look to a person at the floor's viewing distance, by NVIDIA's FLIP (`lod-check/flip.ts`). FLIP is judged on the whole model and at its worst spot, a patch a few pixels across, since a part that goes outright but is a small share of the model, a car's wheels and lights, hardly moves the whole's mean. The limits are set where art matched by hand and accepted in the game stays and where the owner has seen a swap: the cars' stand-ins seen at the swaps the mean alone gave them and accepted at the swap they had before, and `check.mjs --score` reads a model's measures at any swap to set them again. A change only shrinks with distance, so it finds each model's nearest swap at which the change isn't noticed, from the nearest its picture holds to most of its reach, and `--write` puts it in `lod-check/swaps.json` under a fingerprint of the model's two versions. Every kit's model constructor reads that table (`lod-swaps.ts`), so the game swaps each model where it was measured, and the swap a kit writes is only for art the check can't draw. The check fails while the table disagrees with the art, so a changed model is measured again with `--write`. It is run by hand, not by the test suite, whenever a kit's art or `swaps.json` changes: `npm run slot -- e2e -- node src/projects/aland-drive/art/lod-check/check.mjs` (the `e2e` slot, for its four GPU pages; a full run takes about a quarter of an hour). It prints what each moved swap costs, the near version's extra triangles over the ground its swap now covers, and ends every run by ranking the models by what their swap costs as it stands, the near version's extra triangles times the swap squared: the top of that list is where a far version that matches better pays most. A costly swap is cured by a far version that matches better, never by a number. A model no swap up to most of its reach hides is in `lod-check/known-lod.json`, whose far version has to be remade, and that list may only get better. The one exception is the terminal: it is nearly as long as its swap is far, and the check draws its near and far versions at different distances, so perspective alone moves its outline past the limit even when its far version is a copy of its near one. It stays on the list until the check can judge a model that size. A model's swap is for its own size: the plantings (verge, reeds, bushes and birches, granite) multiply it by each place's size, and `render/measured-swaps.test.ts` holds that every model they plant has been measured. Where a kit staggers its places' swaps so a row doesn't swap on one line, each swaps at or past its model's swap, never nearer. The world cars draw their versions their own way and read the table themselves (`modelSwapM`, `cars/fleet.ts`), and so do the deer, each kind measured in every gait and swapping at the furthest (`deerSwapM`, `deer/herd.ts`). The drivable cars, people, homes, dealers and the landmarks draw theirs their own way and aren't in it yet, nor are the forest's far crowns, and a roadside place's own scale (a linden's height) doesn't yet scale its swap.

**A drivable car comes in paints, the player's choice.** Each car's spec lists its paints, four to eight, the first its default (`cars/paints.ts`): a colour token (`--color-aland-drive-car-paint-*` in `globals.css`, the one place a paint's colour comes from) and a finish, and for a car whose body has stripes, their colour, which makes a livery of them. Adding a paint is one line in the car's spec and, for a new colour, one token; a car's list only grows at its end, because a paint travels to other players as its index in it. The finish is how the coat takes the light: solid, a deep gloss that carries the horizon along the doors; metallic, a tighter, brighter highlight, a reflection tinted by the paint, and flakes that lift at a glancing angle and glint round the sun's highlight, in cells of the car's own metres that fade out before they're smaller than a couple of pixels; matte, a soft, wide sheen with no horizon in it; pearl, a gloss whose colour turns to a flip colour at a glancing angle. One car's geometry is still shared by every car of its kind: the colour, finish and stripes are uniforms of each drawn car's material, one shader for every paint, and the near and far models take the same ones, so a swap keeps the paint. A world car keeps its one paint, solid, from its geometry. `cars/gallery.mjs --paints` shoots a car in every paint, by day and night, near and far, on one sheet a car: the sheet a car's colour set is judged on.

`docs/ART.md` is the inventory: every kit, and whether it has been placed and seen in the game.
