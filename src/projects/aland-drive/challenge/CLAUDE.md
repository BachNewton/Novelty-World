# Åland Drive: challenges and races

Bucket list challenges, co-op and races. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down.

Bucket list challenges, as `ideas/bucket-list.md` decides them: two kinds, a Route against the clock through gates in order and a Speed trap's camera, each one car, one place on the real roads and one target, passed or failed. Solo and co-op for two are built, the co-op with its synced quick retry and the average. **The system is the foundation; the list on it is still being found**: the first twelve were too short and beaten at the first try, and the second list, longer, with targets from a driver that brakes late (`sim/autopilot.ts`'s speed plan) and every car suited to its road, hasn't been played yet. Neither is a standard for new ones. A challenge is played in the weather of the moment, though its targets were measured dry. Co-op passes its tests, but the owner hasn't played it.

- **The rules** are `challenge/`, with no renderer in it. `specs.ts` is the hand-written list, `places.json` and `targets.json` what `tools/challenge-places.ts` and `tools/challenge-par.ts` measured from it on the built tiles (start places, gates or camera, par and target), joined in `list.ts`. `run.ts` is one attempt as a pure state machine: the countdown, each line found by intersecting a car's path between two steps with it, results and the outcome, for one player or two. `line.ts` is a challenge's route as the GPS's own routes between its waypoints, the one line its gates, starts, par and the GPS during a run all come from.
- **A session** (`challenge/session.ts`) is one challenge played on this client, start to leave: the run, this client's seat on the start line, the car the player had before, and whether the car is still on its way to the line, which the run waits for. Every restart goes through its `retry` and a partner's measured result through its `report`, so the network drives a co-op run through the same doors as the menu does a solo one. It never moves the car itself: the game, its host, swaps the car (`Drive.setCar`) and puts it on the line.
- **The board** (`challenge/board.ts`) is what a drive knows of them: the list and any challenge of the page's own (a test's), those whose start is in this world, which are passed with their best, solo and co-op kept apart (`passes.ts`, kept in this browser with `getProjectStorage` as finds are, the maps showing a pass in either), and whose start ring the car is in, 12 m from the middle of its two start places.
- **On the maps** every start in the world is a rounded square, a chequered flag for a Route and a dial for a Speed trap, in the challenge colour, and the pass colour once passed (`drawChallenges`, `map/draw.ts`). The world map names them when zoomed in to streets, names one under the cursor, and a pick on one routes the GPS to its ring, as a place does. During a run the mini map also draws the lines still to cross as bars across the road (`drawGates`): the next one bold in the challenge colour, the later ones pale, the finish in the pass colour.
- **In the world** (`render/challenge-marks.ts`) each start is a ring of short posts with a tall pale beam over it, marking it from kilometres off, and during a run only the next line to cross stands: an arch over the road, the finish's banner chequered, or a dark gantry with its camera for a Speed trap. All boxes and prisms, flat shaded, in the challenge's own colours: the posts and beams are one instanced list each and the line one small mesh rebuilt when it changes, three draw calls and a few thousand triangles in all. Each stands on the ground as the world has it, read again as tiles sharpen it.
- **Starting.** In a ring the HUD prompts the challenge's own button (X, or E). With no one else in the world it starts the challenge solo at once; with anyone else in the world, wherever they are, it opens a choice of Solo or Co-op, and Co-op invites everyone (see Co-op). Starting swaps to the challenge's car and puts it on the first start place, the solo one, and the countdown's three seconds run with the car held on the handbrake and the player's input ignored until green. During a run the HUD shows the lights, the clock against the target and the gates passed (a Route) or the speed to reach (a Speed trap), the next line stands in the world, and the GPS holds the challenge's route, never rerouting from it, since its gates are on it. R or Y still puts the car back on the road, and a car put somewhere crosses no line on its way.
- **The HUD in a run** is one panel at the top middle, the distance still to go, to the finish line or the camera rather than the route's end, which can run on past them, with the challenge's title, lights, clock against the target and gates under it, with the road's name and a find's card below, clear of the frame meter at the top right.
- **The menu in a run** holds Resume, Retry and Leave challenge. Solo it pauses, and the run's clock only moves with the simulation's steps, so a paused run stands still. In co-op it doesn't pause, the result card neither: the clock and both cars run on, and the car's controls let go while it's open. When an attempt is over the menu is the result card: passed or failed, the time or speed against the target and the best kept, Retry first and Leave on B and Esc.
- **Retry** puts the car back on its start place at once where the start's tiles are still loaded, and otherwise goes back the way a dev menu jump does, loading round the start with the first load's bar and putting the car down once it has landed: the start is never kept loaded through a run, since a short load is better than a run that draws worse. **Leaving** gives back the car the player had before, where the car is now, except for a player who joined a co-op invite: the invite took them from wherever they were, so leaving puts them back there, on the nearest road facing the way they faced, loading round it like a jump if it has gone.
- **Co-op** (`challenge/coop.ts`) is exactly two players over the online world's messages (`net/protocol.ts`), each naming the challenge, the attempt and, through the host, the sender. Pairing is a world-wide invite, then accepts, then start. Co-op sends the invite to everyone in the world, and to anyone who arrives while it's open. Every invited player's HUD shows it wherever they are, who asked and which challenge or race, and prompts their challenge button, which opens Join or Decline; ignoring it is the default and holds nothing up. A player in a challenge of their own, or in co-op, holds an invite unseen and sees it once free, if it's still open; of several, the newest shows. The first accept the inviter hears takes seat 1, and the inviter's start goes to everyone, which withdraws the invite everywhere else at once; a player who joined too late hears it was taken, and by whom. Nothing times an invite out. It ends only by the inviter: taking it back (the same button), starting it with someone, driving out of the ring (an invite stands for the ring the inviter is waiting in: driving off means they've moved on, and an accept shouldn't pull them back from wherever they went), being unable to start when an accept comes (on the map or a jump), or leaving the world. An invite taken back while its accept was on the wire begins nothing. The inviter takes seat 0 and the one who joins seat 1, each on `starts[seat]`, both in the challenge's car; the joiner's car goes to its place from wherever it was, loading round it like Retry where it's far, and the lights wait at the ready gate as always. Start and leave go to everyone, so no client offers a paired player to a third. Either player's Retry, mid-run or on the result card, is applied at once and sent with the next attempt's number; the partner follows it unless that attempt is already under way there, so two retries at once are one restart. Each client puts its car on its place and sends ready once it's there, after any load, and the lights wait for both, the ready gate: an event, and each client's countdown runs from its own moment of hearing it, so each player is timed from their own green and no shared clock is read. Each client measures only its own car and sends its result, which the partner reports into its run, so both settle on the same average; while one is still driving after the other has finished, the HUD shows what it now needs ("Make it by 2:41"). A Route's loss is called only by a client still timing a player of its own, since its own clock is what bounds that player's time, and it sends the loss with the results it had, which the other takes as it stands: two clients never settle one attempt two ways. Either player leaving, or dropping out of the world, ends it for the other with a short notice, and both get their previous cars back, the joiner where the invite took it from (see Leaving).
- **The probe** has `addChallenge` (a challenge of the page's own), `startChallenge` (by id, resolving once the car is on the line), `challenge` (the run's phase, attempt, clock, gates and outcome), `driveChallenge` (the scripted driver along its route) and `challengeBest`; for co-op, `challenge` also gives the seat, the players, whether the partner is ready and each seat's result, and there are `coop` (where this client stands and the messages it has heard), `coopInvite` (to everyone, from the ring) and `coopAccept` (the newest invite held), `retryChallenge`, and `holdChallengeMessages`, which holds what arrives so two pages can both press before either hears the other.

**Races** (`ideas/races.md`, whose section 8 is the order they're built in) are a third kind of challenge on the same machinery, `kind: "race"`: the board, the ring, the session, co-op for two, the GPS, the menu and the maps all take them as they take a Route. They differ where a race does:

- **The list** is `challenge/race-specs.ts`, the 32 tracks by hand (number, title, shape, ground, car, waypoints from the plan's measured lines, a circuit's last waypoint its first), joined in `list.ts` (`RACES`) with `race-places.json` and `race-laps.json`, which `tools/race-places.ts` and `tools/race-laps.ts` measure, and a test holds them to the list as it is. A race's ground is what its road is laid in, as the places tool reads it (road all asphalt, off-road all gravel, cross country both), and a test holds each to it, so a change to the road surface that moves a race shows; the plan's own labels came from OpenStreetMap's tags and four of them moved. The grid is six places two abreast where a two-lane road (a main road, a tertiary or a street) takes two of the race's car, and four in single file on a lane; a circuit's start line is a little ahead of the pole, and its lap's lines run round to it. The laps tool drives a circuit's lap with `tools/par-driver.ts`, the scripted driver the challenge tool shares, which counts a missed line as a failure at it and eases a spot on every lap, and sets the laps for a race of about seven minutes and never more than ten.
- **The run** (`run.ts`) crosses one lap's lines lap after lap, the last line of the last lap the finish, and can't be lost against the clock: it ends once everyone has finished. **A race shows no time**: only the place counts. Until the AI racers exist, a race has no one in it but its players, who race as a team, so a finish is first and gold (`standings.ts`); the racers bring places, and Forza Horizon's team points for co-op, with them. A race keeps its best place and medal, solo and co-op apart (`passes.ts`).
- **On the maps** a race is a diamond in its ground's colour (road, cross country, off-road), a loop for a circuit and a flag for a sprint, with its best medal on its shoulder; the world map draws its whole route and a card under the cursor. Its ring's posts and beam in the world are its ground's colour. **In the ring** its button opens the race's card (the track, laps, length, car, and the medal and best place won solo and in co-op, then Start, and Co-op with anyone else in the world). **In a race** the panel shows the lap and the lines this lap; the result card the place and its medal. The probe's `challenge` adds the lap.
