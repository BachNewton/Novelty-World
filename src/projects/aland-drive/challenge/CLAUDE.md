# Åland Drive: challenges and races

Bucket list challenges, co-op and races. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down.

Bucket list challenges, as `ideas/bucket-list.md` decides them: two kinds, a Route against the clock through gates in order and a Speed trap's camera, each one car, one place on the real roads, its own date, hour and weather, and one target, passed or failed. Solo and co-op for two to four are built, the co-op with its shared quick retry and the average. The list is the 25 of the draft, a car each, 21 Routes and 4 Speed traps, with targets from the AI racer, and the owner hasn't played it yet. Co-op passes its tests, but the owner hasn't played it.

- **The rules** are `challenge/`, with no renderer in it. `specs.ts` is the hand-written list, `places.json` and `targets.json` what `tools/challenge-places.ts` and `tools/challenge-par.ts` measured from it on the built tiles (start places, four for co-op, gates or camera, par and target), joined in `list.ts`. `run.ts` is one attempt as a pure state machine: the countdown, each line found by intersecting a car's path between two steps with it, results and the outcome, for one player to four, a player who leaves co-op dropped from it. `line.ts` is a challenge's route as the GPS's own routes between its waypoints, the one line its gates, starts, par and the GPS during a run all come from.
- **Its target** (`targets.ts`, the owner's ruling): **there are no difficulties; a target is the fastest the AI gets** in the challenge's car on its road, in its conditions, through every line in order. Two drivers have a go: the AI racer on the racing line, at its own skill and at bolder shares of the car's grip, which sets every Route, and the old scripted driver (`tools/par-driver.ts`), which keeps to the GPS line and goes flat out where the racer's plan stops pulling at the top of a car's measured pull table, and so sets the Speed traps. Where the racer still makes a mistake on a road (`ideas/racer-method.md`'s gaps) its reset or knock only slows it, so that target is softer than a clean run's; the tool's table says which. The targets are a baseline: the owner means to play them and set some by hand, rather than improve the racers for them. A test holds each target to the car's tuning, the places and the ground and tyres it was measured on.
- **Its conditions** (`conditions.ts`): each challenge names a real date, the hour its attempt starts at and a weather (clear, cloud, wet under an overcast, rain, a thick fog, snowfall, black ice), and starting it puts the player in them, whatever the shared world has, so its target always means the same thing. They are this player's alone: the world's clock runs on beneath (`render/day-and-night.ts` shows them over it), and that is what this browser saves and what a host hands the online world, so no one else's sky moves. A co-op partner reads the same conditions from the same challenge, so nothing is sent. The hour runs on from the green light at the game's pace on the attempt's own clock (a game hour in about a minute of real time), so a challenge can be timed against a sunset or a sunrise, a retry starts at the same hour again, and a paused solo run's sky stands still with it; the weather holds. The ground's condition is the challenge's from the moment it starts, and the car is handed on the tyres for it, winter on snow and ice, as its target was measured; the player's own fitted tyres are untouched. Starting and leaving change the sky behind the time cover the online world's time uses. A race takes the world's conditions as they are.
- **A session** (`challenge/session.ts`) is one challenge played on this client, start to leave: the run, this client's seat on the start line, the car the player had before, and whether the car is still on its way to the line, which the run waits for. Every restart goes through its `retry`, a teammate's measured result through its `report` and a teammate leaving through its `drop`, so the network drives a co-op run through the same doors as the menu does a solo one. It never moves the car itself: the game, its host, swaps the car (`Drive.setCar`) and puts it on the line.
- **The board** (`challenge/board.ts`) is what a drive knows of them: the list and any challenge of the page's own (a test's), those whose start is in this world, which are passed with their best, solo and co-op kept apart (`passes.ts`, kept in this browser with `getProjectStorage` as finds are, the maps showing a pass in either), and whose start ring the car is in, 12 m from the middle of its two start places.
- **On the maps** every start in the world is a rounded square, a chequered flag for a Route and a dial for a Speed trap, in the challenge colour, and the pass colour once passed (`drawChallenges`, `map/draw.ts`). The world map names them when zoomed in to streets, names one under the cursor, and a pick on one routes the GPS to its ring, as a place does. During a run the mini map also draws the lines still to cross as bars across the road (`drawGates`): the next one bold in the challenge colour, the later ones pale, the finish in the pass colour.
- **In the world** (`render/challenge-marks.ts`) each start is a ring of short posts with a tall pale beam over it, marking it from kilometres off, and during a run only the next line to cross stands: an arch over the road, the finish's banner chequered, or a dark gantry with its camera for a Speed trap. All boxes and prisms, flat shaded, in the challenge's own colours: the posts and beams are one instanced list each and the line one small mesh rebuilt when it changes, three draw calls and a few thousand triangles in all. Each stands on the ground as the world has it, read again as tiles sharpen it.
- **Starting.** In a ring the HUD prompts the challenge's own button (X, or E). With no one else in the world it starts the challenge solo at once; with anyone else in the world, wherever they are, it opens a choice of Solo or Co-op, and Co-op invites everyone (see Co-op). Starting swaps to the challenge's car and puts it on the first start place, the solo one, and the countdown's three seconds run with the car held on the handbrake and the player's input ignored until green. During a run the HUD shows the lights, the clock against the target and the gates passed (a Route) or the speed to reach (a Speed trap), the next line stands in the world, and the GPS holds the challenge's route, never rerouting from it, since its gates are on it. R or Y still puts the car back on the road, and a car put somewhere crosses no line on its way.
- **The HUD in a run** is one panel at the top middle, the distance still to go, to the finish line or the camera rather than the route's end, which can run on past them, with the challenge's title, lights, clock against the target and gates under it, with the road's name and a find's card below, clear of the frame meter at the top right.
- **The menu in a run** holds Resume, Retry, Settings and Leave challenge. Settings there is the game menu's (`components/CLAUDE.md`) less the tyres, which are the challenge's car's: the driver's aids, the units, the controls, the sound and the graphics, all the player's own. Solo it pauses, and the run's clock only moves with the simulation's steps, so a paused run stands still. In co-op it doesn't pause, the result card neither: the clock and both cars run on, and the car's controls let go while it's open. When an attempt is over the menu is the result card: passed or failed, the time or speed against the target, each teammate's in co-op, and the best kept, with Retry and Leave, Leave on B and Esc. **After a win it opens on Leave** (`resultItems`, `render/game-menu.ts`): a challenge passed, a race won, or a co-op race's team gold leaves nothing to chase, so the first choice, which keyboard, mouse and the pad's focus all start on, is back to the drive; after a loss it opens on Retry, which is what a player who just missed wants.
- **Retry** puts the car back on its start place at once where the start's tiles are still loaded, and otherwise goes back the way a dev menu jump does, loading round the start with the first load's bar and putting the car down once it has landed: the start is never kept loaded through a run, since a short load is better than a run that draws worse. **Leaving** gives back the car the player had before, where the car is now, except for a player who joined a co-op invite: the invite took them from wherever they were, so leaving puts them back there, on the nearest road facing the way they faced, loading round it like a jump if it has gone.
- **Co-op** (`challenge/coop.ts`) is a team of two to four players (`MOST_PLAYERS`) over the online world's messages (`net/protocol.ts`), each naming the challenge, the attempt and, through the host, the sender. Forming a team is a world-wide invite, then accepts, then start. Co-op sends the invite to everyone in the world, and to anyone who arrives while it's open. Every invited player's HUD shows it wherever they are, who asked and which challenge or race, and prompts their challenge button, which opens Join or Decline; ignoring it is the default and holds nothing up. A player in a challenge of their own, or in co-op, holds an invite unseen and sees it once free, if it's still open; of several, the newest shows. **The invite stays open until the inviter starts it or the room is full**: each accept the inviter hears takes the next seat, in the order heard, and the inviter's HUD counts who has joined; with anyone in, the inviter's button opens Start or take it back, and the fourth player's accept starts it at once. Start goes to everyone naming the team by seat, which withdraws the invite everywhere else at once; a joiner the start doesn't name (who joined as the room filled, or as the inviter started) hears it started without them. A joiner may take their accept back before the start (the same button), and their seat goes to whoever accepts next. Nothing times an invite out. It ends only by the inviter: taking it back, starting it, driving out of the ring (an invite stands for the ring the inviter is waiting in: driving off means they've moved on, and an accept shouldn't pull them back from wherever they went), being unable to start when the room fills (on the map or a jump), or leaving the world; anyone who had joined hears it's off. The inviter takes seat 0 and the joiners the next, each on its own place (a challenge's `starts[seat]`, a race's grid behind the racers, see Races), all in the challenge's car; a joiner's car goes to its place from wherever it was, loading round it like Retry where it's far, and the lights wait at the ready gate as always. Start and leave go to everyone, so no client offers a teamed player to anyone else. Any player's Retry, mid-run or on the result card, is applied at once and sent to the team with the next attempt's number; each teammate follows it unless that attempt is already under way there, so retries at once are one restart. Each client puts its car on its place and sends ready once it's there, after any load, and the lights wait for every car still in, the ready gate: an event, and each client's countdown runs from its own moment of hearing it, so each player is timed from their own green and no shared clock is read. Each client measures only its own car and sends its result to the team, which each teammate reports into its run, so all settle on the same average; while some are still driving after others have finished, their HUD shows the average they now need between them ("Make it by 2:41"). A Route's loss is called only by a client still timing a player of its own, since its own clock is what bounds that player's time, and it sends the loss with the results it had, which the rest take as it stands: two clients never settle one attempt two ways. **A player who leaves, or drops out of the world, is dropped and the rest carry on** (the owner's ruling), each told with a short notice: the gate stops waiting for them, their result leaves the average, so the target stands for the players still in, and an attempt waiting only on them settles at once. The one who left gets their previous car back, a joiner where the invite took it from (see Leaving). Once only one player is left their co-op ends too, with their car back. In a race the leaver's place empties: putting an AI racer in it waits for racers shared online (below), where one spawn reaches every page.
- **The start places for four**: a bucket list challenge's first two places stand side by side, as solo's, and `tools/challenge-places.ts` measures a second row of two 10 m behind them where the road there takes two, else ahead of them. Every challenge starts 8 m from the junction its route begins at, so every second row stands ahead: the third and fourth players drive about 10 m less, a third of a second or so on a Route, which the target, measured from the first place alone, doesn't count. Par and target are driven from the first place, so the places' print they're held to leaves the extra places out (`placesPrint`).
- **The probe** has `addChallenge` (a challenge of the page's own), `startChallenge` (by id, resolving once the car is on the line), `challenge` (the run's phase, attempt, clock, gates and outcome), `driveChallenge` (the scripted driver along its route) and `challengeBest`; for co-op, `challenge` also gives the seat, the players, whether every teammate still in is ready and each seat's result, and there are `coop` (where this client stands, who has joined its invite, its teammates and the messages it has heard), `coopInvite` (to everyone, from the ring), `coopAccept` (the newest invite held), `coopStart` (the team so far), `retryChallenge`, and `holdChallengeMessages`, which holds what arrives so two pages can both press before either hears the other.

**Races** (`ideas/races.md`, whose section 8 is the order they're built in) are a third kind of challenge on the same machinery, `kind: "race"`: the board, the ring, the session, co-op, the GPS, the menu and the maps all take them as they take a Route. They differ where a race does:

- **The list** is `challenge/race-specs.ts`, the 56 events of `ideas/races.md` section 9 by hand, tier by tier from the fastest (tier, title, shape, ground, the car of its tier it hands the player, waypoints on the real roads, a circuit's last waypoint its first), a race's number its tier and its place among the tier's races (A5.2), joined in `list.ts` (`RACES`) with `race-places.json` and `race-laps.json`, which `tools/race-places.ts` and `tools/race-laps.ts` measure, and a test holds them to the list as it is. A race's ground is what its road is laid in, as the places tool reads it (road all asphalt, off-road all gravel, cross country both), and a test holds each to it, so a change to the road surface that moves a race shows; the plan's own labels came from OpenStreetMap's tags and four of them moved. **An event is for one class and tier** (`sim/tiers.ts`) and, until cars are owned, hands the player one car of it, as a challenge does; its racers drive only its tier's cars (`racers/field.ts`). The grid is six places two abreast where a two-lane road (a main road, a tertiary or a street) takes two of the widest car of the tier, and four in single file on a lane, with two places in reserve for a co-op field bigger than four: behind the back of a circuit's grid, where the loop runs on, and ahead of a sprint's pole, since behind its back is the junction the route starts from. A circuit's start line is a little ahead of the pole, and its lap's lines run round to it. Every place and line stands on the road as laid (`racers/laid-road.ts`), never on the GPS's own line, which is OpenStreetMap's and parts from the laid road by a road's width and more in places: a gate measured on it once stood 14 m into a field. The laps tool drives a circuit's lap with the racer itself on the racing line, in the race's car, dry, and sets the laps for a race of about seven minutes and never more than ten.
- **The run** (`run.ts`) crosses one lap's lines lap after lap, the last line of the last lap the finish, and can't be lost against the clock: it ends once everyone has finished. **A race shows no time**: only the place counts. The AI racers (`racers/CLAUDE.md`) take the grid's other places and run the same rules for themselves, and `standings.ts` places every car: a finisher by its time, a car still racing by how far round it is, and a player's place is settled as they cross the line, since no racer behind can pass them then; with no racer in a race, a finish is first. **A co-op team is scored as Forza Horizon scores a team** (the owner's ruling): each player scores a hundred points for every racer they finish ahead of and fifty for beating them all, teammates never scoring off each other, and the team's medal is its points per player against the racers (`teamMedal`): beating every racer on average is gold, all but one silver, all but two bronze, so a strong run carries a weaker one. Each client scores its own player as they cross the line and sends the points with the result. **Who starts**: the players take grid places the racers would have (`challenge/grid-places.ts`), the racers keeping the grid from the pole down to two, never fewer while there's room: on a grid of six, five racers solo, then four, three and two with two, three and four players; on a single file of four, three, then two, the reserve's places making room. Until racers are shared online (`ideas/races.md` section 8, step 6) **each page runs its own copy of the racers**, so each player is scored against the racers on their own screen. A race keeps its best medal and place, solo and co-op apart (`passes.ts`), a better medal first; in co-op the medal kept is the team's.
- **On the maps** a race is a diamond in its ground's colour (road, cross country, off-road), a loop for a circuit and a flag for a sprint, with its best medal on its shoulder; the world map draws its whole route and a card under the cursor. Its ring's posts and beam in the world are its ground's colour. **In the ring** its button opens the race's card (the track, laps, length, car, and the medal and best place won solo and in co-op, then Start, and Co-op with anyone else in the world). **In a race** the panel shows the player's place among the cars, the lap and the lines this lap; the result card the place and its medal, in co-op the team's, with each player's points. The probe's `challenge` adds the lap, the place and how many racers are in it.
