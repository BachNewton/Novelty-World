# Åland Drive: the racers

The AI drivers who race the player (`ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`, "Racers"; `ideas/races.md`, section 8), and the tool that is their ruler. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down. The method behind this folder, as the `machine-learning` skill asks for it, is `ideas/racer-method.md`.

**The decided rules.** A racer drives the same car through the same `DriveInput` as the player, a full simulated car in the player's world (`sim/drive.ts`), so whatever it does a player could do too. It never gets more grip, power or luck than its car has, never speeds up out of sight, and has no rubber-banding. **Every racer drives at one skill, as fast as it legally can be**, the owner's ruling (`ideas/racer-method.md`): no difficulty levels, and the work is making its top pace clean, not grading it down. It recovers as a player would, by driving back to the road or, when stuck, by its own reset, at the player's cost in time. A race shows no time, only places.

- **The road as laid** (`laid-road.ts`): the baked road surface's sections, in a grid of cells, read at any place for the laid middle under it, its carriageway's and whole surface's reach to either side, and whether it is sealed. The GPS's line is OpenStreetMap's and parts from the laid road by a road's width and more in places, so everything a racer drives on, and everything a race measures (its grid and its lines, `tools/race-places.ts`), is read from the laid road, never the GPS line. `route-plan.ts` reads it along a route, every 4 m; where the GPS line strays further than a sample can see (it runs 20 m and more beside a bend of the built road in places), the road is followed on from where it was last found, by the road's own way, a step at a time, while it runs much as the line does and stays within sight of it. At a junction's apron the road ends or turns, and the gap is bridged instead.
- **The racing line** (`racing-line.ts`): the route's corridor, the laid middle with the carriageway's edges, bridged straight across the junction aprons where no section is laid, and in it the minimum-curvature line, the textbook racing line (outside, apex, outside): the least-squares answer to a banded linear system in the offset from the middle at every sample, bounded by the edges less the car's half width and a margin, solved by an active set (the unbounded answer, then every offset over an edge held on it, until none moves). The corridor knows what stands in it (`walls.ts`: the world's walls, joined into outlines, a closed outline solid all through): where a wall reaches into the carriageway the bounds draw in to pass it, or to one way round it a car's width and a little wide; a gap in the laid road with something standing across the straight bridge is bridged along the GPS line instead; across a bridged gap the corridor has no room to either side for racecraft, since nothing says what stands beside the line there, so a racer keeps its line over a junction's apron; and past the road's end the line stops short of whatever stands there. `tools/race-lines.ts` says each stretch still nearer a wall than its car can pass, and a finish with too little line past it. The middle itself is the line a cautious driver keeps, and what the racing line is measured against. A line lies on the road's surface where it runs, the carriageway's crown or bank read from its laid edges, not at the middle's height: the plan reads its crests from it and the player's driving line is drawn on it. Both are pure functions of the route and the road.
- **The speed plan** (`speed-plan.ts`): the fastest speed at every sample from the car's measured limits (`sim/limits/`) on the ground under it, in the condition, on the tyres for it, at a share of its cornering grip and a share of its braking: the speed the bend allows on the grip the car holds at that speed, no lighter over a crest than keeps its wheels down (the road's height along the line says where it crests), no faster than the steering's lock at that speed, on that footing, turns the bend, and never slower than a crawl, since a standstill in a plan stops a car for good; then backwards, braking for every slower sample ahead with what the bend there leaves of the tyres; then forwards, no faster than the car's pull from its standing start, again with what the bend leaves (the friction circle both ways: out of a corner the throttle comes in as the steering unwinds). It holds no standstill: its start reads as the sample after it, since a car put down behind a standstill in its plan would wait there for ever. Pure arithmetic on a table: a plan costs nothing a frame. A car's plan on a line is made in one place (`car-plan.ts`), for every racer and for the line a player is shown (`render/CLAUDE.md`, The driving line), from the line's shape, read once for every car, and the car's limits on the ground under each stretch, sealed or gravel as laid, in the condition on its tyres; it carries those limits, and the footing there (how much the tyres grip on that ground and how far they slip to their best grip), for its driver.
- **The driver** (`driver.ts`): pure pursuit along the line, the steering every computer-driven car shares (`sim/autopilot.ts`), aiming much nearer than traffic does, with a little steering towards the line by how far off it stands, softened at speed, and throttle and brake holding the car under the plan's speed a moment ahead. Held to a creep, going round something that stands in the road, it aims nearer still, for the lock a car's length of room asks of the wheel. Held to a stand it stands on its handbrake, as on the grid, and rolling backwards the throttle is what stops it: the brake pedal is reverse once the car has stopped, as a player's is, and a racer that held it there backed away from whatever it had stopped for. Four things make it a driver at the limit, each read from the vetting tool's traces of what went wrong without it:
  - **It aims along the way the car travels, not the way its nose points.** A car cornering hard runs its rear tyres at a slip that points the nose into the bend, by several degrees at speed and half as much again on gravel, and its middle swings about its rear axle, which points the nose out of a slow, tight bend. Pursuit along the nose reads the first as pointing too far in and steers out, so a car hangs a metre and more outside every fast bend, further on the loose, and cuts the inside of every slow one. The driver works out the attitude a car cornering cleanly has here, from the line's bend, its speed and the tyres' own curve (`slipForGrip`, `sim/car.ts`), and pursues from that; what is left over is a real slide, which the same steering then turns into.
  - **The wheels turn for the tyres' slip as well as the path.** The angle that turns a car a bend is the path's own angle, and the slip its front tyres need to give their share of that turn, less the slip its rear tyres are running at, read from the way the rear axle travels. The path's angle alone leaves a car short of every turn asked of it, by more the faster it goes and the further its tyres slip for their grip: half a turn short on gravel at speed, and on snow it never caught a bend it had run wide of. Reading the rear as it is, not as a clean corner would have it, is what makes one rule right for a car whose front lets go first and one whose rear does, and a rear that swings wide takes the wheels with it, the opposite lock a driver gives a slide. The wheel's whole travel is the lock on the footing under the car, which on ice is a third of a dry road's, so the share of it that turns the wheels as far as asked is worked out on that footing.
  - **The pedals get what the turn leaves of the tyres.** A tyre's grip is one budget: turning comes out of it first, and the throttle and the brake are each held to the rest. The turn is the share of their grip the tyres are giving sideways, front or rear, read from the slip each axle runs at as the simulation reads it (`gripAt`), and never less than the line's bend asks of the car here; the brake's whole travel is more than the tyres hold on loose ground, so its share is of the pedal that reaches their grip, and so is the engine's on snow and ice, so the throttle's share is of the push the car put down there when it was measured. A tyre past the slip it grips best at is sliding and bears no pedal. One thing comes before turning: a car well over the speed it is to hold can't make its turn whatever it steers, so from a couple of metres a second over, the brake's share grows to the whole of the tyres' grip, sliding or not. Pedals that go to full whenever the car is off its planned speed, mid-bend as on a straight, take a third to a half of the tyres' sideways grip there and hold full throttle through a slide.
  - **The pedals push as the plan does.** What is asked at the road is the push that gains or loses speed as the plan itself does where the car is (or as the speed it is held under falls, closing on a car ahead: chasing that speed alone arrives at the car still faster than it), and a little more for each metre a second the car is off the speed it is to hold; the pedal is the share of the engine's pull at that speed (`engineAt`, `sim/gearbox.ts`), or of the brakes, that gives it. So a braking zone is one press and a straight is steady throttle. Pedals that only chase the error go from full to nothing and back several times a second, and the brake lamps with them: the vetting tool counts the presses, three a kilometre against ten for a car alone.
  **The racer** (`racer.ts`) is that driver with racecraft and a player's recovery: off the road, it drives back to its line at a careful speed; stuck, it backs out and tries again; spun round, it turns about in the road. Its own R is the last resort it is for a player: stuck once more, lost (further off its line than it could have driven back from, or off it for longer than a player would keep trying), on its roof, in the water, or past a line it was to cross without having crossed it, which it could otherwise never finish. The R puts it back on its line at the nearest point, and never past the line it has yet to cross; where it is for being stuck behind a car that stands in its road, a player's left there or a wreck, the racer is put down past that car where the road is next clear, since put down behind it again it would be stuck for as long as the car stood. Over the finish it cools down along the road's right edge to its line's end, so the line stays clear for those still racing, and stops there. It runs the race's own rules for itself (`challenge/run.ts`), so its lap, its next line and its finish are read as the player's are, and its progress (lines crossed, and how near the next) orders the places (`challenge/standings.ts`). Its record is small and its drive is a pure function of it, so whoever runs it next online carries on from exactly there.
- **Racecraft** (`awareness.ts`, `racecraft.ts`): every car a racer sees (the other racers, the player, traffic) is placed on its own line, as far ahead as its fastest car needs to slow to traffic's pace without a panic stop, so each question is one-dimensional: how far ahead or behind, how far to the side, going its way or against it, closing how fast, and how far to the side it will be when the racer reaches it if both carry on as they are, since a car in a lane the line crosses further on, into a roundabout or across a bend, is beside the line now and on it then. The rules the owner decided are pure functions of that, giving the driver two numbers, a speed to hold under and how far right of its line to run: it holds a gap to the car ahead that is in its path or will be when reached, a gap that grows with speed and is longer behind one that has stopped, which leaves the room to pull out round it, braking firmly rather than driving through it, and holds it until it stands clear of that car sideways, not from the moment it means to pass; passes a car slower than the plan where it stands, when the racer is closing on it, or is held behind one much slower as it is behind traffic at the road's limit, from further back the faster it closes; a side is a way by only with room beside the car all the way to where the racer will be clear ahead of it, nothing coming the other way that it would meet in the time the pass takes, and nothing else running or standing in that side that would leave it no place to come back into, and it is the inside into a bend or the side with more room; the pass is called off if the road runs short of that room or something comes while the racer is still behind, it switches to another car stopped in its way once it has stood behind that one, and the racer comes back once clear; off its line into a bend's inside its turn is tighter than the line's, and it slows for the difference; a car stopped in the road it goes round, slowly, once it has stood behind it a moment or come on it at speed, and where the road has no room, by the verge, but only round one that has stayed stopped for some seconds, since a car waiting at a junction or for one coming the other way moves off sooner, and never over a junction's apron, where the verge may be an island: there it queues, and waiting behind a car that is only slow, or in a queue for as long as a queue lasts, is not being stuck; caught from behind before a bend, it moves a car's width or so to that bend's inside once, holds that side through it and never weaves, and defends nothing when stopped or when the car behind is through already; side by side it leaves a car's width; with traffic coming the other way on the carriageway it keeps right of the middle and of it until that has passed, and where the road hasn't the room for both at speed it slows to pass, takes the verge, or stops as far over as it goes and waits for it, for a while, after which it is stuck and sees to itself; one coming on the racer's own side and to its right, a walker at the road's edge, is left on that side instead; a walker or a cyclist on a path beside the road is no one to meet. The grid is two abreast, so off the line everyone ahead is slow and no one has room: a racer holds its lane for the first forty metres from its place, which is what keeps the start in order.
- **Its skill** (`skill.ts`) is one pair of shares, of the car's cornering grip and of its braking, the highest the vetting tool finds clean; they go up as the plan learns what still catches it out.
- **The field** (`field.ts`): the cars of the race's tier, the owner's ruling, the race's own car first; a tier holds two or three cars and a grid six, so the racers take them in turn, a repeated car in another of its paints.
- **In a race** (`grid.ts`, wired in `render/shared-racers.ts`): the racers take the grid from the pole (`?racers=<count>` for fewer), each in a car of the field in turn, and the player starts at the back, behind them all (the owner's call, having held a lead from the pole without a fight: taking it through the pack is the race); a co-op team starts behind them in seat order, taking places the racers would have, down to two racers and never fewer (`challenge/grid-places.ts`); once the player's car is on its place the racers fill theirs, and the lights wait for them; traffic is told of every racer as it is of the player's car, so it gives way to them and brakes for them, and while a grid stands at the lights the traffic on its stretch of road is taken away; they step before the world each step, held through the countdown as the player is; the game draws each as it draws another player's car. The HUD shows the player's place among the cars, and a finish's place is settled when the player crosses the line, since no racer behind can pass them then. A race's line comes from its file (`public/aland-drive/race-lines/<id>.json`, `race-line-file.ts`, written by `tools/race-lines.ts`): the line needs the whole route's laid road to draw and the game only has the tiles round the car, so it is drawn once from the built tiles for the widest car of the field, and every page and the vetting tool drive the very same line. A race of the page's own (a test's) draws its line from the roads loaded here.
- **Online, every player in a race races the same racers** (`shared.ts`, `render/shared-racers.ts`; `ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`, "Who runs the racers online"). Each racer is a computer-driven car (`net/computer-cars.ts`), run by the player in the race nearest it, kept by them while it's in a fight, and seen by everyone in the world, those not in the race too, who never run one. One rule from the grid on, solo the same as co-op: each grid racer is put down by the player whose start place is nearest its grid place, which every page works out alike from the race, once their car is on its place; a retry takes every racer of the attempt before out of the world, and the grid is put down again. A racer's record (`RacerDriver`: its snapshot, and what its plan is made from) goes with its car 20 times a second, and the page it's handed to builds the same plan and resumes the racer from it, so a handover changes nothing about how it drives; racecraft names every car alike on every page, the player by their id in the world. The places read every racer of the race, those run here from their racers and the rest from their records. A player says with their car which race and attempt they're in and how far round (`RaceProgress`): only a player in a racer's race and attempt may run it, and a runner who leaves the race hands each racer to the nearest player still in it, or takes it out of the world when no one is left. **A player who leaves a race unfinished**, by Leave or by dropping out of the world, **is replaced by a racer on the racers' side** (the owner's ruling): in their car where it last raced, with the lines they had crossed, put down by the player still in the race nearest them, the lower id on a tie; a retry puts a racer on the leaver's own grid place, as it does on every racer's.
- **The vetting tool** (`tools/race-vet.ts`, `vet.ts`): every race driven alone in the simulation, by the race's car or its whole field, in every condition on the tyres for it, from every place on the grid, on the GPS line (the par driver), the laid middle or the racing line, at the racer's skill or shares of the grip, through the race's own rules, over the shared worker pool, and counted: finished or not and where it failed, each lap's time, metres past the carriageway's edge and off the whole surface, how far off its line it ran, hits (a loss of speed no brake makes, never the reset that puts a car down), the times its brake lamps came on with the flashes among them, as the car behind sees them, and the presses of the pedal under them, airborne steps, resets and why each. It eases nothing. A clean finish is a finish with no stop, no reset and no hit, never further past the laid surface than a gate reaches. **Its grid mode** (`vet-grid.ts`, `--grid`) fills every place on a race's grid with a racer in a car of the field, all in one simulation, each seeing the others, and counts the race: places, each one's hits and resets, the passes made among them and the pile-ups (three or more stopped together), and says each hit and reset by its kind (ran into a car, hit from behind, side by side or something standing, while passing or not; a reset by its cause and what it followed); `--trace` says what each racer does every second, with the cause of each reset. Four more readings are for what a clear road and a clean field never ask of a racer. `--knocks`, alone, gives it what another car's hit leaves it with every 800 m of its race (a nudge, a shove, or a tap on the rear quarter that spins it) and counts the knocks a reset followed, by how fast the car was going: human racers hit the racers, and a racer is read on gathering the car up. `--human`, in a grid, gives the last place to a careless human, a car that races as if alone on the road and nearer its limit than a racer, which goes through the pack from the back; every touch between two cars is counted, with whether the racer drove on. `--parked`, in a grid, leaves a car standing square on the racing line 600 m past the grid, as a player who has stopped leaves theirs, met again each lap of a circuit, and counts the resets and hits there. `--traffic`, in a grid, puts the game's own traffic on the race's roads (`traffic/CLAUDE.md`: cars, cyclists and walkers on their lanes, at a share of Åland's own), kept round the middle of the pack as the game keeps it round a player, what that racer has in view left alone as the game leaves what a player sees, off the grid's stretch until the lights: the racers see it and it sees them, a car of it hit hard is knocked out of its lane as in the game, and the hits on it, the cars knocked out and the cyclists and walkers driven through are counted. What neither mode holds is the game itself: `tools/race-count.mjs` runs a whole race in the headless game, the racers on the grid among the traffic on its lanes with the player's car trundling round, and counts each racer's finish and resets, and `tools/race-film.mjs` films one from behind a racer. It is the ruler every change to a racer is read on, by exact difference, since a run is deterministic: one change a run, predicted before it runs, kept only when the prediction holds (`ideas/racer-method.md` has the readings). It counts the mistakes; `tools/race-incidents.ts` reads each one: one car on one race in one condition, every excursion from its line with what led to it (its speed against the plan, the grip the bend asked, the wheel at its lock, a wheel past the edge, which side of the bend it left by) and its trace a tenth of a second at a time, so a change is aimed at a cause that was measured.
