# Åland Drive: the racers

The AI drivers who race the player (`ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`, "Racers"; `ideas/races.md`, section 8), and the tool that is their ruler. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down. The method behind this folder, as the `machine-learning` skill asks for it, is `ideas/racer-method.md`.

**The decided rules.** A racer drives the same car through the same `DriveInput` as the player, a full simulated car in the player's world (`sim/drive.ts`), so whatever it does a player could do too. It never gets more grip, power or luck than its car has, never speeds up out of sight, and has no rubber-banding. **Every racer drives at one skill, as fast as it legally can be**, the owner's ruling (`ideas/racer-method.md`): no difficulty levels, and the work is making its top pace clean, not grading it down. It recovers as a player would, by driving back to the road or, when stuck, by its own reset, at the player's cost in time. A race shows no time, only places.

- **The road as laid** (`laid-road.ts`): the baked road surface's sections, in a grid of cells, read at any place for the laid middle under it, its carriageway's and whole surface's reach to either side, and whether it is sealed. The GPS's line is OpenStreetMap's and parts from the laid road by a road's width and more in places, so everything a racer drives on, and everything a race measures (its grid and its lines, `tools/race-places.ts`), is read from the laid road, never the GPS line. `route-plan.ts` reads it along a route, every 4 m; where the GPS line strays further than a sample can see, the road is followed on from where it was last seen, only while the line runs on much as it did, since at a junction's apron the road turns and the gap is bridged instead.
- **The racing line** (`racing-line.ts`): the route's corridor, the laid middle with the carriageway's edges, bridged straight across the junction aprons where no section is laid, and in it the minimum-curvature line, the textbook racing line (outside, apex, outside): the least-squares answer to a banded linear system in the offset from the middle at every sample, bounded by the edges less the car's half width and a margin, solved by an active set (the unbounded answer, then every offset over an edge held on it, until none moves). The middle itself is the line a cautious driver keeps, and what the racing line is measured against. Both are pure functions of the route and the road.
- **The speed plan** (`speed-plan.ts`): the fastest speed at every sample from the car's measured limits (`sim/limits/`) on the ground under it, in the condition, on the tyres for it, at a share of its cornering grip and a share of its braking: the speed the bend allows on the grip the car holds at that speed, no lighter over a crest than keeps its wheels down (the road's height along the line says where it crests), no faster than the steering's lock at that speed turns the bend; then backwards, braking for every slower sample ahead with what the bend there leaves of the tyres; then forwards, no faster than the car's pull from its standing start, again with what the bend leaves (the friction circle both ways: out of a corner the throttle comes in as the steering unwinds). Pure arithmetic on a table: a plan costs nothing a frame.
- **The driver** (`driver.ts`): pure pursuit along the line, the steering every computer-driven car shares (`sim/autopilot.ts`), aiming much nearer than traffic does (a long look cuts every bend's inside and runs wide out of it; the vetting tool read every step nearer as cleaner, down to where it stands), with a little steering towards the line by how far off it stands, softened at speed, and throttle and brake holding the car under the plan's speed a moment ahead. **The racer** (`racer.ts`) is that driver with racecraft and a player's recovery: stuck, it backs out and tries again; spun round, it turns about in the road; stuck once more, off the road for good, on its roof or in the water, it presses its own R, back on its line at the nearest point, and never past the line it has yet to cross (put down past it, it could never finish). Over the finish it cools down along the road's right edge to its line's end, so the line stays clear for those still racing, and stops there. It runs the race's own rules for itself (`challenge/run.ts`), so its lap, its next line and its finish are read as the player's are, and its progress (lines crossed, and how near the next) orders the places (`challenge/standings.ts`). Its record is small and its drive is a pure function of it, so whoever runs it next online carries on from exactly there.
- **Racecraft** (`awareness.ts`, `racecraft.ts`): every car a racer sees (the other racers, the player, traffic) is placed on its own line, so each question is one-dimensional: how far ahead or behind, how far to the side, going its way or against it, closing how fast. The rules the owner decided are pure functions of that, giving the driver two numbers, a speed to hold under and how far right of its line to run: it holds a gap to the car ahead in its path that grows with speed, braking firmly rather than driving through it; passes a car slower than the plan where it stands, when the racer is closing on it and there is room beside it, on the inside into a bend or the side with more room, and comes back once clear; caught from behind before a bend, it moves to that bend's inside once, holds that side through it and never weaves, and defends nothing when stopped or when the car behind is through already; side by side it leaves a car's width; with traffic coming the other way it keeps to its own side until that has passed. The grid is two abreast, so off the line everyone ahead is slow and no one has room: the closing rule is what keeps the start in order.
- **Its skill** (`skill.ts`) is one pair of shares, of the car's cornering grip and of its braking, the highest the vetting tool finds clean; they go up as the plan learns what still catches it out.
- **The field** (`field.ts`): the race's own car, which the player is handed, and the cars nearest it in pace of the kinds its ground suits (sports and road cars on a road race, the rally cars and four-by-fours off it), by the measured sprint to 100 km/h the tiers rank cars by. The racers drive the others.
- **In a race** (`grid.ts`, wired in `render/game.ts`): the racers take the grid from the pole (`?racers=<count>` for fewer), each in a car of the field, and the player starts at the back, behind them all (the owner's call, having held a lead from the pole without a fight: taking it through the pack is the race); once the player's car is on its place the racers fill theirs, and the lights wait for them; they step before the world each step, held through the countdown as the player is; the game draws each as it draws another player's car. The HUD shows the player's place among the cars, and a finish's place is settled when the player crosses the line, since no racer behind can pass them then. A race's line comes from its file (`public/aland-drive/race-lines/<id>.json`, `race-line-file.ts`, written by `tools/race-lines.ts`): the line needs the whole route's laid road to draw and the game only has the tiles round the car, so it is drawn once from the built tiles for the widest car of the field, and every page and the vetting tool drive the very same line. A race of the page's own (a test's) draws its line from the roads loaded here.
- **The vetting tool** (`tools/race-vet.ts`, `vet.ts`): every race driven alone in the simulation, by the race's car or its whole field, in every condition on the tyres for it, from every place on the grid, on the GPS line (the par driver), the laid middle or the racing line, at the racer's skill or shares of the grip, through the race's own rules, over the shared worker pool, and counted: finished or not and where it failed, each lap's time, metres past the carriageway's edge and off the whole surface, how far off its line it ran, hits (a loss of speed no brake makes, never the reset that puts a car down), airborne steps, resets and why each. It eases nothing. A clean finish is a finish with no stop, no reset and no hit, never further past the laid surface than a gate reaches. **Its grid mode** (`vet-grid.ts`, `--grid`) fills every place on a race's grid with a racer in a car of the field, all in one simulation, each seeing the others, and counts the race: places, each one's hits and resets, the passes made among them and the pile-ups (three or more stopped together); `--trace` says what each racer does every second, with the cause of each reset. It is the ruler every change to a racer is read on, by exact difference, since a run is deterministic: one change a run, predicted before it runs, kept only when the prediction holds (`ideas/racer-method.md` has the readings).
