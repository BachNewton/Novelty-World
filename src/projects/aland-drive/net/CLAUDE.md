# Åland Drive: multiplayer

Online free roam on the relay. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down.

Online free roam, on the relay (`src/shared/lib/peer/`, see the root `CLAUDE.md`): every player **claims** one fixed room (`net/protocol.ts`'s `WORLD_CODE`), so the first to open the game hosts and everyone after joins, with no code to type, and when the host leaves the rest claim it again among themselves. Names are the shared profile's. `net/online-world.ts` is the whole of what's shared: each player's `CarState`, which names their car, 20 times a second (every sixth step), with when their client stepped it on its own clock, sent up to the host, which hands each on to every other guest naming whose it is, a co-op challenge's messages (see `challenge/CLAUDE.md`), and the world's time, which each guest asks the host for once on joining (see `time/CLAUDE.md`). Every state is the whole car, so a new host or a newcomer has the world back within one send, and a handover is no more than the relay's claim. A paused player (the map, the dev menu, a jump's load) sends their car once standing still, and again whenever someone arrives or their car is put somewhere. That's about 430 bytes a state as the relay sends it, 8.7 kB a second for each car each player sees; the relay fans the host's frames out, so the host's own upload grows with the players and no one's grows faster. A challenge message goes to one player or to everyone, sent up to the host like a car state, which hands it on to the one it's for, or to every other guest, naming who it's from, and keeps it through a reconnect rather than lose it. The host only relays, and the relay needed no change for any of it.

**Each player is the authority over their own car.** Steering never goes through the host, so nobody drives on someone else's lag. Every other car is a stand-in body in this player's own Rapier world (`sim/remote-cars.ts`): its own car's box, mass and inertia (a player who swaps car gets a new stand-in), steered each step towards where that car is shown now, which is a little in the past (`sim/remote-timeline.ts`): every state is placed on a timeline by when its client stepped it, not when it arrived, and the car is played out a lag behind, the delay four messages in five arrive within plus half a send, which also learns the difference between the two clients' clocks. Between two states it follows the curve their positions and velocities make; past the newest, when a message is late, it carries on round its bend by its own velocity and spin for up to half a second, after which it waits, and the stand-in's pull closes on where the late state then puts it. A state with no time (a version of the game that sends none, a computer-driven car run here, traffic) is the car as it is now, carried on from when it was heard. The twitch other cars used to have was the game's own: a state shown as of when it arrived carries its age wrong by up to a frame and a send's unevenness, and the stand-in's pull turned each such error into a lurch, as much on a perfect network as on a real one. `tools/remote-twitch.ts` measures it by counting, a known path sent through a seeded network of the owner's real routes (Helsinki through Stockholm, and from the US) and compared frame by frame with the path itself. It also picked the rate: 30 a second was no smoother and only 8 ms less behind, for half as much again on the wire. It's dynamic, not kinematic, so in a step where the two touch the solver trades momentum between the two cars by their weights: a van shoves a roadster aside and barely moves, and the next step the stand-in is steered back on its way. The other driver's client sees the same contact against this car's stand-in there, so both see the hit and each reacts with their own car. With latency, both sides see the other car slightly late and the hit comes out livelier than a real one, a parked car taking most of the speed of the one that hit it; `sim/remote-cars.test.ts` drives one car into another on two clients a tenth of a second apart and holds that the parked one is shoved, neither passes through the other, and nothing is thrown. The stand-in carries how fast it may close on its car (4 m/s, 3 rad/s), so a late state never fires it into anyone.

A stand-in touches only the car being driven (`sim/groups.ts`): no gravity and no ground, so a car whose ground isn't loaded here can't fall through it, and two other cars meeting is for their own clients. Wheel rays don't see stand-ins. A stand-in put somewhere rather than steered there, more than 6 m from where it should be (a first sight, a reset, a jump), touches nothing until it's clear of this car, so two cars on one spot are never thrown apart. The same goes the other way: this car put down near a stand-in (a reset, or a challenge's retry on to a start place where the partner's last state still stands) leaves it touching nothing until the two are clear. A remote car is drawn (`render/remote-cars.ts`, the same `art/cars` art, one set of geometry per kind of car) only where its ground has detail here, because its height is its own client's, from detailed ground; further off it is a marker on the maps. Both maps draw every other player wherever they are, in their own colour: off the map's edge a marker waits on the rim, the way they lie, and the world map names them.

A page on tiles of its own (`?tiles=`, every tool and test) or one driven by automation drives alone unless it names a relay (`?relay=local:<port>`), so a tool never turns up in the real world; `?junction-lab` always drives alone. `?drive-world=<name>` gives a test a world of its own.

**The GPS destination is the room's, one for everyone** (`net/online-world.ts`), so two players can meet up or drive somewhere together. Anyone may set it from the world map, and the latest pick replaces it; anyone's clear clears it for all. Only the point goes over the wire: each player's GPS routes to it from where they are. A guest's pick goes up to the host, which puts picks in order, stamps each with who made it and sends it to every guest, the picker too, so two picks at once end on the same one everywhere. Every client keeps the last, so a new host hands it to whoever arrives next. One another player set has its pin in the players' colour, with their name on the world map, and a short notice (their name and a pin) says when another player sets or clears it. Reaching it ends the route for that player only. A challenge's route shows over it while the challenge lasts, and the GPS goes back to it after.

Computer-driven cars go the same way (`net/computer-cars.ts`, piece 6 of `ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`): each is run by the player nearest it, who sends its state and its driver's record to everyone as their own car's, and hands it to another player once they've been clearly nearer for a moment and no other car is within a few metres of it; the host hands on the cars of a player who leaves. Everyone else sees it as a stand-in, as another player's car. Two players who take one car at once settle on the lower id, and the player running one can take it out of the world for everyone. In play the only ones are traffic cars hit out of the traffic (below); a test also has a scripted car (`net/scripted-car.ts`).

**Traffic is shared** (`net/shared-traffic.ts`, piece 14 of `ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`): every car, cyclist and walker on the lanes is run by the player nearest it, by the same rule, and every page near it drives it too, with the same driver, between the runner's messages, so a message only puts right what has drifted. A vehicle's whole record is its place on its lane, its speed, its body and a few flags (`LaneRecord`), and its choices at junctions are its seed's, so two pages driving it choose alike. A slot is filled only by the player nearest it, by 10 m, and only beyond every other player's reach as well as out of its own sight, since another player's camera isn't known here (`traffic/bubble.ts`); two players together fill each stretch once. Every distance in that is from a player's car, the place the others are told of, even with the free camera elsewhere, and a page fills nothing until it has heard where every player is and each has sent it what they have round it, or one joining a world would fill what's already there: every traffic message names the players its sender knows the place of, and a sender sends one as soon as it knows a new player's, empty or not. Online a vehicle's id says who made it, so a slot refilled where its old vehicle is far off and unknown makes a twin, never a clash. A vehicle is taken away only by its runner, once it's past every player's bubble edge; a page lets go of one it doesn't run as it leaves its own. Each runner sends what it runs only while another player is near enough to hold it, all of it in one message: a car twice a second, ten times within 60 m of another player's car, a cyclist or walker once a second, about 120 bytes a vehicle. A player who leaves (the host too) leaves each of their vehicles to whichever player still here is nearest it and holds it, and two that claim one at once settle on the lower id. A paused page (the map, a menu, a jump's load) keeps stepping its share of the traffic, since everyone else sees it go on. A traffic car hit by a player's car is hit on that player's page only, since stand-ins touch only the car being driven, so that page takes it out of everyone's traffic and runs it as a computer-driven car (above), sent 20 times a second, drawn by everyone as the world car it was with its hazards on; whoever runs it when it rejoins its lane puts it back in the traffic, and one that can't waits until every player is 150 m off. `tools/traffic-bandwidth.ts` measures what's sent, players standing together on the built tiles' lane maps with no browser: two players at the busiest spot of central Mariehamn (147 vehicles round them: 53 cars, 21 cyclists, 73 walkers) send 25 kB a second between them, each about 12 kB up and as much down; in Godby 10 kB; on a country road 3 kB. A third player there adds almost nothing to anyone's upload and downloads the other two's, 26 kB. Players apart send nothing. `e2e/aland-drive-online.spec.ts` plays it on the run's own relay: two pages meet with no code, see each other drive, hit, show on both maps, and the one left hosts when the host goes. `e2e/aland-drive-shared-traffic.spec.ts` has two pages on the fixture with its lane map, which only that spec serves (`serveTiles`' `withLanes`), see the same cars, cyclists and walkers in the same places both ways, after vehicles change hands and after the host goes; `net/shared-traffic.slow.test.ts` holds, with no browser, that two players fill a road once, and that nothing they could see jumps or vanishes through a handover or a player leaving.
