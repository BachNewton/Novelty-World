# Åland Drive: sound

All of the game's sound, made in code. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down.

`audio/` is the sound, all made in code: the continuous sounds on one small Web Audio graph (`audio/graph.ts`) whose parameters each frame steers, and the one-shots as ZzFX designs on the shared sound library (`audio/sounds.ts`). `audio/model.ts` holds the rules with no Web Audio in them (what the game tells the sound each frame, the jolts heard in the car's motion, and the levels and pitches those ask for), so they are unit-tested. `audio/synthesis.ts` renders every loop once as samples, seeded, so a loop is the same every visit.

- **Each car has its own engine** (`audio/voices/<id>.ts`, registered in `audio/vehicles.ts`), a voice on that car's own gearbox from `sim/cars.ts`. In the game the sound hears the simulation's real gear and revs, so a car swapped mid-drive changes voice with its handling. Each voice file opens by saying what engine it is, and "How to voice a car" below says what every number does. A combustion engine is a source and its resonances, as a real one is. Its firings are rendered (`synthesis.ts`) as pressure pulses at the crank angles its layout fires at (`audio/firing.ts`), each bank down its own header, the cylinders' lope and each firing's stray drawn from the voice's seed, into a channel for the exhaust and one for the engine bay (the intake breathing, the valve gear, a diesel's knock, an air-cooled fan). Four loops are rendered, at a low and a high revs, on load and off, each a whole number of cycles so they stay in step, and the graph blends them by revs and load (`loopWeights`, `model.ts`), so the timbre itself changes with the throttle, not only the level. The exhaust then rings in resonances fixed in Hz, as a pipe's are whatever the revs (a feedback delay at the pipe's note, the silencer's peak and low-pass), so the note swells and drones through the revs rather than every partial sliding with them. A turbo spools with load and revs after its lag, whistles, and vents a lift through a valve or in a flutter; the exhaust pops on a lift at high revs, and anti-lag bangs off the throttle; the limiter bounces; a supercharger and the gearbox whine; a motor whines in its orders with its inverter's tones over it. What the sound remembers from frame to frame (the turbo, the last lift) is `stepEngineSound`, and every rule is in `model.ts`, unit-tested.
- **Where the car is heard from** is the frame's `perspective`: outside by default, behind the car with the chase camera, hearing the exhaust; or inside, from the cockpit, with the intake, the mechanicals, the turbo and the whines close and the exhaust muffled through the body to its boom, or straight in for an open car (the voice's `cabin`). The game sets it from its camera mode where it builds the frame with `stateFromCar` (`render/game.ts`).
- **Tyres** are shared by every car and follow what each wheel is on, as the simulation has it (`SimulatedCar.surfaces()` and the drive's condition, `sim/surfaces.ts`): each surface's bed plays by its share of the wheels touching the ground, so two wheels on the verge are half grass and a wheel in the air is on nothing, and crossing the road's edge is heard. Tarmac roars, rock is a harder gritty roll with knocks at its joints, gravel and grass are their own loops. In a slide tarmac squeals, and loose ground scrabbles in its own bed and throws stones (gravel and rock) or turf (grass) into the arches as one-shots, at a rate the mix sets; fast gravel throws the odd stone just rolling. The condition lies on every surface at once: wet dulls the beds and adds a spray loop and a lower, splashier hiss in a slide, and keeps most stones down; snow covers the ground with a soft crunch of its own and squeaks in a slide, with no stones; ice is near silent rolling and hisses thin in a slide. Studded tyres tick and hum on a bare hard road, a loop of four wheels' studs, duller when wet and on ice and buried by snow. The tables of surface and condition are in `audio/model.ts`. **Known gap:** gravel is a gritty grind with short crunches and a few stone knocks over it, better than the pitched clicks it was (heard as rain), but the noise pass under the crunches still doesn't sound great, and it could be better.
- **Wind and places**: wind is the car's own, by its speed, and nothing else adds wind. Each kind of place round the car (`sim/surroundings.ts`: the sea's shore, a lake's bank, which lies above a metre where the sea never does, town, and forest, a stand-in for the land that's neither, because canopy data is only on the GPU) is heard by what's there, never by a bed of filtered noise, which reads the same everywhere: the sea's swells rising, breaking and washing back fizzing over the stones, with gulls calling over the water (placed sounds, off the nearest sea); a lake's small wavelets slapping and gulping at the bank; the forest's birds and in spring a woodpecker, and on a winter's day a crow or a tit now and then; a frozen sea or lake the ice's creaks and the falling whistle of a crack running across it; and in town each church striking the hour from 8 to 21 from where it stands (placed sounds), the rest of a town's sound being its traffic and people, heard where they are. They follow the season, the hour and the weather the render draws (`placeMix`, `audio/model.ts`), easing rather than switching: birdsong from mid-March, full from May to mid-July and gone by September, by daylight from the sun's height, loudest in a spring dawn chorus and hushed by rain, the sea's waves and a lake's lapping giving way to the ice as each freezes, and gulls from April to September by day.
- **Sounds placed in the world** (`audio/placed.ts`, the rules in `audio/model.ts`): a sound from a place in the scene, as loud as its loudness 8 m off and falling off as one over the distance past that, from its own side of the listener (the camera, its place and its right, which the game hands the sound each frame as the state's `listener`). A loop sounds while its caller names it each frame, `placeLoops` with an id that keeps it one voice; a one-shot is `placeOnce` with a sound, a place, a loudness and a rate, its side and loudness following the listener while it plays. Only the loudest heard play, up to a budget of voices (`PLACED_VOICES`, `setPlacedBudget`), a number a frame reads that a setting can turn down; a loop past it stops and starts again when it's among the loudest, and a one-shot asked for with the budget full isn't played. A new kind of placed sound is a row in `placed.ts`'s list, a loop rendered once by `synthesis.ts`. The gulls and the church bells use it, and the traffic's voices, pass-bys, horns and indicators, and later the cyclists' and walkers', are to use it too (`ideas/traffic-and-racers.md`, "How it sounds").
- **The weather**: rain on the roof and glass, two loops of drops, each a short broadband tap with the roof panel's dull thump under some, softened as a roof and glass would: light rain's drops heard one by one, and heavy rain's dense hiss, which takes over as it falls harder and as the car drives faster into it, partly covered by the car's own noise at speed; wet snow is a soft patter of it; and the weather's own wind, a gusting band from a breeze up to a gale, apart from the wind of the car's speed. Both play while the drive is stopped, as the place does. The audition has a slider for each.
- **`?sounds` is the audition** (`audio/sound-lab.tsx`), on the shared `SoundLab`: the forest, shore and lake sounds, with a season and an hour for them (a day in each season, with the game's own sun and season for it), every car and the bicycle, each car on its own gearbox, where flooring it pulls as that car pulls in the game on the flat, through its gears to its own top speed. The left and right wheels each take a surface, so the road's edge can be straddled, and the condition and the tyres are chosen beside them. The revs can be held on one note, the engine heard from outside or from the cockpit, and the one-shots play below. Each car voiced before the new engine synthesis has an A/B switch, and the B key, between that old sound and the new one on the same gearbox and throttle: the old synthesis is kept, frozen, in `audio/old/` only for this comparison, and is deleted with the switch and `auditionEngine` in `graph.ts` once the owner has decided. `audio/tools/` renders scenes headless through the same graph and measures what comes out. The owner judges the sound by ear.

## How to voice a car

A voice is numbers in `audio/voices/<id>.ts` and a line in `vehicles.ts`, on the gearbox of the car's own tuning. Start from the existing car nearest in kind, change what makes this engine itself, and listen on `?sounds`: floor it, lift at high revs, hold an idle, then the same from the cockpit. `audio/tools/check.mjs --engines --car <id>` measures it alone, `--cost` counts it, and the plain run fails any scene that clips.

**The engine (`combustion`)**, null for an electric car:

- `firing`: when each cylinder fires and which exhaust it leaves by, from `firing.ts`. `inline(n)` for a straight three, four, five or six, petrol or diesel; `vee(n)` for an even-firing V6, V10 or V12; `crossPlaneV8` for the burble; `flatPlaneV8` for a supercar's shriek; `oddFireV6` for a lumpy old 90° V6; `boxerFour` for a flat four; `airCooledFlatFour` for the people's car.
- `banks`: the second exhaust's gain, its header's ring as a multiple of the first's, and how much later its pulses arrive, ms. One exhaust is 1, 1, 0. A vee's banks differ a little (0.9 to 0.95, 1.05 to 1.1, 0.2 to 0.5 ms); a cross-plane V8 wants them clearly apart (0.85, 1.15, 0.8) so each pipe's lope is heard; a boxer's unequal-length headers are a delay of about 2 ms, which is the whole of its rumble.
- `lope` and `jitter`: how differently the cylinders burn (0.03 a V12, 0.05 a sharp four, 0.12 a lumpy V8 or a rally engine, 0.25 a tired old four) and how much each firing strays from the last time round (0.05 smooth to 0.18 rough). Jitter is what keeps a held note alive.
- `pulseMs` and `headerHz`: a pulse's length (0.9 ms hard and barky for a high-revving engine, 2 ms or more soft and thudding for a big lazy one) and the header's ring in it (400 to 800 Hz, higher for short race headers).
- `roughness`: turbulence in each pulse, 0.2 smooth to 0.4 hard-worked, 0.6 a diesel. `knock` and `knockHz`: a diesel's clatter, about 1 at 2 to 3 kHz, and 0 for petrol. `chatter`: valve gear ticking, 0.1 modern, 0.25 to 0.3 old, 0.5 or more air-cooled. `fan`: an air-cooled engine's cooling fan, its whine's order (its pulley's ratio times its blades, around 40 crank turns) and level (about 0.2); null otherwise.
- `pipeHz` and `pipeRing`: the exhaust pipe's lowest note (90 Hz for a big engine's long pipe to 200 for a short race pipe, never above 300) and how long it rings (0.35 tight to 0.5 droning). `exhaustHz`: the silencer's low-pass closed, and what the revs and the load each open it by: a quiet saloon or a diesel keeps it low and shut (about 600, 800, 800), a sports exhaust opens wide (1100, 3400, 2200), and no silencer starts open. `silencerHz` and `silencerDb`: its resonance, 170 Hz deep to 450 bright, 4 to 9 dB. `rasp`: how hard the load drives the exhaust into distortion, 1.2 a diesel to 2.8 a race engine.
- `intakeHz`, `intakeQ` and `intake`: the airbox's resonance (180 to 400 Hz), its sharpness (1.2 to 3), and the induction roar under load (0.35 muffled, 0.5 typical, 1 for twin carbs, individual throttles or a howling high-revving four). The intake is heard mostly from the cockpit.
- `limiterHz`: the redline's bounce, 6 to 10 Hz a road or old car, 14 to 18 a race car.

**Around the engine:**

- `turbo`, null without one: `boostFrom`, the share of the redline it makes boost from (0.2 a small modern turbo, 0.3 to 0.4 a big rally or Group B one); `spoolS` (0.45 small and quick, 0.7 rally, 0.9 diesel, 1.2 or more a 1980s Group B lag); `whistleHz` from barely turning to flat out (about 2 to 8 kHz); `whistle` and `hiss` (0.06 a quiet road car, 0.12 a rally car); `blowOff`, `"valve"` for a road car's psssh, `"flutter"` for a rally or tuned car with no valve; `blowOffLevel`, 0 for a diesel, which has no throttle to shut.
- `supercharger`, null without one: its whine's order (8 to 14 crank turns, a twin-screw's higher) and level (0.05 to 0.1).
- `overrun`: `crackle`, pops a second just after a lift at high revs (0 a saloon or diesel, 1 to 2 a sports car, 4 a race or rally car, more for a muscle car on a loud exhaust), and `antiLag`, bangs a second off the throttle (0 except rally and Group B, 2 to 4).
- `gearWhine`: teeth meshing per turn (17 to 25) and level: 0.01 a road box, 0.04 an old transfer box, 0.06 to 0.1 a straight-cut rally or race box.
- `electric`, null with no motor: `rpmPerMS`, null when the motor is the car's engine and its revs are the gearbox's, and for a hybrid's motor on another axle its turns a minute per m/s; the whine's `orders` with their levels (a motor's pole pairs and slot harmonics, about 8, 24 and 48 turns); `polePairs`; the inverter's `carrierHz` (2 to 5 kHz) and `inverter` level; and `regen`, the share of the whine heard on a lift.
- `cabin`: how much of the outside reaches the seat, 0 a sealed saloon, 0.1 a coupé, 0.8 an open four-seater, 1 an open roadster.
- `level` and `idleLevel`: loudness (0.3 to 0.36; an electric car about 0.2) and the share kept at idle with no load (0.32 a V12 saloon to 0.55 a lazy V8 or a diesel).

**By kind**, the choices that matter most:

- A cross-plane V8: `crossPlaneV8` on banks well apart, lope 0.12, long pulses, a low pipe and silencer, crackle 1 to 3. A 1970s muscle car is the same louder, with the silencer barely there.
- A flat-plane V8: `flatPlaneV8`, short pulses, a high pipe, rasp near 3, a fast limiter, crackle.
- A straight six: `inline(6)`, lope 0.06, bright up high. A V12: `vee(12)`, lope 0.03, a quiet idle; a 1960s V12 GT rougher (jitter 0.1, chatter 0.25) and open-throated. A V6: `vee(6)` gruffer than a six, its banks apart. A five: `inline(5)`, warbling from its odd count.
- A flat four: `boxerFour`, rumbling only on unequal pipes (a 2 ms bank delay). An air-cooled flat four: `airCooledFlatFour`, chatter 0.5, a fan, a short open pipe, low revs.
- A turbo four hot hatch: `inline(4)`, a small quick turbo with a valve; a 1990s turbo-four rally saloon adds a flutter, anti-lag and a straight-cut whine.
- A turbo diesel: knock 1, roughness 0.6, a shut silencer, a slow turbo, no blow-off, no crackle.
- Rally and Group B: anti-lag, a flutter, a straight-cut whine and crackle; Group B's turbo spools late and slow and whistles hard.
- An electric car: `combustion: null` and its motor, the motor's revs the gearbox's; an electric four-motor car is the same voice louder with more inverter; a hybrid keeps its engine and adds a quieter motor on its own axle (`rpmPerMS`).
