# Åland Drive: rendering and streaming

How the world streams in and is drawn: terrain, forest, plantings, buildings, everything placed, water and sky, and the light and weather on all of it. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview, with the graphics principles every change here follows.

Counts below (draw calls, triangles) are context from when a feature landed, not budgets: the tools in `tools/CLAUDE.md` give today's.

## Streaming

- **Tiles load round the car** (`render/stream.ts`): detail within 2.2 km, dropped at 2.8 km so an edge doesn't load back and forth. The reach is worked back from the top speed: detail is first seen at 1.4 km, and 800 m is 11 seconds at 72 m/s, room for the tiles that fall due together at a corner over a slow connection. At most four tiles are at full detail. Loading follows the car's position and each tile landing, with no timers; in the free camera it follows the camera.
- **Three steps of land, so nothing grows with the island.** The overview (`pipeline/overview.py`) is the whole world in two small files from the start: heights every 80 m with the forest on them, and the land's colour at 40 m with its light baked in. Far data (heights at 16 m, shading at 8 m, a surface mask at 4 m) is fetched per tile within 6 km and dropped past 7.5 km, into a fixed pool of 16 layers. Then detail. Only the tiles round the start come before the drive does. The terrain fades from far images to the overview between 4.4 and 5.8 km, inside the reach every tile is sure to hold them, so arriving tiles never show as a line.
- **Off the main thread** (`render/tile.worker.ts`): the fetch, inflating, image decoding and mipmaps, and turning heights and roads into the game's arrays. The main thread only copies to the GPU, a slice of a tile's images a frame, and a tile counts as loaded once all of it is there. A failed fetch stops the game with a message on screen, and a test holds that.
- **A landing costs a frame nothing** at a capped frame rate. Uncapped (a browser run with vsync off), frames outrun the GPU and a texture copy blocks the main thread for hundreds of milliseconds, which no player meets, so `tools/drive.mjs --clock` and `tools/landings.mjs` measure capped. What keeps it so:
  - **No pipeline is compiled mid-drive.** Every mesh is built once before the drive starts. three keys an instanced mesh's shader by the mesh and bakes its count in, so an `InstancedMesh` is made once with room to spare and rewritten in place.
  - **A big shader is shared, not instanced.** Keyed by the mesh, a material on several instanced meshes compiles once for each, so a big shader's instances are placed by attributes of their own on a plain mesh: the villas' add-ons draw through the walls' own material (`standAsBuilt`), one pipeline.
  - **No big job is left to three.** It measures a new geometry's bounding sphere over its whole position buffer the frame it is first drawn, so a mesh sharing a tile's buffer is handed its sphere from the worker.
  - `tools/shader-count.mjs` counts what a cold page compiles; `tools/map-zoom.mjs` counts the programs compiled and slow frames zooming the world map into Mariehamn.
- **First load** shows a bar counting the overview, each far tile round the start, the start's detail and the shaders. The road network comes after the drive starts, so it can't hold the start up.

## The order of a frame

three sorts its opaque list and then, under a reversed depth buffer, reverses the whole list, which rights the depth order but turns `renderOrder` upside down: the sky, meant last, was drawn first and shaded every pixel. So the renderer's sorting is off, and `render/draw-order.ts` keeps the scene's children in one order by the name of the material each draws with, which every material has (the tools read pipelines by it too): the car, the walls' depth, the walls, the roads, the placed things, the plantings and the forest, then the ground, the sea plane and the sky, each drawn once everything it could be behind has been, and the transparent things after. No pixel depends on the order; only what is shaded for nothing does. What makes the order pay:

- **The walls' depth goes first.** The wall shader is the biggest the game has, and a town's walls stand two and three deep from any street, so every mesh it draws has a depth twin drawn first (`wallDepthMaterial`, `depthTwin` in `render/buildings.ts`), the same vertices writing depth and no colour, and the wall material shades only where the depth is its own. three turns every depth function round under a reversed depth buffer, Equal to NotEqual among them, so the material asks for NotEqual.
- **The forest and the plantings come out near to far.** Their compute passes walk their lattices in a square spiral from the camera's cell outwards (`render/spiral.ts`), so each list is appended near to far, more or less, and the depth test spares the crowns and tufts behind those drawn first. Row by row, a list came out back to front for half the ways a camera can face.
- **A list holds only the places its version can draw** (see Placed models).
- **A pixel of terrain works out only what it shows**: the land's colour where it's land, the water's where it's water, the overview's where the far images give out, and the ground's relief and the town's ground only within their fades, each in a real branch (`terrainColour` in `render/game.ts`, `groundDetail`, `townGround`). Off its band each weight is exactly 0 or 1, so the blends give the colour that working everything out everywhere gave. A node two branches share is written in the first and read unset in the second (`tools/unset-reads.mjs` is the check), so what branches share is worked out before them, the open sea has a water and an ice look of its own, and the derivatives every tile read samples by are given to the stack before the branches: a derivative is only defined where every pixel of the quad takes the same path, which a branch isn't.

`tools/frame-cost.mjs` counts what each draw shaded, and `tools/frame-diff.mjs` holds a change to the order pixel-identical (`tools/CLAUDE.md`).

## Terrain, roads and ground

- **The terrain is one mesh** (`render/clipmap.ts`), a clipmap: seven square rings of 192 cells following the camera, each ring's cells twice the last's, out to 25 km. Each ring's heights are a layer of a small array texture, copied from the world when it moves, and one material reads its own ring's layer, so the terrain is one pipeline. A vertex is only a place in that texture, so tiles don't exist for the mesh and a seam can't show. Each ring's vertices fold on to the next ring's lattice with distance, so rings meet without a crack, and the innermost ring is exactly the 4 m mesh the car collides with. Chosen over per-tile chunks with skirts, which need geometry per tile and hide seams rather than having none.
- **One shader for every tile** (`render/tile-layers.ts`): each kind of image is an array texture with a layer per tile, and a pixel finds its tile from where it is, so tiles cost no draw calls and meet within a pixel. Image coordinates jump at a tile's edge, so the sampling rate comes from world position, or the blurriest mip would mark every seam.
- **The terrain under a moving sun**: the 2 m bake holds one fixed sun's light, cast shadow and open sky in one channel. The terrain moves it by how much more or less the ground faces the sun now than the baked one, from a smooth per-vertex normal: identical to the bake where the sun stands as baked. Cast shadows stay where baked, which shows little on Åland's low relief. `?terrain-light=` (`render/terrain-light.ts`) shows the bake as it was, or flat, to compare.
- **Roads with distance.** Ribbons are cut into 600 m cells, shown within 1.2 km. The terrain paints its own roads from the mask, fading in from 250 m: under the ribbons it shows only where a coarser ring rises through one, in the same colour, and past them it is the road. Paint is drawn as each mark's share of the pixel (`art/surfaces/coverage.ts`), so marks thin into the road far off and never fade or vanish. A ribbon's look is `art/surfaces/road.ts`, in metres: asphalt or gravel by OSM's `surface` (the pipeline writes each chain's `sealed`), else by class, with service and unclassified paved only in town (a path wears its own class, see Paths in `pipeline/CLAUDE.md`); wear, patches, cracks, kerbs and paving in town, and marks where a lesser road meets a main one, from per-vertex attributes (`road-attributes.ts`). An apron takes the road nearest it, with no marks; a car park or bus station is paving of its own, held last in the road surface file. The 2 m shading gives way to the far shading between 1 and 1.4 km.
- **The land's colour** is the photo at 2 m near and 8 m for every tile from the start. Its alpha fades the photo out over open water, for what reads the shallows from it. Close up the ground is drawn over it (`art/surfaces/ground.ts`): each pixel's colour says grass, earth, rock or gravel, and that material's relief goes over the photo, gone by 150 m. A road's shoulder fades to the same image.
- **Snow on the land** (`art/surfaces/snow.ts`) changes the ground's colour before the roads are painted and before the hour's light, by the season's `snowCover` and `snowDepthCm`; the overview past the tiles takes the same, so no line shows. It comes and goes patch by patch, each place's threshold rolled from noise on whole cells (`art/hash.ts`) and leaned by what the place is: thin cover lies first on north slopes, open fields and the ploughed bank by a road, last on south slopes, under trees and on bare rock. Far off the patches settle on their share, so nothing crawls. Under full cover the ground still reads (trodden town ground, a brighter ploughed verge); the carriageway is the road surface's own. Colours are the `snow` and `snow-shade` tokens every snow shares. Roofs, the rural kit's upward faces and the granite take the same snow on thresholds of their own. It adds only per-pixel arithmetic, and at `SUMMER` the ground is exactly as without it.
- **The town's ground** (`art/town-ground`) is tabled and off (see `docs/HANDOFF.md`): lawn, beds, gravel, slabs, setts and car-park tarmac between a town's roads and buildings, drawn by the terrain from the tile's town image (see `pipeline/CLAUDE.md`), a near layer like the photo's. A lawn is left as the terrain's own ground, with the verge on it. The image's colour channels are signed distances to class edges, so an edge stays sharp at any range and no filter blends two classes; its alpha is the paving's direction, read at the nearest texel. Slabs and setts lie on a street grid's own lattice. Patterns fade by pixel size, and the whole returns to the photo between 320 and 760 m. At 2 m the image doubles the near photo's memory; 4 m is a quarter of that but rounds corners and loses narrow strips, and the choice is the owner's, from pictures.
- **Water is a surface of its own** (`render/clipmap.ts`, `render/water.ts`): the clipmap's two nearest rings again, at the water's baked level, on the same triangles. Where the line falls is the shore's smooth line, from the baked distance to it at every node, not where the 4 m mesh crosses the water, which would draw squares and teeth. Past the line water is drawn; short of it, land. Depth sets the colour (pale and green-brown over the first two metres, dark past that), and land a hand's height above the water is darker, wet. Past those rings the ground under water is drawn as the water above it. Past the tiles the sea plane lies under the deepest seabed and takes its shallows from the overview. A triangle with a corner where no water is near isn't drawn, so the surface never shows through land.
- **Past the last tile** is the sea plane and haze. The world's end is a deliberate wall, out at sea or on the far shore of the tiles round the island.

## The forest

- **No list of trees** (`render/forest.ts`): a tree is a place on a 6 m lattice of the world, its height read from the tile's canopy image, its species and whether it stands from the tile's stands, the rest rolled from the place (`render/tree-place.ts`), so a tree comes back the same and moving costs nothing. Each frame a compute pass keeps the places within reach where a tree stands, clear of roads and in view, cheapest test first, into one list per model drawn by indirect draws whose counts the pass writes. So the triangle count follows what's in view. three counts an indirect draw as one tree, so the probe adds the GPU's own count.
- **Nothing thins and nothing changes height.** Every tree stays to the forest's reach: the full shape to 464 m and a far crown past it, handing over by a screen-door crossfade on complementary pixels. A tree's ground is blended across the terrain's nodes and chosen by its distance, not its model, so a handover can't move it. Past the last trees the terrain's own vertices rise by the canopy, so forest stands to the horizon for no triangles.
- **None on a road or in a building.** A tree reads the road mask at its 8 m level, over a tile's edge too, and the tile's clearing round buildings (see `pipeline/CLAUDE.md`): it is no taller than lets its crown, however far any crown spreads and leans (`CROWN_REACH`, `render/tree-shapes.ts`), clear the nearest building; a pine too near to lean grows straight, and one that would be under 3 m isn't there. `tools/tree-check.ts` counts both across the island, and a test holds the road rule.
- **Settings** are numbers a frame reads (`forestKnobs`): full-shape reach, forest reach and far density, with no rebuild. `FOREST_MOST` sizes the buffers for the top setting, and `FOREST_DEFAULTS` in `render/game.ts` is the floor's. Far density below 1 dissolves a share of far places for weaker hardware; the default keeps every tree. `tools/forest-count.mjs` prints the counts at the showcase views; `?forest-lab` is the forest on its own (`tools/CLAUDE.md`), sharing the game's reads through `render/tile-reads.ts`.
- **What a tree is** (`render/tree-shapes.ts`): one table of outlines read by the vertex shader, so a new shape is a row, never a mesh or a draw. A far crown reads a table worked from the same rows, so the distant forest keeps its mix and a handover keeps the outline. Pine, spruce and birch come from the stands; MS-NFI's other broadleaf can't say where an oak stands, so it is two general shapes, round (oak, ash, maple, likelier in the open) and upright (alder, aspen, rowan). Height sets the stage of life, young to grown to old, so a stage can't pop. A pine on rock is told by being short where the canopy round it is much taller, and it is thick, forked into plates and leans downwind of the south-westerly, blended by how sure the signal is. A pine's crown is a known gap: four attempts to make it read as tufts were rejected (`ideas/pine-crowns.md`).
- **How a tree is lit** (`render/forest.ts`, leaves in `render/foliage-light.ts`): its species' colour under light worked out per pixel, with no texture. Each face is lit by a blend of its own direction and the crown's as a whole, so facets show and the crown still turns from light to shade as one: wrapped sun, and sky brightest on faces looking up. The crown shades itself from the outline table (dark at its base, darker where a ring is tucked inside the envelope), deeper among other trees than alone. Each tree rolls its brightness and hue; birch and broadleaf edges glow towards the sun. The far crown carries the near crown's mean tucked shade and matches its brightness, and the forest as a whole matches the terrain's canopy lift, the overview and the photo where they stand in for it.
- **Through the year** (`art/leaves.ts`, `docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`) a broadleaf turns colour, drops its leaves and leafs out, and snow lies on a conifer's upward faces, each tree on thresholds rolled from its place. A broadleaf short of leaves is a different object (`render/bare-tree.ts`): a branching trunk fitted to the crown's outline, clumps of leaves at its twigs' ends that grow out of the shrinking crown and fall one by one, and a see-through haze of fine twigs that thickens with distance as the branches go below a pixel. These are four more lists, one draw each, for trees not fully in leaf.

## Plantings

The verge, reeds, shrubs and granite are plantings (`render/planting.ts`, sharing the lattice passes, lists, counts and far colours), built as the forest is: a plant is a place on a lattice of the world, kept or not each frame by a compute pass reading only images a tile already has (`landAt`, `roadsAt`, `render/tile-reads.ts`). So nothing is stored or streamed and no tile is rebuilt for them. Shared rules:

- **Nothing changes size.** A lattice ends by its plants dissolving one at a time, each at a distance of its own, where a plant is a few pixels; shrinking or growing them in at the reach showed while driving even with colours matched.
- **Near and far versions are the kit's**, swapping where the LOD check measured the change unnoticed, scaled by each place's size (`art/CLAUDE.md`), trading pixels over a band. Far off a plant takes on the photo's colour there.
- **Nothing stands in a building, a wall or the water.** Each reads the tile's clearing round buildings, the wall clearing (see Stone walls) and the shore line the terrain draws by (the finest clipmap ring holding the place), standing only where its whole spread clears.
- **They have no collision**, and they change through the year by the season's dials (`docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`).

The plantings:

- **The verge** (`render/verge.ts`): `art/street`'s grass and flowers beside the roads, on lattices every 0.5 m to about 200 m and every 1.5 m to 500 m. How near a road, how clear of the carriageway, how dry and how paved are each read from a mask or the photo; none grows on a town's hard ground (`pavedAt`), but it does on lawns and beds. A tuft takes a growing share of its ground's colour and baked shade from 140 m. Through the year grass goes to straw, flowers fold away, fallen leaves lie at its feet and deep snow buries it.
- **Reeds** (`render/reeds.ts`): the rural kit's clumps in the water along sheltered shores. Colour can't place them (a reed bed and bare granite are the same tan in the photo); shelter can: OSM's mapped reed beds have far more land round them than its bare rock. So a clump stands within a few tens of metres of land where the water round about is mostly land and the shallows are pale, not round docks and boats (cleared in the 8 m layer, see Surface mask in `pipeline/CLAUDE.md`) nor on a town's shore. Shelter reads the mask's 256 m level on a grid of nine, because a tile's coarser mips stretch over more ground than they hold. A clump is stems and leaves each turned its own way, never a card. Its green goes to straw-gold for winter.
- **Bushes, junipers and young birches** (`render/shrubs.ts`): hazel, sallow (likelier near water) and birch along forest edges; juniper on open rock among trees, by the photo's stone with forest near, since a ploughed field is as pale as rock. Lit as the trees are (`render/foliage-light.ts`). The broadleaves turn and lose their leaves as the forest's do, a bare one (`art/rural/bare-plants.ts`) being its stems near and a haze of twigs further off; a juniper takes snow.
- **Granite** (`render/granite.ts`): Åland's bare rock, over the rock the Topographic Database maps (in the surface mask's alpha). The rock's domes and steps are already the terrain; what the 4 m mesh can't carry is its skin. So an outcrop is the rural kit's dome laid over the ground, every corner on the innermost ring's own triangles (`groundOnMeshAt`, the diagonals the car collides with), raised a few decimetres and sinking at water and carriageways, so its outline is the map's and none covers a road. Because the spring photo bleaches granite near white, the terrain takes the kit's rapakivi red wherever rock is mapped and no grass shows (`rockFloor`, `art/surfaces/ground.ts`), and an outcrop wears the same colour, so from afar it is the photo and it can dissolve by 260 m. A boulder field is the stones' colour in the terrain with a stony relief, plus a planting of the kit's boulder, each stone lasting until it is about three pixels across. Mapped boulders and a field's biggest stones are placed models instead (see Placed models) and stop the car.

## Placed models

Most placed things share one pattern (`render/placed-models.ts`): one list of places per model across every loaded tile, so a model is two draw calls (near and far) however many tiles are near, each place leaving at its kit's reach. A place past its swap or reach folds to nothing in the shader but still runs its vertices, so every list holds only the places within its version's range of the camera and a margin, written again when the camera has moved that margin: the near lists within the swap and the far lists within the reach, the range mirroring the kit's material (`nearRangeM`, `farRangeM` in `art/roadside/material.ts`), which picks each place's version by its own distance, so a list changing shows nothing.

- **Buildings** (`render/buildings.ts`) stream with their tile, merged per 600 m cell, shown within the detail radius; past it a building is its roof in the photo. Walls have no texture: boards, windows, doors, trim and shops are drawn in the shader from each facade's real width and eaves. A wall has one of ten looks named by its tint (the pipeline's timber, plaster and boards, or `art/facades`' villa, brick, block, glass, hall, barn and granite), in 32 paints, rolled per building. Looks are real branches, so a pixel pays only for its own. Every shape is drawn as its share of the pixel (`art/surfaces/coverage.ts`), blurring into the wall's average far off; only what can't be drawn so fades, while still a few pixels across, to its own average. Roofs and shopfronts are drawn the same way, in their face's own metres written on the vertices. A shop is only where OSM has a real one: its sign says the trade, or an invented name for a chain, never a real one, in road-sign letters.
- **Boathouses, sheds and the farm** (`render/rural-buildings.ts`) are the rural kit's models fitted to their footprints or places from `models.json`. A far version wears its near version's door, windows and trim painted on as their share of the pixel, so the swap shows nothing. Bales, tractors, sheep and cows reach where each is about a pixel and a half tall. A cow swaps late because its patches are drawn in its own metres and fade to their mean, which its far version wears. A sheep's coat and horns and a cow's breed and patches are each place's roll in both versions' material, so a flock costs no more models than one animal. Boulders draw here too.
- **Windmills** (`render/windmills.ts`) stand 14 m on open farmland, seen for kilometres, so every mill is one list from the start, read from `world.json`: it never leaves with its tile and ends at 12 km, a pixel and a half tall.
- **Docks and boats** (`render/docks.ts`): the pipeline's runs drawn as the rural kit's jetty, pier and pontoon sections end to end, each stretched to fill its run and widened to its width, so any dock is the same few models. A jetty's head and rowing boat are the rural kit's; a marina's boats and those on buoys are `art/boats`', rocking on the water. Out of season the boats are laid up (see Placed things out of season). Every berthed boat's near triangles are counted whether drawn or not, which is most of a marina's cost.
- **People** (`render/people.ts`): `art/people`'s figures where the pipeline placed them, every file a tile lists under `people` baked in the worker into one crowd (`render/tile-people.ts`), so a tile with anyone on it is four draw calls. Each figure's detail is its own by distance in the kit's shader. Their motions run on a clock the game holds when paused. Walkers are the same figures in a crowd that moves (`art/people/walking.ts`): each takes a slot, its shape written once, and each frame writes only its place and stride, run on by the ground covered so its feet keep their place. `tools/people-count.mjs` prints their cost. No collision.
- **Parked cars** (`render/parked-cars.ts`): `art/cars`' eighteen world cars (`fleet.ts`) at the places `pipeline/parking.py` wrote. Model, paint, rack and height are rolled from the place (`render/parked-places.ts`), so the tile worker, which has no art, stands its walls to the same model. Each model is a near list and a stand-in list to 900 m, dissolving at the swap the LOD check measured (`modelSwapM`). The stand-in is the near model simplified (`buildDistant`, `art/cars/body.ts`): the silhouette's own outline, glass where the near model's is, tyres, lamps and indicators where the near model has them. A cruder stand-in was seen as a grey box at its swap. Every world car has indicators (`art/cars/indicators.ts`) flashing by its place's `signal` on a clock the game holds when paused. **A list of world cars uses five of the eight vertex buffers a WebGPU pipeline can read**, the car's own attributes packed into one, so another per-car attribute has to fit in those. `tools/parked-count.mjs` prints their cost. In winter snow lies on a world car's top on a threshold of its own, and on only about a third of the cars in traffic, which have been swept.
- **Stone walls** (`render/stone-walls.ts`): the rural kit's wall laid along each line of `walls.json`. The worker cuts a line into sections near the kit's 2.4 m and stands both ends on the drawn ground (`render/tile-walls.ts`); each section is stretched and sheared to its slope, so a wall follows its ground, and half are laid reversed so stones don't repeat. Sections sit in cells drawn only while in their version's range and view. **The wall clearing** (`render/wall-clearing.ts`) is an image following the camera of each place's distance to the nearest wall, since a wall is too thin for any tile image; every planting reads it, and it carries the street furniture too, each piece a line with a reach of its own (a café table's covers its chairs). Its square reaches past every planting, so no plant vanishes as it moves.
- **The roadside** (`render/roadside.ts`): what `pipeline/roadside.py`, `furniture.py` and `dressing.py` placed, from `art/roadside`, `art/street` and `art/identity`, and the signs, `art/surfaces`' plates and posts from Traficom's drawings, placed through `art/roadside/signs.ts`: plates of one outline share a mesh and a draw, each shifted on the sign sheet, so draws follow outlines, not signs. Versions trade pixels by a screen pattern and the last dissolves at a distance rolled per place, so a row doesn't end on a line; a wire span goes whole at 700 m. Flags move on the roadside's clock, held when paused. A linden yellows and drops its leaves as the forest's broadleaves do (`lindenThroughYear`, `art/street/linden.ts`, bare form `art/street/bare-linden.ts`), whose extra lists fill only while the season needs them. The town detail rides in the same list (`render/town-roadside.ts`): post-top lamps, traffic lights cycling on the renderer's clock, street-name posts and village signs reading name sheets that hold only the loaded tiles' names, redrawn as a texture swap with no new pipeline.
- **Sea marks** (`render/sea-marks.ts`) stand at the official aids to navigation as `art/marina`'s and `art/identity`'s marks, a radar mark and a lighthouse scaled to the register's height (or its light's, `render/lighthouses.ts`). Åland's landmark lighthouses are each a model of their own (`art/lighthouses`, from `docs/LIGHTHOUSES.md`), at real size, taking over the register's mark at their place or standing at their own; one on a skerry no tile draws brings its own rock. Those on a tile take away the map's buildings under them through `art/lighthouses/sites.json` (`landmarks.lighthouse_sites`), written by `render/lighthouse-sites.test.ts`, so **a moved lighthouse or changed far version needs its tile rebuilt**. Each model's list holds only the marks within reach of the camera's kilometre cell. A floating mark stands on the drawn sea, one ashore on the ground.
- **Båtskär's wind turbines** (`render/wind-turbines.ts`, `art/lighthouses/turbine.ts`): six E-70s at OSM's `power=generator` nodes, what marks Nyhamn from Mariehamn's shore. One list from the start, reaching 30 km; the rotor turns about its own z axis on the roadside's clock, and thin parts are held to a pixel so they read on the horizon. No collision: no road reaches them, so play only shows their far versions (`ideas/skerry-landmarks.md`).
- **The regatta** (`render/regatta.ts`): two fleets off Västerhamn and across Lumparn, `art/boats`' `buildRegatta`, one instanced draw, only past 1.5 km, on courses 1.6 km or more from any road so no car sees a fleet dissolve. It sails by the world's clock (`sea/clock.ts`), whole laps in 24 hours so the wrap shows no jump, in season and on open water; pausing doesn't stop it, the probe's `hold` and `holdSea` do. Boats under way are `sea/CLAUDE.md`.
- **Churches and landmarks** (`render/landmarks.ts`): `art/facades`' ten medieval stone churches and `art/landmarks` at their real sites (`art/landmarks/places.ts` for those the data has no building for). Every site's far version is one mesh for the whole island from the start, so the skyline's towers stand at any distance; a tile's near versions are one mesh built when its detail lands, since a churchyard wall follows the ground. Each site shows one version by one flag per site that both meshes read, the other folding to a point, swapping at 1.4 km.
- **What stands at a building** (`render/building-add-ons.ts`, read in the worker by `render/tile-add-ons.ts` from `art/facades/add-on-places.ts`): a villa's veranda or porch at its door, sometimes a bay and fronton, a big shop's canopy and pylon, never on the road surface. The villas' add-ons are one instanced list per kind, made once and rewritten; entrances and the near-only fuel stations join the tile's building cells. The terminal is the town kit's model while its tile has detail.

**Placed things out of season** (`climate/placed-season.ts`, `render/in-season.ts`): moored boats by `boatsAfloat`, cows and sheep by `grazing`, bales by `bales`, a café's terrace by `terraces`, and the people tied to them. Each leaves and returns on its own threshold, rolled from its place on the GPU and CPU alike, a terrace whole on one roll so its sitters go with it. A kit draws through one copy of its material whose vertex shader folds an out-of-season place to its foot, each mesh marked with its kind's share and salt, so the season rebuilds no list and adds no draw or pipeline. A person carries their season on their vertices.

**Nothing but the car is drawn inside it** (`art/car-volume.ts`): every masked material drops its pixels inside the player's car's two boxes, worked out per vertex so no `discard` is added where there was none (see `sim/CLAUDE.md`).

## The sky

The sky (`art/sky`) is one coarse dome round the eye, drawn after everything opaque (`render/draw-order.ts`) and pinned to the far plane so it can't cover the ground from any height: a gradient, the live sun and five reads of one cloud texture. Clouds are a matter of direction, so they stay overhead however high the camera goes. Haze fades to the sky's foot along each pixel's direction, and open water melts into that foot near the horizon (`meltAlong`), so land, sea and sky meet with no line. Water reflects the clear sky.

## Light through the day

The calendar these follow is `time/CLAUDE.md`.

- **One live sun** (`art/sun.ts`): every material reads a vector towards the sun whose length is its strength, falling to nothing as it sets, so a kit's own `dot(normal, toSun)` is the direct light at any hour; a gallery hands a kit a fixed place instead. `render/day-and-night.ts` moves it each frame.
- **The night follows the sun's height**: `art/night`'s dial is set from it, and below the deepest midsummer sun (−6.5°) a second dial darkens land and sky towards a winter night, lightened by a risen moon but never so dark the road is hard to read. Everything drawn goes through `hazedAtHour` (`render/game.ts`): the hour's light on a colour, the headlights and lamps' light, then the haze. Headlights are the player's and the three nearest other players' (`OTHERS_LIT`, `art/night/headlights.ts`), a fixed number of pools so no shader rebuilds as players come and go. Rooms go dark by the clock in the small hours.
- **The night's lights** (`render/night-lights.ts`): every lamp's glow, and the sea's lights from OSM with their colours, sectors and rhythms (`art/night/lights.py`, from one kept Overpass answer), each on the official register's mark at its place and shining from that mark's lamp (`lampOnMark`, `render/sea-marks.ts`, `render/lights-on-marks.ts`); a light with no mark to shine from is left out. A glow is pulled towards the eye so it stays over its lamp. A lamp's light is light, not a patch on top: each lamp within 700 m draws its pool from above into a light image following the camera (`createLampLight`, `art/night/lamps.ts`), and every colour is lit by it in `hazedAtHour`, so a lit road keeps its paint and a car or tree under a lamp is lit too. Lit rooms are the wall shader's own.
- **The winter's lights** (`docs/TIME-AND-WEATHER.md`, Winter lights) follow the season's dials, never a date. In the wall shader (`art/night/festive.ts`): Advent candlesticks and paper stars in a home's panes and strings along its eaves, homes far more than flats and shops, drawn as their share of the pixel; out of season the shader skips them on a branch every pixel takes alike. Mariehamn's own (`render/winter-lights.ts`, `art/night/winter-town.ts`): Torget's tree, Julgatan's strings and the Esplanade's swags, as glows as `lightsTown` rises. Garden trees strung with lights are still to do.
- **The aurora** (`art/night/aurora.ts`): a curtain low in the north, one draw call, as strong as the weather says (`day.weather()`) and only on a night darker than midsummer's. `?aurora=0.8` holds one.
- **The season** reaches every material as the sun does (`art/season.ts`): the season's dials (`climate/season.ts`) as uniforms moved each frame (`render/season-feed.ts`), read by each layer against thresholds rolled from its own places, so nothing seasonal switches on a date and no list is rebuilt for it.

## The weather drawn

The weather itself is `climate/CLAUDE.md`. The render eases towards it (`render/weather.ts`), and it changes how things look, never so far the road is hard to read.

- **The sky**: a grey lid by the cloud cover, a darker storm under rain or snow, fog paling the horizon, all at the hour's own brightness so night stays night. The sun's disc fades into the lid, drawn only ever brighter than the sky behind. The gloom goes on the night's dials, and the live sun is shortened by the lid, so every material's direct light falls with it.
- **Near the camera** (`render/precipitation.ts`): rain as wind-slanted streaks and snow as drifting flakes, one instanced draw over a fixed lattice in a box repeating round the camera, as many drawn as the weather is hard. Nothing moves on the CPU but one offset; none is drawn inside the car or from the map.
- **The road** (`render/road-weather.ts`): water darkens it and mirrors the sky at a low angle, with the sun's glint down the road; fresh snow covers it but for two wheel tracks a lane; black ice is a glassier sheen. The terrain's painted far roads match. Once the season's snow holds the land, a road's shoulder lies under the ploughed bank's snow whatever falls (`render/season-feed.ts`).
- **Fog** closes the haze (`hazeM`) by the square of its thickness, so light fogs stay light and a thick one is still a road's length.
