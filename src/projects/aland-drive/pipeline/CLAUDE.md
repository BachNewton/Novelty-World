# Åland Drive: the pipeline

How tiles are built from the geodata. The sources and what was measured in them are `docs/DATA.md`. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview.

## Running it

`pipeline/` is Python in its own venv: rasterio and pyproj bring GDAL's readers and PROJ's transforms, and NumPy does the rest. Nothing in it touches the game's code.

    python src/projects/aland-drive/pipeline/run.py L2343C [more tiles]

That builds the venv on first use, downloads what is missing into `.data/raw/` (gitignored, one folder per source, read as untrusted data) and writes a folder of plain files per tile to `.data/tiles/`, described by its `tile.json`, and `world.json` listing them all with a version hashed from their contents, beside the overview and the road map. A tile's eight neighbours' elevation is downloaded too, for the seams and the shading. A tile the mirror doesn't have is open sea and stays out of the world. `run.py --island --paving --jobs 7` builds them all, a core each: the published island is paved, and the build's default is still the ribbon roads (`docs/ROAD-SURFACES.md`).

**Order.** `build.py` runs the tile builds, then the steps that need every tile built (the roadside and lanes, then waterfront, seated, parking, farm and clearing, last), then the world file's steps (overview, road map, version). Most steps also run alone on tiles already built, which the bullets below say; a step that reads another's output needs rerunning after it.

**Progress.** Each step prints a line per item finished, with its pace and time left, and the whole build's time left from the last island build's step times (`pipeline/build-times.json`, rewritten by each `--island` build and committed with it). An agent runs a long build in the background with its output to a log, and opens `pipeline/watch.ps1 <log>` in a tab of the owner's Terminal panel, so the owner can watch it without asking.

**Which tiles** is worked out, not listed: `pipeline/island.py` walks OSM's motor roads out from Eckerö, fetching each tile they reach, until none leads anywhere new. Ferries aren't roads, so that's Fasta Åland and what bridges join to it, 54 tiles, plus the 40 neighbours the mirror has, so a shore has land and sea beyond it. `pipeline/island.json` holds the result. Some outer tiles take an older photo, and some have no laser and so no trees. Past those, `pipeline/outlying.py` bakes the outer islands for the overview alone: no tile streams there, and an island the overview's edge would cut goes whole.

**Publishing.** `pipeline/upload.py` publishes the folder (see Hosting in the project's `CLAUDE.md`), and won't unless `pipeline/check_seams.py` passes. Once the new version is deployed and checked live, `upload.py --clear-old` deletes every other version.

**OSM** comes from its own API, not Overpass, whose public servers are too often down: a tile's box at a time, in quarters past the API's 50,000-node limit, each answer kept in `.data/raw/osm-api/` so a stopped fetch resumes. What the build reads is kept in `.data/raw/osm/` and remade from the kept answers, with no fetch, whenever `OSM_KEPT_VERSION` in `pipeline/sources.py` changes. Modules with OSM objects of their own (roadside, furniture, dressing, town) keep them in their own folder under a version of their own.

**Tiles don't live in git.** One does: `fixture/` is L2343C with a `world.json` of its own, a world of one tile for the tests. It needn't match what's published, and only needs refreshing when the format changes. `fixture/paved/` is a small paved tile on made-up land that the game's paving decoders are held to; `pipeline/test_paving_file.py --fixture` writes it again after a change to that format.

**Tests** are `pipeline/test_*.py`, run with `pipeline/.venv/Scripts/python -m unittest discover -s src/projects/aland-drive/pipeline`. The audits measure rather than test: `rock_audit.py` (rock against OSM, canopy and photo), `audit.py` (OSM's roads on a built tile), `road_audit.py` (the surface built from them), `stand_audit.py` (the planted lattice), `check_edges.py` (real sea, shore and neighbouring tiles) and `check_seams.py` (every shared edge).

## What a tile holds

- **Space:** every position is ETRS-TM35FIN metres minus one world origin for the island (`pipeline/grid.py`). The grid runs 0.155% larger than the ground, and its north is 6.25° off true north. The game uses grid metres as they are; anything needing a real compass bearing allows for that.
- **Heights:** 1501×1501 nodes, 4 m apart, in centimetres, delta coded and gzipped. Nodes sit on the corners of the 2 m samples, each the mean of four, so two neighbours compute the same row. The car collides with these same nodes; 2 m heights would change the ground by centimetres and only if the drawn mesh changed too.
- **Shading:** 3000×3000 at 2 m of sun, cast shadow and open sky, baked from the ground as the roads leave it, carrying the relief the 4 m mesh can't.
- **Water** is the mask's red channel. A lake or the sea is one exact level in the elevation model, which real ground never is, so water is any flat area over a hectare, and smaller flat patches near it within a few centimetres of its level. The model writes each patch of sea at its own level, so a patch cut small by a sheet's edge, or the line where two levels meet, would otherwise read as land.
- **Water levels and the seabed** (`pipeline/terrain.py`): each height node within 40 m of water gets the level of the water nearby (`water-levels.bin.gz`, every fourth in `-far`), averaged over the flat water near it so two tiles agree at a seam; farther out it's `dryLevel`. `shore.bin.gz` (and `-far`) holds each node's metres from the shore, positive into the water, to 64 m, with a road's bank over water counted as land: the line the game draws the waterline on and the car tests water by. The drawn surface sits 15 cm under the flat water so the lowest shore stands clear. The seabed falls from the shore along a blurred line, so the shore is a curve rather than steps, and ground a few centimetres above water right at it goes under too, as the photo shows. Nodes the roads shaped keep their height, so a culvert stays a road.
- **Surface mask:** 1500×1500 at 4 m: water, then coverage of main and lesser roads drawn from the road surface, and in alpha where reeds may grow, half where docks keep them off. Half, not none: the game decodes the mask through a 2D canvas, which premultiplies, so a zero-alpha pixel would lose its water and roads. The game moves that alpha into the 8 m boulder-field layer's green as the far data lands, and puts the rock in the mask's alpha instead.
- **Far data:** every fourth height node, the same numbers as the 4 m grid where they meet, and the shading at 8 m.
- **Land colour** (`pipeline/ground.py`): the 2025 orthophoto at 2 m and 8 m. One fixed grade for every tile takes off the spring haze, so seams don't step; colour fades out in shadow, the deepest shadow is lifted, and under trees it's darkened since the photo there is treetops. The photo leans, so round every building the nearest ground replaces the roof. Ploughed fields are bare: it's a real spring.
- **Forest** (`pipeline/laser.py`, `pipeline/canopy.py`): the highest laser return in each 2 m cell less the elevation model (kept in `.data/derived/`), then what's tall, not bright in the photo (the laser is older, so this drops what's been felled), off water and clear of roads and buildings, at 4 m in quarter metres. Chosen because the laser's shape is exact: field edges and clearings land where the photo has them.
- **Forest stands** (`pipeline/stands.py`): `stands.bin.gz`, 125×125 at 48 m, bytes of the shares of pine, spruce and birch (other broadleaf the rest) and the share of lattice places that hold a tree. Raw bytes, not an image, because a canvas premultiplies alpha. MS-NFI is read through a stand-sized kernel (σ 48 m), species by volume, with a rim so neighbours agree; land with no value takes the neighbourhood's, then the island's. Density is the look, not stems: the share of places for the game's crowns to cover the ground as the real canopy does, from a table made on the real jittered lattice. **It restates the lattice, height range and crown widths of `render/tree-place.ts` and `render/tree-shapes.ts`** (a test holds the widths equal), so a change to those is a rebuild of this. `stands.py` alone adds the file to tiles already built.
- **Buildings** (`pipeline/buildings.py`): `solids.bin.gz`, 16-bit columns by 600 m cell, then the outlines the car hits. Solids come from a tile's sheet and its neighbours', kept by their middle, repaired, ground faces dropped and walls run down past the ground. A Topographic Database footprint less than half covered by solids gets a generated shape (a ridge along its long side, or a flat top on a big non-house) as tall as the laser saw, within limits by class, walls run down past the ground at every corner. `pipeline/facades.py` maps classes to the plain look and gives a building one of `art/facades`' looks where the data says, in order of worth: OSM's material, then class, size, roof and street, then a seeded roll, so a rebuild gives the same town. Wood isn't assumed for being central: a villa needs a street where Mariehamn's wooden houses survive. A real shop in OSM within a few metres gives the building a front. The tint bytes carry look, paint, front, word and door. Roofs are the median photo colour inside the outline, with an overhang strip; each roof face's vertices carry its own metres for the shader. The street-facing facade gets the door.
- **Boathouses and sheds** (`pipeline/outbuildings.py`): a footprint the data says is one gets a rural kit model in `models.json` (ODbL) instead of a shape, where a solid is that one building alone. An outbuilding within 4 m of water is a boathouse (nine in ten of OSM's tagged boathouses are); a small, compact one away from it is a shed. Each gets its smallest rectangle, a boathouse's doors to the water and a shed's to the nearer road; a boathouse stands at the water line, a shed at its outline's median ground (one on too steep ground keeps its generated shape). The outline still goes to the car as a wall.
- **Rock** (`pipeline/rocks.py`): `rock.bin.gz`, each 4 m pixel's share of mapped bedrock (34100), off water, carriageways and buildings, then 8 m shares of boulder field (34700). Mapped boulders and a scatter over boulder fields go into `models.json` as the kit's boulder seeds, at their height on the mesh's own triangles. The map, not the photo, places rock: most of it stands under trees, and on open ground the photo reads stone on rock and other land alike; OSM's bare rock adds nothing to the map's. `rocks.py` alone adds both to tiles already built.
- **The clearing round buildings** (`pipeline/clearing.py`): `clearing.png`, each 4 m canopy pixel's signed distance to the nearest building, the one thing every tree and planting reads to stay out of buildings. A building is what the game draws or stops the car at, read off finished tiles: every roof triangle and wall of `solids.bin.gz`, the rural kit's sheds, boathouses and windmills, and every sea mark ashore as a disc; neighbours' count. Measured on a 1 m raster, where a straight wall's distance is linear, so bilinear reads at 4 m serve. In the game it rides in the canopy layer's green. It reads only finished tiles, so it runs last; `clearing.py` alone adds it.
- **Docks and boats** (`pipeline/docks.py`): every OSM pier becomes a dock in `docks.json` (ODbL) as runs (start, direction, length, width) at the water's level. An outline is cut into its largest straight rectangles along its own edge directions; overlapping decks are cut back, or stand 2 cm apart, since two at one height flicker. A floating pier is a pontoon; one by a marina, long or wide is a harbour pier; the rest are private jetties. Where a quay is solid fill the laser took its deck for ground, so that part is left to the ground. A run is trimmed to where its deck clears the land (a pontoon's to where it floats). Boats are by rule: a jetty's head, sometimes a rowing boat or a moored sloop; berths down a marina's piers and pontoons, each boat chosen to fit its berth's water, since the seabed shelves from the shore; and boats on mooring buoys in sheltered water off harbours and jetties, each only where its whole swing is deep, clear water. Boats on buoys lie head to the south-westerly. The water round docks and boats is kept clear of reeds in the mask, a run's outline goes to the car as walls, and a dock stays 4 m clear of a motor road. Non-motor roads on a pier are cut off (see Bridges).
- **People** (`pipeline/people.py`): each module that places people writes its own `people-<source>.json` (ODbL where OSM placed them), listed under `people` in `tile.json`, so a module never touches another's. A figure is its place, yaw, pose, seed and look, checked against what the game knows (a test holds the two lists equal), its seed from where and why it's placed. The waterfront (`pipeline/waterfront.py`), sparse as a quiet summer's day: people fishing, sitting or sunbathing on jetty heads, by rowing boats and berthed boats, along marina piers, and groups round each landmark. The seated (`pipeline/seated.py`): on benches and café tables by a roll from the seat. Both run after the roadside and alone on built tiles (`--count` counts without writing); **a roadside rebuilt alone needs `seated.py` run again**, since the seats move.
- **Sea lanes** (`pipeline/sea_lanes.py`): every boat under way's lanes in one file, `sea/lanes.json`, written into the code rather than the tiles since it changes with no tile. Its licences are ODbL, CC BY 4.0 and Traficom's hydrographic data licence (no navigational or commercial use), which the file states. It reads the built tiles, the kept OSM and the official sea chart (see `docs/ART.md`'s Data); `--data` reads another checkout's, since a worktree has none. The water is the masks less roads over water and docks and boats, and only water joined to the open sea. A lane is the cheapest path over 8 m cells (`MCP_Geometric`), barred near land by the vessel's clearance, cheaper mid-sound and cheapest on the official fairways, then OSM's lines. A keeled small boat avoids charted water shallower than its draft. Corners are cut and rounded to the vessel's turn, and the build fails if a hull's breadth ever crosses land, at a berth too: a single-ended ship's lane says where it comes round, and its berth is moved until the hull, laid as the game lays it, is on water from there in. Mariehamn's berth is the town kit's `FERRY_BERTH` (a test holds them equal). Small boats run from each harbour (`art/marina/places.json`) to its two nearest others.
- **Sea marks** (`pipeline/sea_marks.py`): every buoy, beacon, leading mark, cairn and light in one file, `sea/marks.json`, from Väylävirasto's aids to navigation: kind, IALA side or cardinal, daymark colours, whether it floats, height. A leading mark's front or rear comes from its name and it faces the nearest navigation line. OSM's `seamark:*` fills in only where the chart has no mark near. `render/sea-marks.ts` draws them (see "Sea marks" in `render/CLAUDE.md`).
- **Stone walls** (`pipeline/walls.py`): lines in `walls.json` (ODbL). No source says where field walls are (OSM maps a few, mostly town walls; the Topographic Database has no class; the elevation model doesn't show them), so a wall is OSM's where it maps one in the country, and otherwise placed by rule along the edges of fields and meadows (32611, 32800), where cleared stones were laid. A field's outline is cut into sides; each side's kind (lane, field, forest, open land) sets the share walled, rolled from its middle so a rebuild gives the same walls, both sides of a lane sharing a roll. Walls stop short of every road, keep off buildings and water, and leave churchyards and landmarks to their own walls. The shares are a judgement, kept low so walls stay a feature rather than a frame round every field. `walls.py` alone adds the file; `--audit` prints each kind and how the rule meets OSM.
- **The countryside** (`pipeline/farm.py`): post mills where OSM maps them (`man_made=windmill`, each checked by hand against the laser and the photo, the ones with nothing there left out), sails to the south-west; a mill replaces whatever stands under its outline, as a landmark does (`Landmarks.replace_also`), and `world.json` carries every tile's mills. By rule, on fields and meadows, each rolled from its field's middle so tiles agree across an edge: bales on grass fields (green in the spring photo; ploughed ones aren't), in a row by the road or loose; tractors at field edges and barn yards; Åland sheep on open meadows, often by the shore; dairy cows nearer farms. Everything keeps clear of roads, buildings, water, trees, walls, boulders, the roadside and each other, so it runs after the roadside. `farm.py` alone adds the props and mills, but **a mill only replaces what stands under it when its tile is rebuilt**.
- **The roadside** (`pipeline/roadside.py`): what stands beside the roads, read off the road surface as laid, in `roadside.json` (ODbL). OSM places what it has (lamps, poles and minor power lines, give-way and stop signs, bus stops and shelters, crossings, guard rails, traffic signals), and rules place the rest: lamps along lit roads and a town's streets (a town street's post-top lamp, a main road's galvanised one); traffic-light heads on each arm's right; street-name posts at junctions of differently named town streets; village signs on the way into a village; edge posts on unlit fast roads; guard rails at bridges and embankments; give-way signs where a lesser road meets a greater; a speed sign where the limit changes; mailboxes at drive mouths in the country. Traffic keeps right, so a sign stands on the right of its traffic, about 1 m past the carriageway, facing it; nothing stands on a carriageway, an apron or by a building. The list also holds the walls the car can't pass. **In town** is one test the roadside, furniture, parked cars and seated share (`pipeline/built_up.py`): the floor main buildings cover within 250 m, which separates villages from farms better than counting buildings; `built_up.py` alone prints the verdict at OSM's named places. It needs every tile built, since a pole by an edge stands on its neighbour's ground, so it runs after the builds over the tiles and their neighbours, each tile writing only its own files; `roadside.py` alone adds it, with `--jobs`.
- **Street furniture** (`pipeline/furniture.py`): benches, bins, bike racks and café terraces, placed in the roadside's lists after its objects so they keep clear. OSM places what it maps, and outdoor seating at cafés, bars and restaurants becomes a terrace against the venue's street wall, with parasols at most. Rules place the rest: benches along park paths, pedestrian streets and waterside paths, bins by benches and bus stops, racks at shops, schools and terminals. Mapped things landing on paving are moved off it; nothing stands on a carriageway, path or apron. Each bench and café chair writes a seat in the list's `seats` by `art/people`'s pose (`sit-bench`, `sit-cafe`), so a person put there sits on it (`TileRoadside`, `render/tile-models.ts`).
- **Town dressing** (`pipeline/dressing.py`): lindens, flag and midsummer poles, fences, planters, picnic tables, viewing towers and viewpoint platforms, in the roadside's lists: OSM's before the furniture, rules after. A linden stands at OSM's broadleaf trees in town and along its tree rows, unevenly spaced so an avenue isn't one tree on a ruler, its height in the list's `scales` (a tile baked before lacks them); it keeps 0.6 m off a carriageway and its crown (`LINDEN_REACH`, held to `art/street` by a test) clears every building, or it isn't planted under 6 m. Flags fly Åland's flag in one south-westerly. Fences are picket in town and roundpole in the country, broken at paving and buildings. Nothing stands within 8 m of a landmark.
- **Parked cars** (`pipeline/parking.py`): where each car stands, in `parked.json` (ODbL), after the roadside. A car park has 2.5×5 m bays along its mapped aisles (`service=parking_aisle`) or its longest side, a share taken by who it's for; an empty bay keeps its room. Kerbs take cars where OSM's `parking:*` allows, and on a town's untagged residential streets, on the kerb as Mariehamn's do, facing their side's traffic (OSM names a side as its way runs, which may be against the chain). No car stands on a carriageway or path, near a junction, crossing, bus stop or bridge, or against a building. `parking.py` alone adds them.
- **Churches and landmarks** (`pipeline/landmarks.py`): each site's place and what it replaces come from the art, `art/landmarks/sites.json`, written by `tools/landmark-sites.ts` (a test holds it equal): NLS solids by name, every solid inside its plan, or nothing. A ground site (`art/landmarks/places.ts`) stands on the ground and names its walls, or none. A site's plan is its far version from above, and it and whatever it replaced are walls the car hits. The tile a site stands in lists it in `tile.json` and `world.json` carries the lists, so far versions stand from the start. Near-only sites stand over roads and are drawn with their tile's detail (`nearSites`): the Västerhamn terminal (`art/town-kit`), whose raised ways are taken off the road surface, and the fuel stations with canopies (`art/facades`); each names its own walls so the roads under stay open.
- **Where a road meets a building** (`pipeline/give_way.py`), on OSM's whole ways before any is cut to a tile: every building is a wall, and NLS's laser-measured buildings beat OSM's sketchier lines. A road entering a building stops at its wall unless OSM tags a passage (`tunnel=building_passage`, `covered=yes`), where the wall opens as wide as the road. A public road that only clips a building's edge is OSM's line off, so it's moved clear and the building keeps its walls. A motor road is moved clear of every building, verges included, down the middle of the gap its width fits, easing over a length so it never bends sharply; **a road is never narrowed to clear a building**, since its verges are part of what a racer drives. Each tile reads every building within 400 m (`OUTLINE_REACH_M`) so both tiles move a crossing road alike. Every build reports each place the drawn surface lies on a roof (`drawn_over_buildings`). A raised building is no wall to a road.
- **Raised buildings** (`pipeline/raised.py`): NLS has only an outline for a canopy or raised walkway, so OSM says which stand above the ground: `building=roof` and `carport` are a slab on posts; a `bridge`, a building with a minimum height or level, or one on a `layer` over a ground-level motor road is a box on pillars. A layer alone never raises a building a laser model has. Floor and roof heights come from the whole OSM outline, so a building two tiles share stands as one. Posts are all the car hits.
- **The town's ground** (`pipeline/town.py`, the bake `pipeline/town_ground.py`), tabled and off by default (`build.py --town`): OSM's land use, parks, squares, pitches, car parks and the photo decide each yard's lawn, gravel and paving, with paving along streets and from kerb to facade. It writes `town.rgba.gz`, the image the terrain draws, and `town-ribbons.bin.gz`, a class per road section (see Paths). `town.py` alone adds it; `--pixel-m 4` writes it at 4 m to compare. Hedges and fountains are classified but not placed.
- **The lane map** (`pipeline/lanes.py`): the lines traffic, cyclists and walkers move along, in `lanes.json.gz` (ODbL), read off the road surface as laid, so lanes need no tile rebuild. A lane is one direction of one chain between junctions, at the middle of its half (spread across a one-way road), so it lies on the drawn crown and banking. Connectors join every lane into a junction to every lane out of its other arms, built to stay on the paving and never tighter than a car's 5 m turning circle; a turn no shape keeps on the paving is the road surface's fault, and the audit lists it. Each connector lists what it gives way to: the roadside's signs (`Roadside.junction_sign`, the same test that places them), a ring over its entries, a drive to its street, signals in two phases, else the right, and a left turn to oncoming traffic. A path meeting a road is a link on to its lane. Speed is OSM's limit, else by class and place. Each road lane carries its road's vehicles a day (`pipeline/traffic_volume.py`) from kartor.ax's traffic counts (`.data/raw/kartor-traffic/`), by nearest count, same-named road, or class; **a lane map without them doesn't load**: run `lanes.py` on its tile again. Points are whole centimetres, each a step from the last, so a seam's halves match. `--audit <page address>` lists, with `?lane-lab&at=` links, every connector off the paving or too tight, a lane under another road, steps and grade changes, and lanes unreachable or leading nowhere: where the road surface needs work. The game's side is `traffic/CLAUDE.md`.

## The road surface

Roads are baked (`pipeline/roads.py`), so the render and the physics read one surface. OSM's lines are the only source so far. A ribbon triangle steeper than 45° that rises over 1.2 m is dropped, or a track crossing high above a road would dive under it as a cliff across the carriageway. The raw roads, footprints and surface are ODbL, each stating it. Each step:

- **Chains.** Ways are cut at every shared point and rejoined into continuous drives, greatest road first, each going straight on through a junction until it meets a road already laid. A lesser road ends on the greater; a crossroads is two T-junctions. A roundabout's ring is laid first, whole, with a rounding that keeps its real size. Chain 0 is the tile's main road, or a ring if it has one.
- **Line.** Stations every 2 m, rounded with a Gaussian scaled by class, ends fixed.
- **Height.** A Whittaker smoother over the median ground across the road, its shortest kept wavelength by class (280 m on a primary road, 30 m on a path). A bridge's stations carry no weight, so it spans.
- **Cross-section.** Width by class, a crown, and banking on bends to a class's limit; five points: carriageway edges and centre, and a shoulder each side reaching the ground, fading to the ground's colour, which hides the join from a low camera. Where the land falls away (a bridge, an underpass) the shoulder stops a metre down and stays a deck, or it would be a wall across what passes beneath.
- **Junctions.** The greater road carries on whole. A lesser road of service class or above stops at its first section clear of the road it meets and eases on to that road's surface over 16 m. An apron (`lay_aprons`) paves between, a shape of its own with corners rounding by the sine of the angle squared, so a shallow merge keeps its nose. Its edges take the ribbons' heights; aprons that touch are one, bare with no paint. Where it overlaps a ribbon it sinks a centimetre, and a shoulder under it drops well clear, **because two surfaces at one height flicker**. The land round an apron is shaped as round a ribbon. A chain's centre line stops at its cut; respawning takes the nearest line, never more than about 15 m from an apron. A shoulder that would rise through another road is dropped. A lesser road crossing a greater one with no junction passes just under it, so a crossing is always the greater road's surface. `docs/JUNCTIONS.md` holds the junction work still to do.
- **Paved ground.** Every OSM line as its own ribbon breaks in town, where lines run packed together. So a car park or bus station (`vectors.paved_areas`) and a yard of side-by-side service lanes (`vectors.lane_yards`) is one surface (`lay_paved`) in 4 m cells on a world lattice, lying on the land and easing to the height of any road it meets. Service ways and paths mostly on it are its aisles and aren't laid, or they'd be a comb of strips; what runs outside stays a road, so entrances still join the street. Then any strip of land under 3 m between laid surfaces is paved (`lay_gaps`), pinned to both sides, unless they stand over 0.4 m apart (an embankment or an overpass). An apron's shoulder that would lie on another surface is dropped.
- **Paths.** A footway, cycleway, path, bridleway or steps is a ribbon like a road's, so the car, roadside, parked cars, furniture and walkers read one surface: a small road with a slight crown and the 30 m profile. (Laying paths flat on the land waits with the town's ground, `docs/HANDOFF.md`.) Each section's class is its OSM way's surface by `pipeline/path_surfaces.py`: tagged surface (grass as gravel, since no way is drawn as a lawn), a sidewalk's slabs, an untagged path's gravel, a pedestrian street's slabs, else pale walked tarmac; steps are treads with risers. On a paved tile (`build.py --paving`) each paving point carries it with its way's direction, so slabs and treads lie along it; with a town image, from `town.py`. A motor road keeps its look, save setts where OSM says. Paths are ribbons, not painted into the town image, because a strip under 3 m can't live in a 2 m distance field.
- **Ground.** Cut or filled to 12 cm under the road for the shoulder and one mesh cell beyond, eased back to the land over a blend by class, then any node a carriageway crosses is pressed under it, so no triangle comes up through the road.
- **Thinning.** Cross-sections are kept only where the road would otherwise move 2 cm, up to 14 m apart.

**Seams.** A tile's edge row of heights is never changed: the blend fades to nothing over 24 m and the build fails if the row moves. A road crossing an edge ends on a seam section built only from what both tiles see alike: the whole way's direction from a chord across the edge, its class's widths, no banking, and a height and slope from a plane fitted to the elevation model near the crossing, lifted clear of the edge row. The last 40 m ease on to it, and the section is rounded to the millimetre, so two tiles built apart write the same numbers whichever way their chains run. Dashes are counted from a seam. Shading beside a road at a seam comes from each tile's own view of its neighbour, which steps slightly but doesn't show.

## Bridges

The elevation model has no bridges: it drops to the water under them. So a stretch of road held up over a gap is a span (`pipeline/roads.py`): a tagged motor bridge, or any road over water in the model, at least 12 m long; shorter is a culvert and stays an ordinary road. A span's stations shape no ground, so the water stays water under it; its profile runs bank to bank, rising a little over open water, and the shoulder stays a flat deck. Each chain lists its spans in `tile.json`. `bridgeKit` (`render/bridges.ts`) is the one function that says what a bridge looks like and which walls the car can't pass; replacing the kit is replacing it. No span crosses a tile's edge. Causeways are real ground and need nothing.

**A pier is a dock, not a bridge.** OSM maps many marina piers with a footway along them, which laid as a road became concrete road bridges with parapets. So a non-motor road is cut off wherever it runs on a pier (`pipeline/docks.py`), and the pier is drawn as a dock (see "Docks and boats" in `render/CLAUDE.md`); the car can't drive on to a dock, so a footway ends at the pier's root. A wooden footbridge or boardwalk is taken as a pier too, with no berths. A pier carrying a motor road (a ferry or fish quay) stays the road's: its span is a quay in `tile.json` (`quays`), with kerb and fascia but no parapet. Any other footbridge is a span drawn by the bridge kit, except the Västerhamn terminal's walkways and ramps, which are the terminal model's (see Churches and landmarks).

## How rough the roads are

`tools/road-check.ts` drives the scripted driver down every motor road at a speed its class can carry and reports jolts and wheel drops. A wheel drop at the very start of a road is the car being put down and not a fault; drops on the way are real, nearly all narrow town roads where a wheel runs off the edge.

`tools/junction-check.ts` drives every way through every junction of a tile in the simulation alone. Its movements come from the map's network (`tools/junction-moves.ts`), not the surface, so any two builds can be compared: every road in to every road out, roundabouts both ways, one-way roads honoured, hairpins at a plain junction left out. It judges the junction and 40 m either side: whether the car got through, had its middle off the surface for over 3 m, its worst jolt and wheel drops, and where. A failure is a place to look, not always a fault: most are turns of 100° or more that a car can't make on a narrow road without running wide.

A scripted drive can't follow a route that turns back on itself (a U-turn at a junction): `startLine` refuses one, saying where.
