# Åland Drive: menus

The game menu, the Graphics page and the dev menu. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down.

## The game menu

The game menu (`components/game-menu.tsx`) opens on Resume, which B, Menu and Esc press too, and holds the car's tyres (summer, friction and studded side by side, named as the Nordics name them in English, the fitted set marked, each fitting its own when picked, so no button has to say whether it's the set on or the next), the driver's aids each on or off (ABS, traction control and stability, see `sim/CLAUDE.md`), the units the player reads in (metric or imperial side by side the same way, `units.ts`: the speed, the GPS's distance, a speed trap's speeds, the temperature, and a 24- or 12-hour clock; the simulation, the tools and the dev pages stay metric), the sound on or off, Graphics, and in a challenge Retry and Leave (see `challenge/CLAUDE.md`). The same dialog is a challenge's small choices on its own button: Solo or Co-op, and Join or Decline, each with Cancel. N toggles the sound too, with no prompt on the HUD. Esc opens it only when nothing else is open for it to close. It is driven by the shared pad navigation, and the game decides what it holds and whether it pauses (`render/game-menu.ts`): each time it opens, the game works out its items, which the page draws by id, and whether the drive stops. It pauses as the map does unless the game says otherwise, and a menu that doesn't pause lets go of the car's controls while the cars and the clock run on. Graphics opens a page of its own (`graphicsMenu`), the home of every graphics setting as they're added: it opens on its first setting, and Back, B or Esc returns to the game menu. It holds the render scale (see Floor in the project's `CLAUDE.md`) for now; the page keeps the settings (`render/graphics-settings.ts`) and the game follows them live.

## The dev menu

`~`, or the key under Esc on any layout, opens the dev menu (`components/dev-menu.tsx`), which pauses the drive as the map does. Its copy button puts where the car is on the clipboard, for the owner to paste into a chat when they see something wrong: the tile, the car's scene and ETRS-TM35FIN position, its heading, speed and camera, and the view on screen as `tools/look.mjs` arguments (east, north, height above the ground, yaw and pitch in radians). That look recreates what they saw, and the scene position finds the spot for any other tool. It copies a link too: `?at=east,north,height,yaw,pitch` (`render/start-place.ts`) opens the game there, loading that place first instead of the usual start, with the car on the nearest road and the free camera on the view; east and north alone look from a little above the ground. Its buttons jump to the showcase spots, closing the menu. Its Traffic switch turns the cars, cyclists and walkers on the lanes off and on again, offline only: in the shared world they are everyone's, and always on. And pick any of the fourteen cars, and the drive carries on in it from where it was, at the same speed: its looks, handling and sound all change. The same key or Esc closes it. It has no prompt and no controller button, and the choice lasts until a reload.

The dev menu also jumps the car to the end of the GPS route, facing the way the route arrives, or to any of the ten showcase spots (`render/showcase.ts`) by name, each with the first load's bar while the tiles there land and the spot's name on arrival. `tools/showcase.mjs` screenshots all ten through the probe, the repeatable view set for art and performance checks.
