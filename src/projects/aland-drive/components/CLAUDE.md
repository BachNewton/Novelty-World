# Åland Drive: menus

The game menu, its Cars and Settings pages, and the dev menu. Paths are relative to the project's folder, `src/projects/aland-drive/`; the project's own `CLAUDE.md` there is the overview and says where everything else is written down.

## The game menu

The game menu (`components/game-menu.tsx`) opens on Resume, which B, Menu and Esc press too, and holds Cars and Settings, or in a challenge Retry, Settings and Leave (see `challenge/CLAUDE.md`). The same dialog is a challenge's small choices on its own button: Solo or Co-op, and Join or Decline, each with Cancel. N toggles the sound too. The HUD shows no controls while driving: they are Settings' Controls tab; the world map shows its own while it's open. Esc opens it only when nothing else is open for it to close. It is driven by the shared pad navigation, and the game decides what it holds and whether it pauses (`render/game-menu.ts`): each time it opens, the game works out its items, which the page draws by id, and whether the drive stops. It pauses as the map does unless the game says otherwise, and a menu that doesn't pause lets go of the car's controls while the cars and the clock run on. Settings opens a page of its own (`settingsMenu`, drawn by `components/settings-menu.tsx`), the home of every setting as they're added, in tabs: Gameplay, Controls, Audio and Graphics. LB and RB, or Q and E, step through the tabs, the focus following to the new tab's first setting; a click or A on a tab opens it. It opens on Gameplay's first setting, and Back, B or Esc returns to the game menu. In a challenge it holds everything but the tyres, the challenge's car's, so Gameplay opens on the aids, and it pauses only as the challenge's menu does: solo, not in co-op. Every setting is kept in this browser. Gameplay holds the car's tyres (summer, friction and studded side by side, named as the Nordics name them in English, the fitted set marked, each fitting its own when picked, so no button has to say whether it's the set on or the next), the driver's aids each on or off (ABS, traction control and stability, see `sim/CLAUDE.md`) and the units the player reads in (metric or imperial side by side the same way, `units.ts`: the speed, the GPS's distance, a speed trap's speeds, the temperature, and a 24- or 12-hour clock; the simulation, the tools and the dev pages stay metric); these are the game's, the page's items. Controls lists every control on the keyboard and a controller side by side, read from the one map of them (`BINDINGS`, `input/controls.ts`), so a new control is a binding there and a row here. Audio holds the sound on or off and the master volume, 80% to start (the shared sound settings, `audio/settings.ts`), a slider for a mouse and buttons stepping it by tens for a pad, and how many of the nearest traffic cars are heard, none to four. Graphics holds the render scale (see Floor in the project's `CLAUDE.md`), and what the traffic costs: how many cars, cyclists and walkers are out, a quarter of Åland's own to all of it, and how far round the player they're kept moving, half the reach to all of it; the page keeps them (`render/graphics-settings.ts`, `traffic/settings.ts`) and the game follows them live. Cars opens a page of its own too (`carsMenu`, drawn by `components/car-select.tsx`), where a car and its colour are chosen in one place. On the left is every car, a row for each tier in its class's colour (Asphalt, Gravel, Classic, and Kobb outside the tiers, with a spanner), in the registry's order, each with a dot of the paint it's in; on the right, the spec sheet of the car under the focus or the pointer, as a racing game shows one: its maker, name and where it comes from (shipped to the harbour, a dealer, the motor club or a barn find, as an icon), its class and tier, which wheels drive (four wheels from above, the driven ones filled), its gears and its engine in a few words, its power, torque, weight and power to weight, and bars for top speed, 0–100 km/h (on gravel in B, as the tiers are ranked), the stop from 100 and the grip held through a corner at 100 on asphalt and on gravel, each along the whole field's reach, the first two with the tier's band behind them. Every figure is read from the car's own files (`garage/car-facts.ts`: its tuning, its measured limits, and the maker, engine and origin its entry carries), and they follow the units. It opens on the car being driven. Picking another drives it at once, from where the car is, as a challenge's swap does, and keeps it in this browser for the next drive (`garage/car.ts`); picking the car it's already in goes to its paints, so a pad reaches them from anywhere in the grid. The paints are swatches under the sheet, a chip of each coat showing its finish in how it takes the light (a gloss's horizon line, a metallic's tight highlight and flakes, a matte's soft fall, a pearl's turn to its flip colour), its stripes down it, a small glyph for the finish, and the paint's name as its accessible name and tooltip; another car's are shown and can't be picked until it's driven. Picking one paints the car at once behind the menu, keeps it for that car in this browser (`garage/paint.ts`) and sends it to everyone. Back, B or Esc returns to the game menu. Like the tyres it isn't in a challenge's menu, whose car is the challenge's. `?paint=<index or colour>` starts a drive's first car in a paint, for that drive only, so a tool can shoot each one.

## The dev menu

`~`, or the key under Esc on any layout, opens the dev menu (`components/dev-menu.tsx`), which pauses the drive as the map does. Its copy button puts where the car is on the clipboard, for the owner to paste into a chat when they see something wrong: the tile, the car's scene and ETRS-TM35FIN position, its heading, speed and camera, and the view on screen as `tools/look.mjs` arguments (east, north, height above the ground, yaw and pitch in radians). That look recreates what they saw, and the scene position finds the spot for any other tool. It copies a link too: `?at=east,north,height,yaw,pitch` (`render/start-place.ts`) opens the game there, loading that place first instead of the usual start, with the car on the nearest road and the free camera on the view; east and north alone look from a little above the ground. Its buttons jump to the showcase spots, closing the menu. Its Traffic switch turns the cars, cyclists and walkers on the lanes off and on again, offline only: in the shared world they are everyone's, and always on. The same key or Esc closes it. It has no prompt and no controller button.

The dev menu also jumps the car to the end of the GPS route, facing the way the route arrives, or to any of the ten showcase spots (`render/showcase.ts`) by name, each with the first load's bar while the tiles there land and the spot's name on arrival. `tools/showcase.mjs` screenshots all ten through the probe, the repeatable view set for art and performance checks.
