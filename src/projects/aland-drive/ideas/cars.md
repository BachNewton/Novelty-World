# Cars

The plan for the cars a player owns, once cars are bought: the classes and tiers they're sorted into, how many there are, how each one reaches the island, who makes them and where they're sold. The owner has decided the shape (see "The owner's decisions"); the car list itself, slot by slot, is still to be drafted. Nothing here is attached to what exists: any of today's fourteen cars, the three makers and the dealer art may be kept, changed or dropped to fit. The events these cars are entered in are `ideas/races.md`, section 9.

## Classes and tiers

Test Drive Unlimited 2's scheme, with each tier's performance moved to today's cars: TDU2's car list is from 2011, and today's Golf GTI is about as quick as an A4 car was then. An event asks for a class and tier, and any car in it may enter.

| Class | Tiers | What it is | Today's counterparts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: Asphalt** | A1–A7, A1 fastest | Road and sports cars | A1 hypercars (0–100 km/h under 2.8 s, 350+ km/h), A2 supercars (about 2.8–3.2 s), A3 grand tourers and fast coupés (3.2–3.8 s), A4 and A5 sports cars and fast saloons (3.5–5 s), A6 hot coupés and quick hatches (4.5–5.5 s), A7 hot hatches (5.5–7 s) |
| **B: Gravel** | B1–B4, B1 fastest | Cars built for gravel: rally cars, fast everyday cars on loose roads, four-by-fours | B1 the rally specials, down to B4 the slow four-by-fours |
| **C: Classic** | C1–C4, C1 fastest | Older cars, today's cut-off taking in the 1990s and early 2000s | From a homage to the F40 down to a 2CV |

**B is gravel, not only off-road.** TDU2's B was its smallest class, four-by-fours for dirt tracks. On Åland gravel is ordinary public road, and most of it: of the 2,072 km a race can use in the game's OpenStreetMap data, 716 km is tagged paved, 526 km gravel or dirt and 830 km untagged, which laid by the game's rule for untagged roads is 37–47% paved, against about two thirds asphalt on TDU2's islands. The main roads are nearly all asphalt; the country lanes, the largest part of the network, mostly gravel. So B covers the cars that drive it, and gets back the B1 tier TDU2 dropped after its beta.

**Today's cars on today's tiers** sit in the middle and below, measured from their limits table on dry asphalt: Sund A4; Ankar, the Skerry GT and Ström A5; Fjärd, Sten and Bris A6; Grus B2, Holm B3, Berg B4; Vik C3 and Sälta C4. Ljus and Kobb are slower than any A tier.

## How many

**About 38 cars: two to three per tier**, so about 18 in A, 12 in B and 8 in C. The count follows from the events: a tier with about four events needs enough cars that entering one is a choice between driving styles. Most of the cars to add are in A1–A4, where today there is only Sund, then across B and C.

## How the list is built

From the tiers down, not from today's cars up:

1. **Slots.** Each tier's two or three places, each with target figures (0–100 km/h, top speed, weight, drive) and a role in its tier, so a tier offers different ways to drive: a front-engined grand tourer, a mid-engined sports car and a light track car in one tier, say.
2. **The best fit for each slot.** A car of today's close to a slot is retuned into it, a slot with nothing close gets a new car, and a car that fits no slot leaves the list. Ljus and Kobb are cut unless they keep a job outside the events: a bucket-list challenge, or a first car before a player can afford anything.
3. **Each slot's cost written beside it**, so the cheap choices show next to the dear ones. The challenges, the races, the player's start (Grus) and the tools and tests (the Skerry GT) name cars by id, so a removed car means repointing them; a retuned car's limits are measured again and the challenges' targets set again from them; reusing a car's art is cheap, a new body isn't.

Each slot also says how its car reaches the island and who makes it.

## How cars reach the island

TDU2's intent is a dream garage: a range from hot hatches to hypercars, and the pleasure of choosing, buying and owning them. A supercar dealer on every holiday island was only how it did that. Åland, a town of about 12,000 on an island of 30,000, has no supercar dealer, so each kind of car reaches the island as it would in life:

| Tiers | Where it comes from | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| A5–A7, the everyday B cars | **Dealers and the used lot**, at the real car businesses' places, at the island's own scale | What a small town has |
| A1–A4 | **By sea**: the finest on a visiting superyacht in the marina, the rest in the importer's delivery hall at the ferry terminal | Åland lives by its ferries, which already call in the game's nights. A hypercar here is bought in Stockholm or Helsinki and shipped |
| C classics | **Found, not bought**, in barns and outbuildings, as TDU2's wrecks and Forza's barn finds | A farming island of old outbuildings, and finding them rewards exploring |
| B1–B2 rally cars | **The motor club**, from the folkrace scene at Vessingsboda | The folkrace's claim rule lets anyone buy a winning car at a capped price (`docs/DRIVING-CULTURE.md`) |

So the number of dealerships follows Åland's own scale, not TDU2's density, and the variety comes from the harbour, the barns and the club instead of more showrooms. No permanent supercar store stands in the town, which would be implausible here: the dream cars are seen where ships bring them.

## Barn finds

The classics are found, as Forza Horizon's barn finds were: Forza Horizon 2 had 10 and Forza Horizon 3 15, and TDU2 six wrecks in its base game. **8 to 10 here**: the eight C cars, and Berg, the old four-by-four, as a ninth if it fits.

- **A barn or a boathouse.** Åland has hundreds of each, and the game already builds both from the map's data. A classic under a tarp in a boathouse by the shore is as much a find here as one in a barn.
- **A rumour marks an area, and the player searches it.** Forza's came over the festival radio; here a rumour is heard in town, and an area lights up on the map. The search is the places-to-find machinery (`map/`): an area to explore, and a note on arriving.
- **Finds appear as the player goes**, not all at once, as Forza's did.
- **The village garage restores the car**: free for a player who waits, sooner for one who pays, as in Forza's later games. It gives the garage a job.

## Makers

The makers are a separate decision from the buildings. TDU2 had 32 car makers across about 89 base-game cars, very unevenly: Ferrari 11, Audi 9 and Mercedes-Benz 8 carried the list, Aston Martin, Pagani, RUF and Spyker had four to six each, and a long tail of a dozen makers, Koenigsegg, McLaren and TVR among them, had one car each. A few big makers and a long tail is the pattern to follow, with invented makers, since the game's cars carry no real brands. A small maker needs no building of its own when its car comes by ship or turns up in a barn. How many makers, and which of the three of today (Tärna, Västan, Klippa) stay, comes with the car list.

## Dealers

TDU2 sold through 11 dealerships, most of them grouping makers: three single-brand (Audi, Ferrari, Mercedes-Benz), five by country (Italian, British, European, U.S. and Asian cars) and three of classics; some cars came only as wrecks, prizes or club rewards. Åland's count comes from the island's scale instead, as above.

**Six places sell cars**, grouped by maker as most of TDU2's were, and ranked as the cars are: the finest cars get the finest room. Four stand on sites `art/dealers/` has already drawn, each at a real car business on the map; the yacht and the delivery hall are new.

The yacht is the one place the game leans into TDU's glamour, and it is less far-fetched than it sounds: superyachts call at Mariehamn in summer, some carry cars on a garage deck, and makers hold sales on yachts. A yacht's car deck holds about five cars, which keeps it exclusive. It stays moored all year for now; visiting only in summer, as a real one would, is for later, since it would hide the A1 and A2 cars all winter. Its hull is a large model of its own in the marina, beside the boats the game already has.

The other places a car comes from aren't shops: the **motor club** at Vessingsboda (the three B1 rally cars) and the **barns and boathouses** (the eight classics). The **tyre shop** on the north edge is a service, not a seller.

Most of it is in Mariehamn, as it would be: it is the island's only town, and the harbour is where its ships come in. The village garage, the club and the barns take the player out to the country.

The dealer art is still a first draft the owner hasn't reviewed: the sites above are decided, their exteriors and interiors aren't, and the yacht and delivery hall have none yet.

## Inside a dealership

As TDU2's were, a dealership is walked round in first person, among its cars.

- **A separate interior.** Entering leaves the drive for the dealership's own room, for performance: inside, only the room and its cars are drawn.
- **The real view through the windows.** On entering, the island round the dealership is already loaded, since the player drove there. So the game renders the outside once, all round from the dealership's real place, at that moment's hour, season and weather, and shows it through the windows. The view holds still while the player is inside (no traffic or boats moving), which through a window at a distance is hard to see. This is to be tried and judged by eye.
- **No body, and nobody else.** The player is a camera on foot. Other players aren't seen inside, and a shared dealership is for later.
- **At a car**, the player can rev its engine, sit in its driver's seat (its cockpit), and read its figures. The figures are the measured ones from the limits table, so a showroom tells the truth. Nothing more for now: no doors or bonnet opening, no engine bay, no test drive.
- **Each place is lit as its kind would be** (the table above), not by the sun.
- **Every car is free while the game is built.** Buying and selling go through the dealerships as they will, but no money moves until the economy exists, and the Cars menu still offers every car. A bought car has nowhere to be kept yet.

**What's built** (`dealership/CLAUDE.md`): the walk-in, on the drafts of the premium showroom, the town dealer and the village garage. A dealership is data, the interior definition (`dealership/interior.ts`) its art follows: its room, its stock by the table above as rules on the car list, its cars' spots, its floor and solids, its windows, where its view is captured from, its own lamps and its door. Each door is a ring on the island; E or X there captures the island all round from the dealership's place at that moment's hour, season and weather and opens the room, where the player walks among its cars, revs one, sits in its seat and reads its measured figures, and takes it as their car outside. The yacht, the delivery hall and the used lot's indoor hall come with their art, to the same definition. The draft garage holds three of its five cars; selling at the used lot waits for a garage that holds more than one car.

## The owner's decisions

- **Classes and tiers are TDU2's, with today's cars in each tier**: A1–A7 asphalt, B1–B4 gravel (widened from off-road, with B1 back), C1–C4 classic.
- **About 38 cars, two to three per tier**, built from the tiers down: today's cars, makers and dealer art are candidates, kept, changed or dropped to fit.
- **Each kind of car reaches the island as it would in life**: dealers at Åland's own scale for the everyday tiers, the harbour for the fastest, barns for the classics and the motor club for the rally cars.
- **8 to 10 barn finds**, in barns and boathouses, searched for from a rumour and restored at the village garage.
- **About 25 bucket-list challenges, a car each** (`ideas/bucket-list.md`), each choosing its car, road, hour, date and weather.
- **Makers follow TDU2's pattern** of a few big ones and a long tail, all invented; their number comes with the car list.
- **Six places sell cars**: the yacht (A1–A2), the delivery hall at the ferry terminal (A3–A4 and the two shipped B cars), the premium showroom, the town dealer, the village garage, and the used lot for trade, an indoor used-car hall, each lit as its kind would be (an outdoor lot can't be walked in: the view captured through a dealership's windows stands in for scenery far off, and outdoors it would replace the place the player stands in). The tyre shop is a service, and the motor club and the barns aren't shops.
- **Dealerships are walked round in first person**, as separate interiors with the real view captured through their windows; no body and no other players inside; at a car, rev it, sit in it and read its measured figures. Buying is free and the Cars menu stays until the economy exists.
