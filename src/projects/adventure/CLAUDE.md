# Adventure

A cozy 3D adventure in the spirit of *Stardew Valley* and *Animal Crossing*: a gentle world to live in rather than a challenge to beat. It is assembled from Kenney's CC0 3D asset packs (kenney.nl), and grown in small slices, each one seen working before the next is chosen. It is its own game, unrelated to Hearthwood.

## Goals

- **Cozy, not testing.** Nothing about play is decided yet beyond the feel: what a day holds, what the player does, whether anything can fail.
- **The look is Kenney's.** Art comes from Kenney's packs as made, so the game shares one simple, toy-like style without art built in code. Where packs leave a gap, how to fill it is open.
- **Built in slices.** Each slice settles only the decisions it meets; the rest stay open below.

## Open

To decide when a slice meets them, by the game's goals:

- Who it is for, and the floor device: desktop only, or phones too.
- Inputs: whether touch plays.
- The world: its scale, whether it streams, and its units (the characters are about 0.7 units tall as made).
- Real-time or turn-based, and whether play goes online.
- The camera in play.
- How long the game is meant to live, and so whether saves must stay readable.
- The game's own visual style beyond the packs: palette tokens, light, sky.

## Decided

- **Rendering.** Plain three.js on `WebGPURenderer` (`three/webgpu`), which falls back to WebGL 2 by itself, so its reach is WebGL's while it stays on the path three.js is developing. The page shows which backend it got, and a lost device stops drawing and says so. No React Three Fiber: the frame loop owns the scene, React only the overlay.
- **The performance target is the integrated GPU** (the laptop's Radeon 780M) at the monitor's 100 Hz, with the eGPU plugged in or not: the 4070 is far stronger, so what plays well on the integrated GPU plays well on it, and the game is measured on the integrated GPU alone.
- **Assets are served as Kenney ships them**, for now: the packs' GLB files copied unchanged into `public/adventure/`, and where kits differ from one another, each is fitted to the world as it loads (see "Fitting the kits together"). A build tool (compression, merged meshes, one shared texture, a chosen world scale) comes when a slice needs one, most likely the first world built from several packs.
- **Clips are read from the file**, never listed in code: every character carries the same set, and the viewer offers whatever the loaded file holds.
- **Keyboard and mouse, and a controller**, both at once. The pad is laid out as third-person games lay theirs: the left stick walks, the right stick turns and tilts the camera, A jumps, the left stick clicked in runs until it is clicked again or let go to rest, the triggers zoom (right in, left out), and View is the debug overlay's key. The hints show the pad's buttons while a pad was the last thing used, and the keys otherwise.
- **Local co-op, split left and right.** A second player joins on the same machine, as couch co-op games share inputs out: the keyboard and mouse is one input and each pad another. Alone, the first player plays on all of them. Menu on a pad, or Enter on the keyboard, joins a second player on that input, as long as it isn't the one the first player is using (so the keyboard and a pad, or two pads, one each); the second player holds that input alone and the first keeps every other, and the second leaves by pressing it again, or when their pad is unplugged. Two at most, since the screen splits in two. Pressing join never counts as using an input, so it can't make the joining input the first player's. The mouse goes with the keyboard. The second player walks as Male F, the villager by the cart, who steps out of the village while they play and is back in his place when they leave: a character from the roster that isn't the first player's, and no villager standing twice.

## The debug view

The page is, for now, a debug view: the player, Female F, on a grid floor (a line every unit, a stronger one every five) that the world's ground covers, with a picker for which of her animations plays, and the camera orbiting round her (drag, the arrow keys or a pad's right stick: left and right swing it round, up and down tilt it) and zooming (wheel, pinch, + and −, or a pad's triggers). Panning is off, and the camera never goes below the floor. It opens on idle.

The player and the villagers stand on whatever ground is under them: down in a path's sunk bed, up a step of up to 0.3 (a kit stair's step is about half that), and off an edge deeper than that, falling to what is below. The ground is found by a ray straight down onto what is walkable (the ground's tile in that cell, the pieces made to be stood on that reach into it: the town's paving, stairs, solid blocks such as a plinth, a terrace or a rampart's walk, and plank floors, and the country's ground raised on a rise, its cliff blocks and steps, bridges, boardwalks and stepping stones; and the grid floor where there is nothing else), so the player climbs stairs a step at a time and walks on a balcony or along the town wall, and a step of the ground under the player is drawn as a short settle, the camera following more slowly, so walking into a path's bed reads as stepping down rather than a jolt.

The ground runs 80 cells a side, the village in its middle and the country round it (see "The country"). WASD or a pad's left stick walks the character about, relative to the camera (W, or the stick pushed up, goes away from it), and Shift held with them, or a pad's left stick clicked in, sprints; the stick pushed part way walks slower, down to about a third of the full walk, with the walk clip slowed to match so a planted foot still stays still, while a sprint always runs at full speed; the camera follows, and so does the sun's shadow box, small and sharp round the player. Only the players cast shadows, so nothing else moves in and out of the sun's reach. A village built from the town kit's pieces stands round where the player starts, and uses every one of the kit's pieces somewhere it makes sense, so it doubles as a map of how each piece is put together with the others; only pieces that have no place yet that suits them are left out (the half chamfers, the full stairs' inner corner, the plaster small window, the narrow, corner and spiral stone stairs, the wide stone stairs with a rail, the timber half block, and the spare sail). At its heart, round a green with market stalls and lanterns, stand a stone cottage, an inn of stone below and timber above with a balcony up its side and a chimney at its back, a windmill on a stone tower, a mill house whose wheel turns in a race along its back, a stone smithy with a fenced yard, and a lookout under a pointed roof. South of the green a paved street leads to a plaza with a fountain, a town hall on a stepped stone plinth, a street east round a bend and up a ramp to a guildhall on a raised terrace, a shop with a loggia, a house with bent corners and a balcony, and a small house chamfered at every corner, with a porch. West of the plaza is a walled garden with ponds, a fountain and a pavilion; beyond it, at the village's west edge, the old town wall, its rampart walked behind a parapet, a gate two storeys high between two towers where the street leaves the village paved, a round tower at its north end, stairs up to its walk, and a crumbled end in rubble, and inside it a gatekeeper's hut, two ruined houses and a coach house with a trough. North, past the windmill, a farm lane runs from an L-shaped farmhouse past a granary rounded at its ends, a barn, a privy, a house going up in a frame of poles, a tool shed and a paddock to an eight-sided chapel and an L-shaped manor. East of the mill race, a sawmill turns a wide wheel in it. Trees of every kind the kit has stand round it all. The ground is the Nature Kit's grass, with dirt paths from each door to a street through the village and a lane through the farm, and a few flowers and tufts of grass by the doors. Every other character of the pack stands about the village playing its idle, each placed by what is there (keeping a stall, at a door, by a cart), their idles out of step so none breathe together; like the town, they cast no shadow. A few sit, on the market's bench and stool: the `sit` clip swings the legs forward but leaves the thighs sloping down, and the leg blocks reach below the hips, so no height rests both body and thighs on a seat as made: a sitter's hips go a little ahead of the seat's middle and a little above its top, chosen by eye across every character. Space or a pad's A jumps, on the press, so holding it doesn't bounce; the player can still steer in the air. The jump clips hold a pose without moving the body, so the game supplies the height: `jump` plays while rising and `fall` while coming down. Walking, sprinting and standing still play the `walk`, `sprint` and `idle` clips, blended into each other, and the animation picker shows what plays; a clip picked while standing plays until the character sets off. The ground speeds keep a planted foot still: each is how fast its clip sweeps a foot backward as it touches down, measured from the clip's leg swing. The character moves on the frame's own time step, which is all a debug walk needs: whether play runs on a fixed step stays open with real-time play. The keyboard and a pad are the game's own controls, and a picker lets go of the keyboard once a choice is made, so WASD walks again. The player can hold one of the pack's weapons in either hand; the `holding-*`, `*-shoot` and `attack-melee-*` clips are the ones made for a held weapon. `?weapon=<id>&hand=<right|left>` opens holding exactly that, and the address follows each pick of what is held; the clip playing stays out of the address, so a reload always opens on idle. The shared frame meter sits in the top right.

Enter on the keyboard, or Menu on a pad the first player isn't using, brings in a second player beside the first, to their right as the first's camera sees it, with a camera looking the same way, and the screen splits: the first player on the left, the second on the right, a line between. Each half is its own camera following its player, turned, tilted and zoomed by that player alone; each player has their own ground height, settle, gait and animation on the one world. Each half shows its own player's hints, in the keys or the pad they play on; alone, the hints add how a second joins (Menu, and Enter too while the first plays on a pad), and the second's half adds how they leave. The pickers are the first player's. The sun's shadow box is moved to each player before their half is drawn, a box each: the shadow map holds only the two players, so drawing it again costs little beside the half itself. The two halves together shade as many pixels as one full view, so the second player's cost is the scene's draws and vertices walked twice: at the opening views about one and a half times the draws and triangles of one player.

~, or a pad's View, toggles the debug overlay, for the owner to name what they see in the code's own words: over each thing near the player it floats the name the code gives it (a kit piece's, a plant's, a character's, and over a whole building or part of the town the name of the constant that places it in `town.ts`, such as `INN` or `PLAZA`), numbers the cells round the player in the corner the code names each by, and shows the player's cell and exact place at the top of the screen. With two players it is open for both or neither (either one's key toggles it), each half naming what is round its own player and numbering its cells, with each player's place shown over their hints. Labels at one point, such as two pieces in one cell, stack. A label is drawn pulled a little toward the camera, so what it names doesn't cut through it, while anything between it and the camera still hides it.

### The map editor

M (or the map button at the bottom left) opens the map editor over the town, and closes it back to play with the camera where it was. While it is open the left button is the editor's: click a piece to select it (outlined in pink, drawn over whatever hides it), drag it to move it by whole cells (a see-through copy shows where it will go), and press R to turn it a quarter (Shift+R the other way), F to mirror it, E and Q to raise and lower it a quarter storey (with Shift, a twentieth) and Delete to take it away. The palette on the left lists every piece of both kits, grouped by what each is for and found by the words of its name ("wood door"), a dot marking its kit; pick one and click a cell to place it, as many times as wanted, turned, mirrored and raised by the same keys before it goes down. The cell under the pointer is outlined in blue at the height of what is being placed or moved. Esc lets go of the piece picked, or else of the selection. Ctrl+Z undoes, back through the last two hundred changes, and Ctrl+Y redoes. The camera is the editor's own: the right button turns it, the middle slides it, the wheel and + and − zoom it further out than play's, the arrow keys turn and tilt it, and WASD slide it over the map (Shift faster). The panel shows each action's key, and its buttons do the same.

The map is data. The page opens on the map saved in this browser if there is one, and on the town as the code places it if not, and every change is saved to the browser as it is made (`nw:adventure:map` in local storage). While a saved map is shown, the ↺ button throws it away and goes back to the code's town; a saved map that can't be read says why and keeps the editor shut until it is thrown away. Save downloads the map as a JSON file, and Load reads one in the map's place, as one change undo takes back. A file is read whole or not at all: an unknown piece, a missing or misspelt field, a place named twice or another format's version refuses it, naming every problem.

A map's file holds the pieces and nothing else: `{ "version": 1, "places": [{ "name": "INN", "placements": [{ "piece": "wall-door", "x": -6, "z": -7, "turn": 1 }, ...] }, ...] }`, one placement a line, each a `Placement` as the code writes it (`piece`, `x`, `z`, and `y`, `turn` and `mirror` where it has them). Its places are the code's: `TREES`, each part of the town by its constant in `PLACES`, and `PLANTS`, and pieces placed in the editor go into `PLACED`. So a map maps back onto the code: a session bringing an edited map into the game reads each place's placements into its constant. The ground's paths and the villagers stay in the code: the ground is derived from its paths rather than placed piece by piece, and villagers are characters, not pieces. The version tells a later format's reader an old file from a new one.

Outside the editor the map is drawn as it always is, merged by chunk; in it, a change rebuilds only the chunks it touched, and picking tests each piece's box before its faces, so editing costs play nothing.

## Building with the town kit

A session placing or building with the Fantasy Town Kit starts from these tools, not from guesses at the pieces:

- **`buildings.ts`** holds the kit's rules for putting a building together, in its opening comment: which way a wall's outside faces and so where its piece goes, posts and pillars, the kit's corner walls (squared, rounded, chamfered, cut, bent and curved, four curved making a round tower), open frames, the halves of two-cell openings and their mirrors, storeys and plinths, the roofs (gabled one, two or three cells wide, flat-topped, hipped, round, pointed, with dormers, each high and red or low and teal), what hangs on a wall and what it mustn't cover, chimneys and balconies. `building()` turns a footprint, its storeys and their wall pieces into placements, facing any way, and `winged()` an L of a hipped block and a wing under one roof with a valley. Its tests pin the turns and ends that are easy to get wrong. A shape the kit offers that the builder can't make yet is worked out on the bench, then added to the builder and its rules. Pieces that aren't part of a building's shell (sails, a mill wheel, stalls, fences, hedges, paving, basins, stairs to a wall's walk, lanterns) are placed directly, by the helpers in `town.ts` where a kind of piece makes a shape of its own: curbed paving, a ring of hedge or fence, a basin built from its rim's pieces. A placement can be mirrored, for the pieces the kit makes in one hand only.
- **The piece bench**, `?view=pieces` (`&kit=nature` for the Nature Kit): every piece of a kit on the grid, named, with a pink arrow along its own +x (where a wall stands) and a blue one along +z, to walk round.
- **The kit's own pictures** in Kenney's download: `Previews/` shows each piece alone, and `Sample.png` a village built from them, the one place the kit shows how its pieces go together (a porch, an arch two storeys high, stairs up a building's side). A piece's use is checked against it before it is placed.
- **`tools/look.mjs`** shoots stills of the game or the bench from exact views, headless, so a session sees what it built.
- **`tools/kit-bounds.mjs`** measures pieces from their files: their extent, and where their highest points lie, which tells which end of a slope is up.

A piece's name is not proof of what it is. Some of the kit's pieces aren't what their names suggest (`wall-wood-window-small` is a plaster wall with no timber, `wall-wood-corner-edge` a wall bent across its cell rather than a post), and some decorations are made to lie in a wall's very face. So each new piece is looked at on the bench before it is used.

Pieces are named by `TownPiece`, so a misspelt piece fails the typecheck. A building is judged in the running game from all round, as art is.

## Fitting the kits together

The world is built from several of Kenney's kits, made apart, so each kit is fitted to one ground and one palette as its files load (`scene/kit-fit.ts`), never by editing the files:

- **Height.** The town's pieces and the characters stand on y = 0. The Nature Kit's grass lies 0.05 low and its paths are sunk 0.05 below that, so its pieces are lifted to put the grass a hair under 0: a town piece's base, such as a basin's floor seen through its water, never fights the grass for depth. A path's bed stays sunk, so on a path the player stands a little above it.
- **Materials.** The Nature Kit's files mark every material wholly metallic, and write their colours as the screen shows them where glTF means them linear; both are corrected, so its pieces draw matte in their own colours.
- **Palette.** Its greens are teal and its dirt pale beside the town kit's colour map, so its grass, leaves and dirt are repainted with Adventure's own tokens (`scene/tokens.css`), each a swatch of the town kit's map. Its flowers and other colours keep its own.
- **Scale.** The kits share a one-unit cell and a storey of 1 (the Nature Kit's cliffs climb what a town wall does), and every piece is drawn as made: each kit is in proportion within itself, so the only scale a kit could take is one for the whole kit, and anything but 1 would take its tiles off the shared grid. Neither kit is drawn to real-world sizes but each shrinks the world its own way for the cartoon look (a storey of the town kit's stairs has eight steps, a Nature cliff's five; the Nature Kit's fences and trees are lower than the town kit's), so a difference in size between the kits is their style, not an error, and where two styles sit badly together the fix is which pieces go where, never their size. The characters are drawn as made, 0.67 to 0.85 tall against a storey of 1: a little taller than the town kit's doorways (0.75), which are the outside of a building, not a way in at the characters' own scale.

The two kits' paths are for different places: the town kit's `road` pieces are paving with curbs, raised on the ground, for a village's streets and squares; the Nature Kit's `ground_path*` tiles are dirt tracks sunk in grass, for ways through the country. The Nature Kit lays dirt and water alike, its river tiles the shapes of its path tiles (`ways.ts`): a way one cell wide joins by which neighbours are path (or water) too, its ends closed in a rounded bank; a stretch two cells or more across is open in its middle, banked along its sides, rounded at its outer corners, with a nub at an inner one, and opens between two nubs where a narrow way leaves it; a lone cell is a pit or pond of its own. The kit's `End` is not that joint but a way running out level, onto steps, a ramp or a field, and its `Rocks` and `BendBank` are a straight and a bend made rough.

## The country

Round the village lies the country, built from every piece of the Nature Kit (`country.ts`), each part named in the ~ overlay by its constant. Across the north runs a grey stone `RIDGE` two storeys high, a terrace a storey up along its foot with steps up to it and on to the top, a spur jutting south with a cave in its face and its west corner cut on the diagonal, moss trailing down its faces, and on top a pine wood (`RIDGE_WOOD`), a walk to a lookout's fire ring, and a stream from a spring that crosses the walk under an arched footbridge and goes over the edge as a waterfall into a pool. The `RIVER` runs from the pool south past the town wall, winding and narrow, over rocks, with footbridges where the paths cross it, a spring's brook joining it and a stepping stone, then broadens past the road, which crosses on a stone bridge of three spans, and an arched timber bridge on the way south, into a lake in the south-west. Its `LAKE_SHORE` has an island out on an arched stone bridge, a bay crossed by a timber bridge, a jetty with a canoe tied up, sand along the east shore, lilies, hummocks and reeds; a `FISHING_CAMP` of two tents round a fire pit stands by it. West of the river a pine wood (`WEST_WOOD`) holds a `WEST_CAMP` round its fire; between the river and the wall is the `WILDWOOD`. North of the farm a `QUARRY` is cut back in benches of stone blocks, with ramps up them and a working in its face; north-east, an `AUTUMN_WOOD` of turned leaves with a `CRAG` of stone on a broad base and a `WOODCUTTERS` yard. East of the village the farm track runs between fields watered by a brook and ditches crossed by small bridges (`FIELD_BRIDGES`): `CORN` sown a row at a time, `WHEAT` on looped double rows, a `KITCHEN_GARDEN`, a fenced `PUMPKIN_PATCH` whose four corners are each the kit's own corner (square, diagonal, bent, bent with a post), a `PEN` cut at its corners, bamboo by the water, and `HEDGEROWS`. South-east is red-rock badlands round a dry wash: a `MESA` with a shelf, steps up both, a cave, a cut corner and a waterfall into an `OASIS` of palms; a half-storey `LEDGE` with a ramp, a `KNOLL` stepped all round, a `BUTTE` on a broad foot, `BLOCKS` weathered into a stepped shelf, and spires, boulders and cacti (`BADLANDS`). South of the village, stepping stones lead to a `RUIN_HILL`, a temple fallen to its columns on a hill stepped all round, and branch at a round flag to a `STONE_CIRCLE`; a low `STONE_LEDGE` lies in the `MEADOW`.

- **Height.** A rise is floored with the kit's grass tile a storey up for each storey (half a storey for a half edge), and stood on as the town's blocks are; so are its cliff blocks and steps, bridges, boardwalks, stepping stones and flags. Pieces on a path's or dirt patch's sunk bed stand 0.05 lower, and those floating on water (lilies, a canoe, hummocks) at the water's level. The player wades through water at its surface, and walks through cliff faces and trees as through the town's walls.
- **Scattered growth.** Woods and meadows are scattered on a loose grid from a fixed seed, so they stand where they stood, each kept clear of water, paths, dirt, the village and every cell another piece stands in, and what grows under the trees kept clear of their trunks. Each scatter goes round its list of pieces, so every tree, flower and mushroom it lists appears.
- **The kit's own pictures.** The Nature Kit's download shows each piece from all four corners in `Isometric/<piece>_<NE|NW|SE|SW>.png` and from the side in `Side/`, and in `Sample.png` an island built from them: cliffs storeys high with grass on top, a waterfall, a river under bridges, a tent and a fire, palms on sand, and fields on stepped terraces. A cliff's shape is read from the bench before it is placed: which edge a face stands on, which corner a post fills, which way a ramp, stairs or a cave's mouth faces (`tools/kit-bounds.mjs` and a top-down still settle it).
- **Left out.** Every piece of the kit has a place. Its narrow path crossing is used by the village's paths, where two meet.

## Code map

- `characters.ts`: the Mini Characters roster, by file name, and where each is served.
- `villagers.ts`: which character each player is, where every other one stands or sits and which way they look, and the kit's seats by height.
- `input/controls.ts`: the game's keyboard and controller: actions bound to keys by their place on the keyboard and to a pad's buttons and sticks (through `src/shared/lib/gamepad/`), held keys and every standard pad tracked outside React, and read by the frame loop once a frame as what each player asks for, the keys winning where both ask. It tells the page only the lineup, which the hints follow.
- `input/lineup.ts`: who plays with what, pure: the input the first player uses, the one a second holds, and how join, leaving and an unplugged pad change them. A game's input is not a page's: it is read per frame in actions, not handled per key event, so it doesn't use the shared hooks built for tools.
- `fantasy-town.ts` and `nature-kit.ts`: every piece of each kit by name, and where each is served; `kit.ts` names a piece of either.
- `ways.ts`: the grid's extent and cells, and the Nature Kit's tiles for a stretch of path or water over some cells, each the tile and turn its neighbours call for.
- `ground.ts`: the ground as cells: grass everywhere but the paths, the country's dirt patches and its water.
- `cliffs.ts`: the Nature Kit's cliffs, in its opening comment: how its faces, corners, steps and diagonals stand round a rise, and `rise()`, which raises cells to their heights and stands every face round them.
- `country.ts`: the country as placements, water, dirt and paths, its parts named in `COUNTRY` for the overlay, and the scattering of woods and meadows clear of everything placed.
- `editor/map.ts`: the map as data, pure: the document, the code's map, an edit as a new map, and the JSON file written and read (validated whole). `editor/saved.ts` keeps it in the browser, `editor/palette.ts` groups and searches the kits' pieces, and `editor/keys.ts` holds the editor's keys.
- `scene/map-editor.ts`: the editor in the scene: picking, dragging and placing pieces under the pointer, the selection and cell outlines, the see-through copy, its keys, undo, and its camera. `components/map-editor-panel.tsx` is its panel and the map button, and opens the page on the saved map.
- `town.ts`: the town as the code places it on the grid: the trees, the village's buildings and what stands round them, its paving, gardens and walls, its paths and plants. `PLACES` names each part of the town and the country by its constant, for the overlay and the map's file.
- `buildings.ts`: the kit's rules for a building, and the builder.
- `bench.ts` and `scene/bench-marks.ts`: the piece bench's layout, and its names and axis arrows.
- `scene/debug-overlay.ts` and `scene/name-label.ts`: the ~ overlay's names and cell numbers, and the name plates it and the bench share.
- `scene/town.ts`: builds the piece bench's pieces, each file loaded once and every placement a clone, an object of its own to name and mark.
- `scene/merged.ts`: builds placed pieces that never move (the ground's tiles, and the map a chunk at a time) merged into one mesh for each material in each square of five cells by five. The kits share a handful of materials, so a square is a few draws; it is culled whole, and drawn nearest first, so what it hides is never shaded. The frame is bound by the pixels the GPU shades, so that order is what counts: instanced meshes all sort as if at the world's origin, and draw in no useful order. A mirrored piece's faces are turned back round as it is merged. A placement can still be had as an object of its own, outside the scene, to cast a ray at.
- `scene/town-layer.ts`: the map's pieces drawn merged by chunk, and redrawn a chunk at a time: a chunk whose placements are the same objects as before is kept, so an edit rebuilds only what it touched.
- `scene/kit-fit.ts` and `scene/tokens.css`: fits each kit's pieces to the world as they load.
- `player.ts`: the player on foot, pure: position, facing and gait, stepped from a move, the gaits' ground speeds, and how fast a part-pushed stick walks and its clip plays.
- `scene/character-viewer.ts`: the viewer: the renderer, grid floor, lights, the players and the second's joining and leaving, drawing the screen whole or in two halves, and the villagers, each a clone of its file with its own mixer. Each file loads once.
- `scene/player-view.ts`: one player on foot and the camera that follows them: their place, settle, character and clips, and their camera's orbit, turn, tilt and zoom.
- `weapons.ts`: the pack's weapons, each with the point in its own model the hand closes round.
- `components/adventure.tsx`: the page: the animation and weapon pickers, the key hints, the backend, loading and any failure. In development it puts a probe on the page for tools: `window.__adventure.lookFrom(eye, at, player?)` drives a player's camera (the first's unless told) to an exact view, `players()` says how many play, `drawn()` counts what a frame drew and walked, `loaded()` lists the parts of the world in place, `leaveOut(part, out)` takes one out of the frame (or halves the resolution), and `gpuTime()` reads the GPU's own time when the page opened with `?gpu-time`. `tools/profile.mjs` uses them to price each part of the frame on the integrated GPU, in alternated pairs, and to profile the main thread; it draws uncapped frames on the GPU that drives the owner's screen, so it is run only once the owner has agreed, and a count of the frame's work from `drawn()` is the first evidence; with `--players 2` it joins a second player on a stand-in pad, and its `second` lever prices them.
- `credits.ts`: Kenney's credit for every pack used.

## Assets

- `public/adventure/mini-characters/`: from Kenney's Mini Characters 2.0, the 20 `character-*.glb` (Female A with her crutches taken off by `tools/strip-crutches.mjs`: the pack models them into her arms but gives her no clip for them, so in every clip they splay out with her arms, and she couldn't stand among the others), the 5 `weapon-*.glb`, and `Textures/colormap.png`, the one 512² colour map they all share. Each GLB refers to it by that relative path rather than embedding it, so it must stay beside them. The pack's other props (aids, wheelchairs, luggage, shields) aren't used yet.
- `public/adventure/fantasy-town/`: every GLB of Kenney's Fantasy Town Kit 2.0, and its own `Textures/colormap.png`, separate from the characters' one. Its pieces fill one unit cell each, centred on their origin, walls along the cell's +x edge with their outside facing back into the cell, so a piece is placed by its cell and quarter turns. Its walls stand about a third taller than a character.
- `public/adventure/skyboxes/skybox-day.png`: the day sky from Kenney's Skyboxes, one 4096×2048 panorama wrapped round the world on a dome drawn after everything solid, at the far plane, so only the sky's own pixels are shaded with it (the scene's background would shade every pixel, under everything). The pack's other skies, and how they might make a day and night, are in `ideas/day-night.md`.
- `public/adventure/nature-kit/`: every GLB of Kenney's Nature Kit 2.1. Each carries its own flat colours, with no texture. Its ground tiles fill one unit cell each, centred on their origin, like the town kit's.
- The skeleton has no hand joints: a held weapon is a child of its arm's joint, at a point out along the arm that fits every character, since the arms are rigid. The weapons don't all have their origin where the hand holds them, so each records its own grip point.
- The characters share one rigid seven-joint skeleton and the same clips. They are not all one height (about 0.67 to 0.85), and their bind pose has the arms out, so a bounding box of the model at rest is wider than the character as it stands in any clip.
- Each GLB loads its own copy of the shared colour map, filtered with mipmaps, as the pack sets it. Both stop mattering only once many characters share a scene, or are seen small: then one shared texture, and a look at how mipmaps blend the map's colour swatches, belong in the build step.
