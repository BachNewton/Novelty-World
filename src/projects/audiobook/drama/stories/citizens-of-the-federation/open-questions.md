# Open questions

What the producer still has to decide, or wants to come back to, each with its context and Claude's recommendation. An answered question moves into the file it belongs to and leaves this list.

## Telling canon from real history

Agreed: the book's 2020s are our real ones, with Star Trek's events happening inside them. Open: how a reader tells which is which without being told.

**Recommended:** what the reader lived through gets a mention; what they didn't gets the story. A real event, place or device can be named in passing, because the reader brings it with them. A canon event or an invented thing is new to the reader, so it arrives the way it arrives to the people in the scene, with what it is and what it does to them. The reader never needs a label, because the difference shows as familiarity: one is remembered, the other is news. The past-tense companion can also place an era in a sentence ("That was the autumn when…"). Reading canon as anchor points (`CLAUDE.md`) helps: a canon event and its real counterpart meet at their seams, as the Bell Riots do beside the real ruling on sleeping outside, so the reader is never asked to keep them apart.

## The second half's weight

Twelve chapters cover 2020 to 2030 and seven cover 2032 to 2063. **Recommended:** let the later chapters grow as they come, adding chapters between canon's fixed points where a choice is worth a chapter, rather than planning them all now.

## What readers flagged that hasn't been changed

Cold readers raised these, and they wait for the producer's own reading.

- **Chapter 13's drive east:** Ares IV and Buck Bokai crowd Ruth saying "Leave it" to the radio, and read as winks to someone who hasn't seen Star Trek. **Recommended:** keep Ares IV, for Walt, and cut Bokai.
- **Chapter 13's amnesty:** one family's objection holding nine hundred prisoners can feel built to force Carol's choice. **Recommended:** keep it only if it read as a real system.
- **Chapter 15:** Ben recognises Khan though Carol never named him in that scene, and the consent form has parents admit a crime in writing, on paper inspectors read. **Recommended:** fix both.
- **Chapters 13 to 15** echo their own lines ("small and neat", "I'm telling you so you know", "getting most of it") until the echo is heard before the meaning. **Recommended:** keep each once.
- **Chapter 9:** the history and Anna's application read as briefings inside tense scenes. **Recommended:** judge after reading; both carry what a reader needs.

## Holes in Ruth's road to Finland and the years around it

A fact-checker found these. Each needs a story decision, and they are in order of how much a reader would mind.

- **Chapter 4's choice may cost nothing.** Under the 2024 law Anna can't apply before August 2027 with or without the course, and a passport in October 2028 fits an application made then. Chapter 4's "the August after Theo turned four" was true when she said it. Chapter 7's "With the course, it would have been" the same August is false by then, and it is the only place the choice's cost is paid on the page, so it waits for this decision. The outline's "lost year" rests on it too. **Recommended:** make her pass the exam late, visibly, so that without the course she misses a Ruth who could have left before the war; or give the choice a different cost.
- **October 2030 to October 2032: why doesn't Anna come for Ruth?** She has the letter, Ruth has a passport and an address, and there's a working phone and peace from May 2032. **Recommended:** no road through until the peace, and since then Ruth saying "not yet" every Sunday, which her reason for going can turn.
- **November 2029 to February 2030: why wait three months** with a letter in hand and a bus every week? **Recommended:** the District doesn't countersign the release until February.
- **February 2030: why not Detroit?** It's three hours away, Americans are let out there, and Carol and planes are in Toronto. **Recommended:** the road north is shut to District cards.
- **9 February 2030: why can nobody else take the second seat, and why does a passport holder need a District card?** Pruitt is the obvious escort. The week it takes to put a new name on the release is on the page, and a reader may still ask. **Recommended:** the checkpoints check the District's rolls, whatever papers she holds.
- **2027 to 2028: why is Ruth still inside once she's well?** Anna can sign her out to any address, and Ruth gets out for a morning at the post office, yet she never shares a room with Walt. **Recommended:** releases are only for appointments, and chapter 8's "any address" changes to fit.
- **2028: the visa-free visit.** From Helsinki Anna could file for Ruth on humanitarian grounds and wait lawfully, though she might well lose. An EU route also exists, by living three months in another EU country and returning with a dependent parent. **Recommended:** a lawyer closes both in a line, because losing means no bed and no shot to go back to.
- **Who applies, and who decides.** Ruth must file for herself, as fully dependent, which she swore on the porch she would never be. And her declaration stops giving Anna authority once Ruth is well, yet in 2030 Anna decides for her. **Recommended:** use both. Dolores watches Ruth correct and sign the form, and Anna knows in 2030 that no paper lets her choose.
- **2026: Ben's permanent permit.** From August 2026 he can very likely apply (four years and €40,000 a year, with no language test), which weakens "the permits ride on his job" in chapters 7 and 8. **Recommended:** his application is in and unanswered.
- **The wartime measure asks for "nobody to look after her",** and by 2032 Naomi does. **Recommended:** letters already issued stand.
- **Who carried the fourteen babies?** They were born from donated embryos, and nobody says to whom. **Recommended:** one line in Carol's reading of the box, with no name.
- **Migri's two pages differ** on whether close family life and full dependence are both required or are alternatives. The book follows its FAQ, which gives alternatives. **Recommended:** check section 50 of the Aliens Act.

## What hasn't been verified

- The book's own rules, which are not real law: Finland's wartime measure for its citizens' parents, the District's release to a named escort, the amnesty's one objection to a county, and Finland's register of Augments.
- The exact wording of *Picard* on the organism from Io and on Project Khan, known only from recaps, and the year of young Khan's episode, taken as 2022 from its thirty years after 1992. **Recommended:** check all three against the episodes.
- Many chapters' final revisions have had no cold read, Ruth's reason in chapters 3, 11 and 13 among them. **Recommended:** let the producer's notes say which need one.

## A narrator voice per era

When the book is performed, each era (chapters 1 to 12 are one) could get its own narrator voice from the same description, so a new voice marks a jump in time. The cost is the sense of one companion across all of it, which the voice the producer chose leans toward.

## Where it stops

How far the book goes, and which series count as canon: up to the 32nd century of *Discovery*, or not.
