## Telling canon from real history

Agreed: the book's 2020s are our real ones, with Star Trek's events happening inside them. Open: how a reader tells which is which without being told.

**Recommended:** what the reader lived through gets a mention; what they didn't gets the story. A real event, place or device can be named in passing, because the reader brings it with them. A canon event or an invented thing is new to the reader, so it arrives the way it arrives to the people in the scene, with what it is and what it does to them. The reader never needs a label, because the difference shows as familiarity: one is remembered, the other is news. The past-tense companion can also place an era in a sentence ("That was the autumn when…"). Reading canon as anchor points (`CLAUDE.md`) helps: a canon event and its real counterpart meet at their seams, as the Bell Riots do beside the real ruling on sleeping outside, so the reader is never asked to keep them apart.

## The Bell Riots after chapter 3

The Bell Riots have their own chapter, Labor Day (chapter 3). The ruling (`CLAUDE.md`) says they turned opinion and opinion split. The book now seeds the Districts before it: in chapter 1 Nora waves at a line outside Columbus's new District on the news (autumn 2020), and in chapter 2 the county commissioners are "still arguing" on the front page about whether Dayton needs one. Chapter 3 ends with Dayton voting, the Thursday after the riots, to build its own District by the river. So it is being built, not filled, in 2024 and 2025, while California starts closing its own.

**Recommended:** chapter 4 meets Dayton's District as finished and full: Walt's "Nobody we know, Annie" from chapter 3 under the man on television saying "the dims have to go somewhere". Gabriel Bell's name can come up once, the way each half of America says it, now something Ben has seen for himself. Chapter 9 needs nothing more: its residents tearing the fence down is Sisko's "They'll be torn down".

## Khan in chapter 10

Khan is a small child in Toronto around 2022, so the Eugenics Wars of chapter 10 (the 2030s to 2040s, outside America) are his rise. In "Space Seed" he ruled from Asia through the Middle East. The real counterpart of 1967's fear is the gene-edited twins of 2018. Dolores's daughter, who cared for him in Toronto, carries the chapter.

**Recommended:** chapter 10's question, what a child who was made better is and what they owe or deserve, lands on that real counterpart. The children made better in the 2010s and 2020s are grown by then, the 2018 twins and Khan among their generation, and the war is fought over what they are. Before writing, check what *Strange New Worlds* shows of the Toronto lab, and whether Canada was recruiting nurses from Ohio in 2020 and 2021.

## A narrator voice per era

When the book is performed, each era (chapters 1 to 9 are one) could get its own narrator voice from the same description, so a new voice marks a jump in time. The cost is the sense of one companion across all of it, which the voice the producer chose leans toward.
