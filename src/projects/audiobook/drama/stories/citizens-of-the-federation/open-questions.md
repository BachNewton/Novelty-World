# Open questions

What the producer still has to decide, or wants to come back to, each with its context and Claude's recommendation. An answered question moves into the file it belongs to and leaves this list.

## Telling canon from real history

Agreed: the book's 2020s are our real ones, with Star Trek's events happening inside them. Open: how a reader tells which is which without being told.

**Recommended:** what the reader lived through gets a mention; what they didn't gets the story. A real event, place or device can be named in passing, because the reader brings it with them. A canon event or an invented thing is new to the reader, so it arrives the way it arrives to the people in the scene, with what it is and what it does to them. The reader never needs a label, because the difference shows as familiarity: one is remembered, the other is news. The past-tense companion can also place an era in a sentence ("That was the autumn when…"). Reading canon as anchor points (`CLAUDE.md`) helps: a canon event and its real counterpart meet at their seams, as the Bell Riots do beside the real ruling on sleeping outside, so the reader is never asked to keep them apart.

## The second half's weight

Twelve chapters cover 2020 to 2030 and seven cover 2032 to 2063. **Recommended:** let the later chapters grow as they come, adding chapters between canon's fixed points where a choice is worth a chapter, rather than planning them all now.

## What readers flagged that hasn't been changed

Cold readers raised these, and they wait for the producer's own reading.

- **Chapter 13's drive east:** Ares IV and Buck Bokai crowd Ruth saying "Leave it" to the radio, and read as winks to someone who hasn't seen Star Trek. **Recommended:** keep Ares IV, for Walt, and cut Bokai.
- **Chapter 13's amnesty:** one family's objection holding nine hundred prisoners can feel built to force Carol's choice. **Recommended:** keep it only if it read as a real system.
- **Chapter 15:** Ben recognises Khan though Carol never named him in that scene, and the consent form has parents admit a crime in writing, on paper inspectors read. **Recommended:** fix both.
- **Chapters 13 to 15** echo their own lines ("small and neat", "I'm telling you so you know", "getting most of it") until the echo is heard before the meaning. **Recommended:** keep each once.
- **Chapter 9:** the history and Anna's application read as briefings inside tense scenes. **Recommended:** judge after reading; both carry what a reader needs.

## What hasn't been verified

- The book's own rules, which are not real law: Finland's wartime measure for its citizens' parents, the District's release to a named escort, the amnesty's one objection to a county, and Finland's register of Augments.
- The exact wording of *Picard* on the organism from Io and on Project Khan, known only from recaps, and the year of young Khan's episode, taken as 2022 from its thirty years after 1992. **Recommended:** check all three against the episodes.
- Many chapters' final revisions have had no cold read, Ruth's reason in chapters 3, 11 and 13 among them. **Recommended:** let the producer's notes say which need one.

## A narrator voice per era

When the book is performed, each era (chapters 1 to 12 are one) could get its own narrator voice from the same description, so a new voice marks a jump in time. The cost is the sense of one companion across all of it, which the voice the producer chose leans toward.

## Where it stops

How far the book goes, and which series count as canon: up to the 32nd century of *Discovery*, or not.
