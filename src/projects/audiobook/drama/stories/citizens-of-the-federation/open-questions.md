# Open questions

What the producer still has to decide, or wants to come back to, each with its context and Claude's recommendation. An answered question moves into the file it belongs to and leaves this list.

## Telling canon from real history

Agreed: the book's 2020s are our real ones, with Star Trek's events happening inside them. Open: how a reader tells which is which without being told.

**Recommended:** what the reader lived through gets a mention; what they didn't gets the story. A real event, place or device can be named in passing, because the reader brings it with them. A canon event or an invented thing is new to the reader, so it arrives the way it arrives to the people in the scene, with what it is and what it does to them. The reader never needs a label, because the difference shows as familiarity: one is remembered, the other is news. The past-tense companion can also place an era in a sentence ("That was the autumn when…"). Reading canon as anchor points (`CLAUDE.md`) helps: a canon event and its real counterpart meet at their seams, as the Bell Riots do beside the real ruling on sleeping outside, so the reader is never asked to keep them apart.

## The Bell Riots in chapters 1 to 3

None of the three chapters mentions the Bell Riots, two years before chapter 1 and the canon event the Dorseys' story stands nearest to. The ruling (`CLAUDE.md`) says they turned opinion and opinion split, so Dayton keeps filling its District while California starts closing its own.

**Recommended:** one trace, in chapter 1 or 2, where the District first comes up: Gabriel Bell's name as one half of America says it and the other half says it, which tells the reader why the fences went up rather than down. Chapter 3 then needs nothing more: its residents tearing the fence down is Sisko's "They'll be torn down".

## The immigrant Sanctuary Districts

*Picard*'s 2024 sends detained immigrants "to a sanctuary district on the border". Its real counterpart is in the book's own geography: Springfield, Ohio, about 25 miles from Dayton and about a quarter Haitian. A false claim about its Haitians made national news in September 2024, the week of the Bell Riots, and in June 2026 they lost their protected status.

**Recommended:** a thread rather than a chapter. Haitian names among the Sanctuary District's residents in chapter 3, or a character from Springfield who steps forward later.

## Khan in chapter 4

Khan is a small child in Toronto around 2022, so the Eugenics Wars of chapter 4 (the 2030s to 2040s, outside America) are his rise. In "Space Seed" he ruled from Asia through the Middle East. The real counterpart of 1967's fear is the gene-edited twins of 2018.

**Recommended:** chapter 4's question, what a child who was made better is and what they owe or deserve, lands on that real counterpart. The children made better in the 2010s and 2020s are grown by then, the 2018 twins and Khan among their generation, and the war is fought over what they are.

## A narrator voice per era

When the book is performed, each era (chapters 1 to 3 are one) could get its own narrator voice from the same description, so a new voice marks a jump in time. The cost is the sense of one companion across all of it, which the voice the producer chose leans toward.
