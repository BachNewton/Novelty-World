# The people

Who everyone is and what happens to them, as the outline has it. Birthdays and every other date are in `timeline.ts`, and any age is computed from it with the date tool, never from this page. Details a chapter settles when it is drafted are added here then.

## The Dorseys and the Novaks (San Francisco, Dayton and Helsinki)

- **Anna Novak,** born Dorsey in Dayton, Ohio, the only child of Walt and Ruth. Detail-minded: she has never met a form she couldn't get through. In 2020 she runs a dentist's front desk in San Francisco, where she came for Ben's job and stayed for the city; the office closes for three weeks on 16 March 2020. She goes to her parents' house in March 2020 "for two weeks" to keep them out of stores, and stays until the family leaves for Helsinki in August 2022. Knowing Finland's rule (a grown child's parent may follow only if she moves at the same time, and only a Finnish citizen can ask), she asks her parents to come with them to San Francisco instead, the only place all of them could go together; they say no, and she chooses Helsinki knowing her parents can't follow unless something terrible happens. She tells Nora the truth about the preschool's lockdown drill, keeping the promise she made herself in the year of the county's Thursday visits. In Helsinki she can read none of the forms that were her competence. In February 2023 she turns down the employment office's free full-time Finnish course for newcomers (only in the first three years; Theo would have gone to daycare all day) to stay home through Theo's early years on Kela's child home care allowance, knowing that evening study takes years and so pushes back the certificate, citizenship, and any chance to ask Finland for her mother if something terrible happened. With the course she could have been a citizen the August after Theo turned five (August 2027). By April 2023 she is on chapter three of a blue textbook, reads Finnish numbers, can spell *laiva*, and has no course, no certificate and no date for either. The Saturday after the eclipse, on a bench at Huffman Prairie, she promises Walt never to let them put Ruth "in one of those places" (he refuses "I'll do everything I can" and asks for her word; she knows keeping it may mean bringing the family back from Finland, and doesn't tell Ruth, at his asking), and breaks the promise in April 2027 by using Ruth's declaration to get her the shot inside Dayton's Sanctuary District. In February 2030 she sends Dolores to sit with her dying father rather than take Ruth to Washington for her permit. She tells her children the truth. On Father's Day 2026, about thirty weeks pregnant with Alma, she learns on the Sunday call that Ruth's clinic closed in September and Ruth has relapsed (Ruth takes her for the county's Thursday woman; Anna answers as that woman: "You're doing a good job"); at bedtime she starts to say *ei se mitään* to Nora, hears her father's "fine" in it, and tells Nora the truth about Ruth's illness instead. She learns that Finland's citizenship rules have lengthened since 2024 and that, without the language exam, she is years from being able to ask for Ruth. She chooses not to fly to Ohio (Finnair would fly her only to the end of her 36th week, so she'd have had to be home by 20 December; no insurance for a birth there; Ben's job), rings Dolores that night (Dolores went round twice in October; Ruth called her the one who brought the poison through the screen door; Walt asked her not to come again and tells her weekly that Ruth is fine, and Dolores let him, to keep Anna off a plane; she will go round again tomorrow). In the kitchen Anna finally tells Ben about the Huffman Prairie promise ("Two years." / "You'd have said we'd do it together." / "That's why."): he is hurt, and thinks of the folder he has never told her about, and still doesn't. Then she rings Walt to say she isn't coming, won't let him say "fine"; he admits Ruth has slept for the first time since Thursday, and agrees to let Dolores in. For each renewal of the declaration since the move she signs the proxy's page before a notary at the American embassy in Helsinki and posts it back, so she has never seen the later copies' backs. In April 2027 (Responsible Party) she flies to Dayton on Nora's eighth birthday with Alma; avoids the piano bench for four days; at the county counter is relieved when Ruth signs herself in and says nothing; asks Carol for "none available" in section nine, for Finland someday; after Ruth refuses Dolores, reads the back of the declaration, and on Thursday signs as the person named, becoming the District's responsible party for Ruth, and holds Ruth's right hand reading the front aloud while Dolores gives the shot.
- **Ben Novak,** Anna's husband, a software developer at Brynner (canon's Brynner Information Systems), on the team that builds its video player; half the team is in Helsinki, from a small Finnish company Brynner bought for its player. Until 2020 he walks past the tents near Brynner's tower every day without seeing them, and has never asked where they go. Raised in Toledo by his grandmother, dutiful rather than warm, after his parents were killed in a car crash when he was three, a year after his older sister, Katie, died at nine of an inherited illness his grandmother never spoke of; she died while he was at college. He has never had anyone near who needed him, and can respect Anna's pull toward her parents without feeling it. When Brynner ends its pandemic arrangements (from 1 September 2022 everyone works from their own office or transfers to another where their team works: for Ben, San Francisco or Helsinki; Belfast's one desk on the team is Declan's), he looks for a job he could do from Dayton, has one offered and taken back in the hiring freezes of June 2022, and moves to his team in Helsinki. On the porch he feels relief, and can see Anna's pain without feeling it. His Finnish colleague Mikko, on his screen every morning since the company was bought, finds them the daycare by the sea. At the Bell Riots (a borrowed reviewer on Brynner's video service, Labor Day 2024) he holds back Lee's clip ("We're all right. All six of us.") by the no-hostages rule, which also guarded the whole line from a court order; he half-thinks, ashamed, of his family's permits riding on his job, and never knows whether that moved his hand. He never learns whether the clip mattered: accounts of when the governor decided disagree. He hasn't told Anna about the folder, and doesn't know about her promise to Walt. When AI cuts his team, he and Declan are the two kept, and the family's permits ride on his job. He tells Nora the truth about his parents and Katie when she asks in April 2024, and drives to Toledo alone the next morning to their graves (one stone for his parents, a smaller one for Katie), the first time since his grandmother's funeral; "She was sick" is all his grandmother ever told him, once, and he never asked again; Anna has known about Katie from their first year together and learned to leave it shut. At that supper Ruth asks "Sick with what?" ("Nobody ever said." "Somebody should have."), and the question goes to the grave with him. He knows his parents' faces from six photographs, and comes back to find Walt on the porch watching for his car. He learns why his sister died when Alma is diagnosed. In Helsinki he leans on Antti Laine, a quiet Finnish dad he met at the daycare gate. In spring 2025 Brynner's machine takes over most of the video team's work; the Finns, Mikko among them, are let go after Finland's legally required change negotiations, and Ben and Declan are the two kept, to check what the machine decides (Ben guesses it's because their names are in the Labor Day log). Mikko, who was better at the player, is still looking for work a year and a half later; on Father's Day 2026 he sends Ben a photo of his son's card (*Isi on nyt aina kotona*, "Daddy is always home now": "Best review I've had all year"), and Ben can only send a heart. If Ben's job goes, the family has six months to find another or leave. On that Father's Day he tells Nora the truth about the building's shelter.
- **Nora,** born April 2019 in San Francisco, a baby when the family goes to Dayton and three when they move to Helsinki. At two and a half she starts at the preschool in the basement of Ruth's church (her teacher, Miss Jackie); at three she shows the family the lockdown drill she calls "the quiet game". On the leaving day she sings the preschool's going-home song to Walt through the front window; Anna takes down the plastic stars from her own childhood ceiling for Nora's room in Helsinki. The eclipse and the Europa launch on her fifth birthday light her love of space; she draws a space picture for Walt every birthday after, which he keeps on his fridge. Starts school in Helsinki in August 2026 and walks there alone with a reflector on her coat; reads everything aloud and "improves" it, a family game nobody catches her out on (her Father's Day card gains "You are not good at singing"). On Father's Day 2026 she holds the shelter door while Ben tells her what the room is for (Aino's great-grandmother and the sewing machine; "Even us?" "Everybody who lives upstairs"), and Anna tells her the truth about Grandma's illness; she asks whether Grandma can come and live in the room for everybody, and tells Anna she is "not very good yet" at Finnish. At ten she tells her dying grandfather the crew are coming. She becomes a scientist, and is near Cochrane's work, not on his ship, at First Contact.
- **Theo,** born February 2022 in Dayton, six months old on the leaving day. Finns call him *Teo*. Home with Anna through 2023; walks in March 2023 into the arms of the woman who keeps the playground park's hut. The sea is his from his first ferry, to Suomenlinna on Easter Saturday 2023, where he laughs at the sea and weeps when the ferry leaves without him; his first sure word, the next day, is *laiva*, Finnish for ship, learned from Nora; he becomes a marine scientist, practical where Nora looks to the stars, and in the 2050s decides whether to use the organism from Io to heal the Baltic.
- **Alma,** born January 2027 in Helsinki. Diagnosed at six with the illness that killed Ben's sister; the era's cure can only cure by enhancing, so saving her makes her an Augment. In the Eugenics Wars she is an Augment in a war over Augments.
- **Walt Dorsey,** Anna's father. Polio at five, in 1952, in a ward where his mother could only stand at the window; one good leg and a steel brace on the other. Missed polio's vaccine by three years and is "on time" for COVID's. Thirty-four years at a car-parts plant; follows spaceflight. Says "everything's fine": a flaw out of love and out of pride, since needing help would mean he couldn't manage. Drives (the brace is on his left leg, and an automatic needs only the right); leans on a shopping cart in a store. Keeps going to the store himself in March 2020: three stores in four hours on 14 March. In 2020 he writes the day's COVID numbers from the two o'clock briefing in a notebook from the plant; he lets Ruth stop her shot ("It's your mother's arm"), which is why she names Anna, not him, in her declaration. Decides at supper on the night of the quiet game, before he is asked: when Anna asks them to come to San Francisco he says no to spare her ("Who'd do the tomatoes?"; he knows who would get him up and down its stepped sidewalks, and that she would give up Finland for them), leaving where she goes hers to decide, and sets the Sunday call at eleven, after church, "wherever you end up" (six in the evening in Helsinki). "We'll be fine" is the first time he says it knowing what it costs; Ruth: "Don't you start that with me, Walter." He tore up the envelope on which he worked out how old he'd be when Theo could remember him. From about 2026 post-polio weakness reaches his good leg ("Took its time"); by November 2026 a walker stands by the front door, "for the garbage cans". Votes for the doctor of the 2020 briefings for governor in November 2026 ("I figured I owed her the one vote"). For nine Sundays after Ruth's clinic closes he tells Anna about the gutters, and asks Dolores (who went round twice in October and was turned away at the screen door) not to come again, telling her every week that Ruth is fine. Falls in April 2027 and lies seventeen hours before the neighbour's boy finds him: the alert button Anna sent at Christmas was in its box in the drawer ("the cord would catch on the walker"), and Ruth, believing the phone was the county's, covered him with a blanket and set a can of soup and the opener by his hand. He had been taking the walker's wire basket to the dollar store all winter for her soup (sixty-one cans in the cupboard). His fridge door is covered with Nora's birthday pictures and every other drawing she sent. After the hip is pinned he goes to a nursing home in Kettering for rehabilitation; told that Anna used the declaration, he says only "How's she eating?" and "She wrote your name. Not mine." Dies in February 2030 in a cold nursing home in Kettering, on Dolores's phone at the hour of the Sunday call; his last words, "Tell Annie everything's fine."
- **Ruth Dorsey,** Anna's mother, a piano teacher and the organist at her Lutheran church. Schizoaffective disorder, begun in her twenties; an autumn in hospital in 2000, when Anna was eleven, and a county woman checking on Anna's care every Thursday for a year after. A monthly injection from Dolores keeps her well, and dulls her, with a slight tremor in her left hand she hides at the piano; her left arm is Dolores's. In 2020 she chooses to go without the shot to get her music back, sees the signs herself, asks for Dolores, and then writes a declaration at the bank naming Anna to decide when she can't ("When I am ill... Do not listen to me. Listen to this."). Her no to San Francisco: "It took me a year to let Dolores near that arm. I haven't got another year to spend on a stranger, and you haven't got one to spend on me," and then the fast clean scale she plays when she won't say anything else; that night she plays something slow at midnight. She checks every permit form with her red pen, and on the leaving day tells Anna she stays the one named on the declaration ("They have telephones in Finland. I looked it up."). Plays the *Finlandia* hymn to Anna on Sundays, and never explains it. Renews the declaration word for word every three years at the bank ("Like the car"; the current one dated August 2023), still under the hymnal. From about 2024 Dolores no longer comes to houses, so Ruth has her shot at the clinic on Wayne Avenue. In April 2024 she tells Nora the truth about her shaking hand ("It's my medicine... The hand still knows where the notes are"), reads Renée Picard's fear on the clinic's television ("The frightened ones practise"), prays from the organ bench for Renée's nerve, and has the pastor pray for Vantaa, writing out the stress for him in red. Renews the declaration again in August 2026, a month before her clinic closes. Relapses when it closes in September 2026: by November she doesn't sleep, misses her first vote since she was eighteen, eats only soup from cans she opens herself, plays a few bars of the hymn and stops, and on Father's Day takes Anna on the tablet for the county's Thursday woman ("My daughter is fed. She's clean."); inside Dayton's District from April 2027, on the shot again, teaching children on a locked, out-of-tune piano it takes a signed request to use. Off the shot after Dolores goes out of the gate in June 2030; taken north by Naomi in October; around 2032 she chooses whether to leave for Finland. Her illness is written true: psychosis as it is, and Ruth well as a whole person, proud, funny and difficult, never given wisdom by her illness. Well, she corrects everything in red pen (the church bulletin, the state's forms, a nurse's spelling). Walt first heard her play when she was thirty, on no medicine, from the back of her church. Her relapses begin the same way: no sleep, cleaning and laughing, then coincidences meant for her (the radio in 2000; the governor reading Walt's numbers in 2020). In 2020 she half-sees it in Anna's face. The declaration's front says, in her hand: "When I am ill, I will tell you I am perfectly well. I will tell you the injection is poison and that you are not who you are. Do not listen to me. Listen to this." and "I went without it once, in the summer of 2020, to hear myself play. It was worth it. I will not do it again, and if I try to, stop me." She wrote more on the back, in red, adding a dated note at each signing and copying the old ones out again: 2020, "You are going to have to do something terrible. I would like it in writing that I said you could."; 2023, the Sunday hymn is *Finlandia*, "Be Still, My Soul", written "when Finland belonged to Russia and wasn't allowed to say so", played to the end when she is well ("If I ever stop in the middle, you will know before your father tells you"); 2026, Walt's leg is going, don't let him tell Anna it was wrong, "You love me some other way. That's why it's your name." It is kept in the piano bench under the hymnal; Anna first reads the back in April 2027. On the county's form she is "known as" *Mrs. Dorsey* ("My husband calls me Ruthie. He's not to be encouraged."), her letter is D, and she is ticked Jewish so her food comes sealed. From June 2027 she teaches the District's children on its locked piano, Tuesday and Thursday afternoons, by signed request ("Purpose of use: Lessons. Number of participants: As many as come").
- **Gene Kessler** and **Barb,** across the street. Gene worked the same plant as Walt, clears the Dorseys' walk after every snow, remembers the Cutter batches of 1955 and won't have the COVID shot, though he salts the Dorseys' steps so Walt can go and get his; Barb has "never gone first in her life" (and wears the paper eclipse glasses that Gene, with his shade-fourteen welding glass from the plant, won't). Through 2020 he and Walt talk porch to porch across the street. In 2024 they move to their daughter's, because Barb wants to watch the grandchildren grow up somewhere besides a phone: the first time she has gone first.
- **Caleb Henderson,** the neighbours' boy (about thirteen in 2024), who carries the Dorseys' bins down and back for nothing (Walt: "Won't take a dollar for it. I've tried."), and at sixteen finds Walt, because on that Wednesday nobody came out to offer the dollar.

## Dolores, Joy and Carol

- **Dolores,** from the Philippines at twenty-five, a nurse at the county mental health clinic on Wayne Avenue; Ruth's nurse for years, and a mother to Carol, whom she met at the clinic when Carol came for the counsellor's letter she needed to begin her transition, and didn't turn away. Falls ill with COVID in April 2020, Carol and Joel bringing her groceries; restarts Ruth's injection in July. She left her own mother in the Philippines at twenty-five, and for nineteen years her mother told her on the telephone that she was fine; in August 2022 she tells Anna she, not Walt, will call if Ruth isn't. Keeps her license after the clinic closes; restarts Ruth's shot inside the District; in February 2030 sits with Walt while he dies, at Anna's asking, holding up her phone and changing arms; in June 2030 goes out of the District's gate to find more of Ruth's shot and isn't seen again.
- **Joy,** Dolores's daughter, born in Dayton the year her mother arrived; a nurse, and Carol's near-sister. Works a COVID floor in 2020, holding up iPads for goodbyes, and leaves for a children's hospital in Toronto in February 2021 because she has stopped being able to feel for her patients and wants to again. Later works at the Noonien-Singh Institute (canon's Toronto lab, where Khan was a small child in 2022), where Carol joins her.
- **Carol Flory,** a county clerk, a trans woman who took her name in 2013, when Ohio law printed her old name beside it in the newspaper. Raised Old German Baptist north of Dayton, cut off by her family except for what her sister Naomi carries from their mother; sings tenor with the Methodists on Salem Avenue. Dry, quick, courteous, a pen always on her. Anna first meets her in May 2020 on Dolores's steps, Joel carrying water; asked if she's family: "She'd say so. I'd say so." Believes a person's word about who she is counts. Her license said F from 2013; Ohio's BMV stopped accepting the gender-change form in August 2026 (real), and her renewal in April 2027 comes back M, flagging her county record. Takes Ruth's own signature in April 2027, knowing it isn't informed consent (Ruth is bargaining to keep "the girl" from the county), and initials the box attesting Ruth understands; ticks kosher ("She's Jewish until somebody checks, Gary. Nobody has ever checked."); picks "none available" for Anna, which goes to a supervisor. Knows Ruth's tapping as Methodist hymn "five thirty-four" (she doesn't name *Finlandia*; Ruth's note does); Ruth measures her hand ("You've got a pianist's hands. A tenth, easily. Who told you to stop?"); raised to sing in a meetinghouse with no piano, she sings that way to Alma. Her reason, said aloud at the counter: if they wait, a doctor writes that Ruth can't speak for herself; "She's sitting here asking for a pen. I'm not going to be the one who tells her she can't have it." At Wednesday supper at Dolores's (Joel home from drill; they fight over the last spring roll) she tells Anna on the steps what the declaration will do to her job, "so you know", that she won't say Ruth understood, and gives no answer to "Then what do I do?": no permission. Has worked for the county twenty-three years (since about 2004). Let go on Friday 23 April 2027 for four things on one desk: the capacity contradiction, the kosher box, "none available", the M. Her two names go up beside her son's face in January 2029; she leaves for Joy in Toronto, giving Joel the District's ledger. Finds "Mom" under the last line of the narrow book Naomi posts her, and finds Ruth for Anna. Comes back around 2032 to testify, and must decide whether to accept an amnesty that frees the men who shot Joel. At the Institute she keeps the consent forms for children who can't consent.
- **Joel Flory,** Carol's only child, a sergeant in the Ohio Army National Guard; calls her Carol, because the other words didn't fit. On election night 2028 his unit is at the county elections board. At Gate 12A, bound by an oath to two masters who give opposite orders, he goes to keep his boys from firing, fires, and kills an airman; he says "I fired", never "I returned fire". Guards the District with his mother's ledger, writes "None available" on a colonel's list to save the residents, and is shot by his own side in August 2030.

## Dayton's county and the District

- **Gary Pruitt,** the county caseworker, who makes every call he says he'll make (Walt, November 2026: "Calls when he says he will, I'll give him that"; he is trying to get Ruth on a Columbus list and mentions "places downtown"), writes down everything, and tells people what he has written. A licensed independent social worker ("Nobody ever asks a caseworker"), he is the second examiner, with the District's doctor, who finds Ruth lacks capacity in April 2027, knowing it contradicts Carol's attestation; he told Carol himself. In the war he reports Carol's son.
- **Lucille Odom,** a former school cook in the District, who cooked twenty-six years for four hundred children a day at the school on her street until it closed ("and the rent didn't shut"), wants to be let at a stove inside ("you'll smell it from the river"), meets new residents at the inner door because nobody met her, ties Ruth's ration card round her neck with the lace from her own left shoe, and runs its kitchen.
- **Naomi Garber,** Carol's sister, Old German Baptist (no television, nonresistant), who won't say either of Carol's names and carries their mother's "Mother asks if you're eating". In October 2030 she takes the old people with nobody north on her grain truck, Ruth among them, and posts the narrow book to Toronto without a letter. Planted before chapter 12.
- **Miss Bea Yancey,** children's librarian and choir director at Salem Avenue, who hides banned books and hums the note for hymn 534. **Ti Jak,** from Springfield's Haitian community, who goes east on the colonel's buses. **Dale Hutto,** Carol's neighbour, an engineer on the base. **Private Danny Ruhl,** in Joel's squad. **Airman Eli Kasprzak,** twenty, of Green Bay, killed at Gate 12A.
- **The Governor of Ohio** (the doctor of the 2020 briefings, real, never named), who sides with Philadelphia. Every act done under her authority in the war is fair, specific and plainly the book's own.

## Helsinki

- **Antti Laine,** from Kotka, a port town east of Helsinki; about Ben's age, tall and fair, rides a bicycle with studded tyres all winter with his daughter **Aino** (Nora's friend, in her daycare group) on the back. For a whole autumn says only *moi* at the gate; his first words to Ben, in December 2022: "Aino says that Nora says you are afraid of the dark." "November is worse. December has the lights." Says a thing once and leaves you alone with it, which Ben, raised by a grandmother who thought most talk a waste of breath, finds like home. His grandmother left Viipuri at sixteen in the war, with her mother and a sewing machine, and lived sixty years in Kotka, always "from Viipuri". Against NATO all his life, "in my head", until Ukraine. His brother, a truck driver in Kotka, voted for the Finns Party in 2023: "He is not a bad man. I don't know what he is." Told Ben to take the children on the Suomenlinna ferry. Becomes Ben's friend over the book; Ben leans on him in 2027.
- **Abdullahi,** a father at the daycare gate whom Ben nodded to every morning for eight months before he stopped and introduced himself (the morning Finland joined NATO): "Nora is teaching Hamdi English. Hamdi is teaching Nora Somali. I think they are mostly teaching each other the rude words." His daughter **Hamdi** is Nora's best friend.
- **The woman who keeps the playground park's hut,** in her sixties, with a grey braid: speaks to Anna only in slow Finnish, as if she'll understand any day now, and says Theo's name the Finnish way. **The employment office counsellor,** Anna's age, from Estonia (two years to learn Finnish: "For you, longer").

## The Toners (San Francisco and Belfast)

- **Declan Toner,** Ben's closest friend, seconded from Brynner's Belfast office to San Francisco for a year, from early 2019; it ends early, in March 2020, when Orla goes home, and the Toners fly on 19 March. Ben's first real friend: he was thirty-six before he lived anywhere nobody asked which school you went to. Told Ben about the wall at the bottom of his da's street at a fenced-off lot where the tents had been. The funniest person in any meeting; grew up on a street with a peace wall at the bottom of it. Kept with Ben when AI cuts their team. Comes to Dayton for the eclipse with Niamh (6 to 9 April 2024): the first time he and Ben are in one room since March 2020 ("You've legs. I'd only ever seen the top half."); during totality he watches Niamh, not the sky. Parting: "Eight o'clock, then." Back in San Francisco with Ben for Brynner's meetings at the end of August 2024 (his idea to come early: "a weekend first, to be eejits in"; greets Ben in the hotel lobby with "Eight o'clock"). Said good morning to the woman with the geranium "forty mornings" and never asked her name; recognises her in the District's line (Rosa Delgado) and waves at the screen. A borrowed reviewer beside Ben at the Bell Riots: presses red on the man with the shotgun ("Threat"); tells Ben "Rule's the rule" over Lee's clip, then "You did right"; "It wouldn't have changed anything." / "You don't know that." / "No. I don't." At the District's wall: the broadcast ban (an actor reading the Sinn Féin leader's words; "We used to do the voice"), his da's vote for John Hume every election, and "maybe it just works, and we'd all rather not know."
- **Orla Toner,** Declan's wife, a nurse, who goes home in March 2020 to nurse in Belfast's hospitals, by her own choice: her British registration was no use in California, she rang her old ward and started back on 23 March. She found Fort Baker in her first weeks in America. Measures how far off a thing is in beds. A lifelong nationalist whose patients depend on the North's free health service; in the border poll of November 2028 she votes no, and tells no one. By 2028 she nurses nights on a medical ward of thirty beds at the Royal, a mile from Brendan's door, whose patients come from both sides of every wall (Mr. Gilmore, bed six, a shipyard man from beyond the gate with the old Queen tattooed on his arm; Mrs. Kearney, bed four, fifty-two, a school cleaner with diabetes who in the South would earn too much for a card and too little for sixty euro a visit). Her father was interned without charge at nineteen in 1971, wanted one Ireland all his life, and died on her ward in January 2027 after eleven weeks' free care ("You'll see it. I'll not, but you will."). She votes at eight on the Friday, off nights, at the school at the top of Brendan's road; to Declan's "Did you get voted?" she says "First thing." When yes wins she feels relief that her no didn't stop it, and is ashamed of the relief; she rides in the back on the long way round and nearly tells Norman she voted as he did, and doesn't. On election night 2028 she is on nights again: abiding by a count she voted against is the one thing about that Saturday she is proud of, and she can tell no one.
- **Niamh Toner** (said Neeve), their daughter, ten in San Francisco and adoring baby Nora, whose "Ba" she takes for her name; she gives Nora a knitted rabbit for the drive to Dayton, which Nora names Ba. At fourteen she asks for a total eclipse for her birthday and comes to Dayton with Declan for it (6 to 9 April 2024); Nora doesn't remember her, and Niamh learns the rabbit's name ("That's a stupid name for a rabbit"); she cries without a sound through totality. The border poll is her first vote.
- **Brendan Toner,** Declan's da, a retired bus driver, nationalist, widower, two streets along from the gate in the wall (opened at six by a Department of Justice man, locked at half past ten). Eighty at the poll; never late for anything with a time on it; voted for the Agreement in 1998 in the primary school at the top of his road, with his wife. Wants one Ireland and never by Mickey Quinn's way ("The bombs bought three and a half thousand funerals. That's all they ever bought."; "It was never a song"). Goes to Norman's on Wednesdays for the football always by the long way round, though the gate would save twenty minutes, and couldn't say why. Has a Mass said for Sammy every November. Turns the sound down on the result for Norman's sake. **Norman Kerr,** his friend from across the wall, unionist, Protestant (his church doesn't pray for the dead), thirty years a driver out of the same depot; their wives died within a year of each other; comes through the gate every Saturday at two for the racing and his tea. Votes no openly ("never seen the point of a secret"). His brother Sammy, a milk-lorry driver and part-time soldier, shot by the IRA in his cab on his round in 1976 at twenty-five. Believes the bombs bought it; feels London wanted rid of them ("Off the table, he said"); "A passport's not a country." "You want out, get a majority… The count's the count." Rings Declan at half six on the Wednesday after America's election to ask whether the American will abide by it. **Mickey Quinn,** across Brendan's street, eleven years in prison for the IRA, then politics; the yes poster; stops the IRA chant on count night ("Lads. Not tonight."). Niamh, at the poll: eighteen, went to a school that takes both kinds, calls herself Northern Irish, holds both passports and shows the Irish one; "It's a secret ballot, Granda." / "Good girl."

## Further away

- **Renée Picard** (canon), a French astronaut, mission specialist on the Europa Mission's Shango, launched on Nora's fifth birthday and home on 13 February 2030 with something alive from Io that she believes may be someone. Canon: it later heals the oceans and cleans the air.
