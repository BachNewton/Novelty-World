# The people

Who everyone is and what happens to them, as the chapters have it. Birthdays and every other date are in `timeline.ts`, and any age is computed from it with the date tool, never from this page.

## The Dorseys and the Novaks (San Francisco, Dayton and Helsinki)

- **Anna Novak,** born Dorsey in Dayton, Ohio, the only child of Walt and Ruth. She has never met a form she couldn't get through. In 2020 she runs a dentist's front desk in San Francisco. When Ruth spent an autumn in hospital, Anna was eleven, and a county woman came every Thursday for a year to look in the fridge; on those stairs she promised herself that her children would have the truth, and she keeps it (the quiet game, the Vantaa flag, the shelter, her mother's illness, Walt's dying, Alma's cure). She listens to her mother's playing for how her mother is. She takes the family to Dayton in March 2020 "for two weeks", to keep her parents out of stores, and stays until August 2022. Knowing that only a Finnish citizen can bring a parent, and then only one fully dependent on her, she asks them to come when she can bring them, and then offers to stay; they say no and *fine*, and she takes the *fine*, writes *Helsinki* on Brynner's form, covers the word with her hand, and starts a folder marked *Mom and Dad*. In Helsinki, in February 2023, she turns down the free full-time Finnish course to stay home with Theo ("she wanted to be the one at the window"), knowing evening study takes years and pushes back the certificate, citizenship, and any chance to ask for her mother. On a bench at Huffman Prairie in April 2024 she promises Walt never to let them put Ruth "in one of those places"; she tells nobody until Father's Day 2026, when she tells Ben in the kitchen ("Two years." / "You'd have said we'd do it together." / "That's why."). That day, thirty weeks pregnant, she learns Ruth has relapsed, chooses not to fly, and makes Walt let Dolores in without saying *fine*. In April 2027 she flies to Dayton with Alma, hopes Ruth will sign herself into the District, and then uses the declaration, breaking her promise and ending Carol's job, and holds her mother's right hand reading the front aloud while Dolores gives the shot. She learns Finnish in the evenings, becomes a citizen in October 2028 and applies for Ruth as fully dependent, with the county's *none available* paper; the war swallows the identity appointment, and in November 2029 Finland's wartime measure lets Ruth come on a passport and a letter. In February 2030 she sends Dolores to sit with her dying father instead of taking Ruth east to the plane, and lets his last *fine* stand. From August 2030 she rings Carol every Sunday at the old call's hour. In October 2032 she goes to Naomi's farm resolved not to decide ("I've done enough of that"), hears yes, fetches Nora's pictures off Walt's fridge, and brings Ruth home to Helsinki. She signs Alma's consent in Toronto in April 2035 knowing it wrong as well as unlawful, and from then on tells the neighbours "A trial. In Canada. We were lucky", hearing her father's *fine* in it. At fifty-five, in 2045, she refuses to decide Alma's registration ("I'm not doing it a third time"). At sixty-four, at the shelter door in 2053, she tells Alma "You can", writes the radio's numbers on an envelope as Walt wrote the dead, and on the ninth day counts the sixty-four stairs for the first time in thirty-one years. She still dries every plate with a towel, as Ruth did. In 2063 she prints every form for Montana and tells Nora, at the door, "Bring her home."
- **Ben Novak,** Anna's husband, a developer on the team that builds the video player at Brynner (canon's Brynner Information Systems); half the team is in Helsinki, from the Finnish company Brynner bought for its player. Raised in Toledo by a dutiful grandmother after his parents were killed in a car crash when he was three; his older sister, Katie, had died at nine before that, and "She was sick" is all he was ever told. He walks past the tents near Brynner's tower for years without seeing them. Nobody had ever needed him the way Anna's parents need her, and he can see her pain without feeling it ("Well. It's simpler now, anyway"). He moves the family to Helsinki for his job. At the Bell Riots, a borrowed reviewer on Labor Day 2024, he holds back Lee's clip ("We're all right. All six of us.") under the no-hostages rule, with an ashamed thought of the family's permits, and never learns whether it mattered; he keeps that folder from Anna, as she keeps her promise from him. In spring 2025, when a machine takes the video team's work, he and Declan are the two kept, perhaps because their names are in the Labor Day log; the family's permits ride on his job. On Father's Day 2026 he tells Nora the truth about the shelter. Asked in May 2033 what Katie died of, he finds he never asked ("I'm forty-five years old and I never asked anybody"), and writes for her death certificate; it comes in February 2035: Batten disease, late infantile, Alma's illness, and he remembers his grandmother taking him to an eye doctor every August until he was twelve, watching for it. He fears the records a cure would leave ("What if it's found?"), chooses "Toronto. We hear every page. And we ask her", tells Alma about Katie ("So I'm telling you everything"), and signs. In 2045, at fifty-seven, he argues for hiding (registers outlive the governments that make them; "I've been a criminal since I signed my name in Toronto"), and after she registers learns her number by heart. At sixty-six, in the shelter, he offers to go up in her place, turns the crank far beyond his turns, and has no answer when Nora reminds him there was to be a place for everybody who lives upstairs. Antti's death sends him to the daycare fence where Antti used to leave the sled. He answers Declan's "Eight o'clock" in 2053. To Theo, in 2057: "You'll never find out." In 2063: "Your mother has read it to me three times. I have it by heart."
- **Nora,** born April 2019 in San Francisco. Niamh's knitted rabbit, Ba, is hers from the drive to Dayton. At three she shows the family the preschool's lockdown drill, "the quiet game" (her teacher, Miss Jackie), and on the leaving day sings the going-home song to Walt through the front window. The eclipse and the Europa launch on her fifth birthday make space hers, and every birthday after she draws Walt a space picture for his fridge. In Helsinki she walks herself to school from seven, reads everything aloud and improves it, a game nobody catches her out on. On Father's Day 2026 she holds the shelter door while Ben tells her what it is for ("Even us?" "Everybody who lives upstairs"), and tells Anna her Finnish is "not very good yet". At eight her picture flies to Dayton without her. At ten she tells her dying grandfather the crew are coming ("It's the winter I'm ten") and says *Hyvää matkaa, Grandpa*; after the homecoming she draws the crew behind glass and a jar with one mark, its label blank because Finnish makes you choose *jokin* (something) or *joku* (somebody), and gives it to Ruth when Ruth comes from Ohio. At fifteen she describes the world to Alma (the man in a pineapple), wants Alma cured and the cure said openly ("If we hide her, it's like saying they're right… That's what Grandpa used to do"), promises never to tell ("But I'm not going to say it isn't a lie"), and keeps silent on the ferry. A physicist at the university in Helsinki, on the shape of empty space. At twenty-six she walks Alma to the register's office, holds its heavy door, and reads her the number without improving it. At thirty-four she tells Alma the cloud on the horizon exactly, stands at the shelter door unasked, closes it on her sister with both hands and her shoulder, and on the fourth day turns the crank with Ben. From 2057 Zefram Cochrane writes to her at three in the morning, spelling badly, and she half believes him, as Walt half believed the Europa Mission. At thirty-eight she argues against releasing the Io organism (Toulouse might be the start of a language; whoever comes later will ask what we did with the first one we met), signs the scientists' letter (third from the top), and comes on the release ship anyway: "I'm glad it was you that did it, though. Not somebody who was sure." At forty-three she takes Alma to Cochrane's camp, says *Hyvää matkaa* as the Phoenix goes up, and walks her sister out across the field to a Vulcan with her free hand open and empty, telling her the ground. Before dawn she writes home and, for once, improves nothing. The word for them is *joku*.
- **Theo,** born February 2022 in Dayton, six months old on the leaving day; Finns call him *Teo*. He walks his first four steps in March 2023 into the arms of the woman with the grey braid at the playground hut. On his first ferry, to Suomenlinna at Easter 2023, he laughs at the sea and weeps when the ferry leaves without him; his first sure word, the next day, is *laiva*, ship, learned from Nora. At seven, at the homecoming, he hears the thing from Io comes from a moon with no water: "Then it doesn't know about the sea. Somebody should show it." At thirteen: "If she doesn't have it, she dies. That's the only thing anybody's said that's actually about her"; he has the family take Alma on the ferry to see the sea. At twenty-three he counts what lives in the Baltic for the university, and is called back to the navy for two weeks: "We count the cod… It's the same count. It depends who's holding it… Which one could you do every day?" At thirty-one, called back in June 2053, he lays mines from the stern of a minelayer in the gulf: "All of us fine. The sea's grey. Tell Alma I'm coming." At thirty-five he runs the Finnish tank trial of the Io organism at the university's station at Tvärminne; the commission will release it only if all three trials advise it, and Gdańsk and Kiel have signed. He shows it the sea in a dish (it goes to the black water, which is what it eats, and he doesn't tell himself it said yes), makes himself face that he wants to put something into that water that isn't a mine, signs for release ("Unknown. It cannot be asked."), and pours the first release from the same minelayer's rails: *Hyvää matkaa*. In June 2058 the floor of the gulf has air and a worm comes up in brown mud; every week he logs, under the oxygen, how many of the guests died. In 2062 the eastern cod spawn off Bornholm and the eggs live. In 2063: "Did anybody tell them about the sea?"
- **Alma,** born January 2027 in Helsinki. At six, in May 2033, she is diagnosed with Batten disease, the form called CLN6, the illness that killed Katie: sight first, then falls and fits and lost skills, perhaps ten or twelve years. Ruth teaches her the piano by feel. By February 2035, at eight, she sees light, dark and shapes, reads raised dots, has had her first fit, counts the building's sixty-four stairs aloud, and is the only person who has ever caught Nora out. Ba is hers now. Told the truth at bedtime, she knows the word already (Elias says it to Minea, who wins every race), and says yes: "Then I could see if Nora's making it up." At the Institute Khan plays the twinkling one's bass wrong for her ("It's a rubbish answer"); asked what she likes to be called, she says "Alma". Treated on 10 April 2035 by a needle in her back, she sees brightness again by the end of May ("*Laiva*") and catches Nora's invented dolphin. The network mended what was failing and nothing already gone: for life she sees faces only from a kiss's distance. She is never ill again, heals overnight, and hides it as a habit "like holding in your stomach" (fourth in gym, a pretend flu each February, a plaster over a cut already healed), running the shore path at five, fast only when nobody is near. After Ruth dies the piano is hers, and she gives Carol her Sunday lesson. At eighteen she says no to Joy's offer of passage east and registers on 29 May 2045, naming the Institute, her parents and, after a long moment, Carol, spelled right; she writes in the margin "I was asked. I said yes.", insists on her calling name, *Alma*, and the next morning runs past a stranger at full speed. At twenty-six, teaching the piano at Ruth's bench, she gives her shelter place so that Antti's grandchildren can come in, asking Anna to say she can ("If you say no, I'll stay"), and looks at her mother's face from a kiss's distance before she goes. Nine days in the flat's windowless hall with Antti; in the third week her hair falls out and her blood falls lower than her doctor has seen anyone come back from, and comes back. Her lenses cloud the winter after ("Then I'll have had this summer's"), and for nine years she waits on a list for the operation, which moves for everybody else and hardly at all for her. To Theo, in 2057: "Then whatever you do, don't tell yourself it said yes." At thirty-six she goes to Montana ("I'd like to be somewhere nobody's looked me up"), puts her hands on the Phoenix's hull, tells the camp she is an Augment, hears the launch through her feet and laughs, and asks to see the visitor: "Nora. You're not making it up… I'm terrified. I wanted to see you." Afterward some of the camp won't stand near her; a girl of nine asks what his face was like ("Eyebrows").
- **Walt Dorsey,** Anna's father, thirty-four years at a car-parts plant, his cap from it always on. Polio at five, in 1952, in a ward where his mother could only stand at the window, singing; one good leg and a steel brace. He missed polio's vaccine by three years and is "on time" for COVID's. Follows spaceflight, half believing. Says *fine* the way other people say hello. At seventy-three, in March 2020, he does the shopping in three stores in four hours, leaning on the cart. In 2020 he writes the day's numbers from the two o'clock briefing in a notebook from the plant, and lets Ruth stop her shot ("It's your mother's arm"), which is why her declaration names Anna. At seventy-five, on the porch, he says no to ever coming to Finland ("Who'd do the tomatoes?"), won't say he needs her, so that she won't give up Finland for him, sets the Sunday call ("Eleven o'clock, after church. Wherever you end up"), and says "We'll be fine" knowing for the first time what it costs. At seventy-seven, on the bench at Huffman Prairie, he asks Anna for her word. He tells her the Dayton District is for "Nobody we know, Annie". Post-polio weakness takes his good leg from about 2026; the walker is "for the garbage cans". He votes for the doctor of the 2020 briefings for governor. For nine Sundays after Ruth's clinic closes he talks about the gutters, and asks Dolores not to come. In April 2027 he falls in the kitchen and lies seventeen hours, the alert button in its drawer, Ruth's blanket over him and a can of soup by his hand; sixty-one cans in the cupboard, from the dollar store, for her. His fridge is covered with Nora's pictures. In a Kettering nursing home after the hip is pinned: "How's she eating?" and "She wrote your name. Not mine." He signs a paper there: no more hospitals. He sees Ruth twice more, through the District's visitors' window (she tells him his collar is wrong). An ice storm in February 2030 cuts the home's power and stops the machine that breathes for him at night; pneumonia. "You put your mother on that bus. I'm fine." He dies on Sunday 10 February 2030, at eighty-two, in the cold dining room with his cap on and his hair combed, on Dolores's phone at the hour of the call: first "How's she eating?", last "Tell Annie everything's fine." He missed the homecoming by three days. Dolores sees him buried at Woodland Cemetery in the ice: a small flat stone on the side of the hill.
- **Ruth Dorsey,** Anna's mother, a piano teacher and the organist at her Lutheran church, with a red pen for everybody's commas. Schizoaffective disorder since twenty-two; an autumn in hospital in 2000, when Anna was eleven. Her relapses begin the same way: no sleep, cleaning and laughing, then coincidences meant for her. A monthly injection keeps her well and takes the top off her music, with a tremor in her left hand she hides by keeping it moving; her left arm is Dolores's. Walt first heard her play at thirty, on no medicine, from the back of her church. At sixty-four, in 2020, she sends off the stranger the clinic sends ("The left arm is Dolores's. The right arm's mine"), goes without the shot to hear herself play, plays the Bach to an empty church, sees the signs in Anna's face ("Well. I see it. Get me Dolores"), and at the bank writes the declaration naming Anna ("When I am ill… Do not listen to me. Listen to this."), with more on the back, hidden. She renews it word for word every three years ("Like the car"), adding a dated red note on the back each time: 2020, "You are going to have to do something terrible. I would like it in writing that I said you could."; 2023, the slow hymn is *Finlandia*, "Be Still, My Soul", and she plays it to the end when she is well; 2026, Walt's leg is going, don't let him say it was wrong. In 2032 she goes to Finland to set Anna free, who has looked after her since she was eleven, and to arrive as her mother while she needn't arrive as her patient; she would rather have stayed on the farm, says it is for Nora, tells the truth only to Carol and at Walt's grave ("I'm getting Annie out"), and never to Anna. Her no to Finland in 2022: she has been fully dependent, when Anna was eleven, and won't fill in a form to be it again on her; "I haven't got another year to spend on a stranger"; "I won't tell you stay and I won't tell you go", and the fast clean scale she plays when she won't say anything else. She plays the hymn at the end of every Sunday call and never explains it. She tells Nora the truth about her hand, says of Renée Picard "The frightened ones practise", and prays for her nerve. Her clinic closes in September 2026 and she relapses: she misses her first vote since eighteen, eats only soup she opens herself, stops the hymn after a few bars, and takes Anna for the county's Thursday woman ("My daughter is fed. She's clean"). In April 2027 she signs herself into the District ("Put down that I came quietly"), is "known as" *Mrs. Dorsey*, lettered D, ticked Jewish for the sealed food, and has Lucille's shoelace for her ration card; refusing the shot as a resident, she is given it on the declaration. From June 2027, at seventy-one, she teaches the District's children on its locked piano by signed request ("Number of participants: As many as come"). On 9 February 2030, at the gate with her bag for Washington: "Tell him I'm eating. He'll ask." Told of Walt's death, "He would", and she puts on his cap and goes back to her children. Off the shot from May 2030; in August she stops the hymn in the middle and Miss Bea's singers finish it; Lucille hides her at the sinks from the buses. Naomi takes her north in October, where she eats from jars she hears pop. Well again in the spring of 2032, she chooses tablets in foil she presses out herself ("That arm is Dolores's"), teaches Lydia in silence on a paper keyboard, and at seventy-seven decides to go to Finland: a real piano, nobody near the left arm, and Nora to have something better than that Sunday ("This is a good year… I'd like to spend this one being the one who decides"). She sees Walt's grave ("I'm eating, Walter. You'd ask."), lets the radio play, practises *kiitos* through the dark, and flies to Helsinki in October 2032. There she teaches Carol by phone on Sundays ("Your left hand is visiting") and Alma by feel; at seventy-nine she reads Katie's certificate ("Now somebody's said… Somebody should have, all the same") and argues for Alma's cure ("They counted me… I taught the piano anyway"). She dies in Helsinki on 22 January 2043, at eighty-seven, in her own bed in a good year, her left arm never anyone's but Dolores's. Her ration card, with its D, on Lucille's shoelace, and Nora's jar drawing stay in the piano bench under her music.
- **Katie** (Katherine Ann Novak), Ben's older sister, who died in Toledo in 1989 at nine, of aspiration pneumonia due to Batten disease, late infantile. Nobody told Ben until her death certificate reached Helsinki in 2035. Alma, who has her illness, asks about her often, "because Katie was hers".
- **Gene Kessler** and **Barb,** across the street. Gene worked Walt's plant, talks with him porch to porch through 2020, remembers the Cutter batches of 1955 and won't have the COVID shot, but salts the Dorseys' steps so Walt can go and get his. Barb has "never gone first in her life". Gene drives the family to the airport on the leaving day and fetches them back in 2024, watches the eclipse through shade-fourteen welding glass from the plant, and in July 2024 they move to their daughter's in South Carolina, because Barb wants to watch the grandchildren grow up somewhere besides a phone.
- **Caleb Henderson,** the neighbours' boy, thirteen in 2024, who brings the Dorseys' bins up for nothing ("Won't take a dollar for it. I've tried"). At sixteen he finds Walt on the kitchen floor, because that Wednesday nobody came out to offer the dollar. At nineteen, held under H for refusing a pledge that calls his father, who works on the base, a traitor, he carries water up Building C's stairs all day; he is *The Henderson boy* in the narrow book, and says so to the soldier on the stairs, who lets him pass.

## Dolores, Joy and Carol

- **Dolores,** a nurse from the Philippines, who came at twenty-five and stayed: at the county mental health clinic on Wayne Avenue, and Ruth's nurse, once a month, from 2007; Ruth fought her a year and has trusted her since. She didn't turn Carol away when Carol came to the clinic at thirty-five, and has been Carol's mother since. For nineteen years her own mother told her on the telephone she was fine, so in August 2022 she promises Anna: "Not your father. Me." She has COVID in April 2020, Carol and Joel bringing her groceries; restarts Ruth's shot in July. She stops coming to houses by 2024. When the clinic closes in September 2026 she goes round twice and is called the one who brings the poison, and lets Walt's *fine* stand to keep Anna off a plane. In April 2027 she brings adobo, is refused the arm, and gives the shot on the declaration ("This one's mine. You told me so yourself"). At Carol's leaving: "Tell Joy I've got arms to do." In February 2030 she buys the two bus seats with her own money, and at Anna's asking goes to Walt instead, telling Ruth at the gate first; at seventy-four she holds up her phone through the Sunday call, changing arms, while he dies, takes Ruth his cap, and sees him buried. In June 2030 she goes out of the District's gate to find more of Ruth's shot ("Back Thursday") and is never found; no commission ever hears a word of her.
- **Joy,** Dolores's daughter, born in Dayton the year her mother arrived; a nurse. On a COVID floor in 2020 she holds up iPads for goodbyes and stops being able to feel for her patients, and in February 2021 leaves for a children's job in Toronto ("somebody has to stay and do the arms"). That first year she nurses Khan, eight, whose broken wrist knits in three weeks; he asks her why nobody comes to see him, and when the Noonien-Singh Institute offers her a job as its children's own nurse she takes it, knowing why they want her. Carol lives in her spare room. She has watched eleven children go home cured, and feels it every time. She tells Carol "The law's right. That's the worst of it", and that she would confess beside her, and asks her for nothing. In 2032 she waits for the commissions to say where her mother went ("don't you dare let them go for my sake"). She is present when Carol tells the Novaks about the boxes ("I've nursed here fourteen years anyway"). When Canada begins its register, about 2037, she goes east with the fourteen. She writes to Alma in braille, with mistakes, every birthday from 2036, and in May 2045 offers her passage to Khan's lands ("no massacres… nobody votes, and everybody works for him"; the doctors there can do more for eyes). After Alma says no, a card still comes every January, until Khan's lands fall in the winter of 2051–52; after that, nothing.
- **Carol Flory,** a county clerk with a pen clipped to her collar, a trans woman who took her name in 2013, at thirty-five, when Ohio law printed both her names side by side in the newspaper. Raised Old German Baptist north of Dayton, nonresistant, singing with no instruments; cut off by her family except for what her sister Naomi carries from their mother. She sings tenor in Miss Bea Yancey's Wednesday choir at the Methodist church on Salem Avenue, does taxes in the season, and believes a person's word about herself counts. Anna meets her on Dolores's steps in May 2020 ("She'd say so. I'd say so"). Twenty-three years at the county. In April 2027 the state reissues her licence marked M, flagging her county record; at the counter she takes Ruth's own signature, knowing Ruth doesn't understand ("She's sitting here asking for a pen"), ticks kosher, picks *none available* for Anna, names Ruth's tapping as Methodist hymn 534, and has her hand measured ("A tenth, easily. Who told you to stop?"). On Dolores's steps she tells Anna what the declaration will do to her job ("I'm telling you so you know") and gives no answer to "Then what do I do?"; she is let go that Friday. After that she goes into the District every Thursday with Dolores to help residents with forms, keeping a green cloth-spined ledger ruled by hand (the county's name and letter, what they like to be called, who they have, what they need). In January 2029 her two names go up beside her son's face, her old name is sprayed on her garage door, and a card on her step says THANK YOU FOR YOUR SON; she writes Eli Kasprzak's name and age on its back, paints the door with Joel, gives him the ledger, and leaves so that he won't stand in her drive with a rifle ("If I stay, you'll come"), telling the officer at the Detroit bridge "Two weeks", her first untrue answer about herself. In Toronto she works three days a week at a settlement office on Bloor Street, asking newcomers what they like to be called, and sings tenor at a church on Bloor Street; turned back at Detroit in February 2030, she can't come for Walt. The week Joel dies she stands in the tenor line and doesn't sing. In October 2030 the narrow book reaches her with "Mom" under its last line: "It says here she's eating." From the winter of 2031–32 she learns the piano from a young organist from Kingston, Jamaica, very badly, the most fun she has ever had, and at Easter sings again. At fifty-four, in October 2032, she crosses back on the commission's letter ("Eight days", true this time), affirms rather than swears, testifies that Joel wrote the H's himself, keeps the narrow book, hears Joel's last words from Cody, plays the paper keyboard for Ruth until she can't stop laughing, sings hymn 534 for Lydia's left hand, and tells Naomi "Joel wasn't a sack of wheat". She ticks *The family does not object* and writes "I do not object. I do not forgive them either. There is no box for that." From January 2033 she keeps the consent forms at the Noonien-Singh Institute, writing in each margin what the child likes to be called. She finds the old man's fourteen boxes, and on 11 October 2033 tells a Health Canada inspector there are no records of embryo research, knowing it makes her a party to the crime (ten years; her residence, Canada, everything rebuilt) and that her silence shelters the dead man too. She reads page nine aloud to every family, but tells Tala's grandmother there is nothing else they should know. She promises Anna every page read aloud, and in April 2035 reads them, then tells the Novaks what is on no page. About 2037 she lets the consent files go east, the only records she ever lets out of her hands, and keeps the flat. At sixty-seven she refuses to be a reason, won't run ("I crossed one bridge running"), admits she doesn't want prison, and asks to be spelled right ("Right is better, even on a charge sheet"). In 2046 Canada tries her; Canadian by then, with nowhere to be sent, she pleads guilty, has the clerk read her name back, and serves two years less a day in her own flat, at the piano. At seventy-five, in 2053: "Is everybody there? I'm here. Tell Alma my fourth finger's awake."
- **Joel Flory,** Carol's only child, who has called her Carol since he was nine, because the other words didn't fit; he ate at Dolores's table every Wednesday of his life. Enlisted in the Ohio Army National Guard at seventeen for the college money, Carol signing the consent; a sergeant, his company's armory in Springfield. On election night 2028, at the county board of elections on Third Street, he turns his back on the armed men across the road and talks his squad's fingers off their triggers ("Look at me"), and learns that a line holds if somebody stands in front of it. In January 2029, with two Presidents and two orders, he goes to Gate 12A "because if I'm there, they won't"; after a shot nobody can place he fires twice, and Airman Eli Kasprzak dies. To the Governor's press officer, and to Carol, he says "I fired", never "I returned fire". He tells Carol to go, and keeps her ledger dry. Kept off the line, he has the District's gate from March 2030, asks every new resident what people call them, and writes the Safety Board's H's in his own hand. He opens the gate for Dolores in June. On the night of 13 August 2030 he cuts the ledger's blank pages down to a narrow book, copies only the "known as" column into it, writes "Mom" under the last line and Joy's address inside the back cover, burns the written pages, and writes "None available" beside the rolls on the colonel's list ("It's a lie, Miss Lucille. I'm the reason there aren't"). He considers deserting and doesn't: the buses would be filled anyway, by his squad. Shot on the levee behind the county building by his own side at four on Wednesday 14 August 2030, aged twenty-six; his last words, to the firing party: "She was right. You can smell it from the river."

## Dayton's county and the District

- **Gary Pruitt,** the county caseworker and a licensed independent social worker ("Nobody ever asks a caseworker"), who makes every call he says he will, writes everything down, and tells people what he has written. In April 2027 he lays out the trap for Anna and is the second examiner who finds Ruth lacks capacity ("Same week. Same file. Only one of those can be true"). Unpaid from January 2030, he comes in anyway; he tells the colonel Joel has the book ("I don't know how to not tell a man a thing he asks me"), warns Joel first, writes down the time of the smoke, and reports him ("I said I'd tell them." / "You did, Gary."). His creed: everybody he ever got out of anywhere, he got out with a piece of paper that was true. In 2032 he applies for amnesty though he broke no law ("I didn't want somebody else deciding what I'd written") and tells Carol what Joel said.
- **Lucille Odom,** who cooked twenty-six years for four hundred children a day at the school on her street until it shut "and the rent didn't shut", and has lived in the District since the winter before Ruth came. She meets new residents at the inner door because nobody met her, and ties Ruth's ration card round her neck with the lace from her own left shoe. When the Indiana trays stop she gets her stove at last, in Building C ("Nobody is anything in my line, baby… Move up"), and does a crossword in pen at three every afternoon while the line waits; the insulin runs out in June 2030 and her foot won't heal. She gives Ruth the can of peaches she was saving for the end of the war, holds the steel rule while Joel cuts the book, hides it in her apron ("nobody ever searched a kitchen"), hides Ruth at the sinks, and gives the book to Naomi. After the fence comes down she cooks from a stool for whoever walks in. Told Joel's last words in 2032: "Well. I was right."
- **Naomi Garber,** Carol's sister, Old German Baptist, in a white head covering, who drives a grain truck; her church allows a telephone on the wall and nothing with a screen. She won't say the name Carol chose, and Carol asked her never to say the other, so she says neither; for nineteen years she carries their mother's "Mother asks if you're eating". In April 2030 she brings the Brethren's wheat to the District's gate ("ask nobody what they are"; four sacks taken by each side: "That's the fare. We don't resist it") and says the sentence to Joel. In October 2030, when Lucille tells her there are old people with nobody: "Then they'll have us." She takes nine north to her farm, Troy the nearest town, Ruth among them, pencils herself beside each in the narrow book as responsible, adds "Eating" beside *Mrs. Dorsey*, and posts it to Toronto without a letter. She decides that an instrument is a thing that makes a noise, so the paper keyboard may stay. On 17 October 2032, the sisters' first time in one yard in nineteen years, she says the sentence to Carol's face ("I'll tell her you're eating"), won't advise her ("We don't go to law"), offers only the wheat ("Twenty got through"), and answers "Joel wasn't a sack of wheat" with "No. He wasn't… I'd not blame you." Their mother is alive.
- **Lydia,** Naomi's granddaughter, nine in 2032, with a white cap pinned to her hair, who has never heard a piano and learns the left hand of hymn 534 from Ruth on paper, in silence ("Your fourth finger's lazy. Again"). Ruth and Naomi are "like in the Book".
- **Miss Bea Yancey,** who ran the children's room at the main library for thirty-one years and directs the Wednesday choir at Salem Avenue. When the Safety Board sends a list of books to take off the shelves, she carries them to the church basement and won't say where or sign the pledge; held under H from May 2030 ("Which column do you want me in, Joel?"; *Miss Bea* in the second). On the evening Joel is shot she hums the note and fourteen people finish hymn 534 in four parts, with no one on the tenor line. She walks back to her books when the fence comes down; Carol sleeps on her sofa in 2032.
- **Ti Jak** (Jacques Pierre-Louis), six foot three, from Springfield's Haitian community, who lost his work and then his room when the papers letting Haitians work ended, and came into the District in the winter. He kept goal for a Haitian team in the Sunday league and keeps goal for the District's children between two chalk marks, letting in as many as each needs. On 15 August 2030 he is taken to Columbus on the colonel's buses because he looked to somebody as if he came from somewhere else, and sets his sack of wheat down carefully before he goes. He goes home in October 2032 when the county's list is signed off; Carol never knows the *Ti Jak* in her book was among them.
- **Dale Hutto,** Carol's neighbour across the street, thirty years an engineer on the base, with a STAY sign in his yard that the street's children tell "Good boy". He was two cars back from Gate 12A that morning; he is sorry about her door and wouldn't have that on his street. The sign is still up in 2032.
- **Private Cody Ruhl,** nineteen on election night, in Joel's squad, joined for the college money; his finger was on the trigger at the board, and he fired at the gate and doesn't know at what ("It's just standing, Sarge. Like the board"). Joel sits by him until he eats. One of the six riflemen on the levee; at twenty-three, in a borrowed suit, before the commission: "I was in his squad. I was on the levee. I fired." He reports Joel's last words without knowing what they meant. The sixth rifleman was killed in the fighting that weekend in 2030.
- **Lieutenant Mercer,** Joel's platoon leader, twenty-six, his wife due in February, who checks his phone for her between saying things like "Let one of them try it", and pulls Joel down off the blocks, saying his name as if Joel were the one hurt.
- **Airman Eli Kasprzak,** twenty, of Green Bay, on Gate 12A in thin gloves, his name in the Air Force's brown thread that can't be read under the lights: "They told me Ohio'd be warmer." His mother, on the news: "He hated the cold." Said *Kasper-zack*. His name is on the back of the card that ends up in the narrow book.
- **The colonel** (unnamed), of the Governor's Guard column in August 2030, with a head cold and apologies, who asks whether Joel's men have eaten and had two drivers shot over diesel; he signs Joel's order after an eleven-minute hearing. Held by his own side in Columbus from spring 2031, he applies for amnesty ("I'd sign it again, on that morning, in that city. I'm sorry for his mother") and walks free in October 2032.
- **The pastor from Salem Avenue,** who asks Joel to add the letter H at the gate for those who won't sign the Board's pledge.
- **The Governor of Ohio** (the doctor of the 2020 briefings, real, never named), who sides with Philadelphia and tells Ohio its soldiers returned fire, believing it.

## Helsinki

- **Antti Laine,** from Kotka, Ben's age, tall and fair, who lives in the old stone building across the street and tows his daughter Aino to daycare on a sled. A whole autumn of *moi*; then, in December 2022: "Aino says that Nora says you are afraid of the dark… November is worse. December has the lights." He says a thing once and leaves you alone with it, which Ben finds like home. His grandmother left Viipuri at sixteen with her mother and a sewing machine; he was against NATO all his life "in my head", until Ukraine; his brother, a truck driver, voted for the party against immigrants ("He is not a bad man. I don't know what he is"). He sends the Novaks on the Suomenlinna ferry. When Ben rings at midnight in April 2027: "Yes. What time?" Asked in 2035 what he would do: "I would do it. Then I would not sleep." In 2053, grey, minding Aino's children, he finds the rock shelter full and brings them to the Novaks' door: "Not for me. For these two." He goes up with Alma for the nine days, tells her about every ship that left Kotka, says all operas have the same plot, and dies on the twenty-third day in Aino's hospital, with her; he told her he would not have asked for himself.
- **Aino Laine,** his daughter, Nora's friend from daycare; a nurse in 2053, her husband with the army in the east. Her children **Linnea,** five, in a raincoat, who sleeps on Anna's bunk with her fist in Anna's sleeve, and **Otto,** two, share one place in the shelter.
- **Mikko,** Ben's Finnish colleague, better at the player than Ben, who finds the Novaks their daycare by the sea; his grandfather fought the Russians in the snow in 1939. Let go in 2025, still looking for work a year and a half later, he sends Ben his son's Father's Day card: *Isi on nyt aina kotona*, "Daddy is always home now" ("Best review I've had all year").
- **Seppo Rantanen,** third floor, a retired heating engineer, sixty-nine in 2053: the shelter manager the housing company appointed and the rescue department trained, with the shelter's number on the first page of his binder. He refuses Antti and then takes "two small ones on one place. I'll answer for that", turns the levers, and doesn't answer the knock in the night.
- **Jari,** a frightened young father from the fifth floor with a new baby, who says Alma is "built for this", takes the crank from Ben at two in the morning, and thanks her afterward, to the step.
- **The woman from the second floor,** past eighty in 2053, who for ten years was told "a trial, in Canada": "I told everybody it was a miracle… I'm glad it wasn't."
- **Abdullahi,** a father at the daycare gate whom Ben nodded to for eight months: "Nora is teaching Hamdi English. Hamdi is teaching Nora Somali. I think they are mostly teaching each other the rude words." His daughter **Hamdi** is Nora's best friend.
- **The woman who keeps the playground park's hut,** in her sixties with a grey braid, who speaks to Anna only in slow Finnish and says *Teo*; Theo's first steps are into her arms. **The employment office counsellor,** Anna's age, from Estonia ("For you, longer").
- **Venla,** Alma's friend, the one person outside the family she would have told, and the first she tells after registering. **Elias** and **Minea,** classmates: he says the word to her because she wins every race.
- **Henrik,** the oldest technician at the station at Tvärminne, from a village down the coast, who speaks Swedish to the boat and Finnish to everyone else, runs the winch ("Still dead"), repeats Renée's light test and adds a red light, says "Nobody asks a slime mould", leaves both lines on the boathouse because both are true, and gives the organism its name, *vieras*: guest, stranger, a thing from somewhere else.

## The Toners (San Francisco and Belfast)

- **Declan Toner,** Ben's closest friend, the funniest person in any meeting, sent from Brynner's Belfast office to San Francisco from spring 2019. He says good morning to the woman with the broom, puts his finger through the fence where the tents were, and tells Ben about the wall at the bottom of his da's street ("very, very temporary"). The year ends early when Orla goes home, and they fly on 19 March 2020; his photo of the wall that night: *Home. Da waved from the window. Orla starts Monday.* He and Ben start every working day together on a screen ("Eight o'clock"), and he is the other one kept when the machine takes their team. At the eclipse in 2024 he watches Niamh, not the sky; "Eight o'clock, then." At the Bell Riots he recognises Rosa Delgado, presses red on the man with the shotgun ("Threat"), tells Ben "Rule's the rule" over Lee's clip ("It wouldn't have changed anything." / "You don't know that." / "No. I don't."), and at the District's wall says the broadcast ban, his da's votes for Hume, and "maybe it just works, and we'd all rather not know." On count night 2028 he drives Norman home the long way round ("I'll not tell you Sammy paid for it… Somebody shot him on his milk round"). He takes Ben's mornings in April 2027; at Walt's death: "Sorry for your trouble… I'm up, if you're up." In 2053: "We're here. Are you? Eight o'clock."
- **Orla Toner,** Declan's wife, a nurse who found Fort Baker and measures how far off a thing is in beds. In March 2020 her registration is no use in California and she goes home by choice, to her old ward ("But we'll see it coming"). At fifteen, in 1998, her da took her into the booth with his hand round hers ("That one's for me"). He was interned without charge at nineteen in 1971, wanted one Ireland all his life, and died on her ward in January 2027 after eleven weeks' free care ("You'll see it. I'll not, but you will"). By 2028 she nurses nights at the Royal, where Mr. Gilmore, bed six, a shipyard man from beyond the gate, asks whether he'd pay sixty euro to see a doctor, and Mrs. Kearney, bed four, a school cleaner of fifty-two, would earn too much for a card in the South. In the border poll she votes no ("I'm sorry, Da"), says "First thing", feels relief when yes wins anyway and shame at the relief, nearly tells Norman in the car, and tells no one. Abiding by the count she lost is the one thing about that weekend she is proud of.
- **Niamh Toner** (said *Neeve*), their daughter, nearly ten in San Francisco and adoring baby Nora, whose "Ba" she takes for her name; she gives Nora the knitted rabbit for the drive. At fourteen she asks for a total eclipse for her birthday, learns the rabbit is called Ba ("That's a stupid name for a rabbit"), and cries without a sound through totality. At eighteen, from a school that takes both kinds, calling herself Northern Irish, she casts her first vote in the border poll: "It's a secret ballot, Granda." On election night she sends Declan the Dayton clip.
- **Brendan Toner,** Declan's da, a retired bus driver, nationalist and widower, two streets from the gate in the wall, eighty at the poll, never late for anything with a time on it. He voted for the Agreement in 1998 with his wife, and for John Hume every election. He has argued with Mickey Quinn across the street for fifty years ("The bombs bought three and a half thousand funerals. That's all they ever bought"; "It was never a song"). He visits Norman every Wednesday for the football, always the long way round, though the gate would save twenty minutes, and has a Mass said for Sammy every November. At the result he turns the sound down for Norman's sake.
- **Norman Kerr,** Brendan's friend from across the wall, unionist, thirty years a driver out of the same depot; their wives died within a year of each other, and every Saturday at two he comes through the gate for the racing and his tea. He votes no openly. His brother Sammy, a milk-lorry driver and part-time soldier, was shot by the IRA in his cab in 1976 at twenty-five; his church doesn't pray for the dead, and he has never asked Brendan to stop the Masses. "Eighteen thousand. That's one crowd at the football… A passport's not a country… The count's the count." At half six on the morning after America's election he rings Declan to ask whether the American will abide by his.
- **Mickey Quinn,** across Brendan's street, eleven years in prison for the IRA and then politics, with the YES poster, who stops the chant on count night: "Lads. Lads. Not tonight."

## San Francisco, 2020 and 2024

- **Chris Brynner** (canon), who founded Brynner and switches the District's network back on at the Bell Riots, against the police ("We'll lose the city contract, and we'll have the biggest night we've ever had", reported second-hand); why he did it is never known. **Dana Reyes,** who runs its trust and safety team, helped build its fingerprint takedown system, writes the night's rules (no guns, no threats, no hostages), and shuts herself in the glass room: "The rules are the answer."
- The District's people the world came to know (canon): **Gabriel Bell,** who kept the six alive and died stepping in front of a guard; **Michael Webb,** a former plant manager at a chemical company, cut off mid-sentence and then finishing it ("work, and the door"), dead in the storming; **Henry Garcia,** two years inside and never in trouble with the law; **Lee,** a public health worker at the clinic and one of the six, alive afterward. **Danny,** a boy of about fourteen, holds the phone for the speakers and changes arms.
- **Rosa Delgado** (the book's own), the woman with the broom and the geranium in a coffee can by her tent under the freeway, who told Ben and Declan where the tents went ("Wherever they could carry it to"). Cleared off Market Street in July 2024, she carried a cutting into District A in her pocket and shows it in a milk carton on the night of the line. Her name isn't on the cardboard at the wall that Thursday, and her fate is unknown. Also on the line: an old man offered a bus ticket "home" ("home to who?"), a woman with a split lip who took a ration card and would again, and a big man on a two-year list for a doctor ("hi, Lainie").

## Toronto

- **Khan** (canon: a small boy at a Toronto genetics lab about 2022, *Strange New Worlds*). In the book one of fourteen children born in 2013 from embryos donated "for research only" and changed before birth by hand, before any machine could, by an unnamed scientist at the Noonien-Singh Institute, who gave them the building's name for a surname and named Khan for a king ("I've decided to keep it. It seemed a waste not to"); publicly, the Institute's orphans. At eight he breaks a wrist that knits in three weeks and asks his nurse, Joy, why nobody comes to see him. At twenty, in 2033: unshaven, a university sweatshirt with Milton in the pocket, a big laugh, terrible at the piano ("The one thing they didn't think to put in"), losing at chess on purpose to the clinic's children (Amira among them) and eating their desserts anyway; Joy calls him "the one who asked why". He reads everything (he was fifteen when Joel said "I fired", and has thought about it a great deal), has read his own file, won't ask Carol to keep quiet ("Nobody ever asked me anything"), and of the new word on the news: "I find I prefer frightened." At twenty-two he plays the twinkling one wrong for Alma ("This is me practised"), and won't bless the cure for Ben: "I'm what it was done to… She was asked. That's more than he ever did for us… One day there'll be enough of us that nobody has to ask anybody's permission for anything." About 2037 he goes east; by 2042 he rules more than a quarter of the world, from South Asia to the Middle East, where Augments govern and nobody else votes. In 2045 he has "thought of a better answer" for Alma. In the winter of 2051–52 his lands fall, and he is gone, nobody can say where.
- **The old man,** the scientist who made the fourteen, never named (which leaves room for canon's Adam Soong and his Project Khan) and gone some years by 2033, nobody says where. He signed for each child every year twice, as guardian and as investigator, with an unreadable signature in the same blue pen and no printed name. **Dr. Bastien,** the Institute's director in 2033, who keeps the old man's papers out of the inspection ("They're his own"), asks Carol to stop writing in margins, and, after Carol reads page nine to a family, shuts her door and hires nobody else.
- **Tala,** four, from Manila, whose legs stopped working; her grandmother asks whether there is anything else they should know, Carol says no, and her father signs.

## Montana (2063)

- **Zefram Cochrane** (canon), who builds the Phoenix, the first ship to go faster than light, from a missile in a silo in the old missile field outside Bozeman, and flies it on 5 April 2063; that night he greets the first Vulcan, fails the hand sign, shakes hands, and takes them into the bar. In the book he writes to Nora from 2057, at three in the morning, spelling badly (*feild*), with mathematics under it she has to walk round the room to follow, and signs *Z. Cochrane*; she was the only one of a dozen physicists who wrote back. In person he tells her he built it to get rich and live on a beach, then argues with her about an equation for an hour. His canon scenes and lines are never touched.
- **Lily Sloane** (canon), his partner, who built the ship with him and scavenged titanium for its cockpit for six months; wary of the sisters, she still takes Alma down into the silo to put her hands on the hull ("It was a missile… We had a better idea for it").
- **Lyle,** of the camp, whose son went east to fight in the Eugenics Wars and never came home; he sits outside the sisters' trailer with his rifle across his knees ("Both. Probably both"), stays at his truck at the landing, and when the camp follows the sisters out, leans his rifle against his bumper and puts his hands in his pockets.
- **The visitor,** a Vulcan who comes out of the bar alone, stops halfway to his ship and looks at the rifles, and lets Alma look at him close: "Your people are armed… And you are not?… That is also why we came."
- **The strangers** (canon's time travellers), who work on the ship in the silo the night the sky fires on the camp and are gone by the next night; nobody in the camp ever learns who they were. A **woman at the water tank** links the attack to "the week an Augment turned up"; a **boy of about ten** from the trailer by the water tank is the first to follow the sisters out, waving with his whole arm; a **girl of about nine** asks Alma about the face.

## Further away

- **Renée Picard** (canon), the French mission specialist on the Europa Mission's Shango, launched on Nora's fifth birthday and home on 13 February 2030 with something alive from Io. Ruth sees her fear on television ("The frightened ones practise"). Carried out fourth, short-haired and thin: "We're home. You can all go to bed now." Behind the glass: it moved toward a light the first time and by the tenth "didn't bother"; "Until somebody shows me it isn't someone, I'd like us to be careful with it." Her words are painted on the Tvärminne boathouse, with SHE BROUGHT IT HOME, DIDN'T SHE under them. Her later life is not told.
- **The organism from Io,** canon's, which later heals the oceans and cleans the air. In the book Ricardo's team in Los Angeles (canon's Ricardo, Teresa's son) spends ten years learning to grow it by the tonne; it lives on sulphur, eats the poison of the Baltic's dead floor, and dies after about three weeks, so healing the gulf means pouring it in by the trillion every summer. It habituates and tells a new light from a learned one, as a slime mould does; once, in Toulouse in 2046, an untrained dish ignored a light its neighbour had learned to ignore, across a channel neither could cross, and nobody has repeated it. The Baltic is its first sea, from September 2057. At the station it is *vieras*.
