# The people

Who everyone is and what happens to them, as the outline has it. Birthdays and every other date are in `timeline.ts`, and any age is computed from it with the date tool, never from this page. Details a chapter settles when it is drafted are added here then.

## The Dorseys and the Novaks (San Francisco, Dayton and Helsinki)

- **Anna Novak,** born Dorsey in Dayton, Ohio, the only child of Walt and Ruth. Detail-minded: she has never met a form she couldn't get through. In 2020 she runs a dentist's front desk in San Francisco, where she came for Ben's job and stayed for the city; the office closes for three weeks on 16 March 2020. She goes to her parents' house in March 2020 "for two weeks" to keep them out of stores, and stays until the family leaves for Helsinki in August 2022. Knowing Finland's rule (a grown child's parent may follow only if she moves at the same time, and only a Finnish citizen can ask), she asks her parents to come with them to San Francisco instead, the only place all of them could go together; they say no, and she chooses Helsinki knowing her parents can't follow unless something terrible happens. She tells Nora the truth about the preschool's lockdown drill, keeping the promise she made herself in the year of the county's Thursday visits. In Helsinki she can read none of the forms that were her competence. In February 2023 she turns down the employment office's free full-time Finnish course for newcomers (only in the first three years; Theo would have gone to daycare all day) to stay home through Theo's early years on Kela's child home care allowance, knowing that evening study takes years and so pushes back the certificate, citizenship, and any chance to ask Finland for her mother if something terrible happened. With the course she could have been a citizen the August after Theo turned five (August 2027). By April 2023 she is on chapter three of a blue textbook, reads Finnish numbers, can spell *laiva*, and has no course, no certificate and no date for either. The Saturday after the eclipse, on a bench at Huffman Prairie, she promises Walt never to let them put Ruth "in one of those places" (he refuses "I'll do everything I can" and asks for her word; she knows keeping it may mean bringing the family back from Finland, and doesn't tell Ruth, at his asking), and breaks the promise in April 2027 by using Ruth's declaration to get her the shot inside Dayton's Sanctuary District. In February 2030 she sends Dolores to sit with her dying father rather than take Ruth to Washington for her permit: the plan was Dolores bringing Ruth on the weekly Saturday night bus east to the embassy appointment on Monday 11 February, Anna flying in to meet them and flying home with her mother that night (the bed made up in Nora's room under the plastic stars). Walt, on the phone, tells her to put Ruth on the bus and says he's fine; she tells him he isn't, and has Dolores tell Ruth "it was me". At his death she lets his last "fine" stand. She asks Dolores to take Walt's cap from the plant to Ruth, and sends Ruth a photograph of Nora's drawing. She tells her children the truth. On Father's Day 2026, about thirty weeks pregnant with Alma, she learns on the Sunday call that Ruth's clinic closed in September and Ruth has relapsed (Ruth takes her for the county's Thursday woman; Anna answers as that woman: "You're doing a good job"); at bedtime she starts to say *ei se mitään* to Nora, hears her father's "fine" in it, and tells Nora the truth about Ruth's illness instead. She learns that Finland's citizenship rules have lengthened since 2024 and that, without the language exam, she is years from being able to ask for Ruth. She chooses not to fly to Ohio (Finnair would fly her only to the end of her 36th week, so she'd have had to be home by 20 December; no insurance for a birth there; Ben's job), rings Dolores that night (Dolores went round twice in October; Ruth called her the one who brought the poison through the screen door; Walt asked her not to come again and tells her weekly that Ruth is fine, and Dolores let him, to keep Anna off a plane; she will go round again tomorrow). In the kitchen Anna finally tells Ben about the Huffman Prairie promise ("Two years." / "You'd have said we'd do it together." / "That's why."): he is hurt, and thinks of the folder he has never told her about, and still doesn't. Then she rings Walt to say she isn't coming, won't let him say "fine"; he admits Ruth has slept for the first time since Thursday, and agrees to let Dolores in. For each renewal of the declaration since the move she signs the proxy's page before a notary at the American embassy in Helsinki and posts it back, so she has never seen the later copies' backs. In April 2027 (Responsible Party) she flies to Dayton on Nora's eighth birthday with Alma; avoids the piano bench for four days; at the county counter is relieved when Ruth signs herself in and says nothing; asks Carol for "none available" in section nine, for Finland someday; after Ruth refuses Dolores, reads the back of the declaration, and on Thursday signs as the person named, becoming the District's responsible party for Ruth, and holds Ruth's right hand reading the front aloud while Dolores gives the shot. In September 2032 she reads Ruth, over Naomi's wall telephone, Finland's letter giving the wartime permit until the end of December; she comes to the farm alone in October 2032, resolved not to argue ("It's yours, Mom. I'm not deciding this one. I've done enough of that."), hears yes when she had come ready to bear no, and takes Ruth by Woodland to Washington and home to Helsinki.
- **Ben Novak,** Anna's husband, a software developer at Brynner (canon's Brynner Information Systems), on the team that builds its video player; half the team is in Helsinki, from a small Finnish company Brynner bought for its player. Until 2020 he walks past the tents near Brynner's tower every day without seeing them, and has never asked where they go. Raised in Toledo by his grandmother, dutiful rather than warm, after his parents were killed in a car crash when he was three, a year after his older sister, Katie, died at nine of an inherited illness his grandmother never spoke of; she died while he was at college. He has never had anyone near who needed him, and can respect Anna's pull toward her parents without feeling it. When Brynner ends its pandemic arrangements (from 1 September 2022 everyone works from their own office or transfers to another where their team works: for Ben, San Francisco or Helsinki; Belfast's one desk on the team is Declan's), he looks for a job he could do from Dayton, has one offered and taken back in the hiring freezes of June 2022, and moves to his team in Helsinki. On the porch he feels relief, and can see Anna's pain without feeling it. His Finnish colleague Mikko, on his screen every morning since the company was bought, finds them the daycare by the sea. At the Bell Riots (a borrowed reviewer on Brynner's video service, Labor Day 2024) he holds back Lee's clip ("We're all right. All six of us.") by the no-hostages rule, which also guarded the whole line from a court order; he half-thinks, ashamed, of his family's permits riding on his job, and never knows whether that moved his hand. He never learns whether the clip mattered: accounts of when the governor decided disagree. He hasn't told Anna about the folder, and doesn't know about her promise to Walt. When AI cuts his team, he and Declan are the two kept, and the family's permits ride on his job. He tells Nora the truth about his parents and Katie when she asks in April 2024, and drives to Toledo alone the next morning to their graves (one stone for his parents, a smaller one for Katie), the first time since his grandmother's funeral; "She was sick" is all his grandmother ever told him, once, and he never asked again; Anna has known about Katie from their first year together and learned to leave it shut. At that supper Ruth asks "Sick with what?" ("Nobody ever said." "Somebody should have."), and the question goes to the grave with him. He knows his parents' faces from six photographs, and comes back to find Walt on the porch watching for his car. When Alma is diagnosed (May 2033) he learns he and Anna both carry the illness, his from somebody in Toledo; asked what Katie died of, he doesn't know, tells Carol on the phone ('I'm forty-five years old and I never once asked anybody… I'm going to find out'; Carol: 'Because I'm far enough away'). In February 2035 Katie's 1989 Ohio death certificate reaches him (he wrote for it in May 2033): aspiration pneumonia, due to "neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis (Batten disease), late infantile", his father as informant; he realises his grandmother took him to a Toledo eye doctor every August until he was twelve to watch for it, and never said. Ruth: "Now somebody's said." He knows that had anyone told him, he and Anna would have been tested, and fears the Institute's records ("What if it's found?… When they've got their register"), ashamed that part of him wants the road where nobody ever comes to the door for her, because on it he needn't be afraid; he chooses "Toronto. We hear every page. And we ask her", tells Alma about Katie ("Nobody ever told me anything about her. So I'm telling you everything"), meets Khan, and signs Alma's consent under Anna's, knowing it is a crime in Canada. In Helsinki he leans on Antti Laine, a quiet Finnish dad he met at the daycare gate. In spring 2025 Brynner's machine takes over most of the video team's work; the Finns, Mikko among them, are let go after Finland's legally required change negotiations, and Ben and Declan are the two kept, to check what the machine decides (Ben guesses it's because their names are in the Labor Day log). Mikko, who was better at the player, is still looking for work a year and a half later; on Father's Day 2026 he sends Ben a photo of his son's card (*Isi on nyt aina kotona*, "Daddy is always home now": "Best review I've had all year"), and Ben can only send a heart. If Ben's job goes, the family has six months to find another or leave. On that Father's Day he tells Nora the truth about the building's shelter.
- **Nora,** born April 2019 in San Francisco, a baby when the family goes to Dayton and three when they move to Helsinki. At two and a half she starts at the preschool in the basement of Ruth's church (her teacher, Miss Jackie); at three she shows the family the lockdown drill she calls "the quiet game". On the leaving day she sings the preschool's going-home song to Walt through the front window; Anna takes down the plastic stars from her own childhood ceiling for Nora's room in Helsinki. The eclipse and the Europa launch on her fifth birthday light her love of space; she draws a space picture for Walt every birthday after, which he keeps on his fridge. Starts school in Helsinki in August 2026 and walks there alone with a reflector on her coat; reads everything aloud and "improves" it, a family game nobody catches her out on (her Father's Day card gains "You are not good at singing"). On Father's Day 2026 she holds the shelter door while Ben tells her what the room is for (Aino's great-grandmother and the sewing machine; "Even us?" "Everybody who lives upstairs"), and Anna tells her the truth about Grandma's illness; she asks whether Grandma can come and live in the room for everybody, and tells Anna she is "not very good yet" at Finnish. At ten she tells her dying grandfather the crew are coming ("It's the winter I'm ten") and says *Hyvää matkaa, Grandpa* as he dies. After the homecoming she draws the eight in their white box behind glass and a jar with one tiny mark, and leaves its label blank because in Finnish she would have to choose between *jokin* (something) and *joku* (somebody); the drawing is for Grandma. Walt's fridge pictures are still in the empty Dayton house. At fifteen (March 2035) she still improves what she reads aloud and describes the world to Alma, who catches her out; she has read everything on Alma's cure and wants it given and said openly ("If we hide her, it's like saying they're right… That's what Grandpa used to do"); overruled, she promises at the kitchen door never to tell ('But I'm not going to say it isn't a lie'), and on the ferry in May hears Anna's lie, decides, and stays silent. She becomes a scientist, and is near Cochrane's work, not on his ship, at First Contact.
- **Theo,** born February 2022 in Dayton, six months old on the leaving day. Finns call him *Teo*. Home with Anna through 2023; walks in March 2023 into the arms of the woman who keeps the playground park's hut. The sea is his from his first ferry, to Suomenlinna on Easter Saturday 2023, where he laughs at the sea and weeps when the ferry leaves without him; his first sure word, the next day, is *laiva*, Finnish for ship, learned from Nora; at the homecoming (seven, eight the next week) he is told the thing from Io comes from a moon with no water: "Then it doesn't know about the sea. Somebody should show it." At thirteen, at the family's table over Alma's cure: "If she doesn't have it, she dies. That's the only thing anybody's said that's actually about her"; in May 2035 he has the family take Alma on the Suomenlinna ferry to see the sea. He becomes a marine scientist, practical where Nora looks to the stars, and in the 2050s decides whether to use the organism from Io to heal the Baltic.
- **Alma,** born January 2027 in Helsinki. In May 2033, at six, she walks into door frames and stops putting her hand up to read from the board; on Saturday 14 May she is diagnosed with Batten disease, the form called CLN6 (sight first, then falls and fits, then lost skills; the doctors say perhaps ten or twelve), the illness that killed Ben's sister; Anna and Ben both carry it. Ruth teaches her the twinkling one with her eyes shut, by feel; she counts the stairs aloud on her way to bed, and by October falls on the school stairs when she loses count. The Institute's network for CLN6 is two or three years from ready; the era's cure can only cure by enhancing, so saving her makes her an Augment. Her first fit comes in autumn 2034; by February 2035, eight, she sees only light, dark and shapes, reads raised dots at school, falls (somebody always catches her), her words come slowly when she's tired, and she counts the building's sixty-four stairs aloud, starting again if interrupted. She is the only person who has ever caught Nora improving something (a man on a bicycle past a fourth-floor window, "wearing a pineapple"). Nora's knitted rabbit Ba is hers now. Ruth teaches her by feel on the real piano (the twinkling one with both hands, the start of a minuet); she feels Ruth's tremor through the bench. Told the truth at bedtime on 6 March 2035 (the needle, the change, her children, the word, the secret, that without it she dies like Katie), she knows the word already (Elias at school says it to Minea, who wins every race and cried), asks whether she'd be faster than Theo, and says yes "Then I could see if Nora's making it up"; Ba may know, her friend Venla may not. At the Institute she meets Khan at the lounge piano ("It's a rubbish answer"); asked what she likes to be called, says "Alma". Treated on 10 April 2035 by a needle in her lower back. By the end of May she sees brightness, the islands, gulls and a white ship from the Suomenlinna ferry ("Laiva"), catches Nora's invented dolphin, and says nothing when Anna tells a neighbour "A trial. In Canada. We were lucky." Nobody outside the family, Ruth, Carol and Joy knows what she is. In the Eugenics Wars she is an Augment in a war over Augments.
- **Walt Dorsey,** Anna's father. Polio at five, in 1952, in a ward where his mother could only stand at the window; one good leg and a steel brace on the other. Missed polio's vaccine by three years and is "on time" for COVID's. Thirty-four years at a car-parts plant; follows spaceflight. Says "everything's fine": a flaw out of love and out of pride, since needing help would mean he couldn't manage. Drives (the brace is on his left leg, and an automatic needs only the right); leans on a shopping cart in a store. Keeps going to the store himself in March 2020: three stores in four hours on 14 March. In 2020 he writes the day's COVID numbers from the two o'clock briefing in a notebook from the plant; he lets Ruth stop her shot ("It's your mother's arm"), which is why she names Anna, not him, in her declaration. Decides at supper on the night of the quiet game, before he is asked: when Anna asks them to come to San Francisco he says no to spare her ("Who'd do the tomatoes?"; he knows who would get him up and down its stepped sidewalks, and that she would give up Finland for them), leaving where she goes hers to decide, and sets the Sunday call at eleven, after church, "wherever you end up" (six in the evening in Helsinki). "We'll be fine" is the first time he says it knowing what it costs; Ruth: "Don't you start that with me, Walter." He tore up the envelope on which he worked out how old he'd be when Theo could remember him. From about 2026 post-polio weakness reaches his good leg ("Took its time"); by November 2026 a walker stands by the front door, "for the garbage cans". Votes for the doctor of the 2020 briefings for governor in November 2026 ("I figured I owed her the one vote"). For nine Sundays after Ruth's clinic closes he tells Anna about the gutters, and asks Dolores (who went round twice in October and was turned away at the screen door) not to come again, telling her every week that Ruth is fine. Falls in April 2027 and lies seventeen hours before the neighbour's boy finds him: the alert button Anna sent at Christmas was in its box in the drawer ("the cord would catch on the walker"), and Ruth, believing the phone was the county's, covered him with a blanket and set a can of soup and the opener by his hand. He had been taking the walker's wire basket to the dollar store all winter for her soup (sixty-one cans in the cupboard). His fridge door is covered with Nora's birthday pictures and every other drawing she sent. After the hip is pinned he goes to a nursing home in Kettering for rehabilitation; told that Anna used the declaration, he says only "How's she eating?" and "She wrote your name. Not mine." After the fall he signs a paper at the home: no more hospitals, whatever happens. Since the post-polio weakness reached his chest he sleeps with a machine that breathes for him at night. He and Ruth are never in a room together again after the night he fell; twice Dolores wheels him to the District's visitors' window for the twenty minutes allowed (Ruth tells him his collar is wrong). On the call of 3 February 2030 he tells Anna to get Ruth out. An ice storm cuts the home's power on 7 February; his machine's battery runs down; pneumonia. On Saturday 9 February, on the aide's phone: "You put your mother on that bus. I'm fine." Dies on Sunday 10 February 2030 in the home's dining room, coats on the residents, his cap from the plant on and his hair combed, on Dolores's phone at the hour of the Sunday call: his first question "How's she eating?", his last words "Tell Annie everything's fine." He missed the polio vaccine by three years and the crew's homecoming by three days. Dolores saw him buried at Woodland Cemetery in Dayton, in the ice, before she went out of the District's gate; a small flat stone on the side of the hill.
- **Ruth Dorsey,** Anna's mother, a piano teacher and the organist at her Lutheran church. Schizoaffective disorder, begun in her twenties; an autumn in hospital in 2000, when Anna was eleven, and a county woman checking on Anna's care every Thursday for a year after. A monthly injection from Dolores keeps her well, and dulls her, with a slight tremor in her left hand she hides at the piano; her left arm is Dolores's. In 2020 she chooses to go without the shot to get her music back, sees the signs herself, asks for Dolores, and then writes a declaration at the bank naming Anna to decide when she can't ("When I am ill... Do not listen to me. Listen to this."). Her no to San Francisco: "It took me a year to let Dolores near that arm. I haven't got another year to spend on a stranger, and you haven't got one to spend on me," and then the fast clean scale she plays when she won't say anything else; that night she plays something slow at midnight. She checks every permit form with her red pen, and on the leaving day tells Anna she stays the one named on the declaration ("They have telephones in Finland. I looked it up."). Plays the *Finlandia* hymn to Anna on Sundays, and never explains it. Renews the declaration word for word every three years at the bank ("Like the car"; the current one dated August 2023), still under the hymnal. From about 2024 Dolores no longer comes to houses, so Ruth has her shot at the clinic on Wayne Avenue. In April 2024 she tells Nora the truth about her shaking hand ("It's my medicine... The hand still knows where the notes are"), reads Renée Picard's fear on the clinic's television ("The frightened ones practise"), prays from the organ bench for Renée's nerve, and has the pastor pray for Vantaa, writing out the stress for him in red. Renews the declaration again in August 2026, a month before her clinic closes. Relapses when it closes in September 2026: by November she doesn't sleep, misses her first vote since she was eighteen, eats only soup from cans she opens herself, plays a few bars of the hymn and stops, and on Father's Day takes Anna on the tablet for the county's Thursday woman ("My daughter is fed. She's clean."); inside Dayton's District from April 2027, on the shot again, teaching children on a locked, out-of-tune piano it takes a signed request to use. On 9 February 2030 she is at the District's gate with her bag for the Washington bus when Dolores comes to tell her Walt is dying and Anna has sent Dolores to him: "Tell him I'm eating. He'll ask." Told of his death at the window the next morning, she asks whether he said everything was fine ("He would"), puts on his cap from the plant over her hair, says she has children tomorrow, and goes back in. Her last shot is in May 2030; off it after Dolores goes out of the gate in June: by August she doesn't sleep, cleans clean counters, takes Lucille for the county's woman ('You can put that in your report'), eats only what she hears opened, and wears Walt's cap; on 14 August she starts the Finlandia hymn and stops in the middle while Miss Bea's singers finish it; hidden at the sinks from the buses; stops teaching in September; taken north by Naomi in October, eating at her farm from jars she hears pop, and spreads her hands on the oilcloth; in the narrow book she is *Mrs. Dorsey*, on page one. At the farm she is ill through two winters (Anna rings the farm's wall telephone on Sundays and is taken for the county); the illness ebbs on its own toward spring 2032, and in April, while well, she has Naomi drive her to a doctor in Troy and starts tablets in foil blister cards she presses out herself, declining the shot ("That arm is Dolores's"). She teaches Naomi's granddaughter Lydia on a two-and-a-half-octave keyboard she drew on feed-store paper and taped to the oilcloth, in silence, hearing the hands by watching ("Your fourth finger's lazy. Again."). On Sunday 17 October 2032 she decides, herself, to go to Finland: a real piano ("Not the kind you plug in"), her tablets in foil, nobody near the left arm, and Nora to know her well ("I wrote the last one of these at a bank, in a good year. This is a good year… I'd like to spend this one being the one who decides."). She finishes Lydia's left hand of hymn 534 while Carol sings the tune: it goes to the end. On 19 October she sees Walt's grave at Woodland for the first time ("I'm eating, Walter. You'd ask."), listens to the car radio without fear, practises *kiitos* across Pennsylvania; on 20 October she collects her permit card at the embassy in Washington and flies to Helsinki with Anna. Her illness is written true: psychosis as it is, and Ruth well as a whole person, proud, funny and difficult, never given wisdom by her illness. Well, she corrects everything in red pen (the church bulletin, the state's forms, a nurse's spelling). Walt first heard her play when she was thirty, on no medicine, from the back of her church. Her relapses begin the same way: no sleep, cleaning and laughing, then coincidences meant for her (the radio in 2000; the governor reading Walt's numbers in 2020). In 2020 she half-sees it in Anna's face. The declaration's front says, in her hand: "When I am ill, I will tell you I am perfectly well. I will tell you the injection is poison and that you are not who you are. Do not listen to me. Listen to this." and "I went without it once, in the summer of 2020, to hear myself play. It was worth it. I will not do it again, and if I try to, stop me." She wrote more on the back, in red, adding a dated note at each signing and copying the old ones out again: 2020, "You are going to have to do something terrible. I would like it in writing that I said you could."; 2023, the Sunday hymn is *Finlandia*, "Be Still, My Soul", written "when Finland belonged to Russia and wasn't allowed to say so", played to the end when she is well ("If I ever stop in the middle, you will know before your father tells you"); 2026, Walt's leg is going, don't let him tell Anna it was wrong, "You love me some other way. That's why it's your name." It is kept in the piano bench under the hymnal; Anna first reads the back in April 2027. On the county's form she is "known as" *Mrs. Dorsey* ("My husband calls me Ruthie. He's not to be encouraged."), her letter is D, and she is ticked Jewish so her food comes sealed. From June 2027 she teaches the District's children on its locked piano, Tuesday and Thursday afternoons, by signed request ("Purpose of use: Lessons. Number of participants: As many as come"). In Helsinki (2035), seventy-nine and well, she teaches Alma by feel; reading Katie's certificate: "Now somebody's said… You'd have had the choice. That's all telling anybody ever gets you. Somebody should have, all the same." On Alma's cure: "For forty years I took a medicine that made me less than I was… Now somebody's offering that child one that makes her more"; "They counted me… I taught the piano anyway."
- **Gene Kessler** and **Barb,** across the street. Gene worked the same plant as Walt, clears the Dorseys' walk after every snow, remembers the Cutter batches of 1955 and won't have the COVID shot, though he salts the Dorseys' steps so Walt can go and get his; Barb has "never gone first in her life" (and wears the paper eclipse glasses that Gene, with his shade-fourteen welding glass from the plant, won't). Through 2020 he and Walt talk porch to porch across the street. In 2024 they move to their daughter's, because Barb wants to watch the grandchildren grow up somewhere besides a phone: the first time she has gone first.
- **Caleb Henderson,** the neighbours' boy (about thirteen in 2024), who carries the Dorseys' bins down and back for nothing (Walt: "Won't take a dollar for it. I've tried."), and at sixteen finds Walt, because on that Wednesday nobody came out to offer the dollar. At nineteen, held under H for refusing to sign a paper calling his father (who works on the base) a traitor, he carries water up Building C's stairs all day; in the ledger he is *The Henderson boy* ('I don't know, sir. The Henderson boy, I guess'), which is what he tells the soldier on the stairs, who lets him pass.

## Dolores, Joy and Carol

- **Dolores,** from the Philippines at twenty-five, a nurse at the county mental health clinic on Wayne Avenue; Ruth's nurse for years, and a mother to Carol, whom she met at the clinic when Carol came for the counsellor's letter she needed to begin her transition, and didn't turn away. Falls ill with COVID in April 2020, Carol and Joel bringing her groceries; restarts Ruth's injection in July. She left her own mother in the Philippines at twenty-five, and for nineteen years her mother told her on the telephone that she was fine; in August 2022 she tells Anna she, not Walt, will call if Ruth isn't. Keeps her license after the clinic closes; restarts Ruth's shot inside the District; in February 2030 she buys the two bus seats for Ruth's Washington trip with her own money ("I've got nothing left to spend it on"), then, at Anna's asking, stops at the District's gate to tell Ruth and sits up all night with Walt in the cold home (let in because she still carries her nurse's license), holding up her phone and changing arms through the Sunday call while he dies; at seventy-four. She hears his "fine" as she heard her own mother's for nineteen years. She takes Walt's cap to Ruth; in June 2030 goes out of the District's gate to find more of Ruth's shot and isn't seen again.
- **Joy,** Dolores's daughter, born in Dayton the year her mother arrived; a nurse, and Carol's near-sister. Works a COVID floor in 2020, holding up iPads for goodbyes, and leaves for a children's hospital in Toronto in February 2021 because she has stopped being able to feel for her patients and wants to again. In 2032 she waits for the commissions' disclosures to tell her where her mother went, and tells Carol not to tick the box for her sake. In her first year in Toronto (2021), at the children's hospital, she nurses Khan, eight, whose broken wrist knits in three weeks; the radiologist asks to see him again, and the next week the Noonien-Singh Institute hires her as its children's own nurse so they need never come into a hospital ('I knew why they wanted me… I took it anyway'), because he had asked her why nobody came to see him. She has nursed the Institute's fourteen grown children since, and the clinic's children on nights; she has watched eleven children see or walk again and feels it every time. 'The law's right. That's the worst of it.' 'Mom stayed in Dayton because somebody had to do the arms. These are mine.' She tells Carol she would confess beside her if Carol told the inspectors, and asks her nothing.
- **Carol Flory,** a county clerk, a trans woman who took her name in 2013, when Ohio law printed her old name beside it in the newspaper. Raised Old German Baptist north of Dayton, cut off by her family except for what her sister Naomi carries from their mother; sings tenor with the Methodists on Salem Avenue. Dry, quick, courteous, a pen always on her. Anna first meets her in May 2020 on Dolores's steps, Joel carrying water; asked if she's family: "She'd say so. I'd say so." Believes a person's word about who she is counts. Her license said F from 2013; Ohio's BMV stopped accepting the gender-change form in August 2026 (real), and her renewal in April 2027 comes back M, flagging her county record. Takes Ruth's own signature in April 2027, knowing it isn't informed consent (Ruth is bargaining to keep "the girl" from the county), and initials the box attesting Ruth understands; ticks kosher ("She's Jewish until somebody checks, Gary. Nobody has ever checked."); picks "none available" for Anna, which goes to a supervisor. Knows Ruth's tapping as Methodist hymn "five thirty-four" (she doesn't name *Finlandia*; Ruth's note does); Ruth measures her hand ("You've got a pianist's hands. A tenth, easily. Who told you to stop?"); raised to sing in a meetinghouse with no piano, she sings that way to Alma. Her reason, said aloud at the counter: if they wait, a doctor writes that Ruth can't speak for herself; "She's sitting here asking for a pen. I'm not going to be the one who tells her she can't have it." At Wednesday supper at Dolores's (Joel home from drill; they fight over the last spring roll) she tells Anna on the steps what the declaration will do to her job, "so you know", that she won't say Ruth understood, and gives no answer to "Then what do I do?": no permission. Has worked for the county twenty-three years (since about 2004). Let go on Friday 23 April 2027 for four things on one desk: the capacity contradiction, the kosher box, "none available", the M. After that she does taxes at a storefront on Salem Avenue in the season, and every Thursday goes into the District with Dolores through the visitors' window to help residents with forms, keeping her own green cloth-spined ledger of them, ruled by hand: the county's name and letter, what they like to be called, who they have, what they need (Ruth: *Dorsey, Ruth (D)*, *Mrs. Dorsey*, *Daughter, Anna, in Finland*, *Lessons, Tues & Thurs. Her arm is Dolores's.*). Signed Joel's enlistment consent at seventeen though raised nonresistant; hasn't told him what to do since he was about fourteen. Wore a green tie with yellow ducks, Joel's Father's Day present at seven, for her "clerk of the month" newsletter photograph. Again.") and laughs until she can't stop; answers her mother's question to Naomi's face ("Tell her I'm learning the piano"); sings hymn 534 for Lydia's lesson; tells Naomi "Joel wasn't a sack of wheat." On Tuesday 19 October 2032 she ticks "The family does not object", because the form asks whether she objects, not whether she forgives, and because the men's acts are now on the public record under their own names, and writes, pressing hard: "I do not object. I do not forgive them either. There is no box for that. Carol Flory, his mother." Before ticking she holds the pen on the objection box: the colonel will learn nothing, the next colonel will learn it is free, the pharmacist's mother was right, and Joel is being made to pay twice. She knows from May that Alma's cure can only come from the Institute. On Tuesday 11 October 2033, asked by a Health Canada inspector whether there are records of research on human embryos at the site, she says 'No' and signs the set complete, knowing it makes her a party to the offence (ten years; her permanent residence, Canada, Joy, everything rebuilt), and knowing that unlike Joel's book her silence also shelters the dead experimenter; in the elevator a five-year-old whose form she filed counts the floors. On Sunday 4 March 2035 she rings Helsinki that the CLN6 network is ready; on Monday 9 April, in her office, she reads Ben and Anna every page and then tells them what is on no page, the fourteen boxes and her 'No' to the inspector, as she didn't tell Tala's grandmother ('If you sign this, you're in it with me and Joy'; if it comes out, 'there's a list, and her name's on it. And mine's on the line under hers'); Joy, present, says 'The law's right… I've nursed here fourteen years anyway'; Carol initials as witness and writes 'Alma' in the margin.
- **Joel Flory,** Carol's only child, a sergeant in the Ohio Army National Guard; calls her Carol, because the other words didn't fit. Enlisted at seventeen for the college money; his company's armory is in Springfield. Tells Carol to go to Toronto, and keeps her ledger. He considers deserting before dawn and doesn't: the buses would be filled anyway, by his squad. Next morning he writes "None available" beside the rolls ('It's a lie, Miss Lucille. I'm the reason there aren't.'); Pruitt reports him; he is shot on the levee behind the county building at 16:00 on Wednesday 14 August 2030, aged twenty-six, smelling Lucille's wheat on the wind.

## Dayton's county and the District

- **Gary Pruitt,** the county caseworker, who makes every call he says he'll make (Walt, November 2026: "Calls when he says he will, I'll give him that"; he is trying to get Ruth on a Columbus list and mentions "places downtown"), writes down everything, and tells people what he has written. A licensed independent social worker ("Nobody ever asks a caseworker"), he is the second examiner, with the District's doctor, who finds Ruth lacks capacity in April 2027, knowing it contradicts Carol's attestation; he told Carol himself. In the war, unpaid since January 2030, he still comes in every day; he tells the colonel Joel has the book ('I don't know how to not tell a man a thing he asks me'), warns Joel ('I'm telling you so you know'), watches the smoke and writes down the time, and reports him; his hand shakes at the hearing ('I said I'd tell them.' / 'You did, Gary.'). In October 2032 he applies for amnesty under the levee though he broke no law ("I didn't want somebody else deciding what I'd written"), reads his notebook to the minute, and tells Carol that Joel said "You did, Gary."
- **Lucille Odom,** a former school cook in the District, who cooked twenty-six years for four hundred children a day at the school on her street until it closed ("and the rent didn't shut"), wants to be let at a stove inside ("you'll smell it from the river"), meets new residents at the inner door because nobody met her, ties Ruth's ration card round her neck with the lace from her own left shoe, and runs its kitchen. ## The Toners (San Francisco and Belfast)
