# The people

Who everyone is and what has happened to them. Birthdays and every other date are in `timeline.ts`, and any age is computed from it with the date tool, never from this page.

## The Dorseys and the Novaks (Dayton and Helsinki)

- **Anna Dorsey,** from Dayton, Ohio, the only child of Walt and Ruth. She ran the front desk of a dentist's office for nine years, until it closed in March 2020 and reopened without her; that month she, Ben and Nora moved into her parents' house "for two weeks" so that Walt and Ruth never had to go into a store, and stayed until they left. She booked her parents' vaccine, filled in the forms to leave America in February 2021, asked her parents to come too, and was told no. She lives in Helsinki, where she still dries every plate by hand, and studies Finnish out of a textbook; in November 2026 she isn't a Finnish citizen, for want of a certificate of satisfactory Finnish, and so can't yet ask Finland to take her mother. Her mother named her, in a declaration made while well, as the one to decide her care, and Anna signed Ruth into the Montgomery County Sanctuary District as the only way anyone could ever get her out. By March 2027 she has booked the language exam and filled in Ruth's application, which she can send only once she is a citizen. She passed the exam that autumn, became a Finnish citizen in April 2028, and sent Ruth's application the same week; the permit for Ruth came through in November 2029, and its card waited at the embassy in Washington for Ruth to collect in person on 5 February 2030. The plan was Dolores bringing Ruth on the night bus, on a release Anna had signed, and Anna flying in to meet them at the door; the buses east stopped, then Anna's flight, then the embassy's services, and the appointment went by at five o'clock at her kitchen counter in Helsinki. Five days later she watched her father die on Carol Flory's phone, and told him she would grow his tomatoes on her balcony. Her Finnish is good enough now to follow her children's.
- **Ben Novak,** Anna's husband, from Toledo, Ohio, a software developer. His parents, Dennis, who built Jeeps at the Toledo plant for thirty years, and Joanne, died of COVID in Toledo in December 2020, eleven days apart, before the vaccine; he said goodbye to his father on a nurse's iPad and watched their funeral on a laptop at the Dorseys' kitchen table, having stayed away to keep Walt and Ruth safe. He works for Brynner, a company in San Francisco (canon's Brynner Information Systems), from home; it has a dozen engineers in Helsinki, from a firm it bought (Mikko among them), and in autumn 2020 let its people work from any country where it had an office to pay them from, which is what made Helsinki possible. His team builds the part of Brynner's video service that plays the video. He asked to wait a year, and then, before he had been to see his parents' grave, put his passport on the table (he first stood at it in April 2024, on the family's first trip home). He was at Brynner's offices the week of the Bell Riots, his first trip there since before the pandemic, and on Labor Day sat in the row of borrowed reviewers deciding which residents' stories went out: he sent nearly all of them, and held back one, a hostage saying they were all right, by the rule. He came home with Bell as a martyr, to hear on the Sunday call that Dayton would build a District, and to fear for Ruth, a fear Anna shares; he started to tell Walt about a man on a list for a doctor, and let it go. He flew to Dayton when Walt fell, and stood by while Ruth nearly signed herself away.
- **Nora,** their daughter, two when they moved, so Finnish is her easy language and English the one she keeps for her grandparents. The Europa Mission launched on her fifth birthday, and she began her drawing of Europa that night in her mother's old orange crayon. **Theo,** their son, born in Helsinki. **Alma,** born in January 2027, whose name works in Finnish and in English. At ten, in February 2030, Nora told her dying grandfather that the crew were coming home ("It's the winter I'm ten"), said *hyvää matkaa* to him, watched the homecoming three days later, and used the last of the orange crayon on what Renée found.
- **Walt Dorsey,** Anna's father. He had polio at five, in 1952, in a ward where his mother could only stand at the window, and it left his left leg in a steel brace; in 1955 he lined up for the Salk vaccine anyway, against the kinds he hadn't had, and in February 2021, at seventy-three, for COVID's. He worked thirty-four years at Delco, a car-parts plant. He has followed spaceflight since John Glenn's orbit, when he was fourteen, and followed the Europa Mission in the paper. He declined Helsinki on the porch ("Who'd do the tomatoes?") and set the Sunday call at eleven, after church. Post-polio weakness took his good leg. By November 2026 he has a walker in the front hall, which he says is for wheeling the garbage cans down to the street, and he voted that week for the doctor Ohio elected governor. He fell in March 2027 and lay seventeen hours before a neighbour's boy found him, and never walked again. From the nursing home in Kettering he kept the Sunday call, an aide holding the tablet, and told Anna to get her mother out of the District and not to worry about him. In February 2030 the home's phone went silent; ice took the power, his night breathing machine ran down its battery, and on Sunday 10 February he died in a cold room with one window, in his cap from the plant, with his hair combed, on the phone Carol Flory held up for him at the hour of the Sunday call. His first question was how Ruth was eating, and his last words were "Tell Annie everything's fine".
- **Ruth Dorsey,** Anna's mother, a piano teacher for forty years and an organist at her Lutheran church. Through the pandemic she taught over an iPad, playing a phrase and waiting for it to come back down the line, because the lag makes playing together impossible. Her left arm is Dolores's. She has schizoaffective disorder, which began in her twenties. In 1996 she spent eleven weeks in hospital, and a county worker checked on Anna's care every Thursday for a year. For nineteen years a monthly injection from her nurse, Dolores, kept her well, until the clinic closed in the first week of September 2026, rather than wait for the Medicaid cuts, and she relapsed: by November she has missed her first election since 1972, eats only soup, stops her Sunday piece after a few bars, and takes Anna on the call for the county's Thursday woman. Her illness is written true: psychosis as it is (frightened, suspicious, the injection as poison, people as impostors), and Ruth, when well, as a whole person, proud, funny and difficult, never given wisdom or insight by her illness. On the shot again inside the District, she taught eleven children piano on a keyboard drawn on cereal boxes. Her last injection was in May 2030; Naomi Garber took her north that October.
- **Gene Kessler,** the Dorseys' neighbour across the street, Walt's age, who worked thirty years at the same plant and clears the Dorseys' walk after every snow. He remembers the Cutter batches of 1955, when his mother took him out of the vaccine line, and won't have the COVID shot; his wife, **Barb,** won't go first. He watches the sun through a shade-fourteen welding glass from the plant. In July 2024 he and Barb move to their daughter's in South Carolina, because Barb wants to watch the grandchildren grow up somewhere besides a phone: the first time she has gone first.

## Dayton's county and the District

- **Carol Flory,** a county clerk for twenty-three years and a trans woman, who took her name at thirty-five, out of the county staff directory, because nobody ever looked twice at a Carol; Ohio law then made her print both names side by side in the newspaper a month before the hearing. Raised Old German Baptist, north of Dayton; sings tenor with the Methodists on Salem Avenue on Wednesdays. Dry, quick and courteous ("No, thank you," because she was brought up to answer letters), with a bad knee and a pen always on her. When Ohio stopped letting her license say F, she lost her login and was moved to the District's visitors' window, where she kept its only record, a green ledger she ruled herself, with the things that have no column in the back. In the war's first week her son's face went round the country and her two names went up beside it, and her old one onto her garage door; she painted it over with him, and went to work unpaid. The Safety Board, her own people, had her add a letter, H, to hold people who wouldn't pledge, and that is her guilt. On 10 February 2030, at Anna's call, she drove to Walt's nursing home in Kettering on the last of her gas, held up her phone for his goodbye for two hours, changing arms, put her hand on his when he died, and told Ruth at the window the next morning; she wouldn't take money for the gas ("No, thank you"). She cut the ledger down to its "known as" column, to keep the residents from being sorted, and ended at a folding table on the levee, finding people by what they were called.
- **Joel Flory,** Carol's only child, a sergeant in the Ohio Army National Guard, which he joined at seventeen with her signature. Tall, dry like her, careful with his hands, never wears a hat, never puts in an apostrophe, eats standing up; he calls her Carol, because the other words didn't fit, and was nine when her name changed. He voted for the President in Philadelphia. At Gate 12A he fired twice at Airman Eli Kasprzak after a shot nobody can place, and would only ever say "I fired", never that he returned fire; nine seconds of video made him the Counts' hero and the Stays' butcher. He looks after the younger men (Danny Ruhl). He stayed with the Governor's Guard when Washington ordered it into federal service, served in Dayton under the Safety Board and signed for the colonel's buses, helped his mother cut the ledger, wrote *None available* on the colonel's receipt, and was shot on the levee by his own side on 14 August 2030 after Gary Pruitt reported him.
- **Gary Pruitt,** the county caseworker, who makes every call he says he'll make. In the war's first week he brings Carol the video and tells her he's on her side ("Which one's that, Gary?" / "Yours."). His phone, in the months after, tells him whose fault it all is; he reports Carol and Joel to the Guard's colonel in August, and enters Carol into the District under H.
- **Dale Hutto,** Carol's neighbour across the street, an engineer on Wright-Patterson for thirty years, with a STAY sign in his yard that the street's children tell "Good boy" on their way to school. He was in the stopped line at Gate 12A, two cars back from the gate, and late for work. He won't have Carol's door painted on his street, and opens his mouth at Joel's back and can't say anything to it.
- **Private Danny Ruhl,** nineteen, in Joel's squad, who fired at the gate and doesn't know at what.
- **Dolores,** Ruth's nurse at the county's mental health clinic on Wayne Avenue. She came from the Philippines at twenty-five, when American hospitals recruited nurses there, and kept giving Ruth her injection through the pandemic's lockdowns, at the house, masked, staying when her daughter left because "somebody has to stay and do the arms". She kept her license current after the clinic closed, restarted Ruth's injection inside the District, and went out of the gate in June 2030 to look for more of it. She hasn't been seen since.
- **Lucille Odom,** a former elementary school cook, who lost her food benefits to a work rule and lives in the District. She tied Ruth's ration card round her neck with her own shoelace, and later ran the District's kitchen.
- **Caleb Henderson,** the neighbours' boy, who carried the Dorseys' trash bins up and down the drive for nothing, found Walt, and later carried water in the District.
- **Naomi Garber,** Carol's sister, Old German Baptist, who won't say Carol's name, and who named herself responsible party for Ruth to take her north.
- **Miss Bea Yancey,** librarian and choir director, who hid both sides' banned books.

## Gate 12A, 2030

- **Airman First Class Eli Kasprzak,** twenty, of Green Bay, Wisconsin, a defender at Wright-Patterson in his first Ohio winter, with the wrong gloves. He read Joel's name at once and asked if he was from around here ("Everybody said Ohio'd be warmer"). Killed at Gate 12A. His mother, on the news at her kitchen table with his school photograph in a hockey jersey: "He hated the cold." Carol keeps his name and age on a card in the back of the ledger.
- **The Governor of Ohio** (the doctor of the 2020 briefings, real, never named), a Count, who sent the Guard to the gate with a paper, and said on television that Ohio's soldiers had been fired upon, and believed it. **Lieutenant Mercer** and **the captain,** Joel's officers at the gate. **The woman from the Governor's office,** about Joel's age, kind, in a good wool coat: "We're the lawful side. The count's the count. We didn't start this."

## Further away

- **Renée Picard** (canon), a French astronaut, mission specialist on the Europa Mission's Shango, which launched on Nora's fifth birthday ("We are on our way. Don't wait up."). She came home with the other seven on 13 February 2030, carried out in a chair ("We're home. You can all go to bed now"), with something alive from Io that moved toward a light the first time and by the tenth didn't bother; until somebody shows her it isn't someone, she'd like people to be careful with it. Her commander is Dr. Musa (canon).
- **Joy,** Dolores's daughter, two years younger than Anna, a nurse who worked a COVID floor in Dayton through 2020, holding up iPads for goodbyes. She left for a job nursing children in Toronto in February 2021, because she had stopped being able to feel for her patients and wanted to again. In Toronto she cared for a small child in a genetics lab, who grew up to be Khan.
- **Declan Toner,** a software developer at Brynner in its Belfast office, who had worked with Ben on calls for four years before they met, in San Francisco, the weekend the Bell Riots began. Shorter than Ben pictured, and the funniest person in any meeting. He grew up in a nationalist family on a street with a peace wall at the bottom of it; as a boy he watched an actor read out the words of the IRA's party's leader (Gerry Adams, unnamed on the page) on the news, during the broadcasting ban, and thought it the funniest thing on television; his da voted for John Hume's party every election of his life. Beside Ben at the review queue, he held back a man with a shotgun making threats without a pause, and told Ben "Rule's the rule" about the hostage. At the District's wall he said that maybe violence just works, and that he'd rather it were Hume. He voted yes in the border poll of May 2028, with a wobble in the cross, and on the night of the count drove Norman Kerr home the long way round and told him his brother had paid for nothing. He still can't say which bought it. Married, with a daughter; Niamh's mother is a nurse at the hospital on the Falls Road.

## Belfast, 2028

- **Brendan Toner,** Declan's da, eighty, a widower, who drove the city's buses all his working life and still lives in the house Declan grew up in, on a short street with the peace wall at the bottom of it. He went up to Derry on a bus at twenty to march for one man, one vote on 5 October 1968 ("I was further back"), stayed home from the border poll of 1973 at his party's call, voted for the Agreement in 1998 and for John Hume's party every election of his life, and for unity in 2028, showing his bus pass for ID. "The bombs bought three and a half thousand funerals. That's all they ever bought." Every November since 1976 he has had a Mass said for Norman's brother Sammy. He has never told Declan.
- **Niamh Toner,** Declan's daughter, eighteen in March 2028; the border poll is her first vote. She has both passports and brought the Irish one, won't say how she voted ("It's a secret ballot, Granda"), shows her granda the RTÉ film of Derry on her phone, and calls the IRA chant "only a song".
- **Norman Kerr,** Brendan's friend from the other side of the wall, a Presbyterian and a unionist, who drove buses out of the same depot. Both widowed; he comes through the gate on Saturdays for the racing and his tea, and Brendan goes to him on Wednesdays for the football. They have never agreed about anything that mattered. He voted no and said so, believes the bombs bought unity, and accepts the count anyway, because for fifty years he told the other side "a majority's a majority": "The count's the count." His brother **Sammy,** a milk-lorry driver and part-time soldier, was shot in his cab on his round by the IRA in November 1976, at twenty-five. Norman doesn't hold with praying for the dead, and has never asked Brendan to stop.
- **Mickey Quinn,** Brendan's neighbour across the street, seventy-two, who did eleven years in prison for the IRA, came out into politics, and drives voters to the polls in a car with YES in the back window. He believes the bombs did it, and stopped the young men's chant on the night of the count because there were cameras.

## San Francisco, 2024

- **Chris Brynner** (canon), who founded Brynner and owns most of it. On Labor Day 2024 he switched the District's network back on against the police's request and put the residents on his video service's front page. Nobody at the company learned why. The city cancelled his contract that Thursday; his numbers that week were the biggest Brynner ever had.
- **Dana Reyes,** who runs Brynner's small trust and safety team, wrote its policy on violent live video, and set the queue's rules on Labor Day: no guns on camera, no hostages, no threats.
- **The District's people** whom the public came to know (canon): **Gabriel Bell,** who put trusted residents on the hostages' doors and, by a hostage security officer's account, stepped in front of a Guardsman's bullet meant for him; **Michael Webb,** a former plant manager at a chemical company, who spoke for the residents; both among the dead. **Henry Garcia,** laid off from a brewery for new machines; **Julie Hess,** aged out of foster care (her story is ours); **Lee,** a hostage, who signed new arrivals in. The book's own: the boy who held the phone, an old man offered a bus ticket to Fresno or the District, a woman who stole a ration card in her first week and isn't sorry, a big man who hears things and had been two years on a list for a doctor (his sister Lainie is in Sacramento), a man with a shotgun whose threats were held back, and a hotel cleaner of twenty-two years.
