# The people

Who everyone is and what happens to them, as the outline has it. Birthdays and every other date are in `timeline.ts`, and any age is computed from it with the date tool, never from this page. Details a chapter settles when it is drafted are added here then.

## The Dorseys and the Novaks (San Francisco, Dayton and Helsinki)

- **Anna Novak,** born Dorsey in Dayton, Ohio, the only child of Walt and Ruth. Detail-minded: she has never met a form she couldn't get through. In 2020 she runs a dentist's front desk in San Francisco, where she came for Ben's job and stayed for the city; the office closes for three weeks on 16 March 2020. She goes to her parents' house in March 2020 "for two weeks" to keep them out of stores, and stays until the family leaves for Helsinki in August 2022. Knowing Finland's rule (a grown child's parent may follow only if she moves at the same time, and only a Finnish citizen can ask), she asks her parents to come with them to San Francisco instead, the only place all of them could go together; they say no, and she chooses Helsinki knowing her parents can't follow unless something terrible happens. She tells Nora the truth about the preschool's lockdown drill, keeping the promise she made herself in the year of the county's Thursday visits. In Helsinki she can read none of the forms that were her competence. In February 2023 she turns down the employment office's free full-time Finnish course for newcomers (only in the first three years; Theo would have gone to daycare all day) to stay home through Theo's early years on Kela's child home care allowance, knowing that evening study takes years and so pushes back the certificate, citizenship, and any chance to ask Finland for her mother if something terrible happened. With the course she could have been a citizen the August after Theo turned five (August 2027). By April 2023 she is on chapter three of a blue textbook, reads Finnish numbers, can spell *laiva*, and has no course, no certificate and no date for either. The Saturday after the eclipse, on a bench at Huffman Prairie, she promises Walt never to let them put Ruth "in one of those places" (he refuses "I'll do everything I can" and asks for her word; she knows keeping it may mean bringing the family back from Finland, and doesn't tell Ruth, at his asking), and breaks the promise in April 2027 by using Ruth's declaration to get her the shot inside Dayton's Sanctuary District. In February 2030 she sends Dolores to sit with her dying father rather than take Ruth to Washington for her permit. She tells her children the truth.
- **Ben Novak,** Anna's husband, a software developer at Brynner (canon's Brynner Information Systems), on the team that builds its video player; half the team is in Helsinki, from a small Finnish company Brynner bought for its player. Until 2020 he walks past the tents near Brynner's tower every day without seeing them, and has never asked where they go. Raised in Toledo by his grandmother, dutiful rather than warm, after his parents were killed in a car crash when he was three, a year after his older sister, Katie, died at nine of an inherited illness his grandmother never spoke of; she died while he was at college. He has never had anyone near who needed him, and can respect Anna's pull toward her parents without feeling it. When Brynner ends its pandemic arrangements (from 1 September 2022 everyone works from their own office or transfers to another where their team works: for Ben, San Francisco or Helsinki; Belfast's one desk on the team is Declan's), he looks for a job he could do from Dayton, has one offered and taken back in the hiring freezes of June 2022, and moves to his team in Helsinki. On the porch he feels relief, and can see Anna's pain without feeling it. His Finnish colleague Mikko, on his screen every morning since the company was bought, finds them the daycare by the sea. At the Bell Riots (a borrowed reviewer on Brynner's video service, Labor Day 2024) he holds back Lee's clip ("We're all right. All six of us.") by the no-hostages rule, which also guarded the whole line from a court order; he half-thinks, ashamed, of his family's permits riding on his job, and never knows whether that moved his hand. He never learns whether the clip mattered: accounts of when the governor decided disagree. He hasn't told Anna about the folder, and doesn't know about her promise to Walt. When AI cuts his team, he and Declan are the two kept, and the family's permits ride on his job. He tells Nora the truth about his parents and Katie when she asks in April 2024, and drives to Toledo alone the next morning to their graves (one stone for his parents, a smaller one for Katie), the first time since his grandmother's funeral; "She was sick" is all his grandmother ever told him, once, and he never asked again; Anna has known about Katie from their first year together and learned to leave it shut. At that supper Ruth asks "Sick with what?" ("Nobody ever said." "Somebody should have."), and the question goes to the grave with him. He knows his parents' faces from six photographs, and comes back to find Walt on the porch watching for his car. He learns why his sister died when Alma is diagnosed. In Helsinki he leans on Antti Laine, a quiet Finnish dad he met at the daycare gate.
- **Nora,** born April 2019 in San Francisco, a baby when the family goes to Dayton and three when they move to Helsinki. At two and a half she starts at the preschool in the basement of Ruth's church (her teacher, Miss Jackie); at three she shows the family the lockdown drill she calls "the quiet game". On the leaving day she sings the preschool's going-home song to Walt through the front window; Anna takes down the plastic stars from her own childhood ceiling for Nora's room in Helsinki. The eclipse and the Europa launch on her fifth birthday light her love of space; she draws a space picture for Walt every birthday after, which he keeps on his fridge. At ten she tells her dying grandfather the crew are coming. She becomes a scientist, and is near Cochrane's work, not on his ship, at First Contact.
- **Theo,** born February 2022 in Dayton, six months old on the leaving day. Finns call him *Teo*. Home with Anna through 2023; walks in March 2023 into the arms of the woman who keeps the playground park's hut. The sea is his from his first ferry, to Suomenlinna on Easter Saturday 2023, where he laughs at the sea and weeps when the ferry leaves without him; his first sure word, the next day, is *laiva*, Finnish for ship, learned from Nora; he becomes a marine scientist, practical where Nora looks to the stars, and in the 2050s decides whether to use the organism from Io to heal the Baltic.
- **Alma,** born January 2027 in Helsinki. Diagnosed at six with the illness that killed Ben's sister; the era's cure can only cure by enhancing, so saving her makes her an Augment. In the Eugenics Wars she is an Augment in a war over Augments.
- **Walt Dorsey,** Anna's father. Polio at five, in 1952, in a ward where his mother could only stand at the window; one good leg and a steel brace on the other. Missed polio's vaccine by three years and is "on time" for COVID's. Thirty-four years at a car-parts plant; follows spaceflight. Says "everything's fine": a flaw out of love and out of pride, since needing help would mean he couldn't manage. Drives (the brace is on his left leg, and an automatic needs only the right); leans on a shopping cart in a store. Keeps going to the store himself in March 2020: three stores in four hours on 14 March. In 2020 he writes the day's COVID numbers from the two o'clock briefing in a notebook from the plant; he lets Ruth stop her shot ("It's your mother's arm"), which is why she names Anna, not him, in her declaration. Decides at supper on the night of the quiet game, before he is asked: when Anna asks them to come to San Francisco he says no to spare her ("Who'd do the tomatoes?"; he knows who would get him up and down its stepped sidewalks, and that she would give up Finland for them), leaving where she goes hers to decide, and sets the Sunday call at eleven, after church, "wherever you end up" (six in the evening in Helsinki). "We'll be fine" is the first time he says it knowing what it costs; Ruth: "Don't you start that with me, Walter." He tore up the envelope on which he worked out how old he'd be when Theo could remember him. Falls in April 2027 and lies seventeen hours before the neighbour's boy finds him. Dies in February 2030 in a cold nursing home in Kettering, on Dolores's phone at the hour of the Sunday call; his last words, "Tell Annie everything's fine."
- **Ruth Dorsey,** Anna's mother, a piano teacher and the organist at her Lutheran church. Schizoaffective disorder, begun in her twenties; an autumn in hospital in 2000, when Anna was eleven, and a county woman checking on Anna's care every Thursday for a year after. A monthly injection from Dolores keeps her well, and dulls her, with a slight tremor in her left hand she hides at the piano; her left arm is Dolores's. In 2020 she chooses to go without the shot to get her music back, sees the signs herself, asks for Dolores, and then writes a declaration at the bank naming Anna to decide when she can't ("When I am ill... Do not listen to me. Listen to this."). Her no to San Francisco: "It took me a year to let Dolores near that arm. I haven't got another year to spend on a stranger, and you haven't got one to spend on me," and then the fast clean scale she plays when she won't say anything else; that night she plays something slow at midnight. She checks every permit form with her red pen, and on the leaving day tells Anna she stays the one named on the declaration ("They have telephones in Finland. I looked it up."). Plays the *Finlandia* hymn to Anna on Sundays, and never explains it. Renews the declaration word for word every three years at the bank ("Like the car"; the current one dated August 2023), still under the hymnal. From about 2024 Dolores no longer comes to houses, so Ruth has her shot at the clinic on Wayne Avenue. In April 2024 she tells Nora the truth about her shaking hand ("It's my medicine... The hand still knows where the notes are"), reads Renée Picard's fear on the clinic's television ("The frightened ones practise"), prays from the organ bench for Renée's nerve, and has the pastor pray for Vantaa, writing out the stress for him in red. Relapses when her clinic closes in September 2026; inside Dayton's District from April 2027, on the shot again, teaching children on a locked, out-of-tune piano it takes a signed request to use. Off the shot after Dolores goes out of the gate in June 2030; taken north by Naomi in October; around 2032 she chooses whether to leave for Finland. Her illness is written true: psychosis as it is, and Ruth well as a whole person, proud, funny and difficult, never given wisdom by her illness. Well, she corrects everything in red pen (the church bulletin, the state's forms, a nurse's spelling). Walt first heard her play when she was thirty, on no medicine, from the back of her church. Her relapses begin the same way: no sleep, cleaning and laughing, then coincidences meant for her (the radio in 2000; the governor reading Walt's numbers in 2020). In 2020 she half-sees it in Anna's face. The declaration's front says, in her hand: "When I am ill, I will tell you I am perfectly well. I will tell you the injection is poison and that you are not who you are. Do not listen to me. Listen to this." and "I went without it once, in the summer of 2020, to hear myself play. It was worth it. I will not do it again, and if I try to, stop me." She wrote more on the back that Anna has not read; it is kept in the piano bench under the hymnal.
- **Gene Kessler** and **Barb,** across the street. Gene worked the same plant as Walt, clears the Dorseys' walk after every snow, remembers the Cutter batches of 1955 and won't have the COVID shot, though he salts the Dorseys' steps so Walt can go and get his; Barb has "never gone first in her life" (and wears the paper eclipse glasses that Gene, with his shade-fourteen welding glass from the plant, won't). Through 2020 he and Walt talk porch to porch across the street. In 2024 they move to their daughter's, because Barb wants to watch the grandchildren grow up somewhere besides a phone: the first time she has gone first.
- **Caleb Henderson,** the neighbours' boy (about thirteen in 2024), who carries the Dorseys' bins down and back for nothing (Walt: "Won't take a dollar for it. I've tried."), and finds Walt.

## Dolores, Joy and Carol

- **Dolores,** from the Philippines at twenty-five, a nurse at the county mental health clinic on Wayne Avenue; Ruth's nurse for years, and a mother to Carol, whom she met at the clinic when Carol came for the counsellor's letter she needed to begin her transition, and didn't turn away. Falls ill with COVID in April 2020, Carol and Joel bringing her groceries; restarts Ruth's injection in July. She left her own mother in the Philippines at twenty-five, and for nineteen years her mother told her on the telephone that she was fine; in August 2022 she tells Anna she, not Walt, will call if Ruth isn't. Keeps her license after the clinic closes; restarts Ruth's shot inside the District; in February 2030 sits with Walt while he dies, at Anna's asking, holding up her phone and changing arms; in June 2030 goes out of the District's gate to find more of Ruth's shot and isn't seen again.
- **Joy,** Dolores's daughter, born in Dayton the year her mother arrived; a nurse, and Carol's near-sister. Works a COVID floor in 2020, holding up iPads for goodbyes, and leaves for a children's hospital in Toronto in February 2021 because she has stopped being able to feel for her patients and wants to again. Later works at the Noonien-Singh Institute (canon's Toronto lab, where Khan was a small child in 2022), where Carol joins her.
- **Carol Flory,** a county clerk, a trans woman who took her name in 2013, when Ohio law printed her old name beside it in the newspaper. Raised Old German Baptist north of Dayton, cut off by her family except for what her sister Naomi carries from their mother; sings tenor with the Methodists on Salem Avenue. Dry, quick, courteous, a pen always on her. Anna first meets her in May 2020 on Dolores's steps, Joel carrying water; asked if she's family: "She'd say so. I'd say so." Believes a person's word about who she is counts. Takes Ruth's own signature in April 2027, knowing it isn't informed consent; tells Anna to use the declaration that exposes it; fired. Her two names go up beside her son's face in January 2029; she leaves for Joy in Toronto, giving Joel the District's ledger. Finds "Mom" under the last line of the narrow book Naomi posts her, and finds Ruth for Anna. Comes back around 2032 to testify, and must decide whether to accept an amnesty that frees the men who shot Joel. At the Institute she keeps the consent forms for children who can't consent.
- **Joel Flory,** Carol's only child, a sergeant in the Ohio Army National Guard; calls her Carol, because the other words didn't fit. On election night 2028 his unit is at the county elections board. At Gate 12A, bound by an oath to two masters who give opposite orders, he goes to keep his boys from firing, fires, and kills an airman; he says "I fired", never "I returned fire". Guards the District with his mother's ledger, writes "None available" on a colonel's list to save the residents, and is shot by his own side in August 2030.

## Dayton's county and the District

- **Gary Pruitt,** the county caseworker, who makes every call he says he'll make, writes down everything, and in the war reports Carol's son.
- **Lucille Odom,** a former school cook in the District, who ties Ruth's ration card round her neck with her own shoelace and runs its kitchen.
- **Naomi Garber,** Carol's sister, Old German Baptist (no television, nonresistant), who won't say either of Carol's names and carries their mother's "Mother asks if you're eating". In October 2030 she takes the old people with nobody north on her grain truck, Ruth among them, and posts the narrow book to Toronto without a letter. Planted before chapter 12.
- **Miss Bea Yancey,** children's librarian and choir director at Salem Avenue, who hides banned books and hums the note for hymn 534. **Ti Jak,** from Springfield's Haitian community, who goes east on the colonel's buses. **Dale Hutto,** Carol's neighbour, an engineer on the base. **Private Danny Ruhl,** in Joel's squad. **Airman Eli Kasprzak,** twenty, of Green Bay, killed at Gate 12A.
- **The Governor of Ohio** (the doctor of the 2020 briefings, real, never named), who sides with Philadelphia. Every act done under her authority in the war is fair, specific and plainly the book's own.

## Helsinki

- **Antti Laine,** from Kotka, a port town east of Helsinki; about Ben's age, tall and fair, rides a bicycle with studded tyres all winter with his daughter **Aino** (Nora's friend, in her daycare group) on the back. For a whole autumn says only *moi* at the gate; his first words to Ben, in December 2022: "Aino says that Nora says you are afraid of the dark." "November is worse. December has the lights." Says a thing once and leaves you alone with it, which Ben, raised by a grandmother who thought most talk a waste of breath, finds like home. His grandmother left Viipuri at sixteen in the war, with her mother and a sewing machine, and lived sixty years in Kotka, always "from Viipuri". Against NATO all his life, "in my head", until Ukraine. His brother, a truck driver in Kotka, voted for the Finns Party in 2023: "He is not a bad man. I don't know what he is." Told Ben to take the children on the Suomenlinna ferry. Becomes Ben's friend over the book; Ben leans on him in 2027.
- **Abdullahi,** a father at the daycare gate whom Ben nodded to every morning for eight months before he stopped and introduced himself (the morning Finland joined NATO): "Nora is teaching Hamdi English. Hamdi is teaching Nora Somali. I think they are mostly teaching each other the rude words." His daughter **Hamdi** is Nora's best friend.
- **The woman who keeps the playground park's hut,** in her sixties, with a grey braid: speaks to Anna only in slow Finnish, as if she'll understand any day now, and says Theo's name the Finnish way. **The employment office counsellor,** Anna's age, from Estonia (two years to learn Finnish: "For you, longer").

## The Toners (San Francisco and Belfast)

- **Declan Toner,** Ben's closest friend, seconded from Brynner's Belfast office to San Francisco for a year, from early 2019; it ends early, in March 2020, when Orla goes home, and the Toners fly on 19 March. Ben's first real friend: he was thirty-six before he lived anywhere nobody asked which school you went to. Told Ben about the wall at the bottom of his da's street at a fenced-off lot where the tents had been. The funniest person in any meeting; grew up on a street with a peace wall at the bottom of it. Kept with Ben when AI cuts their team. Comes to Dayton for the eclipse with Niamh (6 to 9 April 2024): the first time he and Ben are in one room since March 2020 ("You've legs. I'd only ever seen the top half."); during totality he watches Niamh, not the sky. Parting: "Eight o'clock, then." Back in San Francisco with Ben for Brynner's meetings at the end of August 2024 (his idea to come early: "a weekend first, to be eejits in"; greets Ben in the hotel lobby with "Eight o'clock"). Said good morning to the woman with the geranium "forty mornings" and never asked her name; recognises her in the District's line (Rosa Delgado) and waves at the screen. A borrowed reviewer beside Ben at the Bell Riots: presses red on the man with the shotgun ("Threat"); tells Ben "Rule's the rule" over Lee's clip, then "You did right"; "It wouldn't have changed anything." / "You don't know that." / "No. I don't." At the District's wall: the broadcast ban (an actor reading the Sinn Féin leader's words; "We used to do the voice"), his da's vote for John Hume every election, and "maybe it just works, and we'd all rather not know."
- **Orla Toner,** Declan's wife, a nurse, who goes home in March 2020 to nurse in Belfast's hospitals, by her own choice: her British registration was no use in California, she rang her old ward and started back on 23 March. She found Fort Baker in her first weeks in America. Measures how far off a thing is in beds. A lifelong nationalist whose patients depend on the North's free health service; in the border poll of November 2028 she votes no, and tells no one.
- **Niamh Toner** (said Neeve), their daughter, ten in San Francisco and adoring baby Nora, whose "Ba" she takes for her name; she gives Nora a knitted rabbit for the drive to Dayton, which Nora names Ba. At fourteen she asks for a total eclipse for her birthday and comes to Dayton with Declan for it (6 to 9 April 2024); Nora doesn't remember her, and Niamh learns the rabbit's name ("That's a stupid name for a rabbit"); she cries without a sound through totality. The border poll is her first vote.
- **Brendan Toner,** Declan's da, a retired bus driver, nationalist, widower, in the house with the wall at the bottom of the street. **Norman Kerr,** his friend from across the wall, unionist, whose brother Sammy the IRA shot on his milk round in 1976. Both carry real losses and real doubts.

## Further away

- **Renée Picard** (canon), a French astronaut, mission specialist on the Europa Mission's Shango, launched on Nora's fifth birthday and home on 13 February 2030 with something alive from Io that she believes may be someone. Canon: it later heals the oceans and cleans the air.
- **Khan** (canon), a small child at the Noonien-Singh Institute in Toronto in 2022, a young man there around 2033.
- **Chris Brynner** (canon), who founded Brynner and switched the District's network back on at the Bell Riots ("We'll lose the city contract, and we'll have the biggest night we've ever had", reported second-hand; why he did it is never known). **Dana Reyes,** who runs its trust and safety team, helped build its fingerprint takedown system and wrote the night's rules (no guns, no threats, no hostages). The District's people the public came to know (canon): Gabriel Bell; Michael Webb (a former plant manager at a chemical company; his boy Danny holds the phone); Henry Garcia (two years inside, never in trouble with the law); Lee (a public health worker at the Processing Centre, one of the six). Book's own: **Rosa Delgado,** the woman with the broom and the geranium in a coffee can under the freeway (chapter 1), cleared off Market Street in July 2024, who carried a cutting into the District in her pocket; her name isn't on the cardboard at the wall that Thursday, and her fate is unknown. A big man on a two-year list for a doctor ("If my sister in Sacramento's watching, hi, Lainie").
