# Outline

What each chapter is for, so the writing can be free while the book holds its direction. The core itself is in `CLAUDE.md`. This says which parts of it each chapter is responsible for.

## Using it

Before a chapter is written, fill in its entry, starting with its idea: the one concept the chapter thinks through, which its people and plot exist to serve. Before it goes to the producer, test it against that entry:

1. **Does it answer its question?** Something open at the start has moved by the end.
2. **Does it go deep on its deep subjects?** Each one must matter to what happens, not only be mentioned.
3. **Is there more it could say from the core?** Look at what it barely touches, and add only what fits.
4. **Does it sit inside the book's truth, and test its values?** However dark it gets, humanity finds a way, and the chapter has something to say about at least one of the nine values.
5. **Is the idea the subject, and the people and plot its case?** Every scene works the idea through or shows what it costs someone. Neither a lecture nor a family story with Star Trek wallpaper.
6. **Does someone face a choice that costs?** The entry's **choice** names it: who chooses, between what, which values pull against each other, and what each road costs whom. It is agreed with the producer before drafting, and no option has a way round it.

An entry guides the chapter and doesn't bind it. If a better idea changes one, change the entry and say why it fits the direction. Deep subjects should rotate, so that over the book every part of the core gets its turn.

## Chapters

### 1. Golden Gate (February to Saturday 21 March 2020, San Francisco)

- **The idea:** home is where the people who need you are, and going to them can be the danger.
- **Question:** When the world shuts, where do you go?
- **Goes deep on:** family, health, labor (work that moves to a screen), geography.
- **Touches:** society (the city's tents, the seed of the Districts), politics (an election year turning angry), technology (Brynner, and the video player Ben's team builds), culture (two families, one American and one from Belfast), how the past is remembered (Declan's wall at the bottom of his da's street).
- **The world it shows:** two young families, close friends, in a city they chose: Ben and Declan at Brynner's tower on Market Street, Anna at a dentist's front desk, Nora a baby whom Niamh, nearly ten, adores. Sundays on the grass at Fort Baker looking at the bridge, the ground where Starfleet will one day stand, never winked at. The tents under the freeway, a fenced lot where they were, the woman with the broom ("Wherever they could carry it to"), and Declan's wall that a general promised would be "very, very temporary". Then COVID: delivery slots gone by midnight, and in Dayton Walt does the shopping in three stores in four hours, on one good leg.
- **The choice:** Ben and Anna's. Go to Dayton and do every bit of the shopping so that Walt and Ruth never enter a store, carrying whatever is on their hands across the country into the house of the two people it would kill most easily; or stay, and let a man on a brace go to three stores a week through the worst of it. A fortnight in a motel first is no use: those two weeks are the ones they are going for. Value 7 against itself. They go, "for two weeks". Orla makes the mirror choice: home to her old ward in Belfast, into the danger, and the Toners go with her. On the path Walt holds out his hands, and Anna has a second to decide what safe means: she gives him the baby.
- **Moves from → to:** two families settled where they chose to be → both scattered home, one toward danger on purpose, one into her parents' arms with five days of the road still on her.

### 2. Two Weeks (March 2020 to February 2021, Dayton)

- **The idea:** what a person will trade to be wholly herself. Ruth's medicine keeps her well and takes the top off her music; the country around the house is choosing, too, what it will give up and for whom.
- **Question:** What is being well worth, and who decides?

- **Touches:** timeline, society, values, technology, conflict, how the past is remembered (Walt's polio).
- **The world it shows:** the real Ohio of 2020: the two o'clock briefings, the health director explaining the virus like a good doctor, Walt writing the numbers in his notebook from the plant; the mask rule made and dropped; armed men outside the director's house, filmed and played on Ben's code without a stutter ("Didn't buffer once"); the director resigns. Dolores ill, and Carol and Joel on her steps ("She'd say so. I'd say so"). Church on a phone, the Bach to an empty church. Columbus's Sanctuary District on the news (the book's), and Ruth: "Now we know where they'll put me when your father dies." The election; the Capitol, and Nora waving. - **The choice:** Ruth's. With Dolores ill she sends the stranger off the porch, then tells Dolores no, to hear herself play: her dignity and her music against her own well self, and her daughter's fear, at the table where the illness is named for the first time. Walt sides with her ("It's your mother's arm"). For weeks she plays as she did at thirty; then the numbers become a hymn the governor is writing for her, she sees Anna's face, and asks for Dolores. - **Moves from → to:** a family keeping two old people safe from a virus → Ruth has chosen her own medicine twice and written down who decides when she can't; the country around the house has begun to frighten them.

### 3. Leaving (May 2021 to Saturday 20 August 2022, Dayton)

- **The idea:** what makes someone leave their country. Not one reason but years of them, each reasonable, until staying is the decision that needs defending.
- **Question:** Who goes, and who stays behind?
- **Goes deep on:** family, values, labor, mortality.
- **Touches:** geography, politics, education (a preschool's drill), health, economy.
- **The world it shows:** a house where nothing is decided and everything is settled: tomatoes in earlier than Walt's father dared, Theo on the way, Nora at the preschool in the basement of Ruth's church. Theo born; Russia invades Ukraine; Brynner's email (from September, San Francisco or Helsinki, where the player came from); a job offered in Dayton at little more than half the pay, sending hospital bills. Finland's rule, real: only a citizen can ask for a parent, and one who didn't move at the same time can come only if she is fully dependent on her child, physically and mentally. So Anna can have her parents on the day they can't manage without her, and while they are fine the answer is no. The week of Uvalde (kept offstage) Nora shows them the quiet game, and Anna tells her the truth. Roe falls. The fast lane, the permits in thirteen days, Ruth's red pen on every form, Dolores's promise ("Not your father. Me"), the plastic stars taken down, Nora singing through the front window.
- **The choice:** Anna's. Dayton, beside her parents, where she could keep her mother out of a District, with Ben in a job he doesn't want and would never complain of; or Helsinki, her children's life without the quiet game, where her parents can't follow for years. On the porch she asks whether they'll come when she is a citizen and can bring them, and they won't: Walt has his tomatoes, and Ruth will not fill in a form to be fully dependent on her daughter again. Then she offers to stay, if they'll say they need her. Ruth won't tell her stay and won't tell her go. Walt, who knows she would stay for the smallest reason, gives her *fine*. Anna has never been fooled by it. She chooses to take it, writes *Helsinki*, covers the word with her hand, and starts a folder marked *Mom and Dad*, four years.
- **Moves from → to:** the family whole in Ohio → leaving, with a six-month-old, on a lie she chose to accept, meaning to bring them anyway; Ben, raised without anyone who needed him, takes Walt at his word, and sees a second too late what it cost Anna.

- **Touches:** geography (the wall and its gate), culture, family (three generations voting), law (a secret ballot), technology (what reaches a screen).

### 13. The Settling (May to Thursday 21 October 2032, Ohio)

- **The idea:** what peace costs the people who are owed justice.
- **Question:** Can a country forgive itself, and who pays for it?

- **Moves from → to:** a war with no end → an end that nobody calls justice, Carol's refusal to forgive written on the form, and Ruth's last choice made by Ruth.

### 19. First Contact (winter 2062 to Friday 6 April 2063, Bozeman)
