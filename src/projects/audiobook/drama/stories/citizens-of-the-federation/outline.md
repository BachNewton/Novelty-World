# Outline

What each chapter is for, so the writing can be free while the book holds its direction. The core itself is in `CLAUDE.md`. This says which parts of it each chapter is responsible for.

## Using it

Before a chapter is written, fill in its entry. Before it goes to audio, test it against that entry:

1. **Does it answer its question?** Something open at the start has moved by the end.
2. **Does it go deep on its deep subjects?** Each one must matter to what happens, not only be mentioned.
3. **Is there more it could say from the core?** Look at what it barely touches, and add only what fits.
4. **Does it sit inside the book's truth, and test its values?** However dark it gets, humanity finds a way, and the chapter has something to say about at least one of the nine values.
5. **Is the world the subject, and the person the lens?** Neither a lecture nor a family story with Star Trek wallpaper.

An entry guides the chapter and doesn't bind it. If a better idea changes one, change the entry and say why it fits the direction. Deep subjects should rotate, so that over the book every part of the core gets its turn.

## Chapters

### 1. Dark by Four (November 2026, Helsinki)

- **Question:** Is the danger out there on the news, or already in the family?
- **Goes deep on:** mental health, politics and government, values, ethics.
- **Touches:** timeline, geography, society, culture, family, economy, labor, power, education, transit, construction, conflict.
- **Leaves for later:** faith, other species, environment, how the past is remembered.
- **Moves from → to:** America is something they watch → America has reached Ruth's door.

### 2. Responsible Party (March 2027, Dayton)

- **Question:** Where does Ruth end up, and who gets to decide?
- **Goes deep on:** law and justice, health, gender and sexuality, food (who can get healthy food, and at what price).
- **Touches:** economy, labor, family, ethics, politics, transit, faith.
- **The world it shows:** inside a Sanctuary District, through its paperwork.
- **The clerk:** Carol Flory, a trans woman, a person first, who fills in other people's identity on forms all day while her own legal identity is under threat.
- **Moves from → to:** the county is about to decide Ruth's life, on a signature she can't understand → Ruth decided it herself, in a good year, by naming Anna, who signs her mother into the District as the only way anyone can ever get her out. Ruth is inside, on the shot, with a Finnish application started, and Carol has put her own file in a supervisor's queue.

### 3. Known As (January to October 2030, Dayton)

- **Question:** How do you keep caring for people when the thing you work for falls apart?
- **Goes deep on:** conflict and how it's resolved, politics and government, art (book censorship), leisure (screen time and social media).
- **Touches:** labor, law, economy, resources, society, ethics, family, gender and sexuality, food and health, faith.
- **The world it shows:** the Second Civil War as most people live it: each side pulling books off shelves, and neighbours turned against each other through their phones.
- **The sides:** today's American politics carried to its extremes, heavily implied and never named. Two Presidents, sworn in Washington and Philadelphia. In Ohio, Stays and Counts. Both sides are made of people, and their ideas aren't equal, and neither gets off.
- **How it starts:** nobody in the middle of it can say when it officially began. On 14 January 2030 the Ohio Guard and the airmen of Wright-Patterson fire on each other at Gate 12A, and that Friday the county's payroll doesn't arrive.
- **Its shape:** one night, 13 to 14 August 2030, in the hours when nobody governs Dayton. Carol reads her paper ledger aloud to her son, a name at a time, and each name opens a part of the war. Then the two of them cut it down to its first column.
- **Moves from → to:** Carol's work is a record of what people are, kept for a county that no longer exists, and any side can sort people with it → all that's left of it is what people are called by those who love them, useless to a man with a bus. It cost her son, and the people with no one to look for them. The Sanctuary District is gone, not abolished, and everyone is hungry. Anna finds her mother anyway.

## Across the book

Keep an eye on what hasn't had its turn, and let subjects return as the world changes. Food, books, screens and gender all come back later: a world with replicators, a world that has stopped censoring, and how a future child learns any of this was ever fought over.
