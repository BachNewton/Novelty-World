# Outline

What each chapter is for, so the writing can be free while the book holds its direction. The core itself is in `CLAUDE.md`. This says which parts of it each chapter is responsible for.

## Using it

Before a chapter is written, fill in its entry, starting with its idea: the one concept the chapter thinks through, which its people and plot exist to serve. Before it goes to the producer, test it against that entry:

1. **Does it answer its question?** Something open at the start has moved by the end.
2. **Does it go deep on its deep subjects?** Each one must matter to what happens, not only be mentioned.
3. **Is there more it could say from the core?** Look at what it barely touches, and add only what fits.
4. **Does it sit inside the book's truth, and test its values?** However dark it gets, humanity finds a way, and the chapter has something to say about at least one of the nine values.
5. **Is the idea the subject, and the people and plot its case?** Every scene works the idea through or shows what it costs someone. Neither a lecture nor a family story with Star Trek wallpaper.
6. **Does someone face a choice that costs?** The entry's **choice** names it: who chooses, between what, which values pull against each other, and what each road costs whom. It is agreed with the producer before drafting, and no option has a way round it.

An entry guides the chapter and doesn't bind it. If a better idea changes one, change the entry and say why it fits the direction. Deep subjects should rotate, so that over the book every part of the core gets its turn.

## Chapters

### 1. Golden Gate (February to 21 March 2020, San Francisco)

- **The idea:** home is where the people who need you are, and going to them can be the danger.
- **Question:** When the world shuts, where do you go?
- **Goes deep on:** family, health, labor (work that moves to a screen), geography.
- **Touches:** society (the city's tents, the seed of the Districts), politics (an election year turning angry), technology (Brynner, and the video player Ben's team builds), culture (two families, one American and one from Belfast), how the past is remembered (Declan's stories of the wall at the bottom of his da's street).
- **The world it shows:** two young families, close friends, in a city they chose. Ben and Declan at Brynner's headquarters, Anna at a dentist's front desk, Nora a baby whom Niamh, ten, adores. Sundays on the grass at Fort Baker looking at the bridge, the ground where Starfleet will one day stand, never winked at. Then COVID: delivery slots vanish, and in Dayton Walt keeps going to the store himself, on one good leg.
- **The choice:** Ben and Anna. Go to Dayton so that Walt and Ruth never have to enter a store, and risk carrying the virus into the house of the two people they mean to protect, leaving their life "for two weeks"; or stay, and let a man on a brace do the shopping through the worst of it. Value 7 against itself: both roads can hurt the people they love. They drive across the country with a baby. Declan's wife makes the mirror choice: she goes home to nurse in Belfast's hospitals, walking into the danger, and the Toners go with her.
- **Moves from → to:** two families settled where they chose to be → both scattered home, one toward danger on purpose, one toward her parents with a risk in the car.

### 2. Two Weeks (March 2020 to February 2021, Dayton)

- **The idea:** what a person will trade to be wholly herself. Ruth's medicine keeps her well and takes the top off her music; the country around the house is choosing, too, what it will give up and for whom.
- **Question:** What is being well worth, and who decides?
- **Goes deep on:** health (mental and physical), politics, family, faith (church on a phone, an organist playing to an empty building).
- **Touches:** timeline, society, values, technology, conflict, how the past is remembered (Walt's polio).
- **The world it shows:** the real Ohio of 2020, dated days each a little hotter. The state's health director on television every afternoon, Walt with a pencil writing the numbers down; armed men outside her house that May. Anna doing the shopping and meeting the mask fights in person. Ben's code playing the videos that threaten the doctor. The election; Columbus's Sanctuary District opening (the book's); the Capitol, and Nora waving at the screen. Carol comes into the book through Dolores, bringing Dolores groceries when she's ill; Joel is sixteen. Ends on vaccine week: Walt "on time" after missing polio's vaccine by three years; Gene, who remembers Cutter, won't; Joy leaves for Toronto, because "somebody has to stay and do the arms."
- **The choice:** Ruth's. With Dolores ill and the clinic closed to visitors, she turns away the stranger sent to give her the shot, and then decides to go without it and get her music back. For weeks she plays the way she did at thirty; then the signs come, which Anna knows from when she was eleven. Ruth sees them herself and asks for Dolores. Afterward, at the bank, she writes the declaration naming Anna to decide when she can't. Values 1, 5 and 6: her dignity and freedom against her own well self.
- **Moves from → to:** a family keeping two old people safe from a virus → Ruth has chosen her own medicine twice and written down who decides when she can't; the country they're sheltering in has begun to frighten them.

### 3. Leaving (February 2021 to August 2022, Dayton)

- **The idea:** what makes someone leave their country. Not one reason but years of them, each reasonable, until staying is the decision that needs defending.
- **Question:** Who goes, and who stays behind?
- **Goes deep on:** family, values, labor, mortality.
- **Touches:** geography, politics, education (a preschool's drill), health, economy.
- **The world it shows:** Brynner ends remote work outside its office cities, so Ben must work from San Francisco or Helsinki, where his team is (the video player came from a Finnish company Brynner bought). Theo is born in Dayton. The week of Uvalde, Nora's preschool sends home a note about its lockdown drill, which Nora calls "the quiet game"; Uvalde itself stays offstage. Roe falls in June. Finland's rule, real: only a Finnish citizen can bring a parent, so Walt and Ruth can't follow the family to Helsinki at all unless, years from now, Anna is a citizen and something terrible forces it.
- **The choice:** Anna's. San Francisco, still America, where her parents could come with them, or Helsinki, her children's future, where they can't. She asks them to come to San Francisco, knowing what it would cost Ruth, who won't let a stranger near her arm. Walt says no to spare Annie: he knows who would get a man with one good leg up San Francisco's hills, and that she'd give up Finland for them; he points her to Helsinki himself. Ruth's no comes through the piano. Walt's "fine" is born on that porch, a flaw out of love and pride.
- **Moves from → to:** the family whole in Ohio → leaving, with a six-month-old, and Walt and Ruth have said no for good reasons. Ben, raised without anyone who needed him, can't quite feel what it costs Anna.

### 4. First Winter (December 2022 to April 2023, Helsinki)

- **The idea:** belonging costs something, and somebody pays for it later.
- **Question:** What does it take to belong?
- **Goes deep on:** culture, society, education, labor (work you can't do without the language).
- **Touches:** geography, environment (the sea, the dark), politics (Finland's election, NATO), conflict (the shelter), faith (a hymn in a school gym).
- **The world it shows:** päiväkoti, the dark at three in the afternoon, the sauna, a quiet Finnish dad Ben meets at the daycare gate, Theo's first ferry and the sea that becomes his. Finland's Independence Day, when the children sing the *Finlandia* hymn, Ruth's Sunday tune, which Anna doesn't recognise yet. Finland's April 2023 election, in which a party running against immigrants comes second; Finland joins NATO, and the shelter under the building is more than a place for skis. Finland is good, and it isn't simple.
- **The choice:** Anna's. Spend the next years learning Finnish, with Theo in daycare full-time as Finns do and every American instinct calling it abandonment, or stay home with him and remain an outsider who depends on Ben's job. She stays home. The cost lands later: without the language certificate she can't become a citizen as soon as the law allows, and only a citizen can bring her mother. The year she loses puts Ruth's permit on the wrong side of America's war (11).
- **Moves from → to:** strangers in the dark → at home in Helsinki, and Anna at home with Theo, outside the language; a cost nobody can see yet.

### 5. Godspeed (4 to 15 April 2024, Dayton)

- **The idea:** hope, for people who stay, is sending someone and waiting for them to come back. Dayton invented flight and has watched nearly all of it on a screen since; for one week everyone is in the same room, and what they hope for is a homecoming.
- **Question:** What does a family hope for, when it has everything it needs this once?
- **Goes deep on:** faith (a congregation praying for the crew, and Ruth praying for a frightened stranger's nerve), technology (spaceflight), family, art (Ruth's music, Nora's drawings).
- **Touches:** timeline (the eclipse, Glenn, Armstrong), geography, culture, mental health, how the past is remembered (the Wrights), conflict (Vantaa).
- **The world it shows:** two days before they fly, a twelve-year-old shoots classmates at a school in Vantaa, beside Helsinki: Finland isn't the safe place on Anna's spreadsheet. Declan and Niamh come for the eclipse weekend; the eclipse of 8 April darkens the street; on Nora's fifth birthday the Shango launches with Renée Picard aboard (canon). From now on Nora draws a space picture every birthday for Walt, who keeps them on his fridge. Walt gets up from a chair in two goes. Gene and Barb are moving to their daughter's. On the front page under the crew, the county is still arguing about whether Dayton needs a District.
- **The choice:** Anna's. Walt asks her to promise that if anything happens to him, she won't let them put her mother "in one of those places." Promise, or tell her father she can't. She promises. Values 7 and 6.
- **Moves from → to:** a family known through a screen → for a week in one house, hope fastened to a ship coming home, and Anna holding a promise she may not be able to keep.

### 6. Labor Day (31 August to 8 September 2024, San Francisco and Helsinki)

- **The idea:** who decides what the world sees. A wall is the first decision; then the police cut the residents off, one rich man switches them back on, and a row of borrowed engineers decides, a story at a time, what goes out four minutes late.
- **Question:** Who gets to be seen?
- **Goes deep on:** technology, society, law and justice, conflict (Declan's peace walls against the District's wall).
- **Touches:** economy and labor, politics, values, culture, health, how the past is remembered.
- **The world it shows:** canon's Bell Riots in Sanctuary District A (DS9 "Past Tense"), beside the real summer of the Supreme Court's ruling on sleeping outside. Ben and Declan, back together at headquarters for the first time since 2020, as borrowed reviewers on Brynner's video service.
- **The choice:** Ben's. He holds back a hostage's clip saying they're all right, because the rule says no hostages; Declan: "Rule's the rule." He never learns whether it would have changed the governor's mind, and neither does the reader. At the District's wall Declan says maybe violence just works. On the Sunday call, Dayton has voted to build a District of its own.
- **Moves from → to:** Ben has driven past the wall and never asked where the tents went → he has sent most of the residents out to the world and held one back, and goes home with a question he can never answer.

*Between 6 and 7: AI lays off everyone on Brynner's video team but Ben and Declan, kept to check what the machine decides. The family's permits ride on Ben's job.*

### 7. Father's Day (Sunday 8 November 2026, Helsinki)

- **The idea:** a country is a decision people keep making. Finland's maternity box and its shelter law, against a vote in Washington that cut Medicaid and a clinic that closed rather than wait; Ohio electing the doctor of its first pandemic spring; and Finland's own rules, tightened, shutting Ruth out.
- **Question:** Is the danger out there on the news, or already in the family?
- **Goes deep on:** mental health, politics, values, ethics.
- **Touches:** economy (Ben, one of two kept), society, family, education, construction (the shelter).
- **The world it shows:** Anna pregnant with Alma. Ruth's clinic closed in September; her relapse shows on the Sunday call (the piano stopping after a few bars, soup she opens herself, taking Anna for the county's Thursday woman). Ben tells Nora the truth about the shelter, and Anna tells her the truth about Grandma, at a level a seven-year-old can hold; the reader learns both through Nora. Anna, who has always known that only a Finnish citizen can bring a parent, learns that citizenship now takes longer, and that without the language she is years from it.
- **The choice:** Anna's. Fly to Ohio before the airlines' late-pregnancy cutoff, risking the pregnancy and leaving Ben alone with two children and a precarious job, or stay while her mother relapses alone. She stays.
- **Moves from → to:** America is something they watch → America has reached Ruth's door, and Finland's door is shut to her twice over.

### 8. Responsible Party (from Tuesday 13 April 2027, Dayton and Helsinki)

- **The idea:** a form decides what a person is, and a person can decide first.
- **Question:** Where does Ruth end up, and who gets to decide?
- **Goes deep on:** law and justice, health, gender, food.
- **Touches:** economy, labor, family, ethics, politics, faith.
- **The world it shows:** Walt falls and lies seventeen hours until the neighbour's boy finds him; his fridge is covered in years of Nora's rockets. Anna flies over with Alma, three months old; Ben is home with Nora and Theo, leaning on the Finnish dad and the Toners. The trap: Ruth refuses the shot, and no pharmacist or clinic will inject someone who refuses; the hospital discharges her, because the county's lawyer counts the District as "the community"; Medicare doesn't pay for long-term care; care homes won't take her unstable. Carol, at the county's counter. Inside the fence, the piano is locked, and using it for its purpose takes a signed request.
- **The choice:** Carol first, then Anna. Carol takes Ruth's own signature, knowing it isn't informed consent: kind, and true to Carol's belief that a person's word about herself counts. Ruth, a voluntary resident, then refuses the shot. Anna must use the declaration Ruth wrote in chapter 2 ("When I am ill... Do not listen to me. Listen to this."), a Ulysses contract, breaking her promise to Walt and exposing Carol's acceptance as invalid. Carol tells her to use it. Carol is fired.
- **Moves from → to:** the county is about to decide Ruth's life → Ruth decided it herself, in a good year; she is inside, on the shot, and the kind act has cost Carol her job.

### 9. Two Counts (Friday 3 to Wednesday 8 November 2028, Belfast and Helsinki)

- **The idea:** a count is the one way of deciding that the losing side can sign, and it works only while the losers sign it.
- **Question:** What makes people accept a result they hate?
- **Goes deep on:** politics and government, conflict, how the past is remembered, faith.
- **Touches:** geography (the wall and its gate), culture, family (three generations voting), law (a secret ballot), technology (what reaches a screen).
- **The world it shows:** Belfast: the border poll under the Good Friday Agreement, passing narrowly in both parts of the island; Niamh's first vote; Brendan and Norman, each given real losses and real doubts; the history carried by people, with only as much as a stranger needs; the peace as well as the wall. Helsinki, four days later: America's election night watched close to live, official channels controlled and spun, the truth coming through phones. Ireland comes together peacefully the same week America comes apart.
- **The choice:** Declan's wife's. A nationalist who has always wanted unity, and a nurse whose patients depend on the North's free health service, which unity would put at risk. In the booth she votes no, and tells no one. The count is won by eighteen thousand.
- **Moves from → to:** two countries counting → one has accepted its count and the other has refused it, and the Toners and Novaks, on the phone, are on either side of that line.

### 10. Gate 12A (7 November 2028, and the week of Saturday 20 January 2029, Dayton)

- **The idea:** everybody fired second. A war nobody declared is made of people each answering someone else's act, and the one way out is a person who says "I did this", whom neither side wants to hear.
- **Question:** Who started it?
- **Goes deep on:** conflict, law, gender (Carol, hunted for her names), family.
- **Touches:** politics, technology (nine seconds of video), economy, labor, faith.
- **The world it shows:** election night: Joel's unit at the county elections board, armed men outside, and what really happens there, which Helsinki saw as a line on a screen. Then two inaugurations, in Washington and Philadelphia. Washington's side is openly hostile to trans people. Washington federalises Ohio's Guard; the Governor countermands, as in California in 2025.
- **The choice:** Joel's. His enlistment oath binds him to obey the President and the Governor, and they give opposite orders; with two Presidents, the law can't say which binds. He goes to the gate to keep his boys from firing, and after a shot nobody can place, fires, and an airman of twenty dies. He says "I fired", never "I returned fire". Carol's two names go up beside his face; Joel tells her to go; she leaves for Joy in Toronto, giving him the District's ledger and leaving him and Dolores behind.
- **Moves from → to:** a country split on paper → a war, a son who has said what he did, and a mother across a border.

### 11. The Winter You're Ten (5 to 13 February 2030, Helsinki)

- **The idea:** what gets through. The farthest journey anyone has made comes home on the day it said it would, in the winter the shortest ones fail.
- **Question:** What can a family reach, from across an ocean and a war?
- **Goes deep on:** mortality, family, technology, other species (begun).
- **Touches:** faith, law, transit, how the past is remembered.
- **The world it shows:** Anna learned Finnish after Responsible Party and became a citizen in 2028; Ruth's permit came on compelling grounds, a parent behind a fence in a country at war, but its card must be collected in person in Washington; Dolores is to take Ruth on the night bus. Then Walt is dying in Kettering, with no one there. The Europa crew come home three days after he dies, carried out in chairs, with something alive from Io that Renée believes may be someone (canon).
- **The choice:** Anna's. Dolores can do only one: take Ruth to Washington for the permit, or sit with Walt so he doesn't die alone. Anna sends her to Walt. He dies on Dolores's phone at the hour of the Sunday call, his last words "Tell Annie everything's fine"; the appointment passes.
- **Moves from → to:** a door to Finland almost open → shut from the other side, a father gone, and the crew home with a stranger nobody yet knows how to treat.

### 12. Known As (13 August to October 2030, Dayton)

- **The idea:** a name against a category. What people are called by those who love them can't be sorted; the letters a ledger keeps can.
- **Question:** What is a person, when the record says otherwise?
- **Goes deep on:** law, society, food and resources, faith (Naomi's church, Miss Bea's hymn).
- **Touches:** family, conflict, mortality, ethics.
- **The world it shows:** Joel guards the District for the Governor's side, with his mother's ledger. The Philadelphia side's Safety Board holds people who won't sign its pledge, its own wrong. Dolores went out of the gate in June to find Ruth's shot and wasn't seen again; Ruth is off it. A colonel's buses are coming to take residents away by their letters.
- **The choice:** Joel's. Write "None available" on the colonel's list, a false record that saves the residents, against his own side. He's shot by them. Naomi takes the old people with nobody north, Ruth among them, and posts the narrow book to Toronto without a letter; Carol finds "Mom" under its last line, and on page one where Ruth went, and calls Anna.
- **Moves from → to:** a ledger any side can sort people with → only what people are called, useless to a man with a bus. It cost Joel's life. Anna finds her mother.

### 13. The Settling (about 2032, Ohio)

- **The idea:** what peace costs the people who are owed justice.
- **Question:** Can a country forgive itself, and who pays for it?
- **Goes deep on:** law and justice, politics, faith, values.
- **Touches:** family, conflict, how the past is remembered, technology (Ares IV), leisure (Bokai's record).
- **The world it shows:** how America's war ends on paper: an amnesty for peace, two new states, a 52-star flag coming in 2033 (canon). Mars and baseball on the news. The country one on paper and still two in spirit.

Carol, back to testify, must accept an amnesty that frees the men who shot Joel, or refuse it and hold up the peace: the nonresistance she was raised in, against justice for her son. ### 14. Toronto (about 2033, Toronto)

- **The idea:** who consents for a child, and who speaks for one who can't yet speak.
- **Question:** What may be done to a child who can't say no, or yes?
- **Goes deep on:** medicine and science, ethics, law, technology (AI).
- **Touches:** faith, values, politics, geography.
- **The world it shows:** Carol and Joy at the Noonien-Singh Institute (canon's Toronto lab); AI-designed gene editing that works; Canada's own law against editing embryos; Khan, a young man. The Eugenics Wars begin.

- **Moves from → to:** Carol keeps a record nobody reads → she knows what the Institute is, and has decided what her silence is for, and what it costs.

### 15. Alma (about 2034, Helsinki and Toronto)

- **The idea:** what a child who is made better is, and what she is owed.
- **Question:** What would you make your child into, to keep her?
- **Goes deep on:** ethics and philosophy, medicine, family.
- **Touches:** faith, values, politics, law, education.
- **The world it shows:** Alma, diagnosed at six (chapter 14) with CLN6, the form of Batten disease that killed Ben's sister Katie at nine, has a few years; Ben learns why his sister died. The era's AI-designed cure installs an engineered gene network that repairs the failing cells and strengthens everything else, and can't yet be separated; the law of her time classes anyone treated as an Augment, whatever their age. Decades later medicine learns to cure without enhancing, which is canon's later rule.
- **The choice:** Ben and Anna's, with Nora, fifteen, holding a view of her own. Let Alma die an ordinary girl, or save her as an Augment: treated outside the law, registered, barred from Finnish school, a target, the family perhaps exiled.
- **Moves from → to:** a girl who is dying → a girl who will live as something her world is learning to fear.

### 17. The Shelter (2053, Helsinki)

- **Goes deep on:** mortality, conflict, construction and resources, how the past is remembered.

### 18. The Sea (the 2050s, the Baltic)

- **The idea:** whether something that might be someone may be used, even to save the world.
- **Question:** What do we owe a stranger who can save us?
- **Goes deep on:** environment, other species, science, ethics.
- **Touches:** family (Nora and Theo), economy, politics, how the past is remembered (Renée).
- **The world it shows:** after the war, the poisoned Baltic; Theo, a marine scientist, and the organism from Io, which can heal the sea and the air (canon) and which Renée believed was someone.

### 19. First Contact (5 April 2063, Bozeman)

- **The idea:** curiosity first: the first step toward the unknown is one ordinary people take with their own hands.
- **Question:** What do ordinary people do on the day everything changes?
- **Goes deep on:** other species, faith, philosophy, environment.
- **Touches:** culture, society, technology, values, how the past is remembered.
- **The world it shows:** Cochrane's warp flight and the Vulcans (canon), from beside it, in a camp that lived through the war by keeping its rifles close. Nora, forty-three, near Cochrane's work but not on his ship; Alma with her.

## Threads

What runs between chapters: something planted in one and paid off or echoed in another. This is the map that keeps the book connected as chapters are added. A thread stays only where it means more each time it returns, and a new chapter is checked against every thread here. Threads from the earlier drafts that still hold are kept here with the new chapter numbers; each is rechecked when its chapters are drafted.

- **Truth and "fine":** Walt's "fine" born on the porch (3), a flaw out of love and pride; it costs him seventeen hours on the floor (8); his last words (11). Anna's promise to Walt (5), broken (8). Ben and Anna tell Nora the truth about the shelter and her grandmother (7), and the family's "fine" stops with them.
- **Ruth's own choices:** the shot (2), the declaration written at the bank (2, used in 8), and leaving for Finland (13). Her arm is Dolores's ("That one's Dolores's", "she won't let a stranger near that arm", "Give me your arm").
- **Ruth's four bars,** *Finlandia*'s hymn ("Be Still, My Soul"): heard by Anna at a Helsinki Independence Day without knowing it (4), played at the Sunday call, explained by Ruth's declaration (8), number 534 in the Methodist hymnal, which Carol sings tenor on and Miss Bea hums in the District (12).
- **Anna's Finnish:** she stays home with Theo instead of learning it (4); without it she can't become a citizen, and only a citizen can bring her mother (7); the permit comes at last on compelling grounds (11).
- **The Sunday call:** set on the porch, "Eleven o'clock, after church. Your mother'll play you something" (3); the hour Walt dies (11).
- **Who holds the screen:** Joy holds up iPads for goodbyes all December (2), and a nurse's iPad tips to a ceiling; Dolores holds up her phone for Walt, changing arms (11).
- **Windows:** Walt's mother at the window of his polio ward in 1952 (2); Nora waving at the front window on the leaving day (3); the visitors' window at the District (8, 12); the crew behind glass (11).
